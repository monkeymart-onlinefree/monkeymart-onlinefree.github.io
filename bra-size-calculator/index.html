The Bra Size Calculator estimates a starting bra band and cup size from your snug underbust and full bust measurements. Enter your measurements in inches or centimeters, choose a sizing system, and the calculator determines the rounded band size, bust-to-band difference, and corresponding cup label.

What Is the Bra Size Calculator?

The Bra Size Calculator is a measurement-based tool for estimating a starting bra size. It uses the circumference directly beneath the bust for the band calculation and the circumference around the fullest part of the bust for the cup calculation.

The calculator supports regional sizing labels including US, UK, EU, FR, and AU/NZ. Because bra sizing is not completely standardized between countries, brands, and bra designs, the result should be treated as a starting size rather than a guaranteed fit. Research has also found that measurement-based sizing can differ from a professionally assessed best fit.

How to Use the Bra Size Calculator

1. Select the Measurement Unit

Choose the unit used for your measurements:

  • Inches
  • Centimeters

The calculator's band and cup calculation is based on inch measurements. When centimeters are selected, the measurements are converted to inches before the sizing calculation.

2. Select the Sizing System

Choose the regional sizing system you want to use. The calculator can map the calculated size to US, UK, EU, FR, and AU/NZ sizing labels.

Cup lettering and band labels can differ between regions, even when the underlying body measurements are the same.

3. Enter Your Snug Underbust

Measure around your torso directly beneath the breasts. Keep the measuring tape level and comfortably snug.

This measurement is used to determine the calculator's band size. Clothing-measurement standards emphasize consistent body-measurement methods because clothing size designations are based on body dimensions rather than finished garment dimensions.

4. Enter Your Full Bust

Measure around the fullest part of your bust while keeping the tape level. The tape should follow the body without significantly compressing the bust.

The calculator compares this measurement with the calculated band size to estimate the cup index. Similar bust and underbust measurements are also used by commercial bra-sizing systems.

Formula / Calculation Method

The calculator first determines an even-numbered band size and then uses the difference between the full bust measurement and that band to estimate the cup size.

Main Formula

Let:

  • U = snug underbust measurement in inches
  • B = full bust measurement in inches
  • Band = calculated band size
  • D = bust-to-band difference in inches
  • C = rounded cup index

The implemented band calculation is:

Band = clamp(28, 52, nearest_even(U))

The bust-to-band difference is:

D = B − Band

The cup index is based on the rounded difference:

C ≈ round(D)

The resulting cup index is then mapped to the applicable regional cup-label sequence.

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
U Snug underbust circumference inches
B Full bust circumference inches
Band Rounded and limited band size US/UK band number
D Difference between bust and band inches
C Rounded cup index used for label lookup index

Unit Conversion

When measurements are entered in centimeters:

Measurement in inches = Measurement in centimeters ÷ 2.54

The converted measurements are then processed using the same inch-based algorithm.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Convert centimeter measurements to inches when necessary.
  2. Take the snug underbust measurement.
  3. Round it to the nearest even-inch band value.
  4. Limit the calculated band to the implemented 28–52 range.
  5. Subtract the calculated band from the full bust measurement.
  6. Round the resulting difference to obtain the cup index.
  7. Map that index to the relevant regional cup label.

Band Size Calculation

The calculator does not use the raw underbust number directly as the final band. It selects the nearest even-inch band.

For example, an underbust measurement of 33.6 inches is closest to a 34-inch band.

The implemented band output is also limited to:

Minimum band: 28
Maximum band: 52

Cup Size Calculation

After the band is established:

Bust-band difference = Full bust − calculated band

The difference is then rounded and used as the cup index.

For common US/UK sizing, for example, a 4-inch bust-to-band difference corresponds to a D cup in the reference sizing table. Regional cup sequences begin to differ at some larger cup sizes, so the same index may not always use the same letters in every system.

Worked Example

Assume the following measurements:

Inputs

  • Measurement unit: Inches
  • Sizing system: US
  • Snug underbust: 33.6 in
  • Full bust: 38.0 in

Step 1: Calculate the Band

The nearest even band to 33.6 inches is:

Band = 34

This value is already within the calculator's 28–52 band range, so no further adjustment is required.

Step 2: Calculate the Bust-Band Difference

D = B − Band

D = 38.0 − 34

D = 4.0 inches

Step 3: Determine the Cup Index

C ≈ round(4.0)

C = 4

A 4-inch difference maps to a D cup in both the US and UK reference sequences at this point in the sizing scale.

Final Result

Band size: 34
Bust-band difference: 4.0 inches
US cup label: D
UK cup label: D

The estimated starting size is therefore 34D.

Understanding Your Results

US / UK Band

The band result is the even-numbered band produced from your snug underbust measurement after the calculator's rounding and 28–52 range limit are applied.

It is not simply your exact underbust measurement.

Bust-Band Difference

This is the difference between your full bust measurement and the calculated band, not the original raw underbust measurement.

This value is used to determine the cup index.

US Cup Label

The US cup result converts the calculated cup index into the US cup-letter sequence.

Cup labels are not universally interchangeable between countries or manufacturers.

UK Cup Label

The UK result uses the UK cup-letter sequence. US and UK cup labels match at some sizes but can diverge higher in the sequence.

For that reason, check the sizing system when comparing bras from different regions.

Assumptions and Limitations

The Bra Size Calculator provides a starting-size estimate, not a guarantee that every bra carrying that label will fit.

Bra sizing is not globally standardized. Garment construction, cup shape, wire width, fabric stretch, breast shape, and differences between manufacturers can all affect actual fit. ISO sizing standards also distinguish body measurements from the dimensions and design choices used in finished garments.

The calculator also uses rounding. Measurements close to a band or cup rounding boundary can therefore produce a different result after a relatively small measurement change.

The implemented band calculation is limited to 28 through 52. If the calculated value would fall outside that range, the calculator uses the applicable boundary rather than producing a smaller or larger band. This can make the result less useful for someone whose actual size falls beyond the calculator's supported range.

Measurement-based sizing alone cannot evaluate how a specific bra fits your body. Research comparing traditional measurements with professional fitting criteria found meaningful differences between measurement-derived sizes and assessed best fit.

A well-fitting band should generally stay level rather than ride upward, while the cups should contain the bust without obvious spilling or gaping. These fit checks can help determine whether you should try a nearby size or a different bra shape.

Frequently Asked Questions

What measurements do I need for the Bra Size Calculator?

You need a snug underbust measurement taken directly beneath the bust and a full bust measurement taken around the fullest part of the bust. Both measurements should use the same unit.

Can I enter measurements in centimeters?

Yes. The calculator accepts the selected measurement unit. Centimeter measurements are converted to inches before the inch-based band and cup calculations are applied.

Why is the calculated band different from my exact underbust measurement?

The tool rounds the snug underbust measurement to the nearest even-inch band. It then limits that band to the calculator's supported range of 28–52.

Why can the same bra size fit differently between brands?

A size label does not fully describe the shape or construction of a bra. Manufacturers can use different cup shapes, wire widths, materials, and sizing conventions. International clothing standards also distinguish body-size designation from the measurements chosen by garment designers and manufacturers.

What should I do if the cups spill or gape?

Spilling can indicate that the cup is too small or that its shape does not suit you. Gaping may indicate a cup that is too large or a mismatch between the bra's shape and your body. The band should also remain level and comfortably snug.

Why do US and UK cup sizes sometimes have different letters?

Different regional sizing systems use different cup-label sequences. The calculator first derives a measurement-based cup index and then maps that index to the appropriate regional label.

Is a calculated bra size guaranteed to fit?

No. The result is a useful starting point for trying bras. Actual fit should also be checked using the band position, cup containment, wire placement, and overall comfort because measurement formulas alone cannot account for every body or bra design.
