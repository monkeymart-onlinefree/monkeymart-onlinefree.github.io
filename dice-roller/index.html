What Is a Virtual Dice Roller?

A virtual dice roller simulates the numbered outcome of physical dice. Standard mode uses six-sided dice. Custom mode lets you specify a different number of equally likely integer outcomes from 1 through the selected number of sides.

How the Random Roll Works

For a fair die with S sides, each face has theoretical probability 1/S on each independent roll. This page uses the browser’s cryptographic random-number generator and rejection sampling to avoid simple modulo bias when mapping random integers to die faces.

P(specific face) = 1 ÷ S

Dice Roll Example

If three six-sided dice produce 2, 5, and 6, the total is 13, the average is 4.33, and the observed range runs from 2 to 6. A different roll may produce any valid combination; past results do not change the theoretical probabilities of the next independent roll.

Common Uses

Virtual dice are useful for tabletop games, classroom probability demonstrations, random turn order, prototypes, and situations where physical dice are unavailable. For regulated gambling or audited random selection, use a system that meets the applicable legal and technical requirements instead of a general-purpose webpage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I roll a d20?

Yes. Choose Custom sides, enter 20, select the number of dice, and roll.

Are all faces equally likely?

The generator maps cryptographically random 32-bit values to the requested face range using rejection sampling, so the software does not intentionally favor a face.

What is the probability of rolling a 6 on a normal die?

For an ideal fair six-sided die, the probability of any specific face is 1/6, or about 16.67%.

Does a previous roll affect the next roll?

No. Each generated roll is treated independently; a previous result does not make a specific future face due.
