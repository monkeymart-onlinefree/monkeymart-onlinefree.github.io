What is a Day of the Week Calculator?

A Day of the Week Calculator tells you whether a calendar date falls on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, or Sunday. It is useful for checking birthdays, anniversaries, historical dates, future schedules, and any date where the weekday matters.

How the calculation works

This page uses Gregorian calendar arithmetic in the browser. A classic manual method is Zeller’s congruence, which converts the day, adjusted month, year-within-century, and century into a value modulo 7.

h = [q + floor(13(m+1)/5) + K + floor(K/4) + floor(J/4) − 2J] mod 7

In the standard mapping, 0 is Saturday, 1 is Sunday, 2 is Monday, and so on. January and February are treated as months 13 and 14 of the previous year.

What do the extra results mean?

ISO week is the week number under ISO week-date rules. Day of year counts January 1 as day 1. Leap year follows the Gregorian rule: years divisible by 4 are leap years, except century years unless they are also divisible by 400.

Example

July 4, 1776 falls on a Thursday in the Gregorian calendar. January 1, 2000 falls on a Saturday. For dates before a region adopted the Gregorian calendar, the historical local weekday record can require calendar-conversion context.

Limitations

The calculator uses the Gregorian calendar and does not reconstruct every country’s historical transition from the Julian calendar. It also does not interpret religious, lunar, or other non-Gregorian calendars.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I find the day of the week for any date?

Enter the date and select Find weekday. The result gives the weekday plus useful calendar details.

Does ISO 8601 use the Gregorian calendar?

ISO 8601 defines date and time representations using Gregorian calendar dates for information interchange.

Why can historical dates be tricky?

Countries adopted the Gregorian calendar at different times, so a proleptic Gregorian date can differ from the calendar actually used locally at the time.

Is the calculation done online?

The JavaScript calculation runs locally in your browser; the page does not need to submit the date to calculate the result.
