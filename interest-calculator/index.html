The Interest Calculator estimates how an initial amount can grow with compound interest and regular monthly contributions. Enter your starting amount, monthly contribution, annual nominal rate, compounding frequency, years, tax-on-interest estimate, and inflation estimate to see your projected ending balance, total principal, interest earned, and adjusted results.

What Is an Interest Calculator?

An Interest Calculator estimates how money may grow when interest is repeatedly added to the balance. With compound interest, future interest is calculated on the existing balance, which can include previously accumulated interest.

This calculator is useful for savings and simplified investment-growth projections. It combines an initial amount with recurring monthly contributions and lets you compare the money contributed with the growth produced by the entered interest assumptions.

How to Use the Interest Calculator

Enter the following values in the order shown by the calculator:

  1. Initial amount ($): Enter the amount available at the beginning of the calculation.
  2. Monthly contribution ($): Enter the amount added each month.
  3. Annual nominal rate (%): Enter the stated annual interest or growth rate as a percentage. For example, enter 6 for 6%.
  4. Compound frequency: Select how often the nominal annual rate compounds from the options available in the calculator.
  5. Years: Enter the length of the projection in years.
  6. Tax on interest estimate (%): Enter an estimated percentage to include the calculator's simplified after-tax result.
  7. Inflation estimate (%): Enter an assumed annual inflation percentage for the inflation-adjusted result.

The tax and inflation percentages are assumptions entered by you. The calculator does not automatically determine a tax rate or inflation rate based on your location or circumstances.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Compound Interest Formula

Without additional monthly contributions, compound growth can be written as:

A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)

Where:

  • A = ending balance, in dollars
  • P = initial amount, in dollars
  • r = annual nominal interest rate as a decimal
  • n = number of compounding periods per year
  • t = time in years

For example, a nominal annual rate of 6% is converted to decimal form as:

r = 6 ÷ 100 = 0.06

Equivalent Monthly Rate

Because this calculator accepts monthly contributions, the selected nominal compounding frequency is converted to an equivalent monthly rate.

m = (1 + r/n)^(n/12) − 1

Where:

  • m = equivalent monthly interest rate
  • r = annual nominal rate as a decimal
  • n = number of nominal compounding periods per year
  • 12 = months per year

This preserves the growth implied by the selected nominal rate and compounding frequency while allowing the balance to be processed monthly.

Monthly Calculation Sequence

The starting balance is:

B₀ = P

For each month, the existing balance grows first and the monthly contribution is then added:

Bₖ = Bₖ₋₁(1 + m) + C

Where:

  • Bₖ = balance after month k
  • Bₖ₋₁ = previous month's balance
  • m = equivalent monthly rate
  • C = monthly contribution, in dollars

The number of monthly calculation periods is:

M = 12t

This sequence means the recurring contribution is added after that month's growth step.

Equivalent Formula With Monthly Contributions

When m ≠ 0, the same monthly sequence can be expressed as:

Ending Balance = P(1 + m)^M + C × [(1 + m)^M − 1] / m

The first term represents growth of the initial amount. The second represents the accumulated value of the recurring monthly contributions.

If the interest rate is 0%, the compound-growth formula is not required:

Ending Balance = P + C × M

Total Principal Contributed

Total principal is the amount contributed directly rather than earned through interest:

Total Principal = P + C × M

Interest Earned

Interest earned is calculated from the difference between the projected balance and total principal:

Interest Earned = Ending Balance − Total Principal

Tax Estimate

The calculator includes a Tax on interest estimate (%) input and reports an After simple tax estimate result.

This is a simplified planning adjustment based on the percentage entered by the user. It does not determine actual tax liability, tax brackets, deductions, exemptions, account-specific treatment, or jurisdiction-specific tax rules.

Inflation Adjustment

The calculator also reports an Inflation-adjusted value using the annual inflation estimate entered by the user.

The inflation assumption is treated as constant for the projection. It is not a prediction of future inflation and does not automatically use current inflation data.

Worked Example

Suppose you enter:

  • Initial amount: $10,000
  • Monthly contribution: $200
  • Annual nominal rate: 6%
  • Compound frequency: Quarterly
  • Years: 5
  • Tax on interest estimate: 0%
  • Inflation estimate: 0%

Using 0% for tax and inflation keeps the example focused on the compound-interest calculation itself.

Step 1: Convert the Annual Rate

r = 6 ÷ 100 = 0.06

Quarterly compounding means:

n = 4

Step 2: Calculate the Equivalent Monthly Rate

m = (1 + 0.06/4)^(4/12) − 1
m = (1.015)^(1/3) − 1
m ≈ 0.0049752063

The equivalent monthly rate is approximately 0.4975%.

Step 3: Calculate the Number of Months

M = 12 × 5
M = 60 months

Step 4: Calculate the Ending Balance

Ending Balance = 10,000(1.0049752063)^60 + 200 × [(1.0049752063)^60 − 1] ÷ 0.0049752063
Ending Balance ≈ $27,411.89

Step 5: Calculate Total Principal

Total Principal = 10,000 + (200 × 60)
Total Principal = $22,000.00

Step 6: Calculate Interest Earned

Interest Earned = 27,411.89 − 22,000.00
Interest Earned = $5,411.89

With a tax estimate of 0%, the simple after-tax result remains $27,411.89.

With an inflation estimate of 0%, the inflation-adjusted result also remains $27,411.89.

Understanding Your Results

Projected Ending Balance

The projected ending balance is the estimated value at the end of the selected period. It includes the initial amount, monthly contributions, and compound growth.

It is a mathematical projection based on the values entered, not a guaranteed future return.

Total Principal Contributed

Total principal shows how much money was contributed directly.

It includes the initial amount plus all monthly contributions made during the projection.

Interest Earned

Interest earned is the portion of the ending balance above the total principal contributed.

It helps separate growth produced by the assumed interest rate from money supplied directly by the user.

After Simple Tax Estimate

This result gives a simplified view of the balance after considering the tax-on-interest estimate entered in the calculator.

It should not be treated as an exact tax calculation or personalized tax advice.

Inflation-Adjusted Value

The inflation-adjusted value provides additional context for the future balance using the annual inflation assumption entered.

Inflation can reduce purchasing power over time, so the adjusted result can be useful when reviewing longer-term projections.

Assumptions and Limitations

  • The annual nominal interest rate is assumed to remain constant throughout the calculation.
  • Monthly contributions are assumed to remain constant.
  • Contributions occur monthly.
  • The existing balance is compounded before that month's contribution is added.
  • The selected nominal compounding frequency is converted to an equivalent monthly rate.
  • The tax estimate is simplified and does not model a complete tax system.
  • The inflation estimate assumes one constant annual inflation rate.
  • Real investment returns can increase or decrease instead of following a constant rate.
  • Investment fees, transaction costs, account charges, penalties, and similar expenses are not separate calculator inputs.
  • Banks and financial institutions may use different accrual conventions, interest-crediting schedules, day-count methods, or rounding procedures.
  • Results are estimates for planning and educational use and should not be interpreted as guaranteed returns.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is compound interest?

Compound interest is growth calculated on the existing balance, including previously accumulated interest. This allows interest to generate additional interest over time.

How are monthly contributions handled?

The calculator processes the balance monthly. It first applies the equivalent monthly growth rate to the existing balance and then adds the monthly contribution.

What does annual nominal rate mean?

The annual nominal rate is the stated annual rate before accounting for the full effect of within-year compounding. The calculator combines this rate with the selected compound frequency when determining equivalent monthly growth.

What is the difference between total principal and interest earned?

Total principal is the money contributed directly: the initial amount plus monthly contributions. Interest earned is the projected ending balance minus that principal.

Does the calculator automatically know my tax rate?

No. The Tax on interest estimate (%) is entered by the user. The resulting value is a simplified estimate rather than an individualized tax calculation.

Does the calculator use the current inflation rate?

No. Inflation is entered as an assumption by the user. The calculator does not automatically fetch or predict future inflation.

Why can another interest calculator give a different answer?

Results can differ because calculators may use different contribution timing, compounding conventions, rate conversions, rounding methods, tax assumptions, inflation methods, or financial-product rules.
