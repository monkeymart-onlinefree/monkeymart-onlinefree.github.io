Use the BMI Calculator to calculate adult Body Mass Index from your height and weight. The calculator shows your BMI, adult BMI category, healthy-weight range, BMI Prime, and Ponderal Index using metric or U.S. customary measurements.

What Is the BMI Calculator?

The BMI Calculator estimates Body Mass Index (BMI), a measure of body weight relative to height. It is designed for adult BMI interpretation and can help you quickly compare your result with standard adult BMI categories.

BMI is a screening measure rather than a direct measurement of body fat. CDC recommends considering BMI together with other health information when evaluating an individual's health.

How to Use the BMI Calculator

Enter the measurements requested by the calculator:

  1. Age: Enter your age in years. Age does not change the adult BMI formula, but it matters when deciding whether adult BMI categories are appropriate.
  2. Weight (kg): In metric mode, enter your weight in kilograms.
  3. Height (cm): Enter your height in centimeters.
  4. Weight (lb): In U.S. customary mode, enter your weight in pounds.
  5. Height: Enter your height using the available U.S. customary height fields. The calculation uses height converted to inches.

Adult BMI categories apply to people aged 20 years and older. People aged 2–19 require BMI-for-age interpretation rather than the standard adult cutoffs.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main BMI Formula

For metric measurements:

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ [height (m)]²

When height is entered in centimeters:

BMI = [weight (kg) ÷ height (cm)²] × 10,000

For U.S. customary measurements:

BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ [height (in)]²

These are the standard BMI equations published by CDC.

Variables

  • BMI = Body Mass Index, expressed as kg/m²
  • weight (kg) = body weight in kilograms
  • height (m) = height in meters
  • height (cm) = height in centimeters
  • weight (lb) = body weight in pounds
  • height (in) = total height in inches
  • 703 = conversion factor used in the U.S. customary BMI equation

Calculation Sequence

  1. Read the entered height and weight.
  2. Convert height to the unit required by the selected formula.
  3. Square the height.
  4. Divide weight by height squared.
  5. Apply the 703 conversion factor when U.S. customary units are used.
  6. Compare the BMI with the adult BMI category ranges.
  7. Calculate the calculator's additional outputs, including the healthy-weight range, BMI Prime, and Ponderal Index.

Healthy-Weight Range

The adult CDC healthy-weight BMI category begins at 18.5 and extends to, but does not include, 25.

The BMI equation can be rearranged to convert these BMI boundaries into weights for a particular height:

Weight (kg) = BMI × [height (m)]²

Lower reference weight:

18.5 × height²

Upper boundary:

25 × height²

Because BMI 25 starts the overweight category, the healthy-weight interval ends just below this upper boundary.

BMI Prime

BMI Prime expresses BMI relative to the commonly used BMI 25 upper reference boundary:

BMI Prime = BMI ÷ 25

A BMI Prime of 1.00 corresponds to a BMI of 25. A value such as 0.90 means the BMI equals 90% of that reference value. BMI Prime is a rescaling of BMI and therefore shares BMI's limitations. The BMI Prime concept was described by James Gadzik in 2006.

Ponderal Index

The Ponderal Index, also called the Rohrer or Corpulence Index in research literature, uses height cubed rather than height squared:

Ponderal Index = weight (kg) ÷ [height (m)]³

It is another height-and-weight index and should be treated as supplementary information rather than a diagnosis or direct body-fat measurement.

Worked Example

Suppose an adult enters:

Inputs

  • Age: 30 years
  • Weight: 70 kg
  • Height: 175 cm

Step 1: Convert Height

175 cm ÷ 100 = 1.75 m

Step 2: Calculate BMI

BMI = 70 ÷ (1.75²)

BMI = 70 ÷ 3.0625

BMI = 22.8571

Rounded to one decimal place:

BMI ≈ 22.9 kg/m²

Step 3: Interpret the Result

A BMI of 22.9 falls within the CDC adult Healthy Weight category of 18.5 to less than 25.

For a height of 1.75 m, the corresponding reference weight boundaries are:

Lower boundary:

18.5 × 1.75² = 56.7 kg

Upper boundary:

25 × 1.75² = 76.6 kg

The upper value represents the BMI 25 boundary, so the healthy-weight category ends just below it.

BMI Prime:

22.8571 ÷ 25 = 0.91

Ponderal Index:

70 ÷ 1.75³ = 13.1 kg/m³

Final Result

The main BMI result is approximately 22.9 kg/m², which is within the standard adult Healthy Weight category.

Understanding Your Results

Your BMI

Your BMI is the main result calculated from your weight and height. It is expressed in kg/m², including when U.S. customary measurements are used as inputs.

BMI Category

For adults aged 20 and older, the calculator compares BMI with standard CDC adult categories:

BMI Adult Category
Below 18.5 Underweight
18.5 to less than 25 Healthy Weight
25 to less than 30 Overweight
30 or greater Obesity
30 to less than 35 Class 1 Obesity
35 to less than 40 Class 2 Obesity
40 or greater Class 3 Obesity

These categories are screening classifications and are not a medical diagnosis.

Healthy-Weight Range

This output shows the approximate body-weight interval corresponding to the standard adult Healthy Weight BMI range for your entered height.

It should not be treated as a personalized ideal-weight prescription.

BMI Prime

BMI Prime shows your BMI as a ratio to the BMI 25 reference point.

For example:

  • BMI Prime 1.00 corresponds to BMI 25.
  • BMI Prime 1.20 corresponds to BMI 30.
  • BMI Prime 0.80 corresponds to BMI 20.

It does not provide different health information from BMI; it presents the same underlying measurement in another format.

Ponderal Index

The Ponderal Index compares weight with height cubed instead of height squared. It is an additional anthropometric index and should not replace a broader health assessment.

Current Adult BMI Reference Data

Current guidance checked: September 2026.

CDC continues to use the following standard categories for adults aged 20 years and older:

  • Underweight: below 18.5
  • Healthy Weight: 18.5 to less than 25
  • Overweight: 25 to less than 30
  • Obesity: 30 or greater

For children and teenagers aged 2–19, BMI must instead be interpreted using age- and sex-specific BMI-for-age information.

Assumptions and Limitations

BMI is based only on height and weight. It does not directly measure body fat or distinguish between fat, muscle, bone, and other body tissues. Someone with substantial muscle mass, for example, may have a higher BMI without having the same body composition as another person with the same BMI.

The adult category ranges are intended for adults aged 20 and older. Children and teenagers require BMI-for-age interpretation.

Measurement accuracy also matters. Incorrect height or weight entries can noticeably change the result, especially because height is squared in the BMI calculation.

BMI Prime is derived from BMI and therefore inherits the same limitations. The Ponderal Index is also based only on height and weight and does not directly measure body composition.

The results are screening estimates and should not be interpreted as a diagnosis, treatment recommendation, or substitute for individualized medical care.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does age change the BMI formula?

No. The standard BMI calculation uses height and weight. Age is important because adult BMI categories apply to people aged 20 and older, while children and teenagers require BMI-for-age interpretation.

Can this adult BMI calculator be used for children?

The mathematical BMI value can be calculated from height and weight, but standard adult BMI categories should not be used for children or teenagers aged 2–19. CDC uses BMI-for-age interpretation for this population.

Is BMI a measurement of body fat?

No. BMI is calculated from height and weight. It can be useful as a screening measure, but it does not directly measure body fat or body composition.

Will metric and U.S. units give the same BMI?

Yes, provided the measurements are equivalent and converted correctly. The metric and U.S. customary formulas calculate the same underlying BMI.

What does a BMI Prime of 1 mean?

A BMI Prime of 1.00 corresponds to a BMI of 25 because BMI Prime uses BMI 25 as its reference value.

What is the difference between BMI and Ponderal Index?

BMI divides weight by height squared. The Ponderal Index divides weight by height cubed. Both are based on height and weight, but they scale height differently.

Why can my BMI differ from another calculator or clinical result?

Differences may occur because of measurement precision, unit conversion, rounding, or differences in how a tool displays category boundaries. A clinical assessment may also consider information that BMI cannot include, such as body composition, medical history, physical examination findings, and laboratory results.
