Use the Horsepower Calculator to calculate mechanical horsepower from torque and engine speed, or convert a power value between horsepower, kilowatts, watts, and metric horsepower. Enter torque in pound-feet and RPM for the horsepower calculation, or enter an amount and select its starting power unit for conversion.

What Is the Horsepower Calculator?

The Horsepower Calculator measures mechanical power using torque and rotational speed. It is useful for engines, motors, rotating machinery, and other applications where torque in lb·ft and speed in RPM are known.

The calculator also converts power values between supported horsepower and metric/SI power units.

How to Use the Horsepower Calculator

For the torque and RPM calculation:

  1. Torque (lb·ft): Enter the measured or specified torque in pound-feet.
  2. Engine speed (RPM): Enter the rotational speed in revolutions per minute.

For power conversion:

  1. Amount: Enter the numerical power value you want to convert.
  2. From: Select the unit of the entered amount, such as mechanical horsepower, kilowatts, watts, or metric horsepower.

Use measurements from the same operating condition when calculating horsepower from torque and RPM. For example, torque measured at one RPM should not be combined with an unrelated RPM value.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

Mechanical horsepower is calculated as:

hp = Torque × RPM ÷ 5252.113

Where:

  • hp = mechanical horsepower
  • Torque = torque in pound-feet (lb·ft)
  • RPM = revolutions per minute
  • 5252.113 = conversion constant connecting lb·ft, revolutions per minute, and mechanical horsepower

The constant is based on mechanical horsepower being defined as 550 foot-pound-force per second and one revolution representing 2π radians. NIST lists mechanical horsepower as approximately 745.6999 watts.

The equivalent expanded relationship is:

hp = (Torque × 2π × RPM) ÷ 33,000

because:

550 ft·lb/s × 60 s/min = 33,000 ft·lb/min

and:

33,000 ÷ 2π ≈ 5252.113

Variables

Variable Meaning Unit
hp Mechanical power horsepower
Torque Rotational torque lb·ft
RPM Rotational speed revolutions per minute
π Mathematical constant pi dimensionless

Calculation Sequence

  1. Read the torque value in lb·ft.
  2. Read the rotational speed in RPM.
  3. Multiply torque by RPM.
  4. Divide the result by 5252.113.
  5. Return the mechanical horsepower result.

Power Unit Conversion

The converter uses standard power-unit relationships. A watt is the SI derived unit of power and is equivalent to one joule per second.

NIST provides these conversion factors:

  • 1 mechanical hp ≈ 745.6999 W
  • 1 metric hp ≈ 735.4988 W
  • 1 kW = 1,000 W

A power value can therefore be converted through watts as the common reference unit.

For example:

Watts = Mechanical hp × 745.6999
Mechanical hp = Watts ÷ 745.6999
Kilowatts = Watts ÷ 1,000
Metric hp = Watts ÷ 735.4988

Mechanical horsepower and metric horsepower are different units, so they should not be treated as identical.

Worked Example

Suppose an engine produces 300 lb·ft of torque at 4,000 RPM.

Inputs

  • Torque = 300 lb·ft
  • Engine speed = 4,000 RPM

Step 1

Apply the horsepower formula:

hp = Torque × RPM ÷ 5252.113

Step 2

Substitute the values:

hp = 300 × 4,000 ÷ 5252.113
hp = 1,200,000 ÷ 5252.113

Step 3

Calculate the result:

hp ≈ 228.4795

Final Result

≈ 228.48 horsepower

Based on these inputs, 300 lb·ft of torque at 4,000 RPM corresponds to approximately 228.48 mechanical horsepower.

Understanding Your Results

Mechanical Horsepower

The horsepower result represents the rate of mechanical work implied by the entered torque and rotational speed.

Higher torque does not automatically mean higher horsepower because RPM is also part of the calculation. Increasing either torque or RPM increases horsepower when the other value remains unchanged.

Converted Power Values

The conversion result expresses the same amount of power in another supported unit. Changing from horsepower to watts or kilowatts changes the numerical unit representation, not the underlying amount of power.

Mechanical horsepower should also be distinguished from metric horsepower because they use different definitions and conversion factors.

Assumptions and Limitations

The horsepower result is only as accurate as the torque and RPM values entered.

The formula calculates power from torque at a specific rotational speed. It does not determine where or how the torque was measured.

Reported engine power can vary depending on whether measurements are taken at the engine or wheels. Dynamometer type, drivetrain losses, atmospheric conditions, correction procedures, and whether a figure is described as gross, net, crank, or wheel horsepower can also affect published values.

The calculator does not automatically estimate drivetrain losses or convert a wheel-horsepower measurement into crank horsepower.

Power-unit conversions are mathematical conversions between unit definitions. They do not change or estimate the physical performance of an engine.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is 5252.113 used in the horsepower formula?

The value comes from combining the mechanical horsepower definition of 33,000 foot-pound-force per minute with the 2π radians in one complete revolution:

33,000 ÷ 2π ≈ 5252.113

That gives the commonly used relationship:

hp = Torque × RPM ÷ 5252.113
Why are horsepower and torque equal near 5,252 RPM?

When torque is measured in lb·ft and power is measured in mechanical horsepower, setting RPM to approximately 5252.113 causes the divisor and RPM to cancel:

hp ≈ Torque

This numerical crossover depends on these specific units.

Is one horsepower equal to one metric horsepower?

No. NIST lists mechanical horsepower at approximately 745.6999 W and metric horsepower at approximately 735.4988 W.

How many kilowatts are in one mechanical horsepower?

Using the NIST mechanical horsepower conversion:

1 hp ≈ 745.6999 W ≈ 0.7456999 kW

.

Can this calculator determine wheel horsepower?

It calculates horsepower from the torque and RPM you enter. If the torque measurement represents wheel torque under an appropriate measurement method, the result relates to that measurement point. The calculator itself does not estimate or correct drivetrain losses.

Why can a dynamometer result differ from this calculation?

Differences can come from torque measurement accuracy, test equipment, drivetrain losses, atmospheric conditions, correction standards, and the location where power is measured.

Does more torque always mean more horsepower?

Not by itself. Horsepower depends on both torque and RPM. An engine can produce less torque at a higher RPM and still produce more horsepower because rotational speed is part of the formula.
