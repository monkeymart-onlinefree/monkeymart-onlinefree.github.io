What Is a Horsepower Calculator?

A horsepower calculator converts rotational torque and engine speed into mechanical power and also converts between horsepower, watts, kilowatts, and metric horsepower. Mechanical horsepower is a unit of power, which is the rate at which work is done.

Torque and RPM Horsepower Formula

hp = Torque (lb·ft) × RPM ÷ 5252.113

The constant comes from the mechanical-horsepower definition of 550 foot-pound-force per second combined with one revolution equaling 2π radians.

Example

An engine producing 300 lb·ft at 5,000 RPM develops about 285.6 mechanical horsepower using the torque-RPM relationship. That is roughly 213 kW.

Horsepower to Watts

NIST lists mechanical horsepower (550 ft·lbf/s) at approximately 745.6999 W. Metric horsepower (PS) is smaller, at approximately 735.4988 W. Because multiple horsepower definitions exist, always identify the convention being used.

Limitations

Torque-based power is only as accurate as the torque and RPM measurement. Engine dynamometer, chassis dynamometer, correction method, drivetrain loss, atmospheric conditions, and whether the figure is gross, net, crank, or wheel horsepower can change reported values.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate horsepower from torque and RPM?

For torque in pound-feet, mechanical horsepower is approximately torque × RPM ÷ 5252. The constant comes from the relationship between rotational work and 550 foot-pounds per second per horsepower.

How many watts are in one mechanical horsepower?

NIST lists mechanical horsepower, defined as 550 ft·lbf/s, as approximately 745.6999 watts.

Is crank horsepower the same as wheel horsepower?

No. Engine or crank horsepower is measured before drivetrain losses, while wheel horsepower is measured at the driven wheels. Test method and correction standards matter when comparing values.
