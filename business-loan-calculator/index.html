What Is a Business Loan Calculator?

A business loan calculator estimates the periodic payment, total scheduled payments, interest cost, and the effect of upfront fees on the borrowing cost of a fixed-rate amortizing loan.

Business Loan Payment Formula

For monthly amortization, payment is based on principal, monthly interest rate, and number of payments: P × r ÷ [1 − (1+r)^−n]. Total interest equals all scheduled payments minus original principal.

Fee-adjusted APR estimate

This page treats upfront fees as reducing net proceeds received by the borrower, then solves for the nominal annual percentage rate, based on the solved monthly rate, that equates those net proceeds with the scheduled payment stream. This helps illustrate why fees can make effective borrowing cost higher than the stated note rate.

Example

A $10,000 five-year loan at 10% has a fixed monthly payment based on 60 months. If $1,250 in upfront fees are deducted from proceeds, the borrower receives less cash while making the same scheduled payments, which raises the fee-adjusted annualized cost.

What to Compare on Business Loans

Compare more than the headline rate. Review payment frequency, term, fees, prepayment rules, collateral, guarantees, variable-rate provisions, late charges, and the amount of cash actually received.

Limitations

This model assumes a fully amortizing fixed-rate monthly loan. It does not model daily or weekly repayment, interest-only periods, balloon payments, variable rates, SBA guarantee fees, prepayment penalties, compounding conventions, or lender-specific APR disclosure rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why can APR be higher than the stated interest rate?

Upfront finance charges reduce the amount of usable proceeds while the scheduled payments may stay the same. When those fees are included in the cost calculation, the annualized borrowing rate can be higher than the note rate.

Does this calculator work for daily or weekly business loans?

No. This version assumes monthly amortizing payments. Short-term products with daily or weekly debits can use different pricing methods, so their stated factor rate or fee should not be treated as directly equivalent to APR.

Does a lower monthly payment mean a cheaper loan?

Not always. Extending the term can lower each payment while increasing total interest. Compare total payments, fees, annualized cost, and loan structure—not only the monthly amount.
