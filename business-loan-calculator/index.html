Use the Business Loan Calculator to estimate the regular payment and total interest for a fixed-rate business loan. Enter the loan amount, annual interest rate, repayment term, and applicable upfront fees to understand the estimated cost of borrowing.

What Is the Business Loan Calculator?

A Business Loan Calculator estimates the cost of repaying a business loan with a fixed interest rate and regular monthly payments.

It can help business owners compare financing scenarios, estimate the payment required for a selected term, understand total interest costs, and see how upfront fees may affect the overall cost of a loan.

The calculator is designed for planning and comparison. Actual lender calculations can differ because of payment timing, compounding methods, fees, loan structures, and lender-specific rules.

How to Use the Business Loan Calculator

Enter the information from your loan offer or financing scenario.

Loan Amount

Enter the principal amount you plan to borrow. This is the starting balance used to calculate the scheduled loan payment.

Interest Rate

Enter the annual interest rate as a percentage. This is the stated rate before the calculator considers any separate upfront fees.

For monthly amortization, the annual rate is converted to a monthly rate by dividing it by 12.

Term in Years

Enter the number of complete years over which the loan will be repaid.

Extra Months

Use this field when the repayment term includes additional months beyond the full years.

For example, a term of 3 years and 6 months equals 42 monthly payments.

Origination Fee

Enter the applicable origination fee shown in your financing terms. Origination fees are charges that may be assessed for processing or arranging the loan.

Documentation Fee

Enter any upfront documentation or paperwork fee associated with the loan.

Other Upfront Fees

Enter other applicable upfront loan charges that you want included in the calculator's fee estimate.

Fees can vary significantly by lender and loan product, so use the amounts stated in the actual offer you are evaluating.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

For a fully amortizing loan with monthly payments, the calculator uses:

Payment = P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)^−n]

Variables

  • Payment = required monthly principal-and-interest payment
  • P = original loan principal
  • r = monthly interest rate as a decimal
  • n = total number of monthly payments

The monthly interest rate is:

r = Annual interest rate ÷ 12

When the annual rate is entered as a percentage, it is first converted to decimal form.

For example:

8% = 0.08

and:

r = 0.08 ÷ 12

The total number of payments is based on the entered years and additional months:

n = (Years × 12) + Extra months

Calculation Sequence

  1. Convert the annual percentage rate to decimal form.
  2. Divide the annual rate by 12 to determine the monthly interest rate.
  3. Convert the repayment term into the total number of months.
  4. Apply the fixed-payment amortization formula.
  5. Calculate total scheduled payments.
  6. Subtract the original principal from scheduled payments to determine total interest.
  7. Account for the entered upfront fees in the calculator's supporting cost estimates.

Total Scheduled Payments

For a monthly amortizing loan:

Total Scheduled Payments = Monthly Payment × Number of Payments

This represents the total of the scheduled principal-and-interest payments over the full loan term.

Total Interest

The calculator determines total interest as:

Total Interest = Total Scheduled Payments − Original Principal

This is the interest generated by the fixed-rate amortization schedule before separately entered upfront fees.

Upfront Fees

Origination, documentation, and other upfront fees can increase the total cost of obtaining financing even when they do not change the scheduled principal-and-interest payment.

The calculator can use these entered charges when presenting supporting loan-cost information.

Fee-Adjusted APR

The calculator may also show an illustrative fee-adjusted APR to help compare the borrowing cost when upfront fees are considered.

This figure should be treated as an estimate rather than a lender's official regulatory APR disclosure because exact APR calculations can depend on fee classification, payment dates, compounding conventions, and lender-specific methods.

Worked Example

Suppose a business borrows $100,000 at a fixed annual interest rate of 8% for 5 years.

To demonstrate the core amortization calculation, assume no additional months or upfront fees.

Inputs

  • Loan amount: $100,000
  • Annual interest rate: 8%
  • Term: 5 years
  • Extra months: 0
  • Origination fee: 0
  • Documentation fee: $0
  • Other upfront fees: $0

Step 1: Find the Monthly Rate

Convert 8% to decimal form:

0.08

Then divide by 12:

r = 0.08 ÷ 12
r = 0.0066666667

Step 2: Find the Number of Payments

n = 5 × 12
n = 60 payments

Step 3: Calculate the Monthly Payment

Using:

Payment = P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)^−n]

Substitute the values:

Payment = 100,000 × 0.0066666667 ÷ [1 − (1.0066666667)^−60]

The estimated monthly payment is approximately:

$2,027.64

Step 4: Calculate Total Payments

$2,027.6394 × 60 = $121,658.37

Using the unrounded payment during the calculation avoids early-rounding differences.

Step 5: Calculate Total Interest

$121,658.37 − $100,000 = $21,658.37

Final Result

For this example:

  • Estimated monthly payment: $2,027.64
  • Total scheduled payments: $121,658.37
  • Estimated total interest: $21,658.37

Because the example uses zero upfront fees, no additional fee cost is included.

Understanding Your Results

Monthly Payment

The monthly payment is the estimated principal-and-interest amount required each month to fully repay the entered loan over the selected term.

A longer repayment term generally reduces the monthly payment but can increase total interest because the balance remains outstanding for longer.

Total Scheduled Payments

This is the combined amount of all scheduled principal-and-interest payments over the loan term.

It does not automatically represent every dollar you may pay if the loan has charges that are not included in the calculator.

Total Interest

Total interest shows how much of the scheduled repayment exceeds the original principal.

It can be useful when comparing loans with different rates or repayment periods.

Fees

Entered origination, documentation, and other upfront charges represent additional borrowing costs.

Two loans with similar interest rates can have different overall costs when one has substantially higher upfront fees.

Fee-Adjusted APR

The fee-adjusted APR provides an additional comparison measure that considers entered upfront fees.

Do not assume it will exactly match the APR, effective rate, or other cost measure shown in a lender's legal documents.

Amortization

A fully amortizing loan gradually reduces the outstanding principal with each scheduled payment.

Early payments generally contain a larger interest component because the outstanding balance is higher. As the balance declines, more of each payment goes toward principal.

Assumptions and Limitations

This Business Loan Calculator assumes a fully amortizing, fixed-rate loan with regular monthly payments.

It does not model:

  • Daily or weekly repayment schedules
  • Variable interest rates
  • Interest-only periods
  • Balloon payments
  • Irregular or skipped payments
  • Prepayment penalties
  • SBA guarantee fees automatically
  • Lender-specific compounding conventions
  • Lender-specific APR disclosure rules
  • Late-payment charges
  • Taxes or legal costs not entered as fees

The calculator also does not determine whether a business qualifies for financing or whether a particular loan is affordable.

Actual payments and loan costs may differ because lenders can use specific payment dates, rounding procedures, fee classifications, and contractual calculation methods.

For SBA-backed financing, program eligibility, maximum rates, maturities, and applicable fees depend on the specific SBA program and current rules. SBA states that participating lenders set or negotiate loan terms subject to program requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a business loan monthly payment calculated?

For the monthly fixed-rate loan modeled here, the payment is calculated using the standard amortization formula:

Payment = P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)^−n]

The result depends mainly on the amount borrowed, monthly interest rate, and number of monthly payments.

Does a longer loan term lower the monthly payment?

Usually, yes. Spreading the same principal across more monthly payments reduces the amount that must be repaid each month.

However, keeping a balance outstanding for longer can increase total interest. Compare both the monthly payment and total borrowing cost.

Do loan fees increase the monthly payment?

Not necessarily. In this calculator, fees are entered separately from the principal-and-interest calculation.

A lender could structure fees differently, including financing some charges into the loan balance. Always use the principal and fees shown in the actual loan documents.

What is the difference between the interest rate and fee-adjusted APR?

The stated interest rate is used to calculate interest on the loan principal.

A fee-adjusted APR is intended to give a broader annualized view of borrowing cost when certain upfront fees are considered. Because real lending disclosures can use specific regulatory and contractual methods, the calculator's APR should be treated as an illustrative estimate.

Can I use this calculator for an SBA loan?

You can use it to estimate a standard fixed-rate, fully amortizing monthly payment when you know the loan amount, rate, term, and relevant upfront fees.

It does not automatically apply SBA eligibility requirements, guarantee fees, program maximums, or other SBA-specific rules. SBA loan terms and fees should be verified using current SBA information and the lender's offer.

Does this calculator support daily or weekly business loans?

No. This version models monthly amortization.

Business financing with daily or weekly withdrawals can use different repayment structures and pricing methods, so its quoted rate or factor rate should not be treated as equivalent to the monthly amortization model used here.

Is the calculator result the same as a lender quote?

Not necessarily.

The calculator provides an estimate based on the values entered and its fixed monthly amortization model. Lenders may use additional fees, exact payment dates, different compounding methods, rounding procedures, or other contractual rules.
