Use this Bond Calculator to estimate the price of a fixed-rate bond from its face value, annual coupon rate, yield to maturity (YTM), years to maturity, and coupon payment frequency. It also calculates annual coupon dollars, coupon per payment, current yield, and Macaulay duration.

What Is the Bond Calculator?

The Bond Calculator estimates the present value of a plain fixed-rate bond by discounting its future coupon payments and final face-value repayment at the entered yield to maturity.

It can help investors, students, and financial analysts compare a bond's coupon, yield, price, and cash-flow timing. A bond represents debt issued to investors, typically paying interest during its life and returning principal at maturity.

How to Use the Bond Calculator

Enter the following values:

  1. Face value ($): Enter the amount the bond repays at maturity, also called par value or principal.
  2. Annual coupon rate (%): Enter the stated annual interest rate used to determine coupon payments.
  3. Yield to maturity (%): Enter the annual yield used to discount the bond's future cash flows.
  4. Years to maturity: Enter the remaining time until the face value is repaid.
  5. Coupon payments per year: Enter how many coupon payments are made each year.

The calculator then estimates the bond price and supporting yield and duration values.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The bond price is calculated as the present value of all future coupon payments plus the present value of the face value repaid at maturity:

P = Σ[C / (1 + r)^t] + F / (1 + r)^N

where the summation runs from t = 1 through N.

This is the standard present-value approach to valuing a fixed coupon bond.

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
P Estimated bond price Dollars
F Face value Dollars
c Annual coupon rate Decimal
Y Annual yield to maturity Decimal
m Coupon payments per year Payments/year
C Coupon amount per payment Dollars
r Yield per payment period Decimal
N Total remaining coupon periods Periods
t Individual payment period Period number

Calculation Sequence

First, percentage rates are converted to decimals.

Annual coupon dollars = F × c

The coupon paid each period is:

C = (F × c) / m

The periodic yield is:

r = Y / m

The total number of payment periods is:

N = Years to maturity × m

Each coupon payment is discounted using the periodic yield. The face value is also discounted from the final payment period back to the present.

The calculator adds those present values to estimate the bond price.

Annual Coupon Dollars

The annual coupon amount is:

Annual Coupon = Face Value × Annual Coupon Rate

For example, a $1,000 bond with a 5% coupon produces:

$1,000 × 0.05 = $50 per year

Coupon per Payment

The annual coupon is divided by the number of coupon payments per year:

Coupon per Payment = Annual Coupon / Payments per Year

Current Yield

Current yield compares annual coupon income with the estimated bond price:

Current Yield = Annual Coupon / Bond Price × 100%

Investor.gov defines current yield as the bond's annual interest relative to its market price.

Macaulay Duration

Macaulay duration measures the present-value-weighted average time until the bond's coupon and principal cash flows are received:

D = Σ[(t / m) × PV(CFₜ)] / P

where:

  • CFₜ is the cash flow in period t
  • PV(CFₜ) is that cash flow discounted at the periodic yield
  • t / m converts the payment period to years
  • P is the calculated bond price

For a fixed-cash-flow bond, Macaulay duration weights each payment date by the present value of the cash flow received at that date.

Worked Example

Assume a bond has the following terms.

Inputs

  • Face value: $1,000
  • Annual coupon rate: 5%
  • Yield to maturity: 6%
  • Years to maturity: 10 years
  • Coupon payments per year: 2

Step 1: Calculate the Coupon and Periodic Yield

Annual coupon:

$1,000 × 0.05 = $50

Coupon per payment:

$50 / 2 = $25

Periodic yield:

0.06 / 2 = 0.03

So the periodic yield is 3%.

Total payment periods:

10 × 2 = 20 periods

Step 2: Calculate the Bond Price

Using the bond pricing formula:

P = Σ[$25 / (1.03)^t] + $1,000 / (1.03)^20

The present value of all remaining coupons and principal gives an estimated price of:

P ≈ $925.61

Step 3: Calculate Current Yield and Duration

Current yield:

($50 / $925.6126) × 100 ≈ 5.40%

Using the present-value-weighted timing of the bond's cash flows:

Macaulay Duration ≈ 7.895 years

Final Result

  • Estimated bond price: $925.61
  • Annual coupon dollars: $50.00
  • Coupon per payment: $25.00
  • Current yield: 5.40%
  • Macaulay duration: 7.89 years

Because the entered 6% YTM is higher than the bond's 5% coupon rate, this simplified valuation produces a price below its $1,000 face value.

Understanding Your Results

Estimated Bond Price

The estimated bond price is the present value of the remaining coupon payments and face-value repayment.

Under this fixed-rate model, a higher required yield generally reduces the calculated bond price, while a lower required yield increases it. Interest-rate changes can therefore affect a bond's value before maturity.

Annual Coupon Dollars

This is the total coupon interest generated during one year based on the face value and coupon rate.

It does not change simply because the estimated bond price changes.

Coupon per Payment

This shows how much coupon interest is assigned to each regular payment period under the entered payment frequency.

Current Yield

Current yield measures annual coupon income relative to the calculated bond price.

It is different from yield to maturity because current yield does not by itself account for the bond's final principal repayment or the gain or loss between its price and face value.

Macaulay Duration

Macaulay duration expresses the weighted average timing of the bond's discounted cash flows in years.

It is normally shorter than the final maturity of a coupon-paying bond because some cash flows are received before the maturity date.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator uses a plain fixed-rate bond model. Results are estimates based on the values you enter.

It assumes regular fixed coupon payments and discounts them using a periodic yield derived from the entered annual YTM and payment frequency.

The calculation does not model:

  • Callable or redeemable bond features
  • Floating or variable coupon rates
  • Changes in credit spreads
  • Accrued interest
  • Settlement dates
  • Day-count conventions
  • Taxes
  • Default risk
  • Transaction costs
  • Brokerage fees
  • Changes in market liquidity

Because accrued interest and settlement-date conventions are not included, the result should not automatically be treated as an actual clean or dirty market quote.

Real bond prices can also be affected by credit quality, interest rates, liquidity, call provisions, and other market risks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why can a bond's price be different from its face value?

A bond's future payments are discounted using its yield to maturity. When the required yield differs from the coupon rate, the calculated present value can be above or below face value.

What is the difference between coupon rate and yield to maturity?

The coupon rate determines the bond's contractual coupon payments using its face value. YTM is the discount rate entered to value the remaining coupon and principal payments.

Is current yield the same as yield to maturity?

No. Current yield compares annual coupon income with the bond's current calculated price. YTM also reflects the timing of all remaining cash flows and the repayment of face value at maturity.

What happens if YTM increases?

With the other inputs unchanged, increasing the discount rate generally lowers the calculated present value of the bond's future cash flows. Bond prices and market interest rates therefore commonly move in opposite directions.

What does Macaulay duration tell me?

Macaulay duration estimates the present-value-weighted average time to receive the bond's coupon and principal payments. It is expressed in years and is different from simply stating the bond's maturity date.

Does the calculator include accrued interest?

No. Accrued interest, settlement dates, and day-count conventions are outside this calculator's plain fixed-rate bond model.

Is the estimated bond price the price I will pay in the market?

Not necessarily. The result is a mathematical valuation based on your inputs. Actual trading prices can reflect credit risk, liquidity, transaction costs, accrued interest, market conditions, and bond-specific terms not included here.
