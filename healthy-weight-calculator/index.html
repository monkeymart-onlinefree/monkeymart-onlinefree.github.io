The Healthy Weight Calculator estimates the adult weight range associated with a BMI from 18.5 to 24.9 for a given height. Enter your height in centimeters or feet and inches to calculate the lower and upper weight limits in kilograms, with pound conversions for convenience.

What Is the Healthy Weight Calculator?

The Healthy Weight Calculator converts the adult healthy BMI range into a corresponding weight range for your height.

Instead of calculating one target weight, it provides a lower and upper weight boundary. This can be useful for adults who want a simple height-based reference range.

The calculator is intended for adults. CDC uses separate BMI interpretation methods for children and teens because their BMI is evaluated in relation to age and sex.

How to Use the Healthy Weight Calculator

Enter your height using the available height fields:

  • Height (cm): Enter your height in centimeters when using metric measurements.
  • Feet: Enter the whole-foot portion of your height.
  • Inches: Enter the remaining inches.
  • Current weight (optional, kg): You may enter your current weight in kilograms. The healthy-weight range itself is calculated from your height and the BMI boundaries.

The calculator determines the lower and upper healthy-weight estimates and converts the results to pounds.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator rearranges the standard BMI equation.

The BMI equation is:

BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ Height (m)²

Solving for weight gives:

Weight (kg) = BMI × Height (m)²

CDC defines BMI as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared.

CalcPro calculates two boundaries:

Lower healthy weight = 18.5 × Height (m)²
Upper healthy weight = 24.9 × Height (m)²

Variables

Variable Meaning Unit
BMI Body mass index boundary kg/m²
Height Person's height after conversion to meters m
Weight Calculated weight boundary kg

Height Conversion

For a height entered in centimeters:

Height (m) = Height (cm) ÷ 100

For feet and inches:

Total inches = (Feet × 12) + Inches
Height (m) = Total inches × 0.0254

NIST defines one inch as exactly 0.0254 meter and one foot as exactly 0.3048 meter.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Convert the entered height to meters.
  2. Square the height in meters.
  3. Multiply the squared height by 18.5 to calculate the lower boundary.
  4. Multiply the squared height by 24.9 to calculate the upper boundary.
  5. Convert the calculated kilogram values to pounds.

Pounds Conversion

NIST defines:

1 pound = 0.45359237 kilogram

Therefore:

Weight (lb) = Weight (kg) ÷ 0.45359237

Worked Example

Suppose an adult is 170 cm tall.

Inputs

  • Height: 170 cm
  • Height in meters: 1.70 m

Step 1: Square the Height

1.70² = 2.89 m²

Step 2: Calculate the Lower Boundary

Lower weight = 18.5 × 2.89
Lower weight = 53.465 kg

Approximately:

53.5 kg

In pounds:

53.465 ÷ 0.45359237 ≈ 117.9 lb

Step 3: Calculate the Upper Boundary

Upper weight = 24.9 × 2.89
Upper weight = 71.961 kg

Approximately:

72.0 kg

In pounds:

71.961 ÷ 0.45359237 ≈ 158.6 lb

Final Result

For a height of 170 cm, the calculator estimates a healthy-weight range of approximately:

53.5–72.0 kg

or

117.9–158.6 lb

Understanding Your Results

The result is a range rather than one ideal weight.

The lower value is the weight corresponding to BMI 18.5 at the entered height.

The upper value is the weight corresponding to BMI 24.9 at the same height.

CDC currently places the healthy-weight category for adults at BMI 18.5 to less than 25. BMI is a screening measure and should be interpreted alongside other health information rather than used by itself to diagnose a medical condition.

Current BMI Reference

Current reference checked: September 2026

CDC's adult BMI categories for people aged 20 years and older are:

BMI Adult Category
Below 18.5 Underweight
18.5 to less than 25 Healthy Weight
25 to less than 30 Overweight
30 or greater Obesity

CalcPro uses 18.5 and 24.9 exactly as the lower and upper values when calculating its healthy-weight range. CDC's calculator also commonly displays the healthy-weight category as 18.5 to 24.9.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator uses BMI-based adult weight boundaries, so several limitations are important.

It is intended as an adult reference, not a method for assessing children or teenagers. For people ages 2 through 19, CDC uses BMI-for-age measures that account for age and sex.

BMI also does not directly measure body fat. It does not distinguish among fat, muscle and bone, and it does not show where body fat is distributed. Two people with the same BMI can therefore have different body compositions.

The calculator estimates a height-based range rather than determining an individual's medically appropriate weight. Medical history, physical findings, laboratory results and other factors may be relevant when evaluating health.

The implemented calculation uses an upper BMI boundary of 24.9. CDC formally describes the adult healthy-weight category as 18.5 to less than 25, so calculations based on an exact 24.9 boundary may differ slightly from calculations that mathematically approach, but do not include, BMI 25.

Frequently Asked Questions

What formula does the Healthy Weight Calculator use?

It uses:

Weight (kg) = BMI × Height (m)²

The lower calculation uses BMI 18.5, while the upper calculation uses BMI 24.9.

Why does the calculator give a weight range instead of one ideal weight?

The calculator converts a range of BMI values into weights for your height. Because multiple weights can fall within that BMI interval, the result is a lower-to-upper range rather than a single target weight.

Does my current weight determine the healthy-weight range?

No. The lower and upper boundary formulas depend on your height and the BMI values 18.5 and 24.9. Current weight is an optional input and is not part of these two boundary formulas.

Is this calculator suitable for children and teenagers?

No. The adult BMI categories used by this calculator should not be applied directly to children and teens. CDC evaluates BMI for people ages 2–19 using age- and sex-specific BMI percentiles.

Does being inside this range mean I am definitely healthy?

No. BMI is a screening measure rather than a diagnosis. Factors such as medical history, blood pressure, laboratory findings, body composition and other health information can also be important.

Why might a BMI-based weight range be misleading for some people?

BMI cannot distinguish muscle from body fat or identify where fat is distributed. This means people with different body compositions can have similar BMI values.

Are the kilogram and pound results calculated differently?

No. The underlying healthy-weight calculation is performed from the BMI boundaries and height. The kilogram result is then converted to pounds using the standard mass conversion.
