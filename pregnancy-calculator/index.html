The Pregnancy Calculator estimates your due date and other key pregnancy dates from the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP) and your average menstrual cycle length. It can estimate your conception date, trimester, 40-week date, and the number of days remaining until your estimated due date.

What Is the Pregnancy Calculator?

The Pregnancy Calculator is an LMP-based pregnancy dating tool. It starts with the first day of your last menstrual period and adjusts the calculation when your usual cycle is shorter or longer than 28 days.

It is useful for getting an early estimate of important pregnancy dates before or alongside clinical pregnancy dating. A calculated due date is an estimate, not a prediction of the exact day of birth. The NHS notes that pregnancy commonly lasts between 37 and 42 weeks from the first day of the last period.

How to Use the Pregnancy Calculator

  1. First day of last menstrual period: Enter the date on which your most recent menstrual period began. The calculation treats this as the starting point for pregnancy dating.
  2. Average cycle length (days): Enter the usual length of your menstrual cycle in days. The calculator compares this value with a 28-day cycle and adjusts the estimated dates accordingly.

After entering these values, the calculator provides the estimated due date, estimated conception date, trimester, 40-week date, and days until the estimated due date.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator estimates the due date with:

EDD ≈ LMP + 280 days + (Cycle Length − 28 days)

The estimated conception date is:

Estimated Conception Date ≈ LMP + 14 days + (Cycle Length − 28 days)

Variables

Variable Meaning Unit

LMP First day of the last menstrual period Calendar date
Cycle Length Average length of the menstrual cycle Days

14 days Baseline estimate of ovulation/conception timing in a 28-day cycle Days

Cycle-Length Adjustment

The calculator first determines:

Cycle adjustment = Cycle Length − 28

For a 28-day cycle, the adjustment is zero.

For a 30-day cycle:

30 − 28 = +2 days

The calculator therefore moves the estimated due date and conception date two days later than the basic 28-day-cycle estimate.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Calculate the difference between the entered cycle length and 28 days.
  2. Add 280 days plus the cycle adjustment to the LMP date to estimate the due date.
  3. Add 14 days plus the same cycle adjustment to estimate the conception date.
  4. Use the resulting pregnancy timeline to display the trimester and related date information.
  5. Display the 40-week date and the countdown to the estimated due date.

Estimated Conception Date

Estimated conception ≈ LMP + 14 days + cycle adjustment

This is a calendar estimate rather than confirmation of the biological date of conception. Ovulation and fertilization can occur earlier or later than a simple cycle-based estimate.

40-Week Date

Forty weeks equals 280 days. Under this calculator's LMP-based dating method, the cycle-adjusted 40-week date corresponds to the estimated due date.

Worked Example

Assume:

  • First day of last menstrual period: January 1, 2026
  • Average cycle length: 30 days
  • Example calculation date: September 24, 2026

Step 1: Calculate the Cycle Adjustment

30 − 28 = 2 days

The estimated dates are shifted two days later.

Step 2: Calculate the Estimated Due Date

EDD = January 1, 2026 + 280 days + 2 days
EDD = October 10, 2026

Step 3: Calculate the Estimated Conception Date

Estimated conception = January 1, 2026 + 14 days + 2 days
Estimated conception = January 17, 2026

Final Result

For this example, the calculator estimates:

  • Estimated due date: October 10, 2026
  • Estimated conception date: January 17, 2026
  • 40-week date: October 10, 2026
  • Trimester on September 24, 2026: Third trimester
  • Days until EDD on September 24, 2026: 16 days

These dates are estimates based on the entered LMP and average cycle length.

Understanding Your Results

Estimated Due Date

The estimated due date is the calendar date at which the calculator's pregnancy timeline reaches 40 weeks after applying the cycle-length adjustment. Birth may occur before or after this date.

Estimated Conception Date

This is an approximate conception date derived from menstrual-cycle timing. It should not be interpreted as proof of the exact date fertilization occurred.

Trimester

The trimester result gives a broad stage of pregnancy based on the estimated pregnancy timeline calculated from the entered information.

40-Week Date

This is the date corresponding to 40 weeks under the calculator's adjusted LMP dating method. For this calculator, it aligns with the estimated due date.

Days Until EDD

This result provides a countdown from the calculation date to the estimated due date. It is intended as a convenient pregnancy-planning reference rather than a prediction of when labor will begin.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator uses the first day of the LMP as its main dating reference. The method works from a 28-day-cycle baseline and adjusts the estimated dates using the average cycle length entered by the user.

LMP-based dating can be less reliable when menstrual cycles are irregular, the LMP date is uncertain, or ovulation did not occur according to the assumed cycle pattern.

The conception date is an estimate. Menstrual-cycle length does not determine the exact day of ovulation or fertilization for an individual pregnancy.

The calculator is also not a substitute for clinical pregnancy dating. ACOG states that first-trimester ultrasound, through 13 weeks and 6 days, is the most accurate method for establishing or confirming gestational age.

Pregnancies resulting from assisted reproductive technology, including IVF, require different dating information. ACOG recommends using ART-derived gestational age when assigning the estimated due date.

Current Clinical Guidance

As of September 2026, ACOG's Methods for Estimating the Due Date is listed as Reaffirmed 2025. Its guidance states that the conventional LMP-based estimated due date is 280 days after the first day of the last menstrual period and notes that this convention assumes a regular 28-day menstrual cycle with ovulation around day 14.

ACOG also recommends using early ultrasound information to establish or confirm gestational age when available, while the NHS advises that a pregnancy dating scan can provide a more accurate estimate than an LMP-only calculation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Pregnancy Calculator calculate my due date?

It adds 280 days to the first day of your last menstrual period and then adjusts the date by the difference between your average cycle length and 28 days.

Why does cycle length affect the due date?

The basic LMP method assumes a 28-day cycle. This calculator adjusts the estimate when the entered average cycle length differs from 28 days.

Is the estimated conception date exact?

No. It is a calendar estimate based on the LMP and average cycle length. Actual ovulation and fertilization timing can vary.

What if I do not know the first day of my last period?

The LMP-based calculation becomes less reliable when the date is unknown or uncertain. The NHS advises speaking with a healthcare professional when you do not know or are unsure of your LMP, and pregnancy dating may be assessed with ultrasound.

Can an ultrasound change my estimated due date?

Yes. Clinical pregnancy dating can differ from an LMP estimate. ACOG considers first-trimester ultrasound the most accurate method for establishing or confirming gestational age.

Is the 40-week date different from the estimated due date?

Not with this calculator's LMP-based formula. The cycle-adjusted 40-week date corresponds to the estimated due date.

Can I use this calculator for an IVF pregnancy?

This calculator's listed inputs are LMP and average cycle length, so it is not designed around embryo-transfer information. ACOG recommends using ART-derived dating for pregnancies conceived through assisted reproductive technology.
