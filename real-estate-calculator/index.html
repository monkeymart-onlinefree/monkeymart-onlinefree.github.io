Use this Real Estate Calculator to estimate a property’s mortgage principal and interest payment, monthly ownership cost, cash to close, total loan interest, future property value, and projected gross equity. Enter the property price, down payment, mortgage terms, ownership costs, closing costs, and an appreciation scenario to calculate the results.

What Is the Real Estate Calculator?

The Real Estate Calculator combines a standard fixed-rate mortgage calculation with recurring homeownership costs and a property appreciation projection.

It can help prospective buyers compare purchase assumptions, estimate ongoing housing costs, understand how much interest may be paid over the loan term, and explore how property value and mortgage balance could affect future equity.

The results are estimates. For an actual purchase, compare them with your lender’s Loan Estimate and local figures for property taxes, insurance, association fees, and other costs. The CFPB notes that principal and interest are only part of total housing costs; taxes, insurance, and other applicable charges may also matter.

How to Use the Real Estate Calculator

Enter the following information:

  • Property price: The purchase price of the property.
  • Down payment (%): The percentage of the property price you plan to pay upfront.
  • Mortgage interest rate (%): The annual fixed interest rate used for the mortgage calculation.
  • Loan term (years): The number of years over which the mortgage will be repaid.
  • Property tax (annual): Your estimated yearly property tax.
  • Home insurance (annual): Your estimated annual homeowners insurance cost.
  • HOA / association fee (monthly): Monthly homeowners association or similar association dues.
  • Maintenance reserve (annual): The amount you want to budget each year for maintenance and repairs.
  • Closing costs paid upfront: Closing costs that you expect to pay in cash rather than finance into the mortgage.
  • Estimated appreciation (%/year): Your assumed annual rate of change in the property's value.
  • Equity projection period (years): How many years into the future you want to estimate property value and gross equity.

Property taxes, insurance, HOA costs, and maintenance can vary substantially by property and location, so use estimates appropriate to the property you are evaluating.

Formula / Calculation Method

The calculator combines fixed-rate mortgage amortization, recurring ownership costs, upfront purchase costs, and compound property appreciation.

Loan Amount

First, the down payment amount is calculated:

Down Payment = Property Price × Down Payment %

The mortgage loan amount is then:

Loan Amount = Property Price − Down Payment

Monthly Principal and Interest

For a fixed-rate mortgage with a non-zero interest rate:

M = P × [r(1 + r)^n] / [(1 + r)^n − 1]

Where:

  • M = monthly principal and interest payment
  • P = mortgage loan amount
  • r = monthly interest rate
  • n = total number of monthly payments

The monthly rate is:

r = Annual Interest Rate ÷ 12

The percentage rate is converted to decimal form before the calculation.

The total number of payments is:

n = Loan Term in Years × 12

For a 0% interest loan, the equivalent payment is simply the loan amount divided by the number of monthly payments.

The CFPB confirms that fixed-rate mortgage principal-and-interest payments are calculated using the loan amount, interest rate, and loan term so that the scheduled payments repay the loan by the end of the term.

Monthly Ownership Cost

The calculator combines the mortgage principal and interest payment with the recurring costs entered by the user:

Monthly Ownership Cost = Principal & Interest + Property Tax ÷ 12 + Home Insurance ÷ 12 + Monthly HOA Fee + Maintenance Reserve ÷ 12

This is a budgeting estimate rather than necessarily the exact amount sent to the mortgage lender each month. For example, HOA fees or maintenance expenses may be paid separately. CFPB guidance similarly distinguishes mortgage principal and interest from taxes, insurance, association fees, and other homeownership costs.

Cash to Close

For this calculator:

Cash to Close = Down Payment + Closing Costs Paid Upfront

The calculator treats the entered closing costs as cash paid upfront rather than adding them to the loan balance.

An actual lender's Cash to Close can include additional credits, deposits, adjustments, prepaid amounts, and other transaction-specific items.

Total Loan Interest

For a fixed-rate mortgage held for its entire scheduled term:

Total Loan Interest = (Monthly Principal & Interest × Number of Payments) − Loan Amount

This represents scheduled mortgage interest under the assumptions entered. It does not include taxes, insurance, HOA fees, maintenance, or upfront closing costs.

Projected Property Value

Future property value is estimated using compound appreciation:

Future Property Value = Property Price × (1 + g)^t

Where:

  • g = estimated annual appreciation rate in decimal form
  • t = equity projection period in years

This is a scenario based entirely on the appreciation rate entered by the user. It is not a prediction or guaranteed return.

Remaining Mortgage Balance

To estimate equity before the mortgage is fully repaid, the calculator determines the remaining principal after the applicable number of monthly payments.

For k completed payments:

Bₖ = P(1 + r)^k − M × [(1 + r)^k − 1] / r

Where:

  • Bₖ = remaining mortgage balance
  • P = original loan amount
  • M = monthly principal and interest payment
  • r = monthly mortgage rate
  • k = number of monthly payments completed

If the projection extends beyond the scheduled loan term, the scheduled mortgage balance reaches zero.

Projected Gross Equity

Projected gross equity is:

Projected Gross Equity = Projected Property Value − Remaining Mortgage Balance

It is called gross equity because selling expenses, taxes, transaction costs, and other deductions are not removed from the result.

Worked Example

Assume the following property purchase:

Inputs

  • Property price: $500,000
  • Down payment: 20%
  • Mortgage interest rate: 6.5%
  • Loan term: 30 years
  • Property tax: $6,000/year
  • Home insurance: $1,800/year
  • HOA fee: $200/month
  • Maintenance reserve: $5,000/year
  • Closing costs paid upfront: $12,000
  • Estimated appreciation: 3%/year
  • Equity projection period: 10 years

Step 1: Calculate the Loan Amount

Down payment:

$500,000 × 20% = $100,000

Loan amount:

$500,000 − $100,000 = $400,000

Step 2: Calculate Monthly Principal and Interest

Monthly interest rate:

6.5% ÷ 12 = 0.5416667% per month

Number of payments:

30 × 12 = 360

Using the fixed-rate mortgage formula:

Monthly Principal & Interest ≈ $2,528.27

Step 3: Calculate Monthly Ownership Cost

Monthly property tax:

$6,000 ÷ 12 = $500

Monthly insurance:

$1,800 ÷ 12 = $150

Monthly maintenance reserve:

$5,000 ÷ 12 ≈ $416.67

Therefore:

$2,528.27 + $500 + $150 + $200 + $416.67 = $3,794.94

Estimated monthly ownership cost:

$3,794.94

Step 4: Calculate Cash to Close

$100,000 down payment + $12,000 closing costs = $112,000

Estimated cash to close:

$112,000

Step 5: Calculate Total Loan Interest

Total scheduled principal-and-interest payments:

$2,528.27209397 × 360 ≈ $910,177.95

Total interest:

$910,177.95 − $400,000 = $510,177.95

Estimated total loan interest:

$510,177.95

Step 6: Project the Property Value

After 10 years at an assumed 3% annual appreciation:

$500,000 × (1.03)^10 ≈ $671,958.19

Projected property value:

$671,958.19

Step 7: Calculate Projected Gross Equity

After 120 scheduled monthly mortgage payments, the estimated remaining balance is approximately:

$339,104.51

Projected gross equity:

$671,958.19 − $339,104.51 = $332,853.68

Estimated 10-year gross equity:

$332,853.68

This figure is a scenario estimate. Actual property values, selling costs, loan activity, and other expenses can change the amount of equity ultimately realized.

Understanding Your Results

Loan Amount

This is the portion of the property price financed through the mortgage after subtracting the calculated down payment.

Principal & Interest

This is the estimated monthly payment required to repay the fixed-rate mortgage principal and interest over the selected term.

It does not by itself represent all monthly homeownership expenses.

Monthly Ownership Cost

This combines principal and interest with the property tax, homeowners insurance, HOA fee, and maintenance reserve entered in the calculator.

It is useful for budgeting because the cost of owning a home can be higher than the mortgage principal-and-interest payment alone.

Cash to Close

This calculator's cash-to-close estimate consists of the down payment plus the closing-cost amount entered as paid upfront.

Your lender's official figure can differ because a Loan Estimate can include deposits, credits, prepaid expenses, adjustments, and other transaction-specific amounts.

Total Loan Interest

This is the estimated interest paid over the full scheduled mortgage term if the fixed-rate loan follows the calculated payment schedule.

Projected Property Value

This is the estimated future property value produced by applying your selected annual appreciation rate for the projection period.

It should be treated as a scenario rather than a forecast.

Projected Gross Equity

This is projected property value minus the calculated remaining mortgage balance.

It does not represent the exact cash you would receive from selling the property because selling costs, taxes, fees, and other deductions are not included.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator assumes a standard fixed-rate mortgage with scheduled monthly payments.

The model does not include:

  • Mortgage insurance
  • Adjustable-rate mortgage changes
  • Tax deductions
  • Utility costs
  • Special assessments
  • Renovation costs
  • Selling costs
  • Capital gains tax
  • Lender-specific fees unless represented by an entered amount

Property taxes, insurance premiums, association fees, and maintenance expenses are based on the values you enter. Actual costs may change over time.

Closing costs are treated as cash paid upfront and are not added to the mortgage balance.

The property appreciation calculation assumes the same annual appreciation rate throughout the projection period. Actual real estate values can increase, decrease, or remain unchanged.

Projected gross equity does not subtract selling expenses or other costs that could reduce the amount received when a property is sold.

For a real mortgage transaction, use your lender's official Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure rather than relying solely on a calculator estimate. CFPB guidance explains that these documents contain the loan amount, monthly payment, closing costs, taxes, insurance, and transaction-specific cash-to-close information.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the Real Estate Calculator calculate?

It estimates the mortgage loan amount, monthly principal and interest, recurring monthly ownership cost, upfront cash requirement, total scheduled mortgage interest, future property value, and projected gross equity.

What is included in the monthly ownership cost?

The calculator adds mortgage principal and interest, annual property tax divided by 12, annual homeowners insurance divided by 12, the monthly HOA or association fee, and annual maintenance reserve divided by 12.

Mortgage insurance and utilities are not included.

Are closing costs included in the mortgage?

No. The calculator treats the closing-cost input as money paid upfront.

If some costs are financed into your actual loan, your lender's financed amount may differ from the loan amount estimated here.

How is total mortgage interest calculated?

For the fixed-rate scheduled loan, the calculator multiplies the monthly principal-and-interest payment by the total number of monthly payments and subtracts the original loan principal.

How is future property value calculated?

The calculator compounds the property's starting value using the annual appreciation rate you enter:

Future Value = Property Price × (1 + Appreciation Rate)^Years

The appreciation rate is an assumption, not a guarantee.

How is projected equity calculated?

Projected gross equity equals the estimated future property value minus the calculated mortgage balance remaining at the selected projection date.

Selling expenses, taxes, and other transaction costs are not deducted.

Why can my lender's numbers differ from this calculator?

Real mortgage transactions can include mortgage insurance, lender fees, escrow requirements, prepaid interest, credits, deposits, government charges, and other adjustments that this model does not calculate.

The CFPB recommends reviewing the lender-provided Loan Estimate for transaction-specific figures.
