What Is a Z-Score Calculator?

A z-score calculator standardizes a raw value by expressing how many standard deviations it lies above or below a mean. Positive z-scores are above the mean, negative z-scores are below it, and z = 0 is exactly at the mean.

How to Use the Z-Score Calculator

Enter the raw value x, the mean μ, and a positive standard deviation σ. The calculator reports the z-score and approximate lower-tail and upper-tail probabilities for the standard normal distribution.

Z-Score Formula

The population standard-score formula is z = (x − μ)/σ. Subtract the mean from the observation and divide the difference by the population standard deviation.

Calculation Example

If x = 85, μ = 70, and σ = 10, then z = (85 − 70)/10 = 1.5. The observation is 1.5 standard deviations above the mean.

Understanding the Probability Output

The lower-tail probability P(Z ≤ z) is the area to the left of the z-score under a standard normal curve. The percentile is that probability expressed as a percentage.

Limitations

A z-score itself is a standardization and can be calculated for any numeric value with a nonzero standard deviation. Interpreting the displayed probabilities as model probabilities assumes the standardized variable follows a normal distribution.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a positive z-score mean?

A positive z-score means the raw value is above the mean. A z-score of 2 means the value is two standard deviations above the mean.

What does z = 0 mean?

It means the raw value equals the mean exactly.

Can a z-score be negative?

Yes. Negative z-scores indicate values below the mean.

Is a high z-score always good?

No. A z-score only describes relative position. Whether a high or low value is desirable depends on what is being measured.
