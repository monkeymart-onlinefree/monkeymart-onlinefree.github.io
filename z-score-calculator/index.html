Use the Z-Score Calculator to find how many standard deviations a raw value is above or below a population mean. Enter the raw value, mean, and standard deviation to calculate the z-score and its standard normal lower-tail and upper-tail probabilities.

What Is the Z-Score Calculator?

A z-score, also called a standard score, measures the position of a value relative to a mean in units of standard deviation. A positive z-score means the value is above the mean, a negative z-score means it is below the mean, and a z-score of 0 means the value equals the mean.

This calculator is useful when you want to standardize a value or compare its relative position within a distribution.

How to Use the Z-Score Calculator

Enter these three values:

  • Raw value (x): The individual value you want to standardize.
  • Mean (μ): The population mean used as the reference point.
  • Standard deviation (σ): The population standard deviation. It must be greater than 0.

The raw value, mean, and standard deviation should use the same measurement unit. The resulting z-score has no unit.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator uses the population z-score formula:

z=x−μσz=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}

OpenStax defines the same standard-score calculation as the difference between the observation and population mean divided by the population standard deviation.

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
z Z-score or standardized score Unitless
x Raw value or observation Same unit as the data
μ Population mean Same unit as x
σ Population standard deviation Same unit as x

Calculation Sequence

  1. Subtract the mean from the raw value:
    x−μx-\mu
  2. Divide that difference by the standard deviation:
    z=x−μσz=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}
  3. Use the calculated z-score to determine the displayed standard normal tail probabilities.

Lower-Tail Probability

For the standard normal model, the lower-tail probability represents the cumulative probability at or below the calculated z-score:

P(Z≤z)=Φ(z)P(Z\le z)=\Phi(z)

where Φ(z) is the cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution.

Upper-Tail Probability

The upper-tail probability is the remaining probability above the calculated z-score:

P(Z>z)=1−Φ(z)P(Z>z)=1-\Phi(z)

NIST's standard normal tables show how cumulative probabilities can be obtained from a standardized value and how the complementary probability is used for the opposite tail.

Worked Example

Suppose:

Inputs

  • Raw value, x = 85
  • Mean, μ = 70
  • Standard deviation, σ = 10

Step 1: Find the Difference

85−70=1585-70=15

Step 2: Calculate the Z-Score

z=1510=1.5z=\frac{15}{10}=1.5

Step 3: Find the Tail Probabilities

For z = 1.5, the standard normal cumulative probability is approximately:

Φ(1.5)≈0.9332\Phi(1.5)\approx0.9332

Therefore:

Lower-tail probability:

0.9332≈93.32%0.9332\approx93.32\\%

Upper-tail probability:

1−0.9332=0.06681-0.9332=0.0668 0.0668≈6.68%0.0668\approx6.68\\%

Final Result

  • Z-score: 1.5
  • Lower-tail probability: approximately 93.32%
  • Upper-tail probability: approximately 6.68%

A z-score of 1.5 means the raw value is 1.5 standard deviations above the mean.

Understanding Your Results

Z-Score

The z-score shows the raw value's distance from the mean in standard deviation units.

  • z > 0: The value is above the mean.
  • z < 0: The value is below the mean.
  • z = 0: The value equals the mean.

For example, a z-score of 2 means the value is two standard deviations above the mean, while −2 means it is two standard deviations below the mean.

Lower-Tail Probability

The lower-tail probability represents the area of the standard normal distribution at or below the calculated z-score.

A result of 0.9332, for example, represents about 93.32% of the standard normal distribution below that z-score.

Upper-Tail Probability

The upper-tail probability represents the area above the calculated z-score.

Because the total probability is 1, the lower-tail and upper-tail probabilities add to approximately 1, apart from rounding.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator uses the population standard-score formula z = (x − μ) / σ.

The standard deviation must be greater than zero. A standard deviation of zero makes the calculation undefined because division by zero is not possible.

A z-score can standardize a numeric value whenever a valid mean and nonzero standard deviation are available. However, interpreting the displayed tail values as probabilities assumes the standardized variable is appropriately modeled by a normal distribution.

A z-score is not automatically the same thing as a percentile. A percentile interpretation requires a distribution model, such as the standard normal distribution used for the displayed tail probabilities.

Results may differ slightly from printed z-tables or other software because of rounding and numerical precision.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a z-score tell you?

A z-score tells you how many standard deviations a value is above or below the mean. Positive scores are above the mean, while negative scores are below it.

What is the formula for a z-score?

For a population mean and population standard deviation:

z=x−μσz=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}

where x is the raw value, μ is the mean, and σ is the population standard deviation.

Can a z-score be negative?

Yes. A negative z-score means the raw value is below the mean. For example, z = −1.5 means the value is 1.5 standard deviations below the mean.

What does a z-score of 0 mean?

A z-score of 0 means the raw value is exactly equal to the mean.

Can the standard deviation be zero?

No. The formula divides by the standard deviation, so a zero standard deviation does not produce a defined z-score.

Does a z-score require normally distributed data?

The standardization calculation itself can be performed from a numeric value, mean, and valid standard deviation. However, using the resulting z-score to interpret standard normal tail probabilities assumes a normal-distribution model.

Is a z-score a percentage?

No. A z-score is a unitless standardized value, not a percentage. The lower-tail or upper-tail probability associated with the z-score can be expressed as a decimal or percentage.
