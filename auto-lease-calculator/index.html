The Auto Lease Calculator estimates a monthly car lease payment from the agreed vehicle price, lease term, upfront reductions, APR-equivalent financing rate, residual value, and sales tax. It separates the estimated payment into depreciation, finance charge, and tax components.

What Is the Auto Lease Calculator?

An auto lease calculator estimates how much you may pay each month to lease a vehicle rather than finance its full purchase price.

A standard lease payment mainly reflects the vehicle's expected depreciation during the lease plus a finance or rent charge. The CFPB explains that lease payments generally cover depreciation during the lease term along with rental charges, taxes, and applicable fees.

This calculator is useful when comparing lease offers or testing how changes in vehicle price, residual value, lease term, upfront reductions, or financing rate affect the estimated monthly payment.

How to Use the Auto Lease Calculator

  1. Agreed vehicle price: Enter the negotiated vehicle value used for the lease calculation. Lease term: Enter the number of months in the lease. Down payment / cap reduction: Enter the amount paid upfront that reduces the capitalized cost. Trade-in credit: Enter the trade-in amount applied toward reducing the lease cost. Lease APR equivalent: Enter the annual percentage rate equivalent used to estimate the lease money factor. Residual value: Enter the vehicle's estimated value at the end of the lease in the same monetary unit as the vehicle price. Sales tax on monthly payment: Enter the percentage of tax applied to the estimated monthly lease payment.

The CFPB defines a capitalized cost reduction as an amount that reduces the gross capitalized cost and notes that it may include cash payments, rebates, noncash credits, and net trade-in allowances.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculation begins with the adjusted capitalized cost:

C=V−D−TC = V - D - T

Monthly depreciation is:

Depreciation=C−RnDepreciation = \frac{C-R}{n}

When the financing input is entered as an APR-equivalent percentage, the approximate money factor is:

MF=APR2400MF = \frac{APR}{2400}

The monthly finance charge is:

Finance Charge=(C+R)×MFFinance\ Charge = (C+R)\times MF

The estimated monthly payment before tax is:

Base Payment=Depreciation+Finance ChargeBase\ Payment = Depreciation + Finance\ Charge

Because this calculator accepts sales tax on the monthly payment:

Monthly Tax=Base Payment×s100Monthly\ Tax = Base\ Payment\times\frac{s}{100}

The estimated monthly lease payment is:

The APR-to-money-factor conversion of APR as a percentage divided by 2400 is a commonly used approximation for lease calculations.

Calculation Sequence

The calculator first reduces the agreed vehicle price by the entered down payment and trade-in credit to estimate the adjusted capitalized cost. It then subtracts the residual value and spreads the resulting depreciation across the lease term.

Next, the APR-equivalent percentage is converted to an approximate money factor. The money factor is applied to the sum of the adjusted capitalized cost and residual value to estimate the monthly finance charge.

Monthly depreciation and the finance charge are added to produce the pre-tax payment. The entered monthly-payment sales tax is then applied to obtain the estimated monthly lease payment.

Adjusted Capitalized Cost

Adjusted Cap Cost=Vehicle Price−Down Payment−Trade-in CreditAdjusted\ Cap\ Cost = Vehicle\ Price-Down\ Payment-Trade\text{-}in\ Credit

Under Regulation M, adjusted capitalized cost is the amount used in calculating the base periodic payment after capitalized-cost reductions are deducted.

Monthly Depreciation

Monthly Depreciation=Adjusted Cap Cost−Residual ValueLease MonthsMonthly\ Depreciation=\frac{Adjusted\ Cap\ Cost-Residual\ Value}{Lease\ Months}

Residual value is the vehicle value expected at the end of the lease and is an important part of determining depreciation.

Monthly Finance Charge

Monthly Finance Charge=(Adjusted Cap Cost+Residual Value)×Money FactorMonthly\ Finance\ Charge=(Adjusted\ Cap\ Cost+Residual\ Value)\times Money\ Factor

This represents the financing component added to the depreciation portion of the estimated payment.

Understanding Your Results

The main result is the estimated monthly lease payment based on the values entered.

Adjusted capitalized cost represents the vehicle amount used for the lease calculation after the entered down payment and trade-in credit reduce the agreed vehicle price.

Monthly depreciation represents the portion of the calculated vehicle value loss allocated to one lease month.

Finance charge represents the financing or rent component calculated using the APR-equivalent money factor.

Pre-tax monthly payment combines monthly depreciation and the finance charge.

Monthly tax applies the entered sales-tax percentage to the pre-tax payment.

These figures are estimates for comparison and planning. Your lessor's lease disclosure and signed contract determine the actual amounts you must pay.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator uses the entered agreed vehicle price as the starting capitalized amount and reduces it using the entered down payment and trade-in credit. It assumes the entered residual value is already available as a monetary amount and applies the APR-equivalent money-factor approximation shown above.

Real vehicle leases may also include acquisition fees, registration or title charges, security deposits, rebates, manufacturer incentives, first payments, prior-loan or lease balances, mileage provisions, disposition charges, and other contract-specific amounts. These items are not automatically included unless represented by one of the calculator's listed inputs.

Tax treatment also varies by jurisdiction. This calculator specifically applies the entered sales-tax rate to the monthly payment; some jurisdictions may calculate vehicle lease taxes differently.

Mileage limits, excess-mileage charges, excessive-wear charges, purchase-option costs, and early-termination charges are not determined by this calculation. CFPB guidance notes that vehicle leases can include mileage restrictions, wear charges, end-of-lease fees, and potentially significant early-termination costs.

The result should therefore be treated as an estimate rather than a replacement for the lessor's official lease disclosure.

Current Rules / Data

As of September 24, 2026, the CFPB lists the current version of Regulation M, 12 CFR Part 1013, as most recently amended on January 1, 2026. Regulation M covers disclosures for applicable consumer leases.

For covered motor-vehicle leases, the disclosure rules address items including the amount due at signing or delivery, gross capitalized cost, capitalized cost reduction, adjusted capitalized cost, residual value, depreciation and amortized amounts, and the rent charge.

This calculator does not replace those required disclosures or determine whether a particular lease transaction is covered by Regulation M.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is an auto lease payment calculated?

This calculator estimates monthly depreciation by subtracting the residual value from the adjusted capitalized cost and dividing by the lease term. It then adds a finance charge based on the estimated money factor and applies the entered monthly-payment sales tax.

How do I convert lease APR to a money factor?

When APR is entered as a percentage, this calculator uses the common approximation:

Money Factor=APR2400Money\ Factor=\frac{APR}{2400}

For example, 6% ÷ 2400 = 0.0025. A lease provider may quote a money factor directly, so the actual contract figures should control.

Why does residual value affect the monthly payment?

Residual value represents the vehicle's expected value at the end of the lease. A larger residual value reduces the amount of depreciation that must be covered during the lease term, assuming the other inputs remain unchanged. CFPB lease disclosures also identify residual value as a value used in calculating the base periodic payment.

Does a larger down payment lower the estimated lease payment?

Under this calculator's method, yes. A larger capitalized-cost reduction lowers the adjusted capitalized cost, which reduces the depreciation portion of the calculation. It also requires more cash upfront, so monthly payment alone should not be used to compare the total cost of competing leases.

Does the calculator include all lease fees?

No. It calculates from the listed vehicle price, lease term, down payment or cap reduction, trade-in credit, APR equivalent, residual value, and monthly-payment tax rate. Actual leases may contain additional fees or credits that change the final payment.

Why might the dealer's lease payment be different?

A dealer or lessor may use additional acquisition fees, rebates, incentives, capitalized taxes, prior-balance amounts, different tax rules, contract-specific money factors, or rounding conventions. The official lease disclosure should be used for the final contractual figures.

Does this calculator calculate mileage or end-of-lease charges?

No. Mileage limits, excess-mileage charges, wear-and-tear charges, disposition charges, purchase-option amounts, and early-termination charges are separate lease terms and are not calculated from the listed inputs.
