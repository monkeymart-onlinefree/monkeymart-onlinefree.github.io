What Is an Electricity Calculator?

An electricity calculator estimates how much electrical energy an appliance or group of appliances uses and what that energy costs at a chosen price per kilowatt-hour. It is useful for comparing appliances, estimating operating costs, and understanding energy usage.

Electricity Usage Formula

Power (kW) = Watts ÷ 1000
Daily kWh = Power(kW) × Quantity × Hours/day
Monthly kWh = Daily kWh × Days/month
Energy Cost = kWh × Rate per kWh

The U.S. Energy Information Administration defines one kilowatt as 1,000 watts and one kilowatt-hour as one kilowatt of power used for one hour.

Calculation Example

A 1,000 W appliance used for 3 hours per day consumes 3 kWh per day. Used on 30 days, that is 90 kWh for the month before any utility-specific fees or adjustments.

Understanding Your Estimate

Power rating and actual average power are not always the same. Refrigerators, air conditioners, heaters, pumps, and variable-speed devices can cycle or change load, so measured average power generally gives a better estimate.

Limitations

This tool uses a single flat energy rate. It does not model taxes, fixed service charges, demand charges, minimum bills, time-of-use rates, tiered rates, power factor, or appliance duty cycle unless you reflect those effects in the entered average usage.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate appliance kWh?

Convert appliance power to kilowatts, multiply by the number of appliances and hours used, then multiply by the number of days. For example, 100 W is 0.1 kW.

Why can my real bill be higher?

A utility bill can include taxes, fixed charges, tiered rates, time-of-use pricing, demand charges, fuel adjustments, and other fees. This calculator models energy charges using one rate per kWh.

Should I use rated watts or measured watts?

Measured average power is usually better when available because many appliances cycle or change power during operation. A nameplate wattage can be only an approximation of actual usage.

What is the difference between kW and kWh?

Kilowatts measure power, a rate of energy use. Kilowatt-hours measure energy used over time. One kWh equals one kilowatt used for one hour.
