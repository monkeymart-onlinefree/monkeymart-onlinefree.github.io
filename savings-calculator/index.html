Use the Savings Calculator to estimate how your money may grow over time from an initial deposit, recurring monthly and annual contributions, contribution increases, compound interest, and an optional tax rate on interest. Enter your savings assumptions to estimate your future balance.

What Is the Savings Calculator?

The Savings Calculator is a planning tool for estimating the future value of savings. It combines money already saved with new contributions and compound growth over the number of years you select.

It can be useful for long-term savings planning, building an emergency fund, estimating the future value of cash savings, or testing how higher contributions may affect a future balance.

How to Use the Savings Calculator

Enter the following values in the calculator:

Initial Deposit

Enter the amount already available at the start of the calculation. This is the starting principal on which future growth begins.

Years to Save

Enter how many years you want the savings projection to cover. A longer period gives compound growth more time to affect the balance.

Monthly Contribution

Enter the amount you plan to add each month.

Monthly Contribution Increase / Year

Enter the percentage by which the monthly contribution should increase each year. For example, a positive annual increase models gradually raising the amount you save each month.

Annual Contribution

Enter any additional amount you plan to contribute each year.

Annual Contribution Increase / Year

Enter the percentage by which the annual contribution should increase from year to year.

Annual Interest Rate

Enter the assumed annual effective interest or return rate as a percentage. This is an assumption entered by the user rather than a live or guaranteed savings rate.

Tax Rate on Interest

Enter the percentage of calculated interest that should be removed for tax in the calculator's simplified tax model. The tax adjustment applies to interest rather than to the principal you deposit.

Formula / Calculation Method

The calculator projects the savings balance over time by combining four components:

  • starting principal;
  • recurring contributions;
  • annual increases in those contributions;
  • compound interest after the optional tax adjustment.

Because monthly and annual contributions can both change over time, the full projection is processed over successive saving periods rather than relying only on one fixed future-value equation.

Main Compound Growth Formula

For a balance with no additional contributions or tax adjustment, compound growth follows:

FV = P × (1 + r)^n

Where:

  • FV = future value of the savings
  • P = initial deposit
  • r = annual effective interest rate expressed as a decimal
  • n = number of years

Compound interest allows later interest to build on both the original principal and previously accumulated interest. Investor.gov also uses initial investment, recurring contributions, time, and an estimated annual interest rate as the main inputs for compound-growth projections.

Contribution Growth

If the monthly contribution increases every year:

Mᵧ = M₀ × (1 + gₘ)^y

Where:

  • M₀ = starting monthly contribution
  • Mᵧ = monthly contribution used after y yearly increases
  • gₘ = annual monthly-contribution increase rate as a decimal
  • y = number of completed annual increases

Annual contributions follow the same structure:

Aᵧ = A₀ × (1 + gₐ)^y

Where:

  • A₀ = starting annual contribution
  • Aᵧ = annual contribution after y increases
  • gₐ = annual contribution increase rate

This lets the projection account for a savings plan in which contributions rise over time instead of remaining fixed.

Tax-Adjusted Interest

The tax input reduces calculated interest in this simplified model:

Net Interest = Gross Interest × (1 − t)

Where:

  • t = entered tax rate on interest expressed as a decimal

For example, a 20% tax assumption means the model retains 80% of the calculated gross interest.

The tax adjustment does not reduce the initial deposit or the contributions themselves.

Balance Calculation

At a high level, each projected balance is built from:

Updated Balance = Previous Balance + Contributions + Net Interest

The updated balance is then carried forward so future compound growth can build on the accumulated savings.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Start with the initial deposit.
  2. Convert entered percentages to decimal rates.
  3. Determine the saving period from the entered number of years.
  4. Apply the scheduled monthly and annual contributions.
  5. Increase those contributions over time using their entered yearly growth percentages.
  6. Calculate compound interest using the entered annual effective interest rate.
  7. Reduce calculated interest using the optional tax rate.
  8. Carry the resulting balance forward through the remaining saving periods.
  9. Return the projected savings result and supporting values.

Worked Example

Consider a simple scenario with no additional contributions so the compound-growth calculation can be shown directly.

Inputs

  • Initial deposit: $10,000
  • Years to save: 10 years
  • Monthly contribution: $0
  • Monthly contribution increase: 0%
  • Annual contribution: $0
  • Annual contribution increase: 0%
  • Annual interest rate: 5%
  • Tax rate on interest: 0%

Step 1: Convert the Interest Rate

5% = 0.05

Step 2: Apply Compound Growth

FV = $10,000 × (1 + 0.05)^10
FV = $10,000 × 1.6288946268
FV = $16,288.9463

Step 3: Calculate Growth

Interest earned = $16,288.9463 − $10,000
Interest earned = $6,288.9463

Because the tax rate in this example is 0%, no interest is removed.

Understanding Your Results

Projected Savings Balance

This is the estimated amount accumulated by the end of the selected saving period based on all entered assumptions.

A larger result can come from a higher starting balance, larger contributions, higher contribution growth, a higher assumed interest rate, a longer saving period, or a lower tax assumption.

Contributions

Contributions represent new money added to the savings projection. The calculator supports both monthly and annual contributions, allowing the two to be modeled together.

Contribution Growth

Increasing contribution percentages show the effect of gradually saving more each year. Even a contribution amount that starts relatively small can become more significant over a long saving period when it increases annually.

Interest

Interest represents growth generated by the balance rather than money directly contributed by the user.

Compound interest can become more important over longer periods because interest can be earned on previously accumulated growth. Investor.gov describes compound-interest calculators as tools for estimating how money can grow through compounding over time.

Tax-Adjusted Interest

When a tax rate is entered, the calculator reduces the modeled interest according to that percentage. It is a simplified planning adjustment and should not be treated as an individualized tax calculation.

Assumptions and Limitations

The Savings Calculator is a projection based on the values you enter. Actual savings outcomes can differ.

The annual interest rate is assumed for calculation purposes. Real savings account rates and investment returns can change over time.

The tax input is simplified. Actual taxation can depend on account type, jurisdiction, taxable income, deductions, timing, and other rules.

Fees are not separately included unless they are already reflected in the assumptions you enter. Bank fees, investment expenses, account charges, and other costs can reduce actual results.

The calculator models contribution increases according to the percentages entered. Your actual future contributions may be higher or lower.

Investment returns, where the calculator is used for an investment-style projection, can be volatile and are not guaranteed.

The result is an estimate for planning purposes and should not be interpreted as a guaranteed future balance, tax advice, or investment advice.

Frequently Asked Questions

What interest rate should I enter in the Savings Calculator?

Enter an annual rate that matches the scenario you want to model. The calculator does not require a fixed current market rate because the interest rate is a user-entered assumption.

If you are modeling a real savings account, you can use a rate based on the account information provided by your financial institution.

Can I include both monthly and annual contributions?

Yes. The calculator has separate inputs for monthly contributions and annual contributions, so both can be included in the same savings projection.

What does the contribution increase percentage do?

It increases the applicable recurring contribution each year by the percentage you enter.

For example, if you expect to increase how much you save as your income grows, the contribution-growth inputs allow that change to be represented in the projection.

Does the tax rate apply to my deposits?

No. In this simplified calculation, the tax rate reduces calculated interest. It does not reduce your initial deposit or recurring contributions.

Does the calculator include inflation?

Inflation is not a separate input in this Savings Calculator. The result therefore represents the projected balance based on the entered savings, contribution, interest, and tax assumptions rather than a separate inflation-adjusted purchasing-power value.

Use the Inflation Calculator if you want to examine how inflation may affect the real value of money.

Are the results guaranteed?

No. The result depends entirely on the assumptions entered. Actual interest rates, taxes, fees, contribution amounts, and investment performance can change, so the final amount may be different.
