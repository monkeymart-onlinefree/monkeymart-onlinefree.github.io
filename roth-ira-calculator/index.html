Use the Roth IRA Calculator to estimate your maximum Roth IRA contribution for tax year 2026 and project potential account growth. Enter your age, filing status, modified adjusted gross income (MAGI), taxable compensation, other IRA contributions, current Roth IRA balance, projection period, and assumed annual return.

The calculator shows your estimated 2026 Roth IRA contribution limit, the base IRA limit used, projected ending balance, projected future contributions, and projected growth.

What Is the Roth IRA Calculator?

The Roth IRA Calculator estimates how much you may be eligible to contribute to a Roth IRA for 2026 based on IRS contribution limits, taxable compensation, filing status, and modified AGI.

It also uses your current Roth IRA balance, projection period, and assumed return to estimate how the account could grow over time. The contribution result is an estimate and does not replace an individual tax calculation.

How to Use the Roth IRA Calculator

Enter the following information:

  1. Age: Your age for the 2026 tax year. Age determines whether the regular IRA limit or the age-50-and-older limit applies.
  2. Filing status: Select the filing status used to determine the applicable Roth IRA income phase-out range.
  3. Modified AGI: Enter your modified adjusted gross income for Roth IRA purposes.
  4. Taxable compensation: Enter your taxable compensation available for IRA contribution purposes.
  5. Other traditional/Roth IRA contributions for the year: Enter contributions already made that use part of your shared annual IRA contribution limit.
  6. Current Roth IRA balance: Enter the amount currently held in your Roth IRA.
  7. Years to project: Enter how many years you want the calculator to model.
  8. Assumed annual return: Enter the annual investment return assumption used for the projection.

The IRS notes that Roth IRA modified AGI can require adjustments to regular AGI, so the MAGI entered should be the amount applicable for Roth IRA contribution purposes.

Formula / Calculation Method

1. Base 2026 IRA Limit

For tax year 2026:

S={8,600ifageis50orolder7,500if under age 50S = \begin{cases} \\$8,600 & \text{if age is 50 or older} \\\ \\$7,500 & \text{if under age 50} \end{cases}

The $8,600 amount consists of the $7,500 regular IRA limit plus the $1,100 age-50 catch-up amount.

2. Compensation-Limited IRA Amount

The contribution amount cannot exceed taxable compensation:

B=min⁡(S, C)B = \min(S,\ C)

Where:

  • S = age-based annual IRA limit in dollars
  • C = taxable compensation in dollars
  • B = contribution limit before the Roth income phase-out

For 2026, total regular contributions across traditional and Roth IRAs are generally limited to the lesser of $7,500, $8,600 for someone age 50 or older, or taxable compensation.

3. Roth IRA Income Phase-Out

Let:

  • M = modified AGI
  • L = lower end of the applicable phase-out range
  • W = width of the phase-out range

When modified AGI falls inside the applicable phase-out range:

F=M−LWF = \frac{M-L}{W}

Where F is the reduction fraction.

The preliminary reduced contribution is:

P=B×(1−F)P = B \times (1-F)

The result is then rounded up to the nearest $10:

D=10×⌈P10⌉D = 10 \times \left\lceil\frac{P}{10}\right\rceil

If the calculated reduced amount is greater than $0 but below $200, the IRS worksheet uses a $200 minimum before applying the remaining shared IRA contribution limit. This follows the IRS reduced Roth IRA worksheet method.

4. Other IRA Contributions

Traditional and Roth IRA contributions share the annual IRA contribution limit.

The remaining annual contribution room is:

R=max⁡(0, B−O)R = \max(0,\ B-O)

Where:

  • O = other traditional/Roth IRA contributions entered for the year
  • R = remaining shared IRA contribution room

The final estimated Roth IRA contribution is limited to the smaller of the income-adjusted Roth amount and the remaining annual IRA contribution room:

Maximum Roth Contribution=min⁡(D, R)\text{Maximum Roth Contribution} = \min(D,\ R)

When no MAGI reduction applies, D = B. When modified AGI reaches or exceeds the applicable upper Roth IRA threshold, the eligible Roth IRA contribution is $0. The IRS worksheet similarly reduces the Roth amount by contributions made to other IRAs.

Projection Calculation

The calculator also uses the current Roth IRA balance, years to project, future contribution amount, and assumed annual return to produce projected ending balance, projected future contributions, and projected growth.

The available calculator specification does not define whether projected contributions are credited before or after each period's investment growth. Therefore, no unsupported contribution-timing formula is stated here. The projection should be treated as an illustration based on the assumptions entered rather than a guaranteed future balance.

Worked Example

Assume the following inputs:

  • Age: 52
  • Filing status: Single
  • Modified AGI: $160,000
  • Taxable compensation: $100,000
  • Other IRA contributions: $1,000
  • Current Roth IRA balance: $50,000
  • Years to project: 20
  • Assumed annual return: 7%

Because the person is age 50 or older:

S=$8,600S = \\$8,600

B=min⁡(8,600, 100,000)=$8,600B = \min(8,600,\ 100,000)=\\$8,600

For 2026, the single/head-of-household Roth IRA phase-out begins at $153,000 and reaches zero eligibility at $168,000.

The phase-out width is:

168,000−153,000=$15,000168,000-153,000=\\$15,000

Amount above the lower threshold:

160,000−153,000=$7,000160,000-153,000=\\$7,000

Reduction fraction:

F=7,00015,000=0.466667F=\frac{7,000}{15,000}=0.466667

8,600−4,013.33=$4,586.678,600-4,013.33=\\$4,586.67

Understanding Your Results

Estimated 2026 Maximum Roth IRA Contribution

This is the calculator's estimate of how much additional Roth IRA contribution room you have after considering your age, compensation, modified AGI, filing status, and other IRA contributions.

Base IRA Annual Limit Used

This shows whether the calculation started with the regular $7,500 IRA limit or the $8,600 limit for someone age 50 or older.

Projected Ending Balance

This is the estimated Roth IRA balance at the end of the selected projection period using the contribution and investment-return assumptions entered.

It is a projection, not a guaranteed investment result.

Total Projected Future Contributions

This shows the future contribution amount included in the calculator's projection over the selected period.

Projected Growth

Projected growth represents the portion of the projected ending value attributed to estimated investment growth rather than the starting balance and future contributions.

Actual investment performance can be higher, lower, or negative.

Current 2026 Roth IRA Rules

The following limits apply to tax year 2026.

Rule 2026 Amount
Regular IRA contribution limit $7,500
IRA limit for age 50 or older $8,600
Single / Head of Household phase-out 153,000–168,000
Married Filing Jointly phase-out 242,000–252,000
Married Filing Separately and lived with spouse 0–10,000

For single and head-of-household taxpayers, the contribution begins phasing out at $153,000 of modified AGI and is unavailable at $168,000 or more. For married couples filing jointly, the corresponding range is $242,000 to $252,000. The married-filing-separately range for someone who lived with a spouse during the year remains $0 to $10,000.

Under the broader IRS rules, married filing separately taxpayers who did not live with their spouse during the year use the same Roth income range as the single/head-of-household group.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator uses the 2026 IRS IRA limit and Roth IRA income phase-out thresholds. These amounts can change for future tax years, so the calculator should be updated whenever the IRS announces new limits.

Traditional and Roth IRA contributions generally share one annual contribution limit. Entering contributions already made to another IRA can therefore reduce the remaining Roth IRA amount.

Modified AGI for Roth IRA purposes can differ from ordinary AGI because IRS adjustments may apply. The calculator relies on the modified AGI entered by the user and does not independently reproduce every tax-return adjustment used to determine Roth IRA MAGI.

Individual eligibility may also depend on compensation rules and tax circumstances not completely represented by the calculator. For example, the IRS has special rules for certain married couples filing jointly where one spouse has little or no taxable compensation.

Investment projections assume the annual return entered by the user. Real investment returns vary and are not guaranteed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Roth IRA contribution limit for 2026?

The regular IRA contribution limit for 2026 is 7,500.Someoneage50oroldercangenerallyusean8,600 limit because the 2026 IRA catch-up amount is $1,100.

Does the $7,500 limit apply separately to traditional and Roth IRAs?

No. The IRS generally applies one combined annual limit to regular contributions across your traditional and Roth IRAs. Contributions already made to another IRA can reduce the amount available for a Roth IRA.

Can my taxable compensation reduce my Roth IRA contribution?

Yes. Your regular IRA contribution generally cannot exceed your applicable annual limit or your taxable compensation, whichever is lower.

What happens if my income falls inside the Roth IRA phase-out range?

The calculator applies the IRS reduced-contribution worksheet approach. It determines how far your modified AGI is through the applicable phase-out range, reduces your contribution limit proportionally, and rounds the reduced amount up to the nearest $10.

Why can the reduced contribution be $200?

The IRS reduced Roth IRA worksheet states that when the calculated reduced limit is above $0 but below $200, the amount is increased to $200 before the remaining contribution room from other IRAs is considered.

Is the projected Roth IRA balance guaranteed?

No. It is an estimate based on the current balance, contribution assumptions, projection period, and annual return entered. Actual returns and future IRS contribution limits can differ.

Does this calculator provide tax advice?

No. It provides an estimate based on the entered information and 2026 IRS limits. Complex MAGI calculations, compensation rules, and individual tax circumstances may require IRS worksheets or professional tax guidance.
