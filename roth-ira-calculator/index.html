What Is a Roth IRA Calculator?

Answer: A Roth IRA calculator can estimate the direct contribution limit for a tax year and project account growth. For 2026, IRS limits and MAGI phase-outs determine whether the full annual contribution is available.

How to Use the Roth IRA Calculator

  1. Enter age, filing status, modified AGI, and taxable compensation.
  2. Add contributions already made to other traditional or Roth IRAs for the year.
  3. Enter current balance, years, and an assumed return.
  4. Review the estimated 2026 contribution limit and growth projection.

2026 Roth IRA Contribution Rules Used

2026 IRA limit: $7,500 Age 50+ limit: $8,600 Single/HOH Roth phase-out: $153,000–$168,000 MFJ Roth phase-out: $242,000–$252,000 MFS lived with spouse: $0–$10,000

Within a phase-out range, the calculator applies the IRS worksheet approach and rounds the reduced limit up to the nearest $10, with the worksheet’s $200 minimum rule when applicable.

Calculation Example

A single filer below $153,000 of modified AGI can generally use the full 2026 IRA dollar limit, subject to taxable compensation and contributions already made to other IRAs.

Assumptions and Limitations

Modified AGI rules can be complex. The shared annual limit applies across traditional and Roth IRAs. Eligibility can also depend on compensation and tax details not fully captured here. IRS limits change over time.

Roth IRA Calculator FAQs

What is the 2026 IRA contribution limit?

IRS states the 2026 total contribution limit across traditional and Roth IRAs is $7,500, or $8,600 if age 50 or older, limited further by taxable compensation.

What are the 2026 Roth IRA phase-out ranges?

IRS states $153,000–$168,000 for single/head-of-household filers and $242,000–$252,000 for married filing jointly; special rules apply to married filing separately.

Does the limit apply separately to Roth and traditional IRAs?

No. The annual IRA contribution limit generally applies to the combined total across your traditional and Roth IRAs.
