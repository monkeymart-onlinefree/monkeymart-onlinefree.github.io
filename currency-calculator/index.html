Use the Currency Calculator to convert an amount from one currency to another using a live exchange rate or a custom rate you enter. Select the source and target currencies, enter the amount, and the calculator returns the converted value along with the direct and inverse exchange rates.

What Is the Currency Calculator?

The Currency Calculator converts money between two currencies using a specified exchange rate. It can be useful for travel planning, international purchases, budgeting, financial comparisons, and checking the approximate value of money in another currency.

The calculator can use live exchange-rate data when available or calculate with a custom exchange rate entered by the user.

How to Use the Currency Calculator

  1. Amount: Enter the amount of money you want to convert.
  2. From: Select the currency the original amount is expressed in.
  3. To: Select the currency you want to convert the amount into.
  4. Rate (1 A = ? B): Use the available exchange rate or enter a custom rate showing how many units of the target currency equal one unit of the source currency.

The calculator then applies the rate to the entered amount and calculates the inverse rate.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The currency conversion formula is:

Converted Amount = Original Amount × Exchange Rate

Or:

C = A × R

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
C Converted amount Target currency
A Original amount Source currency
R Exchange rate Target currency per 1 source currency

If the rate is expressed as:

1 A = R B

then an amount in currency A is multiplied by R to calculate its value in currency B.

Inverse Exchange Rate

The calculator also calculates the inverse rate:

Inverse Rate = 1 ÷ Exchange Rate

Or:

Rᵢ = 1 / R

For example, if:

1 USD = 0.876347 EUR

then:

1 EUR ≈ 1.1411 USD

Calculation Sequence

  1. Read the original amount.
  2. Identify the From and To currencies.
  3. Obtain or use the entered exchange rate.
  4. Multiply the amount by the exchange rate.
  5. Calculate the inverse rate by dividing 1 by the exchange rate.
  6. Display the conversion result, direct rate, and inverse rate.

Worked Example

This example uses an exchange rate derived from the European Central Bank reference table dated September 23, 2026. The ECB published 1 EUR = 1.1411 USD, which is equivalent to approximately 1 USD = 0.876347 EUR.

Inputs

  • Amount: 100 USD
  • From: USD
  • To: EUR
  • Exchange rate: 0.876347 EUR per USD

Step 1

Apply the conversion formula:

Converted Amount = 100 × 0.876347

Step 2

Converted Amount = 87.6347 EUR

Rounded to two decimal places:

87.63 EUR

Step 3

Calculate the inverse rate:

Inverse Rate = 1 ÷ 0.876347
Inverse Rate ≈ 1.1411

Final Result

100 USD ≈ 87.63 EUR

Direct rate:

1 USD ≈ 0.876347 EUR

Inverse rate:

1 EUR ≈ 1.1411 USD

The actual live result may differ when exchange rates are updated.

Understanding Your Results

Converted Amount shows the value of your entered amount in the selected target currency based on the exchange rate used for the calculation.

Rate shows how many units of the target currency correspond to one unit of the source currency.

For example:

1 USD = 0.88 EUR

means each U.S. dollar is valued at approximately 0.88 euros using that rate.

Inverse shows the same currency relationship in the opposite direction. If USD to EUR is shown first, the inverse shows EUR to USD.

Current Exchange-Rate Data

Exchange rates can change throughout the day. For a current reference point, the European Central Bank's September 23, 2026 reference rates listed:

1 EUR = 1.1411 USD

That corresponds to approximately:

1 USD = 0.876347 EUR

The ECB states that its euro reference rates are generally updated around 16:00 CET on working days and are published for information purposes rather than transaction pricing.

A bank, card issuer, payment service, currency exchange office, or money-transfer provider may use a different customer rate and may add a spread, commission, foreign-transaction fee, or other charge.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculation assumes that the exchange rate entered or retrieved by the calculator applies to the entire amount being converted.

Live currency conversion requires an internet connection when current rate data must be retrieved.

The calculator does not automatically add bank charges, transfer fees, credit-card foreign-transaction fees, commissions, taxes, or exchange-rate markups. If you want those costs reflected in the calculation, they must already be incorporated into the custom rate you use.

Exchange rates can change after the calculation is performed, so the result should be treated as an estimate rather than a guaranteed transaction amount.

Displayed results may also be rounded. Financial institutions may use additional decimal precision or their own rounding policies.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is currency conversion calculated?

Multiply the original amount by the exchange rate:

Converted Amount = Amount × Exchange Rate

What does “1 A = ? B” mean?

It describes the exchange rate from the source currency to the target currency. For example, 1 USD = 0.88 EUR means one U.S. dollar converts to 0.88 euros at that rate.

What is an inverse exchange rate?

The inverse rate shows the currency pair in the opposite direction. It is calculated using:

Inverse Rate = 1 ÷ Exchange Rate

Can I enter my own exchange rate?

Yes. A custom rate can be used when you want to calculate with a specific rate from a bank, card provider, exchange service, or another source.

Why can the calculator result differ from my bank or card statement?

Banks and payment providers may use their own exchange rates and add spreads, commissions, foreign-transaction fees, or other charges. Those costs are not automatically included in the calculator.

How often do exchange rates change?

Market exchange rates can move throughout the trading day. Reference-rate providers may publish updates on their own schedules. For example, the ECB generally updates its reference rates once per working day around 16:00 CET.

Is the inverse rate always the exact opposite conversion?

Mathematically, the inverse is calculated as 1 divided by the direct rate. However, separate buy and sell rates offered by financial institutions may not be exact reciprocals because of spreads and fees.
