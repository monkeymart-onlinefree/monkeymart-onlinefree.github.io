What Is a Golf Handicap Calculator?

This page provides two related tools. Course Handicap converts a player's Handicap Index to the strokes applicable to a specific course and tees. The Handicap Index estimator uses the lowest recent Score Differentials according to the World Handicap System table for 3–20 differentials.

Course Handicap Formula

Course Handicap = Handicap Index × (Slope Rating ÷ 113) + (Course Rating − Par)

USGA identifies 113 as the standard Slope Rating used in this calculation. The final rounding depends on how the handicap is being applied; this calculator displays the nearest whole Course Handicap for a practical estimate.

Score Differential Formula

Score Differential = (113 ÷ Slope Rating) × (Adjusted Gross Score − Course Rating − PCC)

Handicap Index from Fewer Than 20 Scores

Current WHS rules use the lowest 1–7 differentials when there are fewer than 20, with adjustments at 3, 4, and 6 scores. At 20, the lowest 8 are averaged. The official Handicap Index can also be affected by safeguards such as caps and exceptional-score reductions.

Not an official handicap service: use an authorized WHS service for posting scores and an official Handicap Index. This page is for calculation and learning.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the current Course Handicap formula?

For an 18-hole round under the World Handicap System, Course Handicap = Handicap Index × (Slope Rating ÷ 113) + (Course Rating − par).

How many scores are needed for a Handicap Index?

Under the current USGA World Handicap System, an initial Handicap Index can be calculated with as few as three 18-hole Score Differentials, including eligible combinations created from 9-hole scores.

Why can an official Handicap Index differ from this estimate?

Official systems can apply safeguards such as exceptional score reductions, soft/hard caps, committee adjustments, and current 9-hole expected-score processing. This page estimates the base calculation from supplied differentials.
