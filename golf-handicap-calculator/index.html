The Golf Handicap Calculator calculates a Course Handicap from your Handicap Index, Slope Rating, Course Rating, and par. It can also estimate a base Handicap Index from 3–20 entered Score Differentials using the applicable World Handicap System calculation table.

What Is the Golf Handicap Calculator?

A golf handicap helps adjust a golfer's demonstrated ability for the difficulty of a particular course and set of tees.

This calculator has two main purposes:

  • Calculate a Course Handicap for a specific course or tee.
  • Estimate a base Handicap Index from 3–20 Score Differentials.

A Course Handicap represents the number of handicap strokes associated with a specific course and tees. The official calculation uses Handicap Index, Slope Rating, Course Rating, and par.

The Handicap Index estimate uses the lowest differentials from the scoring record, with the number used depending on how many Score Differentials are entered.

How to Use the Golf Handicap Calculator

Handicap Index

Enter your current Handicap Index. This value represents demonstrated playing ability and is used to calculate your Course Handicap.

Slope Rating

Enter the Slope Rating for the specific course and tees you are playing. Slope Rating represents the relative difficulty of the course for non-scratch players compared with scratch players.

Course Rating

Enter the Course Rating for the selected tees. Course Rating represents the expected difficulty of the course for a scratch player under normal conditions.

Par

Enter the par for the course or tees being used. Par is included because the Course Handicap calculation adjusts for the difference between Course Rating and par.

Score Differentials

Enter between 3 and 20 Score Differentials, separated by commas.

For example:

10.8, 11.2, 11.9, 12.1, 12.7, 13.1, 13.4, 14.0

These values are used to estimate a base Handicap Index according to the number of differentials entered.

Formula / Calculation Method

Course Handicap Formula

For an 18-hole Course Handicap, the calculation is:

Course Handicap = Handicap Index × (Slope Rating ÷ 113) + (Course Rating − Par)

This is the current USGA Course Handicap formula.

Score Differential Formula

The World Handicap System calculates an 18-hole Score Differential using:

Score Differential = (113 ÷ Slope Rating) × (Adjusted Gross Score − Course Rating − PCC)

where:

  • Adjusted Gross Score is the score used for handicap purposes.
  • Course Rating represents course difficulty for a scratch player.
  • Slope Rating adjusts for relative course difficulty.
  • PCC is the Playing Conditions Calculation adjustment.

Under the current USGA rule, PCC can range from −1.0 to +3.0.

This CalcPro calculator asks you to enter existing Score Differentials rather than Adjusted Gross Score or PCC, so this formula explains how those differential values are determined rather than adding extra calculator inputs.

For fewer than 20 Score Differentials, the applicable adjustment is added after averaging the selected lowest differentials. The result is rounded to the nearest tenth. With 20 differentials, the lowest eight are averaged before any other applicable official safeguards are considered.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Read the Handicap Index, Slope Rating, Course Rating, and par.
  2. Apply the Course Handicap formula.
  3. Count the entered Score Differentials.
  4. Select the required number of lowest differentials.
  5. Average those selected values.
  6. Apply the adjustment required for 3–6 differentials where applicable.
  7. Round the estimated base Handicap Index to the nearest tenth.

Worked Example

Inputs

Suppose a golfer enters:

  • Handicap Index: 12.4
  • Slope Rating: 125
  • Course Rating: 71.8
  • Par: 72
  • Score Differentials: 10.8, 11.2, 11.9, 12.1, 12.7, 13.1, 13.4, 14.0

Step 1: Calculate Course Handicap

Course Handicap = 12.4 × (125 ÷ 113) + (71.8 − 72)

First:

125 ÷ 113 = 1.1061947

Then:

12.4 × 1.1061947 = 13.7168

Course Rating adjustment:

71.8 − 72 = −0.2

Therefore:

13.7168 − 0.2 = 13.5168

Course Handicap Result

The calculated value is approximately 13.52, which is 14 strokes when expressed as a whole-number Course Handicap.

USGA rules retain the unrounded Course Handicap for some subsequent Playing Handicap calculations, while whole-number rounding is used in applicable Course Handicap uses.

Step 2: Estimate the Base Handicap Index

There are 8 Score Differentials.

For 7–8 differentials, the lowest 2 are used with no additional adjustment.

Lowest two:

10.8 and 11.2

Average:

(10.8 + 11.2) ÷ 2 = 11.0

Final Result

  • Course Handicap: 14
  • Estimated Base Handicap Index: 11.0

Understanding Your Results

Course Handicap

The Course Handicap adjusts a Handicap Index for the difficulty and par of the specific tees being played.

A higher Slope Rating generally increases the handicap adjustment for a player with a positive numerical Handicap Index, while the difference between Course Rating and par can increase or decrease the result.

Estimated Base Handicap Index

The base Handicap Index estimate is derived from the lowest Score Differentials in the entered list.

It should not automatically be treated as an official Handicap Index. Official handicap records may include safeguards and adjustments that require information not entered into this calculator.

Score Differentials

Score Differentials make scores from courses of different difficulty more comparable. A lower Score Differential generally represents a better performance relative to the course and playing conditions.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator is intended for calculation and educational purposes. It is not an official handicap-posting service and does not establish or maintain an official Handicap Index.

The Course Handicap calculation depends on the accuracy of the Handicap Index, Slope Rating, Course Rating, and par entered.

The Handicap Index section uses only the 3–20 Score Differentials supplied by the user.

Official Handicap Index calculations can include additional safeguards. For players with 20 scores and an established Low Handicap Index, the WHS soft cap begins when the newly calculated value is more than 3.0 strokes above the Low Handicap Index, while the hard cap limits the increase to 5.0 strokes above it.

Exceptional scores may also trigger reductions.
