What Is an Average Calculator?

An average calculator finds a single value that represents a list of numbers. In basic mathematics, “average” usually means the arithmetic mean: add all observations and divide by the number of observations. This page also reports the median and range so you can compare the mean with other simple summaries.

How to Use the Average Calculator

Enter numbers separated by commas, spaces, or line breaks. Select Calculate average. The calculator shows the arithmetic mean plus the sum, number of observations, median, range, minimum, and maximum.

Average Formula

Average (arithmetic mean) = (x₁ + x₂ + … + xₙ) ÷ n

Formula Variables

x₁ … xₙ are the values in the dataset, and n is the number of values. The numerator is their total sum.

Understanding Your Results

The mean uses every value, so unusually high or low observations can pull it away from the middle of the dataset. The median is the middle value after sorting and may be more resistant to outliers. The range is maximum minus minimum and describes the full spread.

Limitations

The arithmetic mean is not always the best summary for skewed data or data with influential outliers. This tool treats every input equally; it does not calculate a weighted mean unless you explicitly repeat values according to their intended weight.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate the average?

Add all values, then divide the sum by the number of values. This is the arithmetic mean, which is the usual meaning of average in basic mathematics.

Is average the same as median?

Not necessarily. The average or arithmetic mean uses every value in the sum, while the median is the middle value after sorting. Outliers can affect the mean more strongly.

Can the average be a decimal?

Yes. Even when every input is a whole number, the sum may not divide evenly by the count, so the arithmetic mean can be a decimal.

Can I average negative numbers?

Yes. The arithmetic mean works with positive numbers, negative numbers, zero, and decimal values.
