The Average Calculator finds the arithmetic mean of a list of numbers. Enter your numbers to calculate the average and see supporting results including the sum, count, median, minimum, and maximum.

What Is the Average Calculator?

The Average Calculator is a simple tool for finding the arithmetic average, also called the arithmetic mean, of multiple numbers.

It is useful when you want one central value for a set of numbers while also checking important summary values such as the median, total sum, number of observations, smallest value, and largest value.

How to Use the Average Calculator

Numbers

Enter the numbers you want to calculate.

Each entered number is treated as one observation with equal importance. For a meaningful result, the values should normally represent the same type of quantity or use compatible units.

The calculator uses the complete list to determine the average and its supporting summary values.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The arithmetic average is calculated by adding all values and dividing the total by the number of values:

Average = (x₁ + x₂ + ... + xₙ) ÷ n

It can also be written as:

A = Σxᵢ / n

NIST defines the arithmetic mean as the sum of the data points divided by the number of data points.

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
A Arithmetic average or mean Same unit as the entered numbers
xᵢ Each individual number Same unit as the data
n Number of entered values Count
Σ Sum of all included values —

Calculation Sequence

  1. Add all entered numbers to calculate the sum.
  2. Count how many numbers are included.
  3. Divide the sum by the count to calculate the arithmetic average.
  4. Consider the values in sorted order to determine the median.
  5. Identify the smallest and largest values for the minimum and maximum.

Sum

The sum is the total of all entered values:

Sum = x₁ + x₂ + ... + xₙ

or:

S = Σxᵢ

Count

The count is simply the number of values included in the calculation:

Count = n

Each entered observation contributes one to the count.

Median

The median is the middle value after the numbers are arranged from smallest to largest.

For an odd number of observations:

Median = middle value

For an even number of observations:

Median = (two middle values) ÷ 2

This follows the standard median definition described by the NIST Engineering Statistics Handbook.

Minimum

The minimum is the smallest number in the list.

Maximum

The maximum is the largest number in the list.

Worked Example

Suppose the entered numbers are:

12, 18, 25, 35, 40

Inputs

  • Numbers: 12, 18, 25, 35, 40

Step 1: Calculate the Sum

Sum = 12 + 18 + 25 + 35 + 40
Sum = 130

Step 2: Calculate the Average

There are five numbers:

n = 5

Apply the average formula:

Average = 130 ÷ 5
Average = 26

Step 3: Calculate the Supporting Results

The values in ascending order are:

12, 18, 25, 35, 40

Therefore:

  • Median = 25
  • Minimum = 12
  • Maximum = 40
  • Count = 5
  • Sum = 130

Final Result

Average = 26

The five numbers have a total of 130, a median of 25, a minimum of 12, and a maximum of 40.

Understanding Your Results

Average

The average is the equal-share value obtained by dividing the sum by the number of observations. It is expressed in the same unit as the original numbers.

Sum

The sum shows the total value of all numbers before division.

Count

The count tells you how many individual observations were included.

Median

The median shows the center of the ordered data. It can differ significantly from the average when one or more values are unusually high or low.

Minimum

The minimum identifies the smallest entered value.

Maximum

The maximum identifies the largest entered value.

Looking at the average together with the median, minimum, and maximum can provide more context than looking at the average alone.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator uses the arithmetic mean, not a geometric, harmonic, or weighted mean.

Every entered value receives equal weight. There is no separate weight input. Repeating a value multiple times causes that value to be counted multiple times and therefore gives it more influence on the result.

The arithmetic mean may not always represent the typical value well when the data are strongly skewed or contain unusually large or small observations. The median can be useful for comparison because it is generally less affected by extreme observations.

The calculation requires at least one observation mathematically because the formula divides by the number of values.

The arithmetic-mean formula does not require a fixed decimal precision. If a displayed result is rounded, the shown value is an approximation of the exact quotient.

This calculator summarizes the entered numbers but does not by itself measure variability or describe the complete distribution of the data.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is average the same as arithmetic mean?

Yes. In this calculator, average means the arithmetic mean: add the numbers and divide by how many numbers there are.

What is the difference between the average and median?

The average uses every value in the sum. The median is the middle value after the numbers are sorted. With an even number of observations, the median is the average of the two middle values.

Does the order of the numbers change the average?

No. Changing the order does not change the sum, count, or arithmetic average.

Are repeated numbers counted more than once?

Yes. Each entered observation is included separately. If the same number appears multiple times, every occurrence contributes to the sum and count.

Does this calculator calculate a weighted average?

No separate weighted-average calculation is used. Each entered number has equal weight. If a value is repeated, each repetition is treated as another observation.

Can the average and median be very different?

Yes. A particularly large or small value can move the arithmetic mean while having less effect on the median. This is why both values can be useful when reviewing a data set.

Can the arithmetic average be greater than the maximum or smaller than the minimum?

For a non-empty list of ordinary real numbers with equal positive weighting, the arithmetic mean falls between the minimum and maximum values.
