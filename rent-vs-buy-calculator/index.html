What Is a Rent vs Buy Calculator?

A rent vs buy calculator compares the financial paths of renting and owning over a chosen time horizon. This version focuses on estimated wealth at the end of the holding period instead of simply comparing monthly payments.

Methodology

The buyer makes a down payment and purchase closing costs, then pays mortgage principal and interest plus estimated property tax, insurance, and maintenance. Home value changes with the entered appreciation rate. At the end, the calculator subtracts selling costs and the remaining mortgage balance to estimate net sale proceeds.

The renter begins with the cash that was not used for the buyer’s down payment and purchase closing costs. Each month, whichever side has the lower housing outflow invests the difference at the selected alternative investment return. This creates a like-for-like cash-flow comparison.

Why the Result Is Sensitive

Home appreciation, rent growth, interest rates, maintenance, transaction costs, and investment returns are uncertain. A small change in any of these assumptions can shift the result, especially over longer periods.

What This Calculator Does Not Include

It excludes tax deductions or credits, mortgage insurance, HOA fees, renovation value, moving costs, security deposits, rent concessions, capital-gains taxes, and lender-specific closing details. Freddie Mac also describes rent-vs-buy tools as guidance and financial estimates rather than precise transaction calculations.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does this rent vs buy calculator compare?

It compares estimated wealth after the selected holding period. Renting invests the upfront cash not used for a down payment and purchase closing costs, while buying builds home equity and may invest any monthly savings versus rent.

Does the result predict future home prices or rents?

No. Appreciation, rent growth, investment return, taxes, insurance, maintenance, and selling costs are user assumptions. Small changes can materially change the result.

Does the calculator include tax deductions?

No. It does not model income-tax deductions or credits because eligibility and tax effects vary by household and jurisdiction.

Formula / Algorithm Used in Tool

Monthly cash-flow simulation. Buyer: down payment + closing costs, amortizing mortgage payment, tax/insurance/maintenance; home value grows by appreciation and ending equity = sale value − selling costs − remaining mortgage. Renter invests avoided upfront cash and monthly outflow differences at the assumed investment return. Compare ending wealth.
