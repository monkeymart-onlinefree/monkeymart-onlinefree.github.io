Use the Retirement Calculator to estimate how much your savings may grow by retirement and how much monthly income that projected balance could provide. Enter your current age, retirement age, savings, monthly contribution, expected returns, inflation, retirement length, and desired monthly income to compare your projected retirement income with your goal.

What Is the Retirement Calculator?

The Retirement Calculator is a long-term planning tool that models two stages: saving before retirement and withdrawing money during retirement.

It projects your savings from your current balance and monthly contributions, then converts the estimated retirement balance into a level monthly withdrawal over the number of retirement years you enter. It also compares that withdrawal with your desired retirement income and shows a potential monthly surplus or shortfall.

The result is a projection, not a guaranteed retirement outcome. Actual investment performance, inflation, taxes, account rules, fees, Social Security, pensions, healthcare costs, and lifespan can change the amount you ultimately have available.

How to Use the Retirement Calculator

Formula / Calculation Method

The calculator first projects retirement savings and then converts the projected balance into monthly retirement withdrawals.

Savings Before Retirement

Recurring-contribution growth can be represented in general form as:

F = P(1 + i)ⁿ + C × [((1 + i)ⁿ − 1) / i] × (1 + i)ᵈ

If the periodic return is zero:

F = P + nC

This future-value structure combines the growth of the starting balance with the accumulated value of recurring contributions. OpenStax also describes future-value calculations for a stream of periodic annuity payments.

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
F Projected savings at retirement Dollars
P Current savings Dollars
C Contribution per period Dollars
i Investment return per calculation period Decimal rate
n Number of contribution periods Periods
d Contribution-timing factor used by the calculation 0 or 1 in the general formula

Contribution timing is not a selectable input on this calculator, so users do not need to choose a beginning-of-period or end-of-period option.

Inflation-Adjusted Income Goal

Your desired monthly income is entered in today's dollars. Inflation is used to express that target in retirement-year dollars.

A standard compound inflation adjustment is:

Future Income Goal = Current Income Goal × (1 + f)ᵗ

Where f is the annual inflation assumption and t is the number of years until retirement.

This lets the calculator compare the retirement withdrawal with a future income target that represents similar purchasing power.

Monthly Retirement Withdrawal

The implemented withdrawal formula is:

Withdrawal = Balance × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)⁻ⁿ]

Where:

Symbol Meaning Unit
Balance Projected savings when retirement begins Dollars
r Periodic return during retirement Decimal rate
n Number of withdrawal periods Months or other matching periods
Withdrawal Level withdrawal per period Dollars

For monthly withdrawals, the annual retirement return is converted to a monthly periodic rate and the retirement duration is converted to months. The formula is the rearranged present-value formula for a level annuity.

If the retirement return is 0%, the equivalent calculation is simply:

Monthly Withdrawal = Balance ÷ Number of Retirement Months

Monthly Surplus or Gap

The calculator compares the projected monthly withdrawal with the desired monthly income at retirement.

Monthly Surplus / Gap = Projected Monthly Withdrawal − Desired Monthly Income at Retirement

A positive result means the modeled withdrawal is above the income target. A negative result means the modeled withdrawal is below the target.

Calculation Sequence

First, the calculator determines the time from your current age to your retirement age. It then grows the current savings and monthly contribution stream using the pre-retirement return assumption.

Next, it adjusts the desired monthly income to reflect the inflation assumption over the years until retirement.

The projected retirement balance is then converted into a level monthly withdrawal using the return during retirement and the number of years in retirement.

Finally, the calculator compares that withdrawal with your retirement income target and reports the difference.

Worked Example

The following example uses a 0% pre-retirement return and 0% inflation so the accumulation portion can be verified directly without contribution-timing or compounding differences.

Inputs

Input Example
Current age 40
Retirement age 65
Current savings $100,000
Monthly contribution $1,000
Return before retirement 0%
Inflation 0%
Years in retirement 25
Return during retirement 4%
Desired monthly income $4,000

Step 1: Calculate Time Until Retirement

65 − 40 = 25 years
25 × 12 = 300 months

Step 2: Project Savings at Retirement

Because the example uses a 0% return before retirement:

Projected savings = $100,000 + ($1,000 × 300)
Projected savings = $400,000

There is no investment growth in this example, so the balance comes entirely from the starting amount and subsequent contributions.

Step 3: Calculate the Monthly Retirement Rate

Annual retirement return:

4% = 0.04

Monthly rate:

r = 0.04 ÷ 12
r = 0.0033333333

Retirement periods:

n = 25 × 12 = 300

Step 4: Calculate Monthly Withdrawal

Withdrawal = $400,000 × 0.0033333333 ÷ [1 − (1.0033333333)⁻³⁰⁰]
Projected monthly withdrawal ≈ $2,111.35

Step 5: Compare With the Income Goal

Because inflation is 0%, the $4,000 income goal remains $4,000 at retirement.

Surplus / Gap = $2,111.35 − $4,000
Surplus / Gap = −$1,888.65 per month

Final Result

With these example assumptions, the projected retirement balance is $400,000, supporting a modeled monthly withdrawal of approximately $2,111.35 for 25 years at a constant 4% annual return.

Compared with the $4,000 monthly income goal, the projection shows a monthly gap of about $1,888.65.

Understanding Your Results

Projected Savings at Retirement

This is the estimated balance available when you reach the retirement age entered in the calculator. It combines your starting savings, contributions, and modeled investment growth.

Projected Monthly Withdrawal

This is the level monthly amount that the projected retirement balance can support over the selected retirement period under the assumed retirement return.

It should not be interpreted as a guaranteed safe withdrawal amount. Real investment returns are not constant.

Desired Monthly Income at Retirement

This expresses your desired income in retirement-year dollars after applying the calculator's inflation assumption to the amount entered in today's dollars.

Monthly Surplus / Gap

This compares the modeled withdrawal with your desired income.

A surplus indicates that the modeled withdrawal exceeds the goal. A gap indicates that the projected withdrawal is below the goal.

Total Contributions Before Growth

This separates contributed money from investment growth so you can better understand how much of the projected balance comes from money supplied to the savings plan rather than assumed returns.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculation uses assumed investment returns rather than unpredictable year-by-year market performance. Constant returns make projections easier to understand but do not capture market volatility or sequence-of-returns risk.

Actual retirement outcomes may also be affected by investment fees, taxes, contribution changes, withdrawals before retirement, pensions, Social Security, healthcare expenses, longevity, and rules that apply to specific retirement accounts.

The calculator does not automatically apply every IRS contribution, distribution, eligibility, or tax rule. The IRS explains that retirement plans can have specific rules covering contributions, distributions, fees, eligibility, and other plan features.

Results should therefore be used for planning and scenario comparison rather than as a guarantee of future retirement income.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Retirement Calculator estimate my retirement savings?

It projects your current savings and recurring monthly contributions forward to your retirement age using the return assumption you enter.

How is the monthly retirement withdrawal calculated?

The projected retirement balance is converted into level periodic withdrawals using an annuity-payment formula based on the retirement-period return and number of withdrawal periods.

Why does the calculator ask for two investment return rates?

The return before retirement affects how savings grow while you are contributing. The return during retirement affects how much monthly income the projected balance can support while withdrawals are being made.

Why is desired retirement income entered in today's dollars?

Today's dollars are easier to relate to your current lifestyle. The inflation input lets the calculator translate that purchasing-power target into an estimated amount at retirement.

Does the calculator include Social Security or pension income?

No separate Social Security or pension input is part of this calculator's documented input set. If you expect those income sources, consider them separately when evaluating the projection.

Does the calculator include taxes and investment fees?

No. Taxes, account-specific tax treatment, and investment fees can reduce actual retirement balances or spendable income.

Are the results guaranteed?

No. Investment returns, inflation, expenses, lifespan, tax rules, and retirement-account rules can change. Investor.gov provides financial planning tools as educational resources, while actual investment outcomes remain uncertain.
