What Is a GDP Calculator?

This GDP calculator uses the expenditure approach to estimate gross domestic product from consumption, investment, government spending, exports, and imports. The U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis describes this widely used formula as C + I + G + X − M.

GDP Formula

GDP = C + I + G + X − M

C represents consumption; I represents investment; G represents government consumption and investment; X is exports; and M is imports. Exports add domestically produced output sold abroad, while imports are subtracted so foreign-produced output is not counted as domestic production.

Example

If C = 100, I = 30, G = 40, X = 20, and M = 10, then GDP = 100 + 30 + 40 + 20 − 10 = 180 in the same monetary units used for every input.

Nominal vs. Real GDP

This arithmetic does not itself adjust for inflation. If all components are measured at current prices, the total is nominal. Real GDP requires a consistent inflation-adjusted measurement framework, normally produced by a national statistical agency.

Limitations

National accounts involve detailed definitions, source data, seasonal adjustment, revisions, statistical discrepancies, and chain-type quantity measures that a simple calculator cannot reproduce. Use official statistical releases for reported GDP.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the expenditure formula for GDP?

GDP = C + I + G + X − M, where C is consumption, I is investment, G is government spending, X is exports, and M is imports.

Why are imports subtracted from GDP?

Imports can be included in consumption, investment, or government spending but were produced abroad. Subtracting imports removes that foreign production from domestic GDP.

Does this calculate real GDP or nominal GDP?

It calculates an expenditure total from the monetary values entered. Whether the result is nominal or real depends on whether your inputs are current-price values or consistently inflation-adjusted values.
