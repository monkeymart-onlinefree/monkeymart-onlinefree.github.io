What Is the Dew Point Calculator?

A dew point calculator converts air temperature and relative humidity into the temperature at which the air would become saturated if cooled without changing its moisture content. It is widely used in weather analysis and humidity interpretation.

How to Use the Dew Point Calculator

Enter the air temperature and relative humidity. The calculator converts temperature to Celsius when necessary, applies the NOAA/NCEP saturation-vapor-pressure relationship, then converts the dew point back to your preferred unit.

Dew Point Calculator Formula

eₛ = (RH/100) × 6.1365 × exp(17.502T / (240.97 + T)); Td = 240.97 × ln(eₛ/6.1365) / [17.502 − ln(eₛ/6.1365)]

Formula Variables

T is air temperature in °C, RH is relative humidity in percent, eₛ is the actual vapor-pressure term in the cited NCEP implementation, and Td is dew point in °C.

Calculation Example

At 25°C and 60% relative humidity, the calculation gives a dew point of about 16.7°C.

Understanding Your Results

A dew point close to air temperature indicates air near saturation. The result panel shows dew point, the temperature–dew-point spread, and both Celsius and Fahrenheit.

Important Factors

Sensor accuracy matters. A small relative-humidity error can move the calculated dew point, especially under very dry or very humid conditions.

Limitations

This is a meteorological estimate based on measured temperature and relative humidity. It does not model local surfaces, radiative cooling, pressure changes, fog formation, or indoor condensation on a specific material.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is dew point?

Dew point is the temperature to which air would need to cool, at constant pressure and water-vapor content, for saturation to occur.

Can dew point be higher than air temperature?

For ordinary unsaturated conditions, dew point is at or below the air temperature. A relative humidity of 100% makes the dew point approximately equal to air temperature.

Why use dew point instead of relative humidity?

Relative humidity changes with temperature. Dew point more directly reflects the amount of water vapor in the air, though comfort interpretation is still context dependent.
