The Dew Point Calculator estimates dew point from air temperature and relative humidity. It uses the NOAA/NCEP vapor-pressure relationship to determine the temperature at which the air reaches saturation and condensation can begin.

What Is the Dew Point Calculator?

The Dew Point Calculator converts a measured air temperature and relative humidity into an estimated dew point temperature.

Dew point is the temperature to which air must cool for it to become saturated with water vapor. At 100% relative humidity, dew point and air temperature are equal. Under normal atmospheric conditions, dew point is equal to or below the air temperature.

This calculation can be useful for weather observation, environmental monitoring, outdoor planning, and understanding how much moisture is present in the air.

How to Use the Dew Point Calculator

Enter the two values used by the calculation:

  1. Air Temperature — Enter the current ambient air temperature. The implemented dew point formula uses temperature in degrees Celsius (°C).
  2. Relative Humidity (%) — Enter the relative humidity as a percentage. Relative humidity compares the amount of moisture in the air with the amount needed for saturation at the same temperature.

The calculator then estimates the corresponding dew point temperature.

For the mathematical formula, relative humidity must be greater than zero because the calculation contains a natural logarithm. A relative humidity of 100% represents saturated air.

Formula / Calculation Method

The calculator uses the NOAA/NCEP relationship for deriving dew point from air temperature and relative humidity. NOAA's MADIS documentation describes the same constants and temperature/RH-based calculation.

Main Formula

First, calculate the humidity-adjusted vapor-pressure term:

es=(RH100)×6.1365×exp⁡(17.502T240.97+T)e_s = \left(\frac{RH}{100}\right) \times 6.1365 \times \exp\left(\frac{17.502T}{240.97+T}\right)

Then calculate dew point:

Td=240.97×ln⁡(es/6.1365)17.502−ln⁡(es/6.1365)T_d = \frac{240.97 \times \ln(e_s/6.1365)} {17.502-\ln(e_s/6.1365)}

The constants 17.502 and 240.97 are also used in the Buck saturation-vapor-pressure relationship for temperature in Celsius. UCAR documents this form with vapor pressure expressed in hPa.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Divide relative humidity by 100 to convert the percentage to a decimal.
  2. Calculate the exponential temperature term.
  3. Use temperature and humidity to calculate ese_s.
  4. Calculate ln⁡(es/6.1365)\ln(e_s/6.1365).
  5. Substitute that value into the dew point equation.
  6. Return the calculated dew point temperature.

No pressure, wind speed, elevation, or wet-bulb temperature is used by this calculator.

Understanding Your Results

Dew Point Temperature

The main result tells you the temperature at which the current amount of water vapor would bring the air to saturation.

A dew point closer to the current air temperature indicates higher relative humidity. A larger difference between air temperature and dew point indicates drier air.

If the dew point equals the air temperature, relative humidity is approximately 100%.

Why Dew Point Can Be More Useful Than Relative Humidity

Relative humidity changes when air temperature changes, even if the actual moisture content remains similar. Dew point is commonly used to describe atmospheric moisture more directly. NOAA notes that higher dew points indicate more moisture in the air.

The calculator should therefore be interpreted as a meteorological moisture estimate, not as a prediction that dew, fog, or condensation will definitely form at a particular location.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator estimates dew point from measured air temperature and relative humidity using the specified NOAA/NCEP relationship.

Important limitations include:

  • The calculation depends on the accuracy of the temperature and humidity measurements entered.
  • The implemented equation uses air temperature in Celsius.
  • Relative humidity must be positive for the logarithmic calculation to produce a valid result.
  • The calculator estimates atmospheric dew point; it does not directly measure dew point with a sensor.
  • It does not model local surface temperatures.
  • It does not account for radiative surface cooling.
  • It does not model changing atmospheric pressure.
  • It does not predict whether fog will form.
  • It does not determine whether condensation will occur on a particular wall, window, pipe, or other material.
  • Local conditions can differ from the air measurements used as inputs.

NOAA's MADIS documentation also describes dew point as a derived value that can be calculated from measured air temperature and relative humidity.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is dew point?

Dew point is the temperature at which air with its current moisture content becomes saturated. Cooling the air to this temperature can allow water vapor to begin condensing into liquid water.

What information do I need to calculate dew point?

This calculator requires only air temperature and relative humidity. It does not require atmospheric pressure, elevation, wind speed, or wet-bulb temperature.

What happens when relative humidity is 100%?

At 100% relative humidity, the air is saturated. Under these conditions, the dew point is equal to the air temperature.

Can dew point be higher than the air temperature?

Under normal atmospheric conditions, dew point should not exceed the current air temperature. NOAA describes dew point as being equal to or lower than ambient temperature.

Why can't relative humidity be zero in this formula?

The dew point equation uses the natural logarithm of a value derived from relative humidity. At exactly 0% RH, that term reaches zero, and ln⁡(0)\ln(0) is mathematically undefined.

Does a dew point result mean condensation will definitely occur?

No. The result describes the atmospheric dew point. Condensation on an actual surface also depends on the surface temperature and local conditions. The calculator does not model individual materials or surfaces.

Is dew point the same as relative humidity?

No. Relative humidity is a percentage showing how close the air is to saturation at its current temperature. Dew point is a temperature representing the moisture level of the air. Because relative humidity changes with temperature, two locations with the same relative humidity can have different dew points.
