The Statistics Calculator summarizes a numerical data set using count, sum, mean, median, mode, range, quartiles, variance, standard deviation, interquartile range (IQR), and coefficient of variation. Enter your data and choose the available quartile method to calculate descriptive statistics for the values provided.

What Is the Statistics Calculator?

A Statistics Calculator turns a group of numerical observations into descriptive statistics that show the center and spread of the data.

It can be useful for students, researchers, analysts, and anyone who needs to summarize a set of numbers without calculating each statistic manually. The results describe the values entered; they do not establish causation, statistical significance, or whether a sample represents a larger population.

How to Use the Statistics Calculator

  1. Enter the data set. Add the numerical values you want to analyze using the calculator's data-entry field.
  2. Choose the quartile method. Select the available quartile calculation method. Different quartile conventions can produce different Q1 and Q3 values, especially for small data sets.
  3. Calculate the results. Review the count, sum, measures of center, measures of spread, quartiles, variance, standard deviation, IQR, and coefficient of variation.

Formula / Calculation Method

Count and Sum

If the data values are:

x₁, x₂, x₃, …, xₙ

the count is:

n = number of observations

and the sum is:

Σx = x₁ + x₂ + … + xₙ

Arithmetic Mean

The arithmetic mean is the sum of all observations divided by the number of observations:

x̄ = Σx / n

For a complete population, the population mean is commonly written as:

μ = Σx / N

OpenStax defines the mean as the total of the values divided by the number of observations.

Median

First arrange the observations from smallest to largest.

For an odd number of observations, the median is the middle value.

For an even number of observations:

Median = (two middle values) / 2

The median divides the ordered data into two halves and is generally less affected by extreme values than the mean.

Mode

The mode is the value that occurs most frequently. A data set may have one mode, multiple values tied for the highest frequency, or no single unique mode.

Range

The range measures the distance between the largest and smallest observations:

Range = Maximum − Minimum

Quartiles

Quartiles divide ordered data into sections. Q1 represents the first quartile, Q2 is the median, and Q3 is the third quartile.

The exact Q1 and Q3 values depend on the quartile method selected in the calculator. This matters because recognized quartile conventions can return different values for the same small data set.

Interquartile Range

The interquartile range measures the spread of the middle portion of the data:

IQR = Q3 − Q1

OpenStax defines IQR as the difference between the third and first quartiles.

Population Variance

When the values represent the complete population:

σ² = Σ(xᵢ − μ)² / N

Sample Variance

When the values are treated as a sample:

s² = Σ(xᵢ − x̄)² / (n − 1)

Using n − 1 distinguishes sample variance from population variance.

Standard Deviation

Standard deviation is the square root of variance.

Population standard deviation:

σ = √σ²

Sample standard deviation:

s = √s²

Standard deviation is expressed in the same units as the original data, while variance is expressed in squared units.

Coefficient of Variation

The coefficient of variation compares the standard deviation with the mean:

CV = (Standard Deviation / Mean) × 100%

For sample statistics this is commonly written as:

CV = (s / x̄) × 100%

The mean must be nonzero for the percentage calculation. The coefficient of variation is most meaningful for ratio-scale data with a meaningful zero.

Calculation Sequence

The calculator first reads the data set and determines basic values such as the count and sum. It orders the data for median, mode, range, and quartile calculations, then applies the selected quartile method to Q1 and Q3.

The mean is calculated from the sum and count. Deviations from the appropriate mean are then used to calculate population and sample variance. Standard deviation is obtained by taking the square root of the corresponding variance, while IQR is calculated from Q3 minus Q1.

Worked Example

Consider the data set:

2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9

Inputs

Data set: 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9

Quartile method: Use the method selected in the calculator

Step 1: Count, Sum, and Mean

There are eight observations:

n = 8

Their sum is:

Σx = 2 + 4 + 4 + 4 + 5 + 5 + 7 + 9 = 40

Mean:

x̄ = 40 / 8 = 5

Step 2: Median, Mode, and Range

The data are already ordered.

The two middle values are 4 and 5:

Median = (4 + 5) / 2 = 4.5

The value 4 occurs most often:

Mode = 4

Range:

Range = 9 − 2 = 7

Step 3: Variance

Using a mean of 5, the sum of squared deviations is:

Σ(x − 5)² = 32

Population variance:

σ² = 32 / 8 = 4

Sample variance:

s² = 32 / 7 ≈ 4.571428571

Step 4: Standard Deviation

Population standard deviation:

σ = √4 = 2

Sample standard deviation:

s = √4.571428571 ≈ 2.138089935

Final Result

For this data set, the method-independent results are count = 8, sum = 40, mean = 5, median = 4.5, mode = 4, range = 7, population variance = 4, population standard deviation = 2, sample variance ≈ 4.5714, and sample standard deviation ≈ 2.1381.

Q1, Q3, and IQR should be read using the quartile method selected in the calculator rather than assuming a different quartile convention.

Understanding Your Results

Count tells you how many observations are included, while sum gives their total.

Mean describes the arithmetic average. Because every numerical value contributes directly to it, extreme values can move the mean noticeably.

Median represents the middle of the ordered data and can be useful when extreme observations make the mean less representative of the center.

Mode identifies the most frequently occurring observation.

Range measures the full distance from the smallest to the largest value, so a single extreme observation can strongly affect it.

Q1 and Q3 describe locations within the ordered data. Their exact values depend on the quartile method selected.

IQR measures the spread between Q1 and Q3 and therefore focuses on the central portion of the data.

Variance measures squared deviations from the mean. Sample and population variance use different denominators.

Standard deviation expresses spread in the original unit of measurement. A value of zero means all observations are equal; larger values indicate greater spread around the mean.

Coefficient of variation expresses standard deviation relative to the mean. It can help compare relative variation between suitable data sets that use different scales or have substantially different means.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator provides descriptive statistics for the values entered. These statistics summarize a data set but cannot by themselves establish causation, prove statistical significance, or show that a sample is representative of a larger population.

Outliers can strongly influence the mean, range, variance, standard deviation, and coefficient of variation. The median and IQR are generally less dependent on the numerical size of extreme observations.

Quartile results can differ between valid calculation conventions, which is why the selected quartile method matters. When comparing results with another calculator, spreadsheet, or statistical program, make sure both use the same quartile method.

Sample variance and sample standard deviation require enough observations to use the n − 1 denominator. The standard percentage form of coefficient of variation is undefined when the mean is zero and its interpretation is most appropriate for data with a meaningful zero.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a Statistics Calculator calculate?

It summarizes a numerical data set using statistics such as count, sum, mean, median, mode, range, quartiles, variance, standard deviation, IQR, and coefficient of variation.

What is the difference between sample and population variance?

Population variance divides the sum of squared deviations by N, while sample variance divides by n − 1. The sample formula is used when the entered observations represent a sample rather than the entire population.

Why can quartiles differ between calculators?

There is more than one accepted method for locating or interpolating quartiles. Different methods can therefore produce different Q1 and Q3 values from the same data, particularly when the data set is small.

Is standard deviation the same as variance?

No. Variance is based on squared deviations from the mean. Standard deviation is the square root of variance, which returns the measure of spread to the original unit of the data.

What happens if all numbers are the same?

The range, variance, and standard deviation are zero because there is no variation between observations. The mean and median are equal to that repeated value.

Can coefficient of variation be calculated when the mean is zero?

The standard formula divides standard deviation by the mean, so a percentage coefficient of variation is undefined when the mean equals zero.

Does this calculator tell me whether my data are statistically significant?

No. Descriptive measures summarize the entered observations. Statistical significance requires an appropriate inferential statistical method based on the research question and data.
