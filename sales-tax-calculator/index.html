The Calorie Calculator estimates your daily calorie needs from your sex, age, weight, height, and activity factor. It first estimates resting energy using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, then calculates activity-adjusted maintenance calories and displays a goal-calorie estimate based on your daily goal adjustment.

What Is the Calorie Calculator?

The Calorie Calculator is an adult energy-needs estimation tool. It calculates estimated BMR or resting energy, adjusts that estimate for activity, and provides an estimated number of calories needed per day to maintain body weight.

You can also enter a daily calorie adjustment to produce a goal-calorie estimate for planning purposes. The result is an estimate rather than a measured metabolic rate or individualized nutrition prescription.

How to Use the Calorie Calculator

Enter the following information in the calculator:

  1. Sex used by equation: Select the sex used for the Mifflin-St Jeor equation. The equation uses a different constant for men and women.
  2. Age: Enter your age in years.
  3. Weight: Enter your body weight in kilograms (kg).
  4. Height: Enter your height in centimeters (cm).
  5. Activity factor: Enter or select the activity multiplier used to adjust resting energy to estimated daily maintenance calories.
  6. Daily goal adjustment: Enter the calorie adjustment, in kilocalories (kcal), used by the calculator to determine the goal-calorie result.

The available specification does not define separate minimum or maximum input limits, so no unsupported ranges are stated here.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation to estimate resting energy expenditure.

For men:

REE = 10W + 6.25H − 5A + 5

For women:

REE = 10W + 6.25H − 5A − 161

The original Mifflin-St Jeor research developed the equation from 498 healthy men and women and used weight, height, age, and sex to predict resting energy expenditure.

Variables

Variable Meaning Unit
REE Estimated resting energy expenditure, displayed by the calculator as estimated BMR/resting energy kcal/day
W Body weight kg
H Height cm
A Age years
F Activity factor multiplier

Estimated Maintenance Calories

After calculating resting energy, the calculator applies the activity factor:

Estimated Maintenance Calories = REE × Activity Factor

For example, if estimated resting energy is 1,700 kcal/day and the activity factor is 1.20:

1,700 × 1.20 = 2,040 kcal/day

This is an activity-adjusted estimate rather than a direct measurement of daily energy expenditure.

Goal Calories

The calculator also reports Goal Calories using the daily goal adjustment entered by the user.

The available calculator specification identifies the daily goal adjustment as an input and goal calories as an output but does not define the exact secondary arithmetic rule. Therefore, a separate goal-calorie equation is not stated here rather than assuming an unsupported calculation.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Read sex, age, weight, and height.
  2. Apply the appropriate Mifflin-St Jeor equation.
  3. Calculate estimated resting energy/BMR in kcal per day.
  4. Multiply resting energy by the entered activity factor.
  5. Display estimated maintenance calories and the activity factor.
  6. Use the entered daily goal adjustment to generate the calculator's goal-calorie result.

Worked Example

Suppose a 30-year-old man enters:

Inputs

  • Sex used by equation: Male
  • Age: 30 years
  • Weight: 80 kg
  • Height: 180 cm
  • Activity factor: 1.20
  • Daily goal adjustment: 0 kcal

Step 1: Calculate Resting Energy

REE = 10W + 6.25H − 5A + 5

Substitute the example values:

REE = (10 × 80) + (6.25 × 180) − (5 × 30) + 5
REE = 800 + 1,125 − 150 + 5
REE = 1,780 kcal/day

Step 2: Calculate Maintenance Calories

Maintenance Calories = 1,780 × 1.20
Maintenance Calories = 2,136 kcal/day

Step 3: Goal Adjustment

The example uses a daily goal adjustment of 0 kcal, so no calorie adjustment is being requested in this example.

Final Result

  • Estimated BMR / resting energy: 1,780 kcal/day
  • Estimated maintenance calories: 2,136 kcal/day
  • Activity factor: 1.20
  • Goal calories with a zero adjustment: 2,136 kcal/day

Understanding Your Results

Estimated BMR

Estimated BMR represents the calculator's estimate of the energy your body uses at rest. The underlying Mifflin-St Jeor study describes this measure as resting energy expenditure.

It is a predicted value, not a direct metabolic measurement.

Estimated Maintenance Calories

Maintenance calories are the estimated daily calories required after accounting for the activity factor:

Resting energy × activity factor

Eating exactly this amount does not guarantee that body weight will remain unchanged because actual energy expenditure varies between individuals.

Goal Calories

Goal calories provide an adjusted daily calorie figure based on the goal adjustment entered into the calculator. Treat the result as a planning estimate rather than an individualized medical or diet prescription.

Activity Factor

The activity factor is the multiplier applied to resting energy. Because a single multiplier simplifies real-world movement, exercise, work, and daily activity, it should be treated as an approximation.

Assumptions and Limitations

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation is a predictive equation rather than a direct measurement of metabolism. The original study included 498 healthy subjects aged 19 to 78 years, including both normal-weight and obese adults. Estimates for people outside the characteristics studied may have additional uncertainty.

Activity factors are also approximations. Two people with the same age, height, weight, and selected activity factor may have different actual calorie requirements because of differences in body composition, exercise, daily movement, health status, medications, environment, and metabolic adaptation.

Calorie needs can also change over time as body weight and activity change. NIDDK notes that daily calorie needs can depend on factors including age, weight, height, metabolism, sex, and physical activity.

This calculator is intended for general adult estimation and should not be treated as a diagnosis or individualized medical advice. Pregnancy and breastfeeding can require different energy planning; for example, NIDDK's adult Body Weight Planner specifically excludes pregnant and breastfeeding users.

Frequently Asked Questions

What formula does the Calorie Calculator use?

It uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation to estimate resting energy from weight, height, age, and sex. The resulting estimate is then multiplied by the activity factor to calculate estimated maintenance calories.

What is the difference between BMR and maintenance calories?

The calculator's estimated BMR/resting energy represents calories used at rest. Maintenance calories include an additional adjustment for activity, so they are calculated by multiplying resting energy by the activity factor.

Why does activity level affect calorie needs?

Physical activity increases energy expenditure. NIDDK notes that people who are more physically active generally require more calories than people who are less active.

What does the daily goal adjustment do?

It is an additional calorie input used by the calculator when generating the Goal Calories result. It allows the maintenance estimate to be adjusted for a user's planning goal.

How accurate is the calorie estimate?

It should be treated as an estimate. Mifflin-St Jeor is a predictive equation based on population data, while an individual's actual energy expenditure may be different. Direct metabolic measurements and changes observed over time can produce different results.

Why might my actual calorie needs differ from the result?

Actual calorie requirements can vary because of physical activity, body composition, health status, medications, metabolic adaptation, and other individual factors. Changes in body weight or activity can also change energy requirements.

Can children or pregnant people use this result?

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation was originally developed using healthy adults aged 19–78 years. General adult calorie estimates should not be substituted for age-specific pediatric guidance or individualized pregnancy and breastfeeding nutrition advice.
