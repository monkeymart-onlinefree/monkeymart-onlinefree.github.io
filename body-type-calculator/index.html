The Body Type Calculator estimates a descriptive female body-shape category from your bust, waist, high-hip, and hip measurements. Enter the four circumferences in a consistent measurement unit to compare your proportions and receive a measurement-based shape result.

What Is the Body Type Calculator?

A Body Type Calculator compares key body circumferences to describe how the bust, waist, high hip, and full hip relate to one another.

The classification is primarily useful for clothing fit, garment sizing, and understanding body proportions. It does not measure body fat, muscle mass, health, fitness, or medical risk. The research basis for this type of classification comes from the Female Figure Identification Technique (FFIT) used in apparel and anthropometric research.

How to Use the Body Type Calculator

  1. Measurement unit: Choose the unit used for your measurements. Keep all four measurements in the same unit.
  2. Bust: Measure around the fullest part of the chest. Keep the tape level and comfortably snug.
  3. Waist: Measure around the natural waist, generally the narrowest part of the torso.
  4. High hip: Measure around the upper hip or pelvic region, above the fullest part of the hips.
  5. Hip: Measure around the fullest part of the hips and buttocks.
  6. Calculate the result to see the estimated body-shape category.

Stand upright while measuring and avoid pulling the tape tightly enough to compress the body. Consistent tape placement is important because small changes in measurement location can affect the classification.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Method

Body type is determined by a rule-based classification, not by one continuous mathematical equation.

Let:

Symbol Meaning Unit
B Bust circumference selected measurement unit
W Waist circumference selected measurement unit
HH High-hip circumference selected measurement unit
H Full hip circumference selected measurement unit

The classification method compares several derived values:

Bust-to-hip difference

B − H

Hip-to-bust difference

H − B

Bust-to-waist difference

B − W

Hip-to-waist difference

H − W

High-hip-to-waist ratio

HH ÷ W

These comparisons describe whether the bust and hips are relatively balanced, whether one is larger than the other, how strongly the waist differs from the bust or hips, and how the high hip relates to the waist.

The seven-category reference method associated with this calculator includes Hourglass, Bottom Hourglass, Top Hourglass, Spoon, Triangle, Inverted Triangle, and Rectangle. The published approach is based on apparel-focused body-shape research rather than a medical body-composition model.

Measurement Unit Conversion

The classification rules use dimensional differences, so every circumference must be compared using consistent units.

When inch-based thresholds are applied to measurements entered in centimeters:

inches = centimeters ÷ 2.54

NIST lists 1 inch as equivalent to 2.54 centimeters.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Read the bust, waist, high-hip, and hip measurements.
  2. Normalize the measurements to a consistent unit when required.
  3. Calculate the differences between bust, waist, and hip measurements.
  4. Calculate the high-hip-to-waist ratio where the classification rule requires it.
  5. Compare those values with the applicable body-shape conditions.
  6. Return the matching descriptive shape category.

Example of a Rectangle Rule

One published Rectangle condition uses:

H − B < 3.6
B − H < 3.6
B − W < 9
H − W < 10

with the dimensional cutoffs expressed in inches.

Because body-shape rules can overlap near their boundaries, measurements close to a cutoff should be interpreted cautiously rather than as a precise biological distinction.

Worked Example

Suppose the measurements are:

Inputs

  • Bust: 36 in
  • Waist: 30 in
  • High hip: 35 in
  • Hip: 37 in

Step 1: Compare Hip and Bust

H − B = 37 − 36 = 1 in
B − H = 36 − 37 = −1 in

Both are below the 3.6-inch Rectangle limits.

Step 2: Compare Bust and Waist

B − W = 36 − 30 = 6 in

The difference is below 9 inches.

Step 3: Compare Hip and Waist

H − W = 37 − 30 = 7 in

The difference is below 10 inches.

For reference:

HH ÷ W = 35 ÷ 30 = 1.1667

Final Result

These measurements satisfy the published Rectangle conditions shown above, so the estimated body-shape result is:

Rectangle

The example demonstrates a measurement-based classification only. It does not indicate body fat percentage, weight status, fitness, or health.

Understanding Your Results

The main result is a descriptive body-shape category based on the relationship between your measurements.

Result General measurement pattern
Hourglass Bust and hips are relatively balanced with a more pronounced waist difference.
Bottom Hourglass Hips are larger than the bust while the waist remains clearly defined.
Top Hourglass Bust is larger than the hips while the waist remains clearly defined.
Spoon Hips are more prominent, with the high-hip relationship helping distinguish the pattern.
Triangle Hip circumference is larger than bust circumference without meeting an hourglass-type pattern.
Inverted Triangle Bust circumference is larger than hip circumference without meeting a top-hourglass pattern.
Rectangle Bust and hips are relatively close and the waist difference is smaller.

These labels describe proportions rather than ideal or undesirable body shapes. The underlying research was developed primarily for apparel and body-shape classification.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator assumes that bust, waist, high-hip, and hip measurements are taken consistently and entered correctly. Tape position, posture, clothing, bra fit, tape tension, and the exact anatomical point selected for the waist or high hip can all change the result.

A 2021 study evaluating FFIT found that differences in measurement definitions could cause the same body scan to receive different shape classifications, with disagreement occurring in substantial portions of the sample. This means body-shape categories should be treated as descriptive estimates rather than exact anatomical classifications.

The calculator is designed around a female apparel-oriented classification method. It does not measure shoulder width, body composition, visceral fat, muscle mass, BMI, or medical health.

Human bodies also exist on a continuum. A measurement set may sit close to the boundary between categories, so a small change in tape placement or measurement technique can change the displayed result.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is body type the same as body fat percentage?

No. This calculator compares body circumferences to estimate a shape category. It does not calculate how much of your body weight comes from fat, muscle, bone, or other tissues.

Does changing from inches to centimeters change my body type?

It should not when the same physical measurements are converted consistently. One inch equals 2.54 centimeters, so equivalent measurements represent the same proportions.

Why can my body type change when I measure again?

Even a small change in waist, high-hip, bust, or hip measurement can move a value across a classification boundary. Tape position, posture, clothing, and measurement tension can also affect the numbers. Research has found that measurement definitions can materially affect FFIT classifications.

What is the difference between the high hip and full hip?

The high hip is measured around the upper hip or pelvic region. The full hip is measured around the widest part of the hips and buttocks. They are separate measurements because some shape rules use the relationship between the high hip and waist.

Is this calculator designed for men?

The classification method used here is designed for female body-shape assessment in an apparel context. It should not be treated as a general male body-shape classification system.

Is my body-shape result a health assessment?

No. The result is a descriptive measurement-based shape category. It should not be used to diagnose obesity, health risk, hormonal conditions, body-fat level, or any other medical condition.
