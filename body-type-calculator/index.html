What Is a Body Type Calculator?

This body type calculator classifies a set of body measurements into common female body-shape categories such as hourglass, triangle, spoon, inverted triangle, or rectangle. The categories are based on proportions and should be treated as descriptive, not diagnostic.

How to Measure

Use a flexible tape and keep it level. Measure bust at the fullest point, waist at the natural waist, high hip around the upper hip area, and hip around the fullest point. Avoid pulling the tape tight enough to compress tissue.

How the Classification Works

The algorithm compares bust-to-hip differences, waist definition, and the high-hip/waist ratio. Thresholds are based on published apparel/body-shape classification work referenced by Calculator.net.

Understanding Your Result

Clothing-industry shape categories can overlap and do not capture every body. The additional waist-to-hip ratio is simply waist ÷ hip and should not be interpreted as a diagnosis on this page.

Limitations

Measurement technique, posture, bra/clothing, and where the tape is placed can change the result. Human body shapes exist on a continuum, so some measurements may not fit a single category cleanly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What measurements does the body type calculator use?

It uses bust, waist, high-hip, and hip circumferences. The classification compares differences between these measurements and one high-hip-to-waist ratio.

Is body shape a health diagnosis?

No. Body-shape labels are descriptive clothing/anthropometry categories, not medical diagnoses or measures of worth.

Does the calculator work in centimeters?

Yes. When centimeters are selected, the measurements are converted internally for threshold comparisons; ratios remain unitless.

What is waist-to-hip ratio?

Waist-to-hip ratio is waist circumference divided by hip circumference. It is shown separately from the descriptive body-shape label.

Sources and Methodology

This page uses the formulas and assumptions described above. Health-related results are estimates and should be interpreted in context, not as a diagnosis or individualized medical advice.
