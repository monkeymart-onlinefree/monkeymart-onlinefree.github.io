The Time Duration Calculator finds the exact elapsed time between two local date-times. Enter a start date and time and an end date and time to calculate the duration in days, hours, minutes, seconds, and total time units.

What Is the Time Duration Calculator?

A Time Duration Calculator measures how much time passes between two specified local date-times.

It is useful for checking the length of an event, project, trip, deadline period, work interval, countdown period, or any other span where both the starting and ending date-times are known.

The calculation is based on the difference between the end timestamp and the start timestamp.

How to Use the Time Duration Calculator

Enter the two values shown in the calculator:

  1. Start date & time — Enter the local date and clock time when the period begins.
  2. End date & time — Enter the local date and clock time when the period ends.

The calculator converts the entered local date-times into timestamps using the browser's local time-zone rules and calculates the elapsed duration between them.

Local date-time inputs themselves do not include a named time zone such as America/New_York or Asia/Karachi.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator uses timestamp subtraction:

Duration = End Timestamp − Start Timestamp

Or:

Δt = t₂ − t₁

where the result is initially represented as elapsed milliseconds.

JavaScript Date values represent time internally using timestamps, while the local time-zone interpretation is determined by the user's host environment.

Variables

Variable Meaning Unit
Δt Total elapsed duration milliseconds
t₁ Start timestamp milliseconds
t₂ End timestamp milliseconds

Time Unit Conversions

The calculator uses these basic relationships:

1 second = 1,000 milliseconds
1 minute = 60 seconds
1 hour = 3,600 seconds

From the timestamp difference:

Total Seconds = Δt ÷ 1,000
Total Minutes = Δt ÷ 60,000
Total Hours = Δt ÷ 3,600,000

The elapsed duration can also be displayed as a combined days, hours, minutes, and seconds result.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Read the start local date and time.
  2. Read the end local date and time.
  3. Resolve both values using the browser's local time-zone rules.
  4. Convert each date-time to a timestamp.
  5. Subtract the start timestamp from the end timestamp.
  6. Convert the resulting elapsed time into seconds, minutes, hours, and the displayed duration units.

Because the calculation is timestamp-based, a daylight-saving or other UTC-offset change between the two entered date-times can affect the actual number of elapsed hours. MDN notes that local offsets can vary according to daylight-saving and historical time-zone changes.

Worked Example

Suppose there is no local time-zone offset change during the example period.

Inputs

Start: September 20, 2026 at 8:15:00 AM
End: September 22, 2026 at 11:45:30 AM

Step 1

Find the elapsed duration:

2 days + 3 hours + 30 minutes + 30 seconds

Step 2

Convert the duration to seconds:

2 days:

2 × 24 × 3,600 = 172,800 seconds

3 hours:

3 × 3,600 = 10,800 seconds

30 minutes:

30 × 60 = 1,800 seconds

Remaining:

30 seconds

Step 3

Add the values:

172,800 + 10,800 + 1,800 + 30 = 185,430 seconds

Equivalent totals include:

Total minutes = 185,430 ÷ 60 = 3,090.5 minutes
Total hours = 185,430 ÷ 3,600 ≈ 51.5083 hours

Final Result

Elapsed duration: 2 days, 3 hours, 30 minutes, 30 seconds

Total seconds: 185,430

Total minutes: 3,090.5

Total hours: approximately 51.5083

Understanding Your Results

The main result shows how much actual time separates the entered start and end date-times.

Days, hours, minutes, and seconds provide an easy-to-read breakdown of the duration.

Total hours expresses the complete duration as hours rather than splitting it into multiple units.

Total minutes expresses the same duration entirely in minutes.

Total seconds expresses the duration entirely in seconds.

These values represent elapsed time. They are different from simply counting calendar dates because timestamp subtraction measures the time between two resolved instants.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator treats the entered values as local date-times and relies on the time-zone rules available to your browser or operating system. It does not let you attach a separate named time zone to each input.

This matters when the interval crosses a daylight-saving transition or another local UTC-offset change. Two local clock readings that appear a certain number of hours apart may represent a different amount of actual elapsed time when the offset changes.

Local date-time controls also do not contain time-zone information themselves. For calculations between different cities or time zones, a dedicated Time Zone Calculator that records the source and destination zones is more appropriate.

Times around daylight-saving transitions can be unusual. A local time may occur twice during a fall-back transition or may not exist during a spring-forward transition. Browser date handling can therefore affect how such values are resolved.

Current Rules / Data

Time-zone rules can change when governments alter daylight-saving schedules or UTC offsets. The IANA Time Zone Database is a widely used source for current and historical time-zone information.

As of September 11, 2026, the latest IANA Time Zone Database release is 2026d. Browser and operating-system installations may update their time-zone data on different schedules, so results involving newly changed time-zone rules can vary until the user's device receives updated data.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is time duration calculated?

The calculator subtracts the start timestamp from the end timestamp:

Duration = End Timestamp − Start Timestamp

The resulting elapsed time is then expressed using units such as hours, minutes, and seconds.

Does the calculator include both dates and times?

Yes. Both the date and clock time are used. This allows the calculator to measure durations that extend across multiple days rather than comparing only two clock times.

Does the calculator use my local time zone?

It uses the local time-zone rules available through your browser or operating system when resolving the entered date-times. A named time zone is not entered separately.

Can daylight saving time change the result?

Yes. If the interval crosses an offset transition, the elapsed timestamp difference can differ from what simple clock subtraction might suggest. Local UTC offsets can vary depending on the date.

Can I calculate the duration between two different time zones?

This calculator does not attach separate named zones to the start and end inputs. For an interval involving different locations, use a Time Zone Calculator where both time zones can be specified explicitly.

Why might a daylight-saving transition produce an unexpected duration?

Some local times can be skipped when clocks move forward or repeated when clocks move backward. Browser date-time handling resolves these cases according to its date and time-zone behavior.

Is a duration day always the same as a calendar day?

An elapsed-time calculation is based on timestamps, while a calendar day is based on local calendar boundaries. Around time-zone offset changes, the elapsed number of hours between the same local clock time on consecutive dates may not always be exactly 24 hours.
