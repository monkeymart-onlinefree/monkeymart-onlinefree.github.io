The Wind Chill Calculator estimates how cold outdoor air feels when wind increases heat loss from exposed skin. Enter the air temperature and wind speed to calculate the National Weather Service (NWS) wind chill in Fahrenheit, with its Celsius equivalent.

What Is the Wind Chill Calculator?

A wind chill calculator combines air temperature and wind speed to estimate the apparent cold experienced by exposed skin. It is useful for checking cold-weather conditions before outdoor work, travel, exercise, or other activities.

The calculator uses the official NWS wind chill equation. The NWS defines wind chill temperature only when the air temperature is 50°F (10°C) or lower and wind speed is above 3 mph.

How to Use the Wind Chill Calculator

Enter the air temperature used in the calculation. The implemented NWS equation uses degrees Fahrenheit (°F).

Enter the wind speed. The equation uses miles per hour (mph).

The calculator then applies the NWS formula and displays the calculated wind chill temperature. The Fahrenheit result can also be expressed as its Celsius equivalent.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator uses the National Weather Service wind chill formula:

WCT = 35.74 + 0.6215T − 35.75V^0.16 + 0.4275T × V^0.16

The same equation is published by the National Weather Service.

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
WCT Wind chill temperature °F
T Air temperature °F
V Wind speed mph

Calculation Sequence

The wind speed is raised to the power of 0.16. That value is used in both wind-speed terms of the equation. The calculator then combines those terms with the entered air temperature to produce the wind chill temperature in degrees Fahrenheit.

Celsius Result

When the calculated Fahrenheit wind chill is shown in Celsius, the temperature conversion is:

WCT°C = (WCT°F − 32) ÷ 1.8

This Fahrenheit-to-Celsius conversion is exact.

Worked Example

Suppose the air temperature is 20°F and the wind speed is 15 mph.

Inputs

Air temperature: 20°F
Wind speed: 15 mph

Step 1

Calculate the wind-speed exponent:

15^0.16 ≈ 1.5423204

Step 2

Substitute the values into the wind chill formula:

WCT = 35.74 + (0.6215 × 20) − (35.75 × 1.5423204) + (0.4275 × 20 × 1.5423204)

Step 3

Calculate the result without rounding the intermediate values too early:

WCT ≈ 6.2189°F

Convert that result to Celsius:

(6.2189 − 32) ÷ 1.8 ≈ −14.32°C

Final Result

The calculated wind chill is approximately 6.2°F, or −14.3°C.

This agrees with the NWS wind chill relationship for the same temperature and wind conditions.

Understanding Your Results

The wind chill temperature represents how cold the conditions can feel on exposed skin because moving air increases heat loss. It is not a measurement showing that the actual air temperature has fallen to the wind chill value.

The Fahrenheit and Celsius results represent the same calculated temperature expressed on two different temperature scales.

Wind chill should be treated as a weather-exposure estimate rather than an individualized medical or safety limit. Actual exposure risk depends on conditions and personal protection that are not represented by this equation.

Assumptions and Limitations

The NWS wind chill model uses standardized assumptions about exposed skin and heat transfer. It assumes no warming effect from direct solar radiation. The NWS notes that bright sunshine can make the apparent wind chill temperature roughly 10°F to 18°F higher than the no-sun calculation.

The official wind chill equation is defined only for temperatures of 50°F or below and wind speeds above 3 mph. Results produced outside that range should not be interpreted as official NWS wind chill values.

The calculation does not diagnose frostbite, hypothermia, or other medical conditions and does not provide an individualized safe-exposure time.

Current Rules / Data

As of September 2026, the National Weather Service continues to publish the equation:

WCT = 35.74 + 0.6215T − 35.75V^0.16 + 0.4275T × V^0.16

The applicable NWS range remains an air temperature of 50°F or lower with wind speed above 3 mph. These validity limits should be rechecked if official NWS wind chill guidance changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a wind chill calculator calculate?

It estimates how cold conditions feel on exposed skin by combining air temperature with wind speed using the NWS wind chill equation.

When is the NWS wind chill formula valid?

The NWS defines wind chill for air temperatures at or below 50°F and wind speeds above 3 mph.

Is wind chill the same as the actual air temperature?

No. The thermometer still measures the actual air temperature. Wind chill describes the increased heat loss from exposed skin caused by cold air and wind.

Why does wind speed affect wind chill?

Moving air removes heat from exposed skin more rapidly. The NWS equation accounts for this effect through the wind-speed terms raised to the power of 0.16.

Can I use the formula when the wind is 3 mph or slower?

The official NWS wind chill temperature is not defined for wind speeds of 3 mph or less, so calculations outside the stated range should not be treated as official wind chill values.

How is the Celsius wind chill result calculated?

After calculating the wind chill in Fahrenheit, its Celsius equivalent can be found with (°F − 32) ÷ 1.8.

Is the wind chill result an exact measure of how every person will feel?

No. It is a standardized weather index based on defined assumptions. Sun exposure and other real-world conditions can make actual experience differ from the calculated value.
