What Is a Credit Card Calculator?

A credit card calculator estimates how long it may take to pay off one balance with a fixed monthly payment or calculates the monthly payment needed to clear the balance within a selected number of months. It also estimates total interest and total dollars paid.

Credit Card Payment Formula

Payment = Balance × monthly rate ÷ [1 − (1 + monthly rate)−months]

The monthly model uses APR divided by 12 and assumes the rate stays constant, no new charges are added, and payments are made every month.

Why Paying More Matters

When a balance is carried, part of each payment covers interest. A larger payment reduces principal faster, which leaves less balance for future interest. CFPB guidance also notes that paying more than the minimum generally reduces interest costs and payoff time.

Understanding the Schedule

The first 12 months are shown individually, then annual checkpoints are included for longer payoff periods. The last row shows the final payment and remaining balance near zero.

Limitations

Actual credit cards may use daily periodic rates, average daily balances, variable APRs, promotional rates, fees, grace periods, new purchases, or different minimum-payment rules. Use your card agreement and statement for exact account terms.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why can a low payment take so long to pay off a card?

Interest is charged on the remaining balance. When a payment is only slightly above the monthly interest, little principal is reduced, so payoff can take many years and total interest can become large.

What happens if my payment is less than the monthly interest?

Under a constant-APR model, the balance will not amortize to zero if the payment does not cover the interest added each month. The calculator warns when this occurs.

Does the calculator include new purchases or fees?

No. It assumes no new purchases, cash advances, late fees, annual fees, promotional-rate changes, or penalty APR. Those can materially change payoff results.
