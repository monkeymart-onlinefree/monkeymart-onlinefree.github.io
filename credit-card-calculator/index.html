The Credit Card Calculator estimates how long it may take to pay off a credit card balance or how much you need to pay each month to clear the balance within a selected time. Enter your balance, APR, and either a monthly payment or target payoff period to estimate the result.

What Is the Credit Card Calculator?

The Credit Card Calculator is a single-card repayment tool. It can estimate the monthly payment needed to repay a balance within a chosen number of years and months, or estimate the payoff time when you enter a fixed monthly payment.

It is useful for comparing different repayment plans and understanding how the APR and monthly payment affect repayment time and total interest.

How to Use the Credit Card Calculator

Enter the following information:

  1. Credit Card Balance: Enter the current amount owed on the card.
  2. APR: Enter the card's annual percentage rate as a percentage.
  3. Monthly Payment: Enter the amount you plan to pay each month when calculating payoff time.
  4. Years: Enter the number of full years in your target payoff period when calculating the required payment.
  5. Extra Months: Add any months beyond the full years in your target repayment period.

The calculator then estimates either the required monthly payment or payoff duration using its fixed-rate monthly repayment model.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

When calculating the monthly payment needed to repay the balance within a specific period, the calculator uses:

M = B × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)^−n]

This is the standard present-value-of-an-annuity relationship rearranged to solve for the periodic payment.

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
M Required monthly payment Currency per month
B Starting credit card balance Currency
r Monthly interest rate Decimal per month
n Number of monthly payments Months
APR Annual percentage rate entered by the user Percent per year

Monthly Rate

The calculator converts APR to a monthly decimal rate:

r = (APR ÷ 100) ÷ 12

For example, an APR of 18% gives:

r = 0.18 ÷ 12 = 0.015

or 1.5% per month.

Number of Months

The target payoff period is converted to months:

n = (Years × 12) + Extra Months

For example:

3 years and 6 months = 42 months

Payoff Time from a Fixed Monthly Payment

When balance, APR, and monthly payment are known, the same payment equation can be rearranged to estimate the number of repayment periods:

n = −ln(1 − B × r ÷ M) ÷ ln(1 + r)

This calculation requires the monthly payment to be large enough to reduce the balance after interest is applied.

Monthly Payment Schedule

Under the calculator's monthly model, each payment can be separated into interest and principal:

Monthly Interest = Opening Balance × r
Principal Repaid = Monthly Payment − Monthly Interest
New Balance = Opening Balance − Principal Repaid

The process continues until the estimated balance reaches zero.

Total Interest

For a fixed-term calculation with equal monthly payments:

Total Payments = M × n
Total Interest = Total Payments − Starting Balance

If the final payment is smaller than the normal monthly payment, total interest should be based on the actual payments made rather than simply multiplying the rounded displayed payment by the number of months.

Worked Example

Suppose you have a $5,000 credit card balance with an 18% APR and want to pay it off in 3 years.

Inputs

  • Credit card balance: $5,000
  • APR: 18%
  • Years: 3
  • Extra months: 0

Step 1: Calculate the Monthly Rate

r = 0.18 ÷ 12
r = 0.015

Step 2: Calculate the Number of Payments

n = 3 × 12
n = 36 months

Step 3: Calculate the Required Monthly Payment

M = 5,000 × 0.015 ÷ [1 − (1.015)^−36]

Using sufficient precision:

M ≈ $180.76198

Step 4: Estimate Total Payments and Interest

Using the unrounded payment value:

Total Payments ≈ $180.76198 × 36
Total Payments ≈ $6,507.43
Total Interest ≈ $6,507.43 − $5,000
Total Interest ≈ $1,507.43

Final Result

The estimated payment is approximately $180.76 per month to repay a $5,000 balance over 36 months at an 18% APR under the calculator's constant-rate monthly model.

Estimated total interest is approximately $1,507.43.

Understanding Your Results

Required Monthly Payment

This is the estimated amount that must be paid each month to clear the entered balance within the selected years and extra months, assuming the APR remains unchanged and no new charges are added.

Estimated Payoff Time

When you enter a monthly payment instead of a target period, the calculator estimates how long the current balance will take to repay under the same monthly interest model.

Total Interest

Total interest represents the estimated borrowing cost above the original balance during the repayment period.

A higher monthly payment generally reduces the remaining balance faster, which reduces the amount of interest generated by the calculator's constant-rate model.

Payment Schedule

The payment schedule shows how repayment progresses over time. Early payments generally contain more interest because the outstanding balance is higher. As the balance falls, the interest calculated from the remaining balance also falls.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator uses a simplified monthly repayment model. It assumes the APR remains constant, the annual rate is divided by 12, payments are made monthly, and no new purchases are added.

Real credit card accounts can calculate interest differently. Many issuers use daily or average daily balance methods, and a daily periodic rate may be calculated from the APR using 360 or 365 days depending on the issuer.

A real card may also have different APRs for purchases, cash advances, balance transfers, or promotional balances. Fees and grace periods can also affect the amount actually charged.

The calculator therefore provides an estimate rather than a replacement for your card agreement or billing statement.

If the entered monthly payment is not greater than the interest generated by the remaining balance under the calculator's monthly model, the balance will not decline normally and a finite payoff estimate may not be possible.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Credit Card Calculator calculate monthly payments?

It converts the APR to a monthly rate, converts the selected payoff period to months, and applies the fixed-payment formula:

M = B × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)^−n]
Can I calculate how long it will take to pay off my credit card?

Yes. Enter the balance, APR, and planned monthly payment. The calculator uses the same monthly repayment model to estimate the number of payments required.

Why can the calculator result differ from my credit card statement?

The calculator uses APR divided by 12 and assumes a constant monthly rate. Actual issuers may calculate interest daily, use average daily balances, apply different APRs to different transaction types, or charge fees.

Does the calculator include new credit card purchases?

No. Its payoff estimate assumes that no additional charges are added to the balance during the repayment period.

Does it include credit card fees?

The calculation described here does not add separate card fees. Fees charged by an issuer can increase the real amount owed and change the payoff result.

What happens if my monthly payment is too low?

If the payment does not exceed the monthly interest generated by the balance under this model, the principal will not decrease normally. Increasing the payment may be necessary to produce a finite payoff period.

What do Years and Extra Months mean?

They define your target payoff period. The calculator converts them to a total number of months using:

Total Months = (Years × 12) + Extra Months
