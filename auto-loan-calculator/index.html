What Is the Auto Loan Calculator?

Answer: An Auto Loan Calculator helps estimate the cost of financing a vehicle before you accept a loan. You can account for a down payment, trade-in, existing trade balance, cash incentives, estimated sales tax, fees, loan length, and interest rate.

It is useful when comparing vehicle prices or financing scenarios and understanding how different inputs may affect the estimated monthly payment. CFPB guidance also notes that vehicle price, taxes, fees, down payment, trade-in value, interest rate, and loan term can all affect auto-financing costs.

How to Use the Auto Loan Calculator

Enter the following information in the calculator:

  1. Vehicle price ($): Enter the purchase price of the vehicle.
  2. Down payment ($): Enter the cash amount you plan to pay upfront.
  3. Trade-in value ($): Enter the estimated value credited for your current vehicle.
  4. Amount owed on trade ($): Enter any outstanding balance that still needs to be paid on the traded vehicle.
  5. Cash incentives ($): Enter applicable rebates or cash incentives.
  6. Sales tax (%): Enter the estimated sales tax rate that applies to the purchase.
  7. Title / other fees ($): Enter estimated title, registration, or other applicable vehicle fees.
  8. Loan term (months): Enter the number of monthly payments in the loan.
  9. Interest rate (%): Enter the annual interest rate used for the loan estimate.
  10. Finance estimated sales tax and fees in the loan: Choose whether the estimated tax and fees are included in the financed balance or treated as upfront estimated costs.

Then calculate the results to see the estimated monthly payment and other financing figures.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

After estimating the amount financed, the calculator uses the standard amortized-loan payment formula:

Payment = P × [r(1 + r)^n] ÷ [(1 + r)^n − 1]

Variables

  • Payment = estimated monthly loan payment, in dollars
  • P = amount financed, in dollars
  • r = monthly interest rate expressed as a decimal
  • n = total number of monthly loan payments

For monthly payments, the annual percentage entered as the interest rate is converted to a decimal and then to its corresponding monthly rate.

Calculation Sequence

  1. The vehicle price and entered financing adjustments are used to establish the financing base.
  2. The calculator accounts for the entered down payment, trade-in information, cash incentives, estimated sales tax, and fees.
  3. The tax-and-fee financing selection determines whether applicable estimated costs are rolled into the loan or treated as upfront costs.
  4. The resulting amount financed becomes P in the loan-payment formula.
  5. The interest rate is converted to the monthly periodic rate r.
  6. The loan term in months becomes n.
  7. The amortized-loan formula calculates the estimated monthly payment.

This page describes only the inputs and calculations supported by the calculator specification and does not add unsupported features or formulas.

Worked Example

Consider a simple example where tax, fees, trade-in amounts, incentives, and other adjustments are set to zero. This isolates the implemented loan-payment formula.

Inputs

  • Vehicle price: $30,000
  • Down payment: $0
  • Trade-in value: $0
  • Amount owed on trade: $0
  • Cash incentives: $0
  • Sales tax: 0%
  • Title / other fees: $0
  • Loan term: 60 months
  • Interest rate: 6% per year

With all other adjustments at zero, the amount financed is $30,000.

Step 1: Convert the Interest Rate

Annual interest rate:

6% = 0.06

Monthly rate:

r = 0.06 ÷ 12 = 0.005

Number of payments:

n = 60

Step 2: Substitute the Values

Payment = 30,000 × [0.005(1.005)^60] ÷ [(1.005)^60 − 1]

Step 3: Calculate the Payment

Payment ≈ $579.984

Understanding Your Results

Estimated monthly payment is the projected payment for each month under the entered loan assumptions. It is useful for comparing financing scenarios, but it is not a lender quote.

Base amount after trade/down/incentive shows how the entered purchase-price adjustments affect the financing estimate before the final loan calculation.

Estimated sales tax shows the calculator's estimated tax amount based on the information you entered.

Amount financed is the estimated loan principal used to calculate the payment.

Upfront estimated costs indicate costs that remain outside the financed balance under the selected option.

Total loan interest shows the estimated interest cost over the modeled loan term. A longer loan can reduce the monthly payment while increasing the amount of interest paid over time.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator provides an estimate rather than a lender, dealer, or tax-authority quote.

Vehicle sales-tax rules can differ by state, province, country, trade-in treatment, and incentive type. Because the calculator uses a simplified taxable-base assumption, the estimated tax may differ from the amount charged in an actual transaction.

The calculation also depends on the interest rate you enter. CFPB distinguishes an interest rate from an APR: APR can include certain additional loan fees, so entering an APR as though it were only an interest rate may not reproduce a lender's payment calculation.

Real financing agreements may also contain lender fees, dealer products, add-ons, different tax treatment, or other charges that are not represented by the entered values. Small differences may also result from lender-specific rounding.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a larger down payment reduce an auto loan payment?

Generally, a larger down payment reduces the amount that needs to be financed, which can reduce the resulting loan payment when the other assumptions stay the same. CFPB notes that a down payment can lower the amount you need to finance.

What happens if I still owe money on my trade-in?

Enter the remaining balance in the Amount owed on trade field. An outstanding loan balance affects the trade-in position used in the financing estimate. CFPB notes that when a trade-in still has an unpaid balance, part of the transaction must account for paying off that existing loan.

Should I enter the interest rate or APR?

Use the calculator's Interest rate (%) input as labeled. Interest rate and APR are not always the same: APR can include certain additional loan fees as well as interest.

Does a longer auto loan term lower the monthly payment?

A longer term can reduce the monthly payment because repayment is spread across more months. However, CFPB advises looking at total loan cost as well because a longer term can result in more total interest.

Are the estimated sales taxes exact?

No. Vehicle-tax rules vary by jurisdiction and can depend on factors such as trade-in treatment and incentives. Use the applicable rate for your situation and compare the estimate with a dealer or relevant tax authority when making a purchase.

Why might my lender's payment be different?

A lender may use different financed amounts, fees, tax treatment, loan charges, or rounding. Your lender's actual contract terms should therefore be used for the final payment and borrowing-cost figures.
