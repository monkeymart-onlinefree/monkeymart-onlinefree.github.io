The Inflation Calculator shows how the purchasing power of a U.S. dollar amount changes between two price levels. You can compare values using CPI-U data or project an amount forward or backward using a constant annual inflation rate.

What Is the Inflation Calculator?

The Inflation Calculator helps estimate how much money from one period is equivalent to money in another period after accounting for changes in prices.

It supports two calculation methods:

  • CPI-based calculation: compares a starting CPI with a target CPI.
  • Flat-rate calculation: applies a constant annual inflation rate over a selected number of years.

CPI-U measures the average change over time in prices paid by urban consumers for a market basket of goods and services. BLS describes CPI-U as covering more than 90% of the U.S. population.

Formula / Calculation Method

CPI Formula

For CPI-based calculations:

Adjusted Value = Original Value × (Target CPI ÷ Starting CPI)

Variables

  • Adjusted Value = dollar amount expressed at the target price level
  • Original Value = starting dollar amount
  • Starting CPI = CPI index for the starting period
  • Target CPI = CPI index for the target period
  • Multiplier = Target CPI ÷ Starting CPI

The Bureau of Labor Statistics uses the same index-ratio approach when converting dollar amounts between periods. For example, an equivalent dollar value can be found by multiplying the original amount by the ratio of the target-period CPI to the starting-period CPI.

CPI Calculation Sequence

  1. Identify the starting CPI.
  2. Identify the target CPI.
  3. Divide the target CPI by the starting CPI.
  4. Use the resulting value as the multiplier.
  5. Multiply the original dollar amount by that multiplier.

If the target CPI is higher than the starting CPI, a larger dollar amount is generally required to represent the same purchasing power.

Constant Annual Inflation Formula

For a flat annual inflation rate:

FV = PV × (1 + r)ⁿ

Where:

  • FV = projected future value
  • PV = present or starting value
  • r = annual inflation rate expressed as a decimal
  • n = number of years

For example, a 3% inflation rate is converted to:

r = 3 ÷ 100 = 0.03

The inflation multiplier is:

(1 + r)ⁿ

For a backward calculation, the same relationship can be rearranged:

PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)ⁿ

Price-Level Change

Price-level change indicates how the target price level compares with the starting price level. A target CPI above the starting CPI indicates a higher overall price level, while a lower target CPI indicates a lower price level.

Multiplier

For CPI calculations:

Multiplier = Target CPI ÷ Starting CPI

A multiplier above 1 increases the equivalent dollar amount. A multiplier below 1 decreases it.

For flat-rate forward projections, the multiplier is:

Multiplier = (1 + r)ⁿ

Worked Example

Suppose you want to find the August 2026 purchasing-power equivalent of $1,000 in July 2026.

BLS reported the U.S. city average All Items CPI-U at 333.918 for July 2026 and 334.980 for August 2026. These are unadjusted index levels with a 1982–84=100 base.

Inputs

  • Original amount: $1,000
  • Starting CPI: 333.918
  • Target CPI: 334.980

Step 1: Calculate the Multiplier

Multiplier = 334.980 ÷ 333.918
Multiplier ≈ 1.00318042

Step 2: Adjust the Dollar Amount

Adjusted Value = $1,000 × 1.00318042
Adjusted Value ≈ $1,003.18042

Step 3: Round the Final Value

Rounded to the nearest cent:

$1,003.18

Approximately 1,003.18inAugust2026representsthesamegeneralpurchasingpoweras1,000 in July 2026, based on these CPI-U index values.

The index ratio represents an increase of approximately 0.318%, which rounds to the 0.3% unadjusted monthly change reported by BLS.

Understanding Your Results

Adjusted or Projected Value

This is the dollar amount produced after applying either the CPI ratio or the constant annual inflation formula.

It estimates how much money at the target price level corresponds to the starting amount.

Price-Level Change

This result helps show whether prices increased or decreased between the selected price levels.

It should be interpreted as a broad inflation comparison rather than the exact change in the price of every individual product or service.

Multiplier

The multiplier shows the factor applied to the original amount.

For example:

  • 1.10 means the equivalent amount is 1.10 times the starting value.
  • 1.00 means there is no change.
  • 0.95 means the equivalent amount is 95% of the starting value.

Assumptions and Limitations

CPI represents an average.
CPI-U measures average price changes for urban consumers. Your personal inflation experience can differ because households buy different combinations of housing, food, transportation, healthcare, and other goods and services.

The CPI series must be consistent.
Starting and target CPI values should come from a comparable series with the same geography, item definition, and index basis.

Flat-rate inflation is a scenario, not a forecast.
A constant annual rate assumes inflation remains unchanged throughout the selected period. Actual inflation can vary significantly from year to year.

Rounding can create small differences.
Results may differ slightly when CPI values or intermediate results are rounded before the final calculation.

Some periods may have unavailable data.
A specific CPI value may not always be available for every requested period.

The calculation does not model personal finances.
It does not include taxes, investment returns, wage growth, interest earnings, or changes in individual spending patterns.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Inflation Calculator calculate purchasing power?

It multiplies the original dollar amount by the ratio of the target CPI to the starting CPI:

Adjusted Value = Original Value × (Target CPI ÷ Starting CPI)

This converts the amount from one general price level to another.

What does the inflation multiplier mean?

The multiplier shows how much the starting amount must be scaled to match the target price level. In CPI mode, it equals the target CPI divided by the starting CPI.

Can I calculate inflation without using CPI data?

Yes. The flat-rate method lets you enter an annual inflation rate and number of years. It uses compound growth:

FV = PV × (1 + r)ⁿ
How do I calculate backward with an inflation rate?

The compound inflation relationship can be reversed:

PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)ⁿ

This estimates an earlier equivalent value from a later amount using the selected constant rate.

Why might my personal cost increase differ from CPI?

CPI represents an average market basket for urban consumers. Individual households have different spending patterns, so their actual changes in living expenses may be higher or lower than the published CPI.

Is a flat annual inflation rate a prediction?

No. It is a scenario based on the rate you enter. It does not predict future inflation.

How often should CPI calculations be updated?

CPI-based results should use updated index values when new official CPI data become available. BLS publishes CPI data monthly for the U.S. city average.
