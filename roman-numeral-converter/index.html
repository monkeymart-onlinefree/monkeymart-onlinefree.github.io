How Roman Numerals Work

Roman numerals use seven core symbols: I, V, X, L, C, D, and M. In standard modern notation, values are generally written from larger to smaller and added, with six accepted subtractive pairs: IV, IX, XL, XC, CD, and CM.

Conversion Method

To convert an Arabic number, the calculator subtracts the largest valid Roman value repeatedly, including subtractive forms such as 900 (CM) and 40 (XL). To validate Roman input, it converts the text to a number and then checks that converting the number back produces the same canonical notation.

Example

2026 = 2000 + 20 + 6 = MM + XX + VI = MMXXVI

Why This Converter Stops at 3,999

Modern everyday Roman notation normally uses up to three M symbols, making 3,999 = MMMCMXCIX the practical upper bound. Historical systems used devices such as an overline (vinculum) to indicate multiplication by 1,000, but those notations are less standardized in plain text.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the seven basic Roman numeral symbols?

The seven symbols are I = 1, V = 5, X = 10, L = 50, C = 100, D = 500, and M = 1000.

Why is 49 written XLIX instead of IL?

Standard modern notation builds each place value separately. Forty is XL and nine is IX, so 49 becomes XLIX.

Can Roman numerals represent zero?

Standard Roman numeral notation has no ordinary symbol for zero.
