What Is a Debt-to-Income Ratio?

Debt-to-income ratio (DTI) compares recurring monthly debt payments with gross monthly income, meaning income before taxes and other deductions. Lenders can use DTI as one measure of whether a borrower may be able to manage additional monthly debt.

How to Use the Debt Ratio Calculator

Enter your gross monthly income and the monthly payments for housing and other recurring debts. Use required minimum or scheduled payments rather than the full outstanding balance. The calculator adds the listed obligations and divides them by gross monthly income.

Debt-to-Income Ratio Formula

DTI (%) = Total monthly debt payments ÷ Gross monthly income × 100

Example

If monthly debt payments total $2,000 and gross monthly income is $6,000, DTI is 33.33%. The calculator also shows the housing payment as a separate share of gross income so you can see what portion comes from housing.

Understanding the Result

A lower percentage means less of gross income is committed to the debts you entered. However, there is no single DTI cutoff that applies to every lender, product, borrower, or underwriting method. Lenders may also calculate qualifying income or debt payments differently.

Limitations

This tool does not evaluate credit score, loan-to-value, assets, employment history, taxes, living expenses, or lender-specific underwriting rules. It also depends on the completeness of the debts and income you enter.

Frequently Asked Questions

What income should I use for DTI?

Use gross income before taxes and payroll deductions when following the standard DTI definition. A lender may use its own rules for qualifying or documenting income.

Do I enter credit card balances or minimum payments?

DTI generally uses recurring monthly debt payments, not the entire outstanding balance. Enter the required monthly payment that applies to the debt.

Does a lower DTI guarantee loan approval?

No. DTI is only one underwriting factor. Credit history, collateral, income documentation, loan program rules, and other factors can also affect a lending decision.
