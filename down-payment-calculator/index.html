What Is the Down Payment Calculator?

A Down Payment Calculator helps you estimate the upfront amount you plan to pay toward a home purchase and how much of the purchase price would remain to be financed.

It also provides an LTV estimate and cash-planning information related to estimated closing costs and available cash. This can be useful when comparing different down payment percentages or checking how a larger or smaller down payment changes the amount you may need to borrow.

A larger down payment generally produces a lower LTV because less of the property's price needs to be financed.

How to Use the Down Payment Calculator

Enter the following information in the calculator:

Home Price

Enter the purchase price of the home. This is the base amount used to calculate the down payment and loan amount.

Down Payment (%)

Enter the percentage of the home price you plan to pay upfront.

For example, entering 20% on a $400,000 home represents an $80,000 down payment.

Estimated Closing Costs (%)

Enter the percentage you want to use when estimating closing-related expenses.

Actual closing costs can vary based on the property's price, lender charges, loan type, down payment, and location, so this field is intended for planning rather than determining an exact closing amount.

Cash Available

Enter the amount of cash you currently have available for the purchase. The calculator uses this input for its cash-planning results, including total cash needed and cash remaining.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Enter the home price.
  2. Convert the entered down payment percentage to a decimal.
  3. Multiply the home price by the down payment rate to calculate the down payment.
  4. Subtract the down payment from the home price to calculate the loan amount.
  5. Divide the loan amount by the home price and multiply by 100 to calculate LTV.
  6. Use the closing-cost and available-cash inputs to display the calculator's additional cash-planning results.

Down Payment

The down payment is the portion of the home's price paid upfront.

Down Payment=Home Price×Down Payment PercentageDown\ Payment = Home\ Price \times Down\ Payment\ Percentage

For a 20% down payment, the percentage is converted to 0.20 before multiplication.

Loan Amount

The estimated loan amount is the portion of the home price left after subtracting the calculated down payment.

Loan Amount=Home Price−Down PaymentLoan\ Amount = Home\ Price - Down\ Payment

This is a purchase-price calculation and does not represent a lender's final approved loan amount.

Loan-to-Value Ratio

LTV compares the calculated loan amount with the entered home price.

LTV=Loan AmountHome Price×100LTV = \frac{Loan\ Amount}{Home\ Price} \times 100

For example, financing $320,000 of a $400,000 home gives an LTV of 80%.

Lenders may calculate LTV using the property's appraised value rather than simply the purchase price, so a lender's final LTV can differ from this calculator's estimate.

Understanding Your Results

Down Payment Amount

This is the calculated amount of the home price you plan to pay upfront.

Increasing the down payment percentage increases the upfront payment while reducing the calculated loan amount.

Loan Amount

This is the home price minus the calculated down payment.

It is an estimate of how much of the purchase price would remain to be financed. It does not include lender approval decisions or other financing adjustments.

LTV

LTV shows the relationship between the estimated loan amount and the home price.

For example:

  • $300,000 loan on a $400,000 home = 75% LTV
  • $360,000 loan on a $400,000 home = 90% LTV

A higher down payment results in a lower LTV when the home price remains unchanged. Lenders may use LTV when evaluating mortgage options, interest rates, and mortgage-insurance requirements.

Estimated Closing Costs

This result is intended to help plan for expenses associated with completing the home purchase.

Closing costs are separate from the down payment and actual charges can vary significantly between transactions. CFPB notes that closing costs depend on factors such as home price, lender costs, loan type, down payment, and location.

Total Cash Needed

This result helps show the estimated amount of cash required for the purchase based on the information entered into the calculator.

Use it as a planning figure rather than a lender's final cash-to-close amount.

Cash Remaining

Cash remaining helps you compare your entered available cash with the calculator's estimated upfront cash requirement.

Keeping additional funds available may also be useful for expenses that are outside the calculator, such as moving costs, repairs, furnishings, or emergency savings. CFPB recommends considering these other financial needs when deciding how much cash to commit to a home purchase.

Assumptions and Limitations

The Down Payment Calculator provides estimates based only on the values you enter.

The core down payment and loan calculations assume that the entered home price is the purchase-price basis used for the calculation.

The calculator's LTV uses the entered home price as the denominator. A mortgage lender may instead use an appraised property value or another value required by its underwriting process, which can produce a different LTV.

Estimated closing costs are planning figures. Actual costs can differ because lender fees, third-party charges, loan programs, insurance, taxes, location, and other transaction details may affect the final amount.

The calculator does not determine:

  • Mortgage approval
  • Final interest rates
  • Loan-program eligibility
  • Private mortgage insurance requirements
  • Government-backed loan eligibility
  • Final lender fees
  • Final cash-to-close amounts

Results should therefore be used for comparison and planning rather than as a loan offer, approval, or final closing statement.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate a down payment on a house?

Multiply the home price by the down payment percentage expressed as a decimal.

For example:

350,000×0.10=35,000\\$350,000 \times 0.10 = \\$35,000

A 10% down payment on a $350,000 home is $35,000.

How is the loan amount calculated?

The calculator subtracts the down payment from the home price:

Loan Amount=Home Price−Down PaymentLoan\ Amount = Home\ Price - Down\ Payment

A $400,000 home with an $80,000 down payment leaves an estimated $320,000 to finance.

How is LTV calculated?

Divide the loan amount by the home price and multiply the result by 100:

LTV=Loan AmountHome Price×100LTV = \frac{Loan\ Amount}{Home\ Price} \times 100

A $320,000 loan on a $400,000 home produces an 80% LTV.

Does a larger down payment reduce LTV?

Yes. If the home price stays the same, increasing the down payment reduces the amount that must be financed, which lowers the calculated LTV. CFPB also notes that lenders may consider LTV when determining loan terms and costs.

Are closing costs the same as the down payment?

No. The down payment is the portion of the home's price you pay upfront, while closing costs are expenses associated with completing the mortgage and home purchase.

Actual closing costs depend on several transaction-specific factors.

Why can my lender's LTV differ from this calculator?

This calculator calculates LTV from the entered home price and calculated loan amount. CFPB describes mortgage LTV as comparing the amount financed with the property's appraised value, so an appraisal or lender-specific calculation method can produce a different result.

Does this calculator tell me whether I will qualify for a mortgage?

No. Mortgage approval can depend on factors not included in this calculator, such as income, debts, credit history, property appraisal, loan program requirements, and lender underwriting.
