What Is a Down Payment Calculator?

A down payment calculator converts a percentage of the purchase price into a dollar amount and shows how much of the home would be financed. It also estimates closing costs separately so you can plan total upfront cash rather than focusing on the down payment alone.

Down Payment and LTV Formula

Down Payment = Home Price × Down Payment %
Loan Amount = Home Price − Down Payment
LTV = Loan Amount ÷ Home Price × 100

CFPB explains that a higher down payment generally means a lower LTV and can affect the loan options, rate, and costs offered by lenders.

Do You Need 20% Down?

No. CFPB notes that many mortgages allow less than 20% down, and some options can require much less. However, putting less than 20% down on a conventional mortgage often means mortgage insurance may apply, while FHA, VA, or USDA programs follow different rules.

Closing Costs Are Separate

The closing-cost percentage in this calculator is only a planning assumption. Actual closing costs depend on the lender, property, location, taxes, insurance, points, title services, and other transaction details.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does a larger down payment affect LTV?

A larger down payment reduces the amount financed and therefore lowers the loan-to-value ratio. CFPB notes that LTV can affect approval, pricing, and mortgage-insurance requirements.

Is 20% down always required?

No. CFPB says down-payment requirements vary by loan and lender. Many programs allow less than 20%, though mortgage insurance or a government-backed loan may be involved.

Does the down payment include closing costs?

No. Down payment and closing costs are separate. This calculator estimates both so you can plan total upfront cash.
