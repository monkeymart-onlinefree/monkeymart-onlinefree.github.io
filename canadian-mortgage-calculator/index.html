What Is the Canadian Mortgage Calculator?

The Canadian Mortgage Calculator estimates the payment required to amortize a fixed-rate mortgage over a selected number of years.

It converts the entered nominal annual mortgage rate from semi-annual compounding to an equivalent monthly rate before applying the mortgage payment formula. The calculator is useful for comparing home prices, down payments, mortgage rates, and amortization periods before speaking with a lender.

How to Use the Canadian Mortgage Calculator

Enter the following values:

Home price (C$): Enter the purchase price of the property in Canadian dollars.

Down payment (C$): Enter the amount you plan to pay upfront. The difference between the home price and down payment is the mortgage principal used in the payment calculation.

Amortization (years): Enter the number of years over which the mortgage is being modeled.

Nominal annual rate (%): Enter the quoted annual mortgage interest rate as a percentage.

Property tax (C$/year): Enter the estimated annual property tax.

Home insurance (C$/year): Enter the estimated annual cost of home insurance.

Condo/HOA (C$/month): Enter any monthly condominium, strata, or homeowners' association fees.

Other costs (C$/year): Enter other recurring annual housing costs that you want to include as supporting cost information.

The mortgage principal-and-interest calculation itself is based on the mortgage principal, interest rate, and amortization period.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator first determines the mortgage principal:

P=H−DP = H - D

It then converts the nominal annual mortgage rate to an equivalent monthly rate:

i=(1+j2)1/6−1i = \left(1+\frac{j}{2}\right)^{1/6}-1

The monthly mortgage payment is calculated using:

M=P×i(1+i)n(1+i)n−1M=P\times\frac{i(1+i)^n}{(1+i)^n-1}

Calculation Sequence

  1. Subtract the down payment from the home price to determine the mortgage principal.
  2. Convert the entered annual percentage rate to a decimal.
  3. Divide the nominal rate by 2 for semi-annual compounding.
  4. Convert the semi-annual rate to its equivalent monthly rate.
  5. Multiply the amortization years by 12 to determine the number of monthly payments.
  6. Apply the mortgage payment formula.
  7. Use the calculated payment to estimate total interest and supporting amortization information.

The semi-annual conversion matters because dividing a Canadian nominal mortgage rate directly by 12 does not produce the same monthly rate as the method implemented by this calculator.

Total Interest

If the entered rate and calculated monthly payment are assumed to remain unchanged for the full modeled amortization:

Total Payments=M×n\text{Total Payments}=M\times n Total Interest=(M×n)−P\text{Total Interest}=(M\times n)-P

This is a mathematical projection. Actual Canadian mortgages commonly have shorter contractual terms than their full amortization periods, so future renewal rates can change the real lifetime interest cost. FCAC distinguishes the mortgage term from the amortization period and notes that borrowers may need several terms before the mortgage is fully repaid.

Understanding Your Results

Monthly Mortgage Payment

This is the estimated monthly principal-and-interest payment produced by the mortgage formula. It does not mean that every housing-related expense is included in the mortgage itself.

Mortgage Principal

The mortgage principal is the amount financed after subtracting the entered down payment from the home price.

Total Interest

Total interest estimates how much interest would be paid over the modeled amortization if the entered rate and payment remained unchanged.

A longer amortization generally reduces the regular payment but increases the amount of interest paid over time. FCAC also notes this relationship when explaining mortgage amortization.

Amortization Schedule

The amortization schedule shows how the mortgage balance is reduced over time as scheduled payments are made. Earlier payments generally contain a larger interest component because the outstanding principal is higher.

Property Tax, Insurance, Condo Fees, and Other Costs

These inputs represent additional ownership costs. They are separate from the principal-and-interest formula shown above and may help provide a broader view of housing expenses.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator provides an estimate rather than a lender quote or mortgage approval.

It assumes a fixed nominal annual interest rate converted using the calculator's semi-annual compounding method and regular monthly payments throughout the modeled amortization.

The calculator does not automatically calculate or enforce:

  • mortgage default insurance premiums
  • minimum down-payment eligibility rules
  • provincial sales taxes on mortgage insurance premiums
  • mortgage stress-test qualification
  • future mortgage renewal rates
  • prepayment privileges
  • prepayment penalties
  • variable-rate changes or trigger-rate mechanics

Actual mortgage contracts may also use lender-specific payment dates, rounding practices, fees, insurance charges, and renewal terms.

A Canadian mortgage term may be much shorter than its amortization period. A mortgage with a 25-year amortization, for example, may be renewed several times before it is fully repaid.

Use your lender's mortgage documents for contractual payment amounts, insurance costs, qualification requirements, fees, and penalties.

Current Canadian Mortgage Rules and Data

Current as of September 24, 2026.

Mortgage Qualification Stress Test

For most newly underwritten uninsured mortgages at federally regulated lenders, OSFI's current minimum qualifying rate is the greater of:

Mortgage contract rate+2%\text{Mortgage contract rate}+2\\%

or

5.25%5.25\\%

OSFI notes an exception for certain uninsured straight switches at renewal when neither the mortgage amount nor amortization period increases. The calculator does not perform this qualification test automatically.

Amortization Limits for Insured Mortgages

FCAC currently states that when the down payment is less than 20% of the home's price, the maximum amortization is generally:

  • 30 years for a first-time homebuyer and/or a buyer purchasing a new build
  • 25 years in other cases

When the down payment is more than 20%, the lender determines the maximum amortization period. The calculator accepts an amortization input but does not determine whether the selected period meets a borrower's mortgage-insurance or lender eligibility rules.

Down Payment and Mortgage Loan Insurance

FCAC states that buyers with a down payment below 20% will typically require mortgage loan insurance, subject to applicable eligibility requirements. The calculator does not automatically add a mortgage loan insurance premium to the mortgage principal.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does this calculator not simply divide the annual interest rate by 12?

The implemented Canadian mortgage calculation first converts the nominal annual rate using semi-annual compounding:

i=(1+j2)1/6−1i=\left(1+\frac{j}{2}\right)^{1/6}-1

This produces the monthly rate used in the mortgage payment formula. An FCAC fixed-rate mortgage disclosure example describes interest that is compounded twice per year while payments are charged monthly.

Does the calculator automatically add mortgage default insurance?

No. Mortgage default insurance is not automatically added to the mortgage amount by this calculator. If insurance applies to your mortgage, use your lender or mortgage insurer's figures when estimating your actual financing.

Does this calculator perform the Canadian mortgage stress test?

No. The calculator estimates payments using the interest rate you enter. It does not determine whether you qualify under OSFI or lender underwriting requirements.

Can I use this calculator for a variable-rate mortgage?

The implemented calculation is designed around a fixed-rate mortgage estimate using the entered nominal annual rate. It does not model variable-rate changes, trigger rates, or changing payment allocations.

Why can my lender's mortgage payment be different?

Differences may result from lender-specific rounding, payment timing, mortgage insurance, fees, contractual terms, renewal assumptions, or other costs that are not included in the calculator's core payment formula.

Does a longer amortization lower the monthly payment?

Usually, yes. Spreading repayment over more months reduces the required scheduled payment when the principal and rate are unchanged, but it generally increases total interest over the modeled repayment period.

Are property taxes and home insurance part of the mortgage payment formula?

No. The principal-and-interest formula uses the financed principal, mortgage rate, and amortization period. Property taxes, insurance, condo/HOA fees, and other costs are entered separately as housing-cost information.
