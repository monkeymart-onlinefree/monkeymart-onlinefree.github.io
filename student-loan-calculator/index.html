Use this Student Loan Calculator to estimate your scheduled monthly payment, interest that may build up before repayment, repayment starting balance, total repayment interest, and total amount repaid. Enter your loan balance, annual interest rate, time before repayment, repayment term, and any extra monthly payment.

What Is the Student Loan Calculator?

The Student Loan Calculator estimates the cost of repaying a student loan using a fixed-rate repayment calculation. It also estimates interest that accrues before repayment begins and shows how that interest can increase the balance used for repayment.

The calculator is useful for comparing different loan amounts, interest rates, repayment terms, and extra-payment amounts. It is an estimate only and does not determine eligibility for a federal repayment plan.

How to Use the Student Loan Calculator

Enter the following information:

Original / Current Principal ($)
Enter the current principal amount of the student loan.

Interest Rate (%)
Enter the annual interest rate as a percentage, such as 6.5 for 6.5%.

Months Before Repayment
Enter the number of months remaining before regular repayment begins. The calculator uses this period when estimating pre-repayment interest.

Repayment Term (Years)
Enter the number of years over which you want to estimate fixed repayment.

Extra Monthly Payment ($)
Enter any additional amount you plan to pay each month above the scheduled payment. Enter zero if you do not plan to make extra payments.

The calculator then estimates the scheduled payment and overall repayment cost.

Formula / Calculation Method

Pre-Repayment Interest

The calculator uses a simple daily interest estimate before repayment:

Pre-Repayment Interest ≈ P × i ÷ 365 × D

Where:

  • P = principal balance in dollars
  • i = annual interest rate expressed as a decimal
  • D = number of days in the pre-repayment period
  • 365 = day-count basis used by this calculator

For example, 6% is converted to 0.06 before the calculation.

Student loans commonly accrue interest daily. Actual servicers may use a slightly different day-count convention; for example, MOHELA illustrates daily student-loan interest using 365.25 days.

Repayment Starting Balance

For this calculator's repayment estimate, accrued pre-repayment interest is added to the balance used when repayment begins:

Repayment Starting Balance = Principal + Estimated Pre-Repayment Interest

Actual capitalization rules depend on the loan type and circumstances. Federal and private student loans do not always capitalize unpaid interest in the same situations.

Fixed Monthly Payment Formula

The scheduled repayment payment is calculated with the standard fixed-payment amortization formula:

M = P × [r(1 + r)ⁿ] ÷ [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1]

Where:

  • M = scheduled monthly payment
  • P = repayment starting balance
  • r = monthly interest rate
  • n = total number of monthly payments

The monthly rate is:

r = Annual Interest Rate ÷ 12

when the annual rate has already been converted to decimal form.

The total number of scheduled payments is:

n = Repayment Term in Years × 12

Total Repaid

For the fixed scheduled repayment estimate:

Total Repaid = Monthly Payment × Number of Monthly Payments

Total Interest During Repayment

Repayment Interest = Total Repaid − Repayment Starting Balance

This represents the estimated interest paid after repayment begins under the fixed-payment calculation.

Extra Monthly Payments

The extra monthly payment input lets you examine the effect of paying more than the scheduled amount. Paying extra toward a loan can reduce the balance faster and may reduce interest costs, although the way an actual servicer applies additional payments can affect the result. The CFPB recommends checking how your servicer applies extra student-loan payments.

Worked Example

Assume:

Inputs

  • Principal: $25,000
  • Annual interest rate: 6.5%
  • Months before repayment: 0
  • Repayment term: 10 years
  • Extra monthly payment: $0

Because repayment begins immediately in this example, there is no estimated pre-repayment interest.

Step 1: Convert the Annual Rate

6.5% ÷ 100 = 0.065

Monthly rate:

r = 0.065 ÷ 12
r = 0.0054166667

Step 2: Calculate the Number of Payments

n = 10 × 12
n = 120 payments

Step 3: Calculate the Monthly Payment

M = 25,000 × [0.0054166667 × (1 + 0.0054166667)¹²⁰] ÷ [(1 + 0.0054166667)¹²⁰ − 1]

Estimated scheduled payment:

$283.87 per month

Step 4: Calculate Total Repaid

Using the unrounded monthly result:

Total Repaid ≈ $34,064.39

Step 5: Calculate Repayment Interest

$34,064.39 − $25,000 = $9,064.39

Final Result

The estimated scheduled monthly payment is 283.87.Over10years,theloanwouldcostapproximately34,064.39 in scheduled repayments, including about $9,064.39 in repayment interest.

Actual lender or servicer calculations may differ because of payment dates, daily accrual, rounding, capitalization rules, fees, or repayment-plan rules.

Understanding Your Results

Estimated Scheduled Payment
The fixed monthly amount calculated from the repayment starting balance, interest rate, and repayment term.

Interest Accrued Before Repayment
The estimated interest that builds up between the current point and the start of repayment.

Repayment Starting Balance
The principal plus the pre-repayment interest included in this calculator's repayment estimate.

Total Interest During Repayment
The estimated amount paid above the repayment starting balance during the scheduled repayment period.

Total Repaid After Repayment Starts
The estimated total of the scheduled payments made during repayment.

These values are planning estimates rather than an official payment quote from a lender, servicer, or the U.S. Department of Education. Federal Student Aid similarly notes that its official repayment-calculator results are estimates and that final terms are set after the applicable repayment process is completed.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator uses a simplified fixed-rate repayment model.

The interest rate is treated as constant for the calculation. This may not match a private student loan with a variable interest rate.

Pre-repayment interest is estimated using simple daily accrual with a 365-day basis. Some student-loan servicers may use another convention, such as 365.25 days.

The repayment estimate assumes the balance used at repayment includes the calculated pre-repayment interest. Actual capitalization depends on the type of loan and the event that causes capitalization. Federal Direct Loans, older federal loans, and private loans can follow different rules.

The calculator does not determine eligibility for income-driven repayment, forgiveness, deferment, forbearance, subsidies, or other federal student-loan benefits.

It also does not replace an official payment calculation from your lender or loan servicer.

Current Federal Student Loan Rules — 2026

Updated: September 24, 2026

Federal repayment options changed effective July 1, 2026.

Federal servicing guidance states that borrowers who take out a new Direct Loan or consolidate existing Direct Loans on or after July 1, 2026 are subject to the newer Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP) or Tiered Standard Repayment Plan framework.

Federal Student Aid states that if all of a borrower's loans were disbursed on or after July 1, 2026, RAP is the only income-driven repayment plan available. Direct PLUS Loans made to parents, and certain consolidation loans involving Parent PLUS debt, are not eligible for RAP.

Borrowers with older loans can have different repayment-plan options based on loan type and disbursement date. Federal Student Aid therefore recommends checking the official Repayment Calculator for personalized plan eligibility and payment estimates.

This CalcPro calculator does not calculate RAP, Tiered Standard, IBR, PAYE, ICR, forgiveness eligibility, or other federal-plan-specific payments. It provides a general fixed-repayment estimate based on the values you enter.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the student loan monthly payment calculated?

The calculator uses a fixed-payment amortization formula based on the repayment starting balance, monthly interest rate, and total number of monthly payments.

Does interest accrue before student loan repayment starts?

It can. Interest generally accrues on many student loans before repayment, although subsidized federal loans can receive an interest subsidy during certain eligible periods.

What does repayment starting balance mean?

It is the balance used when calculating the repayment schedule. In this calculator's model, it includes the original or current principal plus estimated pre-repayment interest.

Will an extra monthly payment reduce student loan interest?

Paying additional amounts can reduce the outstanding balance faster and may lower the total interest paid. Actual results depend on how and when your servicer applies additional payments.

Does this calculator calculate income-driven repayment?

No. It does not calculate RAP, IBR, PAYE, ICR, or another income-driven plan. Federal repayment-plan payments can depend on factors such as income, family circumstances, loan type, and disbursement date.

Why can my actual student loan payment be different?

Differences can result from daily interest accrual, exact payment dates, servicer rounding, capitalization, fees, variable rates, repayment-plan rules, subsidies, or other loan-specific terms.

Can I use this calculator for private student loans?

You can use it for a general fixed-rate repayment estimate, but private-loan terms vary by lender. Private loans may also have variable interest rates and different capitalization or repayment rules.
