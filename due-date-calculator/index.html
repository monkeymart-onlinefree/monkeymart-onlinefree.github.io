The Due Date Calculator estimates your pregnancy due date using the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP), a known conception date, or an IVF embryo transfer date. It also shows estimated dates corresponding to 37, 40, and 42 weeks of pregnancy.

What Is the Due Date Calculator?

A due date calculator estimates the expected date of delivery (EDD) using standard pregnancy-dating conventions. It can be useful when you know your last menstrual period, conception date, or the date and embryo age of an IVF transfer.

The result is an estimate rather than a guaranteed delivery date. Clinical pregnancy dating may also consider early ultrasound findings and other medical information. ACOG states that first-trimester ultrasound is the most accurate ultrasound method for establishing or confirming gestational age.

How to Use the Due Date Calculator

Input What to Enter
First day of LMP Enter the first day of your last menstrual period when estimating the due date from menstrual dating.
Cycle length Enter your usual menstrual cycle length in days. The calculator adjusts the standard 28-day calculation for a shorter or longer cycle.
Conception date Enter the estimated or known date of conception when using conception-based dating.
Embryo transfer date Enter the date on which the embryo was transferred during IVF treatment.
Embryo age Use the embryo's age at transfer. The implemented calculation supports day-3 and day-5 embryo dating.

For LMP-based calculations, cycle length matters because the standard 280-day convention assumes a regular 28-day menstrual cycle. NHS guidance also notes that cycle length can be adjusted when a cycle is shorter or longer than 28 days.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator uses a different formula depending on the pregnancy-dating information available.

Last Menstrual Period method

EDD = LMP + 280 days + (Cycle Length − 28 days)

For a 28-day cycle, this simplifies to:

EDD = LMP + 280 days

Conception Date method

EDD = Conception Date + 266 days

Day-5 IVF Embryo Transfer

EDD = Embryo Transfer Date + 261 days

Day-3 IVF Embryo Transfer

EDD = Embryo Transfer Date + 263 days

The LMP and conception formulas match standard pregnancy-dating conventions. ACOG specifically gives 261 days after a day-5 embryo transfer and 263 days after a day-3 transfer for IVF dating.

Calculation Sequence

For an LMP calculation, the calculator first compares the entered cycle length with the standard 28-day cycle. The difference is added to or subtracted from the standard 280-day pregnancy duration, and that adjusted duration is added to the LMP date.

For conception-based dating, the calculator adds 266 days directly to the conception date.

For IVF dating, the transfer date and embryo age determine the formula. A day-5 embryo uses 261 days after transfer, while a day-3 embryo uses 263 days after transfer.

Estimated 37-Week Date

The 40-week EDD acts as the central pregnancy date. The estimated 37-week date is 21 days before the 40-week date:

37-week date = EDD − 21 days

40-Week Date

The 40-week date is the calculated estimated due date:

40-week date = EDD

Estimated 42-Week Date

The estimated 42-week date is 14 days after the 40-week EDD:

42-week date = EDD + 14 days

NHS pregnancy information describes pregnancy as normally lasting from 37 to 42 weeks when dated from the first day of the last menstrual period.

Worked Example

Suppose the first day of the last menstrual period was January 1, 2026, and the usual menstrual cycle length is 30 days.

Inputs

LMP: January 1, 2026
Cycle length: 30 days

Step 1: Calculate the Cycle Adjustment

Cycle adjustment = 30 − 28 = 2 days

The cycle is two days longer than the standard 28-day cycle.

Step 2: Calculate the Standard 40-Week Date

January 1, 2026 + 280 days = October 8, 2026

Step 3: Apply the Cycle Adjustment

October 8, 2026 + 2 days = October 10, 2026

Final Result

Estimated due date / 40-week date: October 10, 2026

The corresponding pregnancy dates are:

37-week date: September 19, 2026
40-week date: October 10, 2026
42-week date: October 24, 2026

Understanding Your Results

Estimated due date is the calculated calendar date corresponding to 40 weeks of gestational age under the selected dating method. It should be treated as an estimate rather than a prediction of the exact day of delivery.

Estimated 37-week date marks the calendar date corresponding to 37 weeks under the same dating calculation.

40-week date is the calculated EDD itself.

42-week date marks the calendar date corresponding to 42 weeks under the same dating calculation.

Your healthcare professional may use additional information when establishing an official clinical EDD. ACOG recommends using ART-derived gestational age for pregnancies resulting from assisted reproductive technology and states that early ultrasound is important for establishing or confirming gestational age.

Assumptions and Limitations

The LMP formula uses 280 days as the standard pregnancy duration and assumes a 28-day menstrual cycle as its baseline. The cycle-length adjustment shifts the estimated date according to the difference between the entered cycle length and 28 days. This method can be less reliable when menstrual cycles or ovulation timing are irregular.

The conception method assumes the entered conception date is sufficiently accurate. When conception timing is uncertain, the resulting EDD can also be uncertain.

For IVF pregnancies, the calculation depends on entering the correct embryo transfer date and embryo age. ACOG recommends using ART-derived gestational age for IVF pregnancies rather than replacing it with ordinary LMP dating.

The calculator does not replace clinical pregnancy dating. Ultrasound findings can be important when the LMP is uncertain or when menstrual and ultrasound dating differ. ACOG identifies first-trimester ultrasound as the most accurate ultrasound method for establishing or confirming gestational age.

Current Rules / Data

As of September 2026, ACOG's Methods for Estimating the Due Date, Committee Opinion No. 700, is marked Reaffirmed 2025. It continues to recommend first-trimester ultrasound for the most accurate establishment or confirmation of gestational age and ART-derived dating for pregnancies resulting from assisted reproductive technology.

NHS guidance, last reviewed August 21, 2024 and scheduled for review in August 2027, continues to describe pregnancy as normally lasting 37 to 42 weeks from the first day of the last menstrual period and allows cycle-length adjustment in its due-date calculation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a calculated due date the exact date my baby will be born?

No. The result is an estimated due date used as a pregnancy-dating reference. The actual delivery date can occur before or after the calculated EDD.

How does cycle length affect my due date?

The LMP method uses 28 days as its baseline. The calculator adds the difference between your entered cycle length and 28 days to the standard 280-day calculation. For example, a 30-day cycle shifts the estimate two days later.

Why does the conception method add 266 days?

Standard obstetric dating counts about 280 days from the first day of the LMP, while conception-based pregnancy duration is about 266 days. The difference reflects the conventional approximately two-week interval between the start of a 28-day cycle and conception.

Which IVF due date formula does the calculator use?

A day-5 embryo transfer uses the transfer date plus 261 days. A day-3 embryo transfer uses the transfer date plus 263 days. These calculations are consistent with ACOG's ART dating guidance.

Can an ultrasound give a different due date?

Yes. Clinical dating can differ from an LMP estimate. ACOG states that first-trimester ultrasound is the most accurate ultrasound method for establishing or confirming gestational age, and clinicians may use it when evaluating pregnancy dating.

What if my periods are irregular?

The LMP and cycle-length method may be less reliable because it assumes that cycle length can reasonably represent ovulation timing. When the LMP is uncertain or cycles are irregular, clinical assessment and early ultrasound may provide better dating information.

Should I use LMP dating for an IVF pregnancy?

The IVF transfer method is more appropriate when the pregnancy resulted from IVF and the embryo transfer information is known. ACOG recommends that ART-derived gestational age be used to assign the EDD in pregnancies resulting from assisted reproductive technology.
