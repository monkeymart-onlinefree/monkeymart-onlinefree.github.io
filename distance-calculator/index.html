Use the Distance Calculator to find the distance between two points in a 2D plane, 3D space, or between two latitude-longitude coordinates. Enter the coordinates for both points, and the calculator returns the distance using the calculation method for that type of coordinate system.

What Is the Distance Calculator?

The Distance Calculator measures how far apart two points are.

For 2D and 3D coordinates, it calculates straight-line Euclidean distance. For geographic coordinates, it estimates the great-circle distance between two latitude-longitude points using a spherical Earth model.

Euclidean distance is the straight-line distance between points in Euclidean space.

How to Use the Distance Calculator

2D Distance

Enter:

  • x₁: x-coordinate of the first point.
  • y₁: y-coordinate of the first point.
  • x₂: x-coordinate of the second point.
  • y₂: y-coordinate of the second point.

Use the same measurement scale for both points. The result is expressed in the same coordinate unit.

3D Distance

Enter:

  • x₁: x-coordinate of the first point.
  • y₁: y-coordinate of the first point.
  • z₁: z-coordinate of the first point.
  • x₂: x-coordinate of the second point.
  • y₂: y-coordinate of the second point.
  • z₂: z-coordinate of the second point.

The z-coordinate adds the third spatial dimension.

Latitude and Longitude Distance

Enter:

  • Latitude 1: latitude of the first location.
  • Longitude 1: longitude of the first location.
  • Latitude 2: latitude of the second location.
  • Longitude 2: longitude of the second location.
  • Geographic output unit: choose the unit in which you want the geographic distance displayed.

For valid geographic coordinates, latitude normally ranges from −90° to 90° and longitude from −180° to 180°.

Formula / Calculation Method

2D Distance Formula

For two points:

P₁ = (x₁, y₁)
P₂ = (x₂, y₂)

the distance is:

d = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]

This is the standard Euclidean distance formula for two-dimensional coordinates.

3D Distance Formula

For:

P₁ = (x₁, y₁, z₁)
P₂ = (x₂, y₂, z₂)

the calculator uses:

d = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)² + (z₂ − z₁)²]

This extends Euclidean distance by adding the squared difference along the z-axis. The general Euclidean formula can be extended to any number of dimensions.

Geographic Great-Circle Calculation

For latitude and longitude, the calculator uses a spherical great-circle model. The calculation can be expressed using the Haversine method:

a = sin²(Δφ / 2) + cos(φ₁) × cos(φ₂) × sin²(Δλ / 2)
c = 2 × atan2(√a, √(1 − a))
d = R × c

The Haversine method calculates distance between points on a sphere from their latitude and longitude coordinates.

Calculation Sequence

For a 2D calculation:

  1. Subtract x₁ from x₂.
  2. Subtract y₁ from y₂.
  3. Square both differences.
  4. Add the squared values.
  5. Take the square root.

For a 3D calculation, the same process is used with an additional squared z-coordinate difference.

For geographic coordinates, the latitude and longitude values are used to determine the central angle between the two locations on a spherical Earth model. That angle is then converted into surface distance.

Worked Example

Suppose the two 2D points are:

Inputs

Point 1: (2, 3)
Point 2: (8, 11)

Step 1: Substitute the Coordinates

d = √[(8 − 2)² + (11 − 3)²]

Step 2: Calculate the Differences

d = √[6² + 8²]

Step 3: Square and Add

d = √[36 + 64]
d = √100

Final Result

d = 10

The straight-line distance between the two points is 10 coordinate units.

Understanding Your Results

2D Distance

The result represents the straight-line distance between two points on a flat coordinate plane.

If the coordinates are measured in meters, the result is in meters. If they are measured in feet, the result is in feet.

3D Distance

The result represents straight-line separation in three-dimensional space. Changes along the x, y, and z axes all contribute to the final distance.

Geographic Distance

The geographic result represents an estimated shortest surface distance between two latitude-longitude positions under a spherical Earth model.

It is not a road, walking, driving, or terrain-following distance.

Assumptions and Limitations

For 2D and 3D calculations, all coordinates should use a consistent measurement scale. Mixing units such as meters and feet within the same calculation produces a result without a meaningful physical unit.

The 2D and 3D formulas calculate straight-line Euclidean distance. They do not account for obstacles, routes, terrain, or curved paths.

The geographic calculation treats Earth as a sphere. Real Earth geometry is better represented by an ellipsoid, so spherical great-circle calculations are estimates rather than exact ellipsoidal geodesic measurements. The geographic calculation also ignores elevation.

Rounding in the displayed result may also cause a small difference compared with calculations that retain more decimal places.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the formula for distance between two points?

For two points in 2D, the formula is:

d = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]

It calculates the straight-line Euclidean distance between the points.

How is 3D distance different from 2D distance?

A 3D calculation includes a z-coordinate:

d = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)² + (z₂ − z₁)²]

The additional term measures separation along the third axis.

Can distance ever be negative?

No. Squared coordinate differences are non-negative, and the calculator takes the non-negative square root. Distance is therefore zero or greater.

What happens if both points are identical?

The difference between every corresponding coordinate is zero, so the calculated distance is 0.

Can I use negative coordinates?

Yes. Negative x, y, and z values can be used in Euclidean distance calculations. Geographic latitude and longitude may also use signed values as long as they represent valid coordinates.

Is latitude-longitude distance the same as driving distance?

No. The geographic calculation estimates great-circle surface distance. Driving distance depends on roads, bridges, route restrictions, and other real-world factors.

Why might geographic distance differ from another mapping tool?

Results may differ because this calculator uses a spherical Earth model and ignores elevation. Other systems may use an ellipsoidal Earth model, routing data, terrain, or different numerical methods.
