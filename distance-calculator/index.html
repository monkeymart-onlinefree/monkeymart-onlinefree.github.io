What Is a Distance Calculator?

A distance calculator finds straight-line separation between points. This page supports Euclidean distance in two and three dimensions and great-circle distance between latitude-longitude coordinates on a spherical Earth model.

2D and 3D Distance Formulas

For 2D points, d = √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²]. In 3D, add the z term: d = √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)² + (z₂−z₁)²].

Latitude and Longitude Distance

The geographic mode uses the haversine formula to estimate the shortest surface arc on a sphere. It uses a mean Earth radius of 6,371.0088 km and can display kilometers, statute miles, or nautical miles.

Calculation Example

For the 2D points (1,2) and (7,10), Δx = 6 and Δy = 8. The distance is √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10.

Great-Circle vs. Driving Distance

Great-circle distance is an air-distance style measurement across the modeled Earth surface. It is not road distance and does not account for street networks, terrain, ferries, traffic, or routing constraints.

Limitations

The geographic mode models Earth as a sphere, so it is suitable for general distance estimates but is not an ellipsoidal geodesic solution. It also ignores elevation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the distance formula in 2D?

The Euclidean distance between (x₁,y₁) and (x₂,y₂) is √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²].

How is 3D distance different?

It adds the squared difference in the z-coordinate before taking the square root.

Is latitude-longitude distance the same as driving distance?

No. This calculator returns a great-circle surface distance on a spherical model, not a route along roads.

Why does the geographic result differ from some mapping services?

Mapping services may use ellipsoidal geodesics or road-routing networks. This page uses a spherical haversine model with a mean Earth radius.
