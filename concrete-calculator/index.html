What Is a Concrete Calculator?

A concrete calculator estimates the volume needed for a pour based on geometry. This version handles rectangular slabs or walls, solid round columns, and hollow circular tubes or rings, then converts the result to cubic feet, cubic yards, and cubic meters.

Concrete Volume Formulas

Rectangular volume is length × width × thickness. A solid cylinder is π × radius² × height. A hollow tube subtracts the inner cylinder from the outer cylinder.

Bag Estimates

QUIKRETE publishes approximate yields of 0.45 cubic feet for a 60 lb bag and 0.60 cubic feet for an 80 lb bag of its standard Concrete Mix. Bag yields vary by product, so verify the bag or manufacturer data sheet before purchase.

Why Add a Waste Allowance?

Real projects can require extra material because of uneven excavation, spillage, form variation, or measurement error. The default 10% is a planning allowance, not an engineering requirement.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many cubic feet are in one cubic yard?

There are 27 cubic feet in one cubic yard.

How many 80 lb bags make a cubic yard?

At an approximate yield of 0.60 cubic feet per standard 80 lb bag, a theoretical cubic yard is 45 bags before any waste allowance.

Should I always add 10% extra concrete?

Not necessarily. Ten percent is a common planning allowance, but the right margin depends on the project, form accuracy, ground conditions, and supplier practices.
