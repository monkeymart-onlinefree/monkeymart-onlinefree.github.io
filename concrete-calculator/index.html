Use this Concrete Calculator to estimate the amount of concrete needed for rectangular slabs, solid round columns, and hollow circular tubes. Enter the project dimensions, quantity, and waste allowance to estimate concrete volume and common 60 lb and 80 lb bag quantities.

What Is the Concrete Calculator?

The Concrete Calculator estimates concrete requirements from the dimensions of a regular geometric shape. It supports rectangular sections such as slabs, solid cylindrical sections such as round columns, and hollow cylindrical sections such as concrete tubes.

It can help when planning slabs, footings, posts, columns, and other projects where you need a volume estimate before ordering ready-mix concrete or bagged concrete.

How to Use the Concrete Calculator

Choose the appropriate shape and enter the measurements requested for that calculation.

Formula / Calculation Method

The calculator first finds geometric volume, accounts for the number of identical sections, applies the entered waste allowance where applicable, and then converts the result into useful concrete-volume and bag estimates.

Rectangular Slab Formula

For a rectangular slab:

V = L × W × (T ÷ 12)

If more than one identical slab or section is being calculated:

Vtotal = V × Q

Solid Round Column Formula

A round column is treated as a cylinder:

V = π × r² × H

Because the calculator takes diameter rather than radius:

r = D ÷ 2

Therefore:

V = π × (D ÷ 2)² × H

For multiple identical columns:

Vtotal = V × Q

The standard cylinder-volume relationship is V=πr2hV=\pi r^2h. (OpenStax)

Hollow Tube Formula

A hollow concrete tube is calculated by subtracting the volume of the inner cylinder from the outer cylinder:

V = π × (Ro² − Ri²) × H

Using diameters:

V = π × [(Do ÷ 2)² − (Di ÷ 2)²] × H

For multiple identical tubes:

Vtotal = V × Q

The inner diameter must be smaller than the outer diameter for the geometry to represent a valid hollow tube.

Unit Conversions

For slab calculations, thickness is entered in inches and converted to feet:

Thickness in feet = Thickness in inches ÷ 12

Concrete volume can then be converted between units.

Cubic yards = Cubic feet ÷ 27
Cubic meters = Cubic feet × 0.02831685

NIST lists 1 cubic foot as approximately 0.02831685 cubic meter and 1 cubic yard as approximately 0.7645549 cubic meter. (NIST)

60 lb Bag Estimate

Published product data from both QUIKRETE and Sakrete lists an approximate yield of 0.45 ft³ per 60 lb bag.

Using that nominal yield:

60 lb bag equivalent = Required concrete volume ÷ 0.45

80 lb Bag Estimate

The same manufacturer data lists an approximate yield of 0.60 ft³ per 80 lb bag.

Using that nominal yield:

80 lb bag equivalent = Required concrete volume ÷ 0.60

Bag yield is an estimate rather than an exact physical constant. The product being purchased should be checked because mix type and manufacturer specifications can differ.

Calculation Sequence

The calculator converts any required dimensions into compatible units, calculates the geometric volume for one section, accounts for quantity and the entered waste allowance, converts the resulting volume into the displayed units, and estimates 60 lb and 80 lb bag requirements from nominal bag yield.

Worked Example

Suppose you are pouring one rectangular slab that is:

Input Example Value
Length 10 ft
Width 6 ft
Thickness 4 in
Quantity 1
Waste allowance 0%

Step 1: Convert Thickness to Feet

4 ÷ 12 = 0.333333 ft

Step 2: Calculate Cubic Feet

V = 10 × 6 × 0.333333
V = 20 ft³

Step 3: Convert the Volume

Cubic yards:

20 ÷ 27 = 0.74074 yd³

Cubic meters:

20 × 0.02831685 = 0.566337 m³

Step 4: Estimate 60 lb Bags

20 ÷ 0.45 = 44.4444

Estimated requirement: 44.44 bag equivalents

Step 5: Estimate 80 lb Bags

20 ÷ 0.60 = 33.3333

Estimated requirement: 33.33 bag equivalents

Final Result

The slab requires approximately 20 ft³, 0.741 yd³, or 0.566 m³ of concrete before any additional waste allowance.

That is approximately 44.44 60 lb bag equivalents or 33.33 80 lb bag equivalents using the cited nominal yields. When purchasing whole bags, the required quantity should normally be rounded up rather than down.

Understanding Your Results

Cubic Feet

Cubic feet is the base volume measurement used for the entered dimensions. It is especially useful for smaller concrete projects and bag-yield calculations.

Cubic Yards

Ready-mix concrete is commonly estimated in cubic yards. There are exactly 27 cubic feet in one cubic yard.

Cubic Meters

Cubic meters provide the metric equivalent of the calculated concrete volume.

60 lb Bags

This result estimates how many 60 lb bags correspond to the required concrete volume when using a nominal yield of approximately 0.45 ft³ per bag.

80 lb Bags

This result estimates how many 80 lb bags correspond to the required volume when using a nominal yield of approximately 0.60 ft³ per bag.

Waste Allowance

The waste allowance lets you include additional material in the estimate for circumstances such as minor dimensional variation, spillage, uneven excavation, or other project losses. Enter the percentage appropriate for your project rather than assuming that one percentage is correct for every job.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator assumes that the entered slab, column, or tube can be represented by a regular geometric shape. Irregular excavation, uneven subgrade, form movement, voids, spillage, over-excavation, and field measurement errors can cause actual concrete use to differ from the estimate.

All dimensions must be entered in the units shown by the calculator. For hollow tubes, the outer diameter must exceed the inner diameter.

Bag estimates depend on nominal product yield. QUIKRETE describes its 60 lb and 80 lb yields as approximately 0.45 ft³ and 0.60 ft³, respectively, and Sakrete publishes the same nominal values for its High-Strength Concrete Mix. Actual yield can vary with the specific product and mixing conditions, so check the bag or technical data sheet before buying.

This calculator estimates material quantity. It does not determine concrete strength, reinforcement requirements, footing dimensions, structural capacity, mix design, curing requirements, or compliance with local building codes.

Current Rules / Data

Bag Yield Data

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate concrete for a slab?

Multiply the slab length by its width and thickness. When length and width are in feet but thickness is in inches, divide thickness by 12 first:

Volume = Length × Width × (Thickness ÷ 12)
How do I calculate concrete for a round column?

Treat the column as a cylinder:

Volume = π × (Diameter ÷ 2)² × Height

Then multiply by the number of identical columns if quantity is greater than one.

How does the calculator find the volume of a concrete tube?

It calculates the outer cylinder and subtracts the empty inner cylinder:

Volume = π × [(Outer Diameter ÷ 2)² − (Inner Diameter ÷ 2)²] × Length
How many cubic feet are in one cubic yard?

One cubic yard contains 27 cubic feet because one yard equals three feet in each dimension.

How many cubic feet does a 60 lb bag of concrete make?

The cited QUIKRETE and Sakrete products publish an approximate yield of 0.45 ft³ per 60 lb bag. Check your specific bag because product yields can differ.

How many cubic feet does an 80 lb bag of concrete make?

The cited manufacturer specifications give approximately 0.60 ft³ per 80 lb bag.

Should I round the concrete bag estimate up?

Yes. Bags are normally purchased as whole units, so a decimal requirement should generally be rounded up after you have included the waste allowance you intend to use. Avoid rounding intermediate volume calculations too early.
