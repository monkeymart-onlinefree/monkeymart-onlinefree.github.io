What Is the Pregnancy Weight Gain Calculator?

This calculator helps you compare your current pregnancy weight gain with a total recommended range based on your BMI before pregnancy.

It can be useful for checking your pre-pregnancy BMI, seeing how much weight you have gained so far, and viewing the lower and upper total gain targets associated with your BMI category and pregnancy type.

The results are population-level guidance, not an individualized pregnancy weight prescription.

How to Use the Pregnancy Weight Gain Calculator

Enter the following information in the calculator:

  1. Height (cm): Enter your height in centimeters. The calculator converts centimeters to meters before calculating BMI.
  2. Pre-pregnancy weight (kg): Enter your weight before pregnancy. This value is used to calculate pre-pregnancy BMI.
  3. Current weight (kg): Enter your current body weight. The calculator subtracts your pre-pregnancy weight from this value to determine your current gain.
  4. Pregnancy: Select whether you are carrying one baby or twins. The selected pregnancy type changes the recommended total weight-gain range.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

Pre-pregnancy BMI is calculated as:

BMI = W ÷ H²

Where:

  • BMI = pre-pregnancy body mass index in kg/m²
  • W = pre-pregnancy weight in kilograms
  • H = height in meters

Because height is entered in centimeters:

Height in meters = Height in cm ÷ 100

CDC defines the main adult BMI categories as underweight below 18.5, healthy weight from 18.5 to below 25, overweight from 25 to below 30, and obesity at 30 or higher.

Current Weight Gain

Current pregnancy weight gain is calculated as:

Current Gain = Current Weight − Pre-pregnancy Weight

The result is expressed in kilograms when the entered weights are in kilograms.

Recommended Total Pregnancy Gain

After calculating pre-pregnancy BMI, the calculator identifies the corresponding BMI category. It then selects the applicable total pregnancy weight-gain range for either a singleton or twin pregnancy.

CDC bases pregnancy weight-gain recommendations on BMI before pregnancy.

Lower and Upper Total Targets

The lower total target is the lower end of the recommended total gain range.

The upper total target is the upper end of the recommended total gain range.

These are total pregnancy gain targets rather than weekly weight-gain prescriptions.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Divide height in centimeters by 100 to convert it to meters.
  2. Square the height in meters.
  3. Divide pre-pregnancy weight by squared height to calculate BMI.
  4. Assign the BMI to the appropriate pre-pregnancy BMI category.
  5. Use the selected pregnancy type and BMI category to identify the recommended total gain range.
  6. Subtract pre-pregnancy weight from current weight to calculate current gain.
  7. Display the BMI, current gain, recommended range, lower target, and upper target.

Worked Example

Suppose a person enters:

Inputs

  • Height: 165 cm
  • Pre-pregnancy weight: 60 kg
  • Current weight: 68 kg
  • Pregnancy: Singleton

Step 1: Convert Height

165 ÷ 100 = 1.65 m

Step 2: Calculate Pre-pregnancy BMI

BMI = 60 ÷ 1.65²
BMI = 60 ÷ 2.7225
BMI ≈ 22.04 kg/m²

A BMI of approximately 22.0 is within the healthy-weight BMI category.

Step 3: Calculate Current Gain

Current Gain = 68 − 60
Current Gain = 8 kg

Step 4: Select the Total Gain Range

For a singleton pregnancy with a pre-pregnancy BMI in the healthy-weight range, CDC lists a total recommended gain of 25–35 lb, which is approximately 11.3–15.9 kg.

Final Result

  • Pre-pregnancy BMI: approximately 22.0 kg/m²
  • Current gain: 8 kg
  • Recommended total gain: approximately 11.3–15.9 kg
  • Lower total target: approximately 11.3 kg
  • Upper total target: approximately 15.9 kg

Understanding Your Results

Pre-pregnancy BMI

This is your BMI based on your height and weight before pregnancy. The calculator uses this value to select the applicable pregnancy weight-gain category.

BMI is a screening measure and does not provide a complete assessment of an individual's health.

Current Gain

Current gain shows the difference between your current weight and your pre-pregnancy weight.

A positive result means your current weight is higher than your pre-pregnancy weight. A negative result means it is lower.

Recommended Total Pregnancy Gain

This result shows the total weight-gain range associated with your pre-pregnancy BMI category and whether you selected a singleton or twin pregnancy.

It is a total pregnancy range, not a personalized recommendation for how much weight should be gained during a particular week.

Lower Total Target

This is the lower end of the selected recommended total pregnancy weight-gain range.

Upper Total Target

This is the upper end of the selected recommended total pregnancy weight-gain range.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator uses pre-pregnancy BMI, not your current pregnancy BMI, to choose a weight-gain range.

The recommendations are population-level ranges. Individual weight-gain goals may differ because of medical history, fetal growth, nausea or vomiting, fluid retention, nutritional status, pregnancy complications, or a clinician's assessment.

The calculator does not use gestational week to create a personalized week-by-week target. It therefore should not be interpreted as telling you whether a specific amount of weight gain at a particular stage of pregnancy is appropriate.

Twin-pregnancy recommendations have additional limitations. ACOG notes that evidence for twin gestational weight-gain recommendations is less extensive than for singleton pregnancies, and recommendations for higher-order multiple pregnancies are not established in the same way.

This calculator is an informational tool and is not a diagnosis or a substitute for advice from an obstetrician, midwife, dietitian, or other qualified healthcare professional.

Current Pregnancy Weight-Gain Guidance

The following guidance was reviewed against current CDC information available in September 2026.

Singleton Pregnancy

Pre-pregnancy BMI BMI Range Recommended Total Gain
Underweight Below 18.5 28–40 lb, about 12.7–18.1 kg
Healthy weight 18.5 to <25 25–35 lb, about 11.3–15.9 kg
Overweight 25 to <30 15–25 lb, about 6.8–11.3 kg
Obesity 30 or higher* 11–20 lb, about 5.0–9.1 kg

CDC currently lists these total weight-gain ranges according to pre-pregnancy BMI.

Twin Pregnancy

Pre-pregnancy BMI Recommended Total Gain
Underweight 50–62 lb, about 22.7–28.1 kg
Healthy weight 37–54 lb, about 16.8–24.5 kg
Overweight 31–50 lb, about 14.1–22.7 kg
Obesity 25–42 lb, about 11.3–19.1 kg

CDC lists these ranges for twin pregnancy and notes that its underweight twin recommendation is an exception to the Institute of Medicine-derived recommendations.

ACOG reports the established twin ranges of 37–54 lb for healthy-weight BMI, 31–50 lb for overweight BMI, and 25–42 lb for obesity.

*CDC's pregnancy guidance displays the obesity category as BMI 30.0–39.9, while CDC's general adult BMI classification defines obesity as BMI 30 or greater.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is pregnancy weight gain calculated?

The calculator first determines your pre-pregnancy BMI using weight in kilograms ÷ height in meters². It then selects a total recommended pregnancy weight-gain range based on your BMI category and whether the pregnancy is singleton or twins.

Does the calculator use my current BMI?

No. The weight-gain recommendation is based on your pre-pregnancy BMI. Your current weight is used to calculate how much weight you have gained since your pre-pregnancy weight.

How is current pregnancy weight gain calculated?

Current gain is calculated as:

Current weight − Pre-pregnancy weight

For example, increasing from 60 kg to 68 kg gives a current gain of 8 kg.

Does the calculator give a weekly pregnancy weight-gain target?

No. This calculator provides a total pregnancy weight-gain range. It does not calculate an individualized weekly weight-gain schedule.

Are the recommendations different for twins?

Yes. Twin pregnancies generally have higher total recommended weight-gain ranges than singleton pregnancies, and the range still depends on pre-pregnancy BMI.

What if my current gain is outside the displayed range?

A result outside the total range should not be interpreted by itself as evidence that something is wrong. Pregnancy stage, fetal growth, fluid changes, medical conditions, and other individual factors can affect weight. Discuss concerns about pregnancy weight gain with your healthcare professional.

Is BMI an exact measure of health during pregnancy?

No. BMI is a screening measure based only on height and weight. It does not directly measure body composition or provide a complete assessment of individual health.
