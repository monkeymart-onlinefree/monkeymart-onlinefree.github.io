Use the House Affordability Calculator to estimate a home price that fits your income, monthly debts, selected debt-to-income limit, and housing costs. You can calculate a monthly housing budget from income and debt or enter a fixed monthly housing budget directly.

What Is the House Affordability Calculator?

The House Affordability Calculator is a planning tool for estimating how much home may fit within a monthly housing budget. It considers household income, recurring debt payments, mortgage assumptions, down payment, property tax, insurance, and HOA costs.

The income-based method uses debt-to-income ratio, or DTI. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau defines DTI as monthly debt payments divided by gross monthly income and notes that DTI limits can differ between lenders and loan products.

This calculator provides an estimate rather than a mortgage approval or lender qualification decision.

How to Use the House Affordability Calculator

Annual Household Income ($)

Enter your household's annual gross income before taxes and other deductions. The calculator converts this amount to gross monthly income when using the income-and-DTI method.

Monthly Debt Payments ($)

Enter the recurring monthly debt payments that should reduce the amount available for housing. Actual lender treatment of individual obligations can vary.

Total DTI Limit (%)

Enter the maximum total debt-to-income percentage you want to use as a planning assumption. This is not a universal approval threshold because lenders and mortgage products can apply different requirements.

Monthly Housing Budget ($)

Enter a fixed monthly housing budget when you want to base affordability directly on a monthly amount instead of deriving it from household income and DTI.

Mortgage Term (Years)

Enter the number of years over which the mortgage will be repaid. The term works with the entered interest rate when estimating the home price supported by the housing budget.

Interest Rate (%)

Enter the annual mortgage interest rate you want to model. This is a user-entered assumption rather than a built-in current market rate.

Down Payment (%)

Enter the percentage of the estimated home price you plan to pay upfront.

Property Tax (%/Year)

Enter estimated annual property tax as a percentage of the home price.

Insurance (%/Year)

Enter estimated annual homeowners insurance as a percentage of the home price.

HOA ($/Month)

Enter the monthly homeowners association fee, if applicable. Enter zero when there is no HOA fee.

Formula / Calculation Method

Debt-to-Income Ratio

Debt-to-income ratio is calculated as:

DTI = Total Monthly Debt Payments ÷ Gross Monthly Income

For the affordability calculation, this relationship is rearranged to determine how much monthly housing cost remains after the other monthly debts entered by the user.

Main Housing Budget Formula

Housing Budget = Gross Monthly Income × Selected DTI − Other Monthly Debts

Where gross monthly income is:

Gross Monthly Income = Annual Household Income ÷ 12

Variables

  • A = annual household income, in dollars per year
  • G = gross monthly income, in dollars per month
  • d = selected DTI limit expressed as a decimal
  • D = other monthly debt payments, in dollars per month
  • H = calculated monthly housing budget, in dollars per month

Therefore:

G = A ÷ 12

and:

H = G × d − D

If the user enters a fixed monthly housing budget instead, that amount is used directly as the housing-budget assumption rather than being calculated from income and DTI.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Convert annual household income to gross monthly income.
  2. Convert the selected DTI percentage to decimal form.
  3. Multiply gross monthly income by the selected DTI.
  4. Subtract the entered monthly debt payments.
  5. Use the resulting monthly housing budget, or the directly entered housing budget, with the mortgage term, interest rate, down payment, property tax, insurance, and HOA assumptions to estimate an affordable home price.

If the DTI calculation produces zero or a negative housing budget, the entered income, debt, and DTI assumptions do not leave a positive monthly amount for housing under that scenario.

Worked Example

Assume the following income-based inputs.

Inputs

  • Annual household income: $96,000
  • Monthly debt payments: $800
  • Selected total DTI: 40%

Step 1: Calculate Gross Monthly Income

$96,000 ÷ 12 = $8,000

Gross monthly income is $8,000.

Step 2: Calculate the Total Monthly Debt Allowance

Convert 40% to decimal form:

40% = 0.40

Then:

$8,000 × 0.40 = $3,200

The selected DTI allows up to $3,200 per month for total debt obligations under this planning assumption.

Step 3: Subtract Other Monthly Debts

$3,200 − $800 = $2,400

Final Result

The calculated monthly housing budget is:

$2,400 per month

The calculator can then apply the mortgage term, interest rate, down payment, property tax, insurance, and HOA assumptions entered by the user when estimating an affordable home price.

Understanding Your Results

Estimated Affordable Home Price

This is the estimated home value that fits the housing-budget and mortgage assumptions entered in the calculator. It is a planning estimate, not a lending decision or guaranteed purchase price.

Monthly Housing Budget

This is either calculated from gross income, selected DTI, and monthly debts or entered directly by the user.

A larger monthly housing budget can support a different affordability estimate, while higher ownership costs such as property taxes, insurance, or HOA fees leave less of that budget available for the mortgage portion.

DTI Assumption

The selected DTI is a planning input rather than a universal lending rule. CFPB states that different lenders and loan products may use different DTI limits, while mortgage underwriting can also consider how particular debts and housing obligations are treated.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator is designed for affordability planning and does not determine whether a lender will approve a mortgage.

The estimate does not model:

  • Closing costs
  • Mortgage insurance triggered by loan-to-value
  • Mortgage points
  • Adjustable interest rates
  • Home maintenance or repairs
  • Utilities
  • Lender reserve requirements
  • Credit-score effects
  • Local insurance pricing differences
  • Detailed lender income-verification rules

Property tax and homeowners insurance are entered as percentages of the estimated home price. This simplifies costs that can vary significantly by property, insurer, and location.

Actual mortgage qualification may also depend on verified income, credit history, assets, loan program, property characteristics, lender underwriting rules, and the exact debts included in the lender's DTI calculation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this calculator a mortgage approval tool?

No. It estimates affordability from the assumptions you enter. A lender may use additional underwriting requirements, verified financial information, credit history, assets, and loan-specific rules.

Should I enter gross or take-home household income?

Use gross household income before taxes and payroll deductions for the income-and-DTI method. DTI is generally based on gross monthly income.

What DTI percentage should I enter?

Use the DTI percentage you want to test or a limit provided for the mortgage product you are considering. There is no single DTI limit that applies to every lender and loan type.

What happens if my monthly debts increase?

Higher monthly debt payments reduce the housing budget produced by the income-and-DTI formula when income and the selected DTI remain unchanged.

Can I use the calculator without entering income?

The calculator supports a fixed monthly housing-budget method. When using that approach, the housing budget is entered directly instead of being calculated from annual income and DTI.

Does the estimate include mortgage insurance?

No. The stated calculator limitations do not include mortgage insurance that may be triggered by the loan-to-value ratio.

Does the calculator include closing costs?

No. Closing costs are not part of this affordability estimate and should be considered separately when planning the cash needed to purchase a home.
