What Is a House Affordability Calculator?

A house affordability calculator estimates a purchase price that fits a selected monthly housing limit. This page offers two methods: an income-and-debt method that derives a housing budget from a chosen total DTI limit, and a fixed-budget method where you enter the monthly amount directly.

How the Income and DTI Method Works

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau defines debt-to-income ratio as monthly debt payments divided by gross monthly income. This calculator rearranges that relationship to estimate the maximum monthly housing amount available after the other monthly debts you enter.

housing budget = gross monthly income × selected DTI − other monthly debts

The DTI percentage is a user-selected planning assumption. CFPB notes that different lenders and loan products can use different limits.

How the Home Price Is Solved

For a trial home price, the calculator estimates the loan after down payment, computes its fixed-rate principal-and-interest payment, and adds percentage-based annual property tax and insurance plus monthly HOA. It then uses a binary search to find the highest trial price whose monthly housing cost remains within the selected budget.

DTI Is Not the Only Underwriting Rule

Fannie Mae’s published guidance shows that DTI limits depend on underwriting method and borrower circumstances. For example, manually underwritten loans generally use a 36% maximum with specified exceptions up to 45%, while DU casefiles may allow up to 50%. These are eligibility rules for particular Fannie Mae processes, not universal affordability targets.

Important Limitations

The estimate does not model closing costs, mortgage insurance triggered by loan-to-value, points, adjustable rates, maintenance, utilities, lender reserves, credit-score effects, local insurance pricing, or income-verification rules. Property-tax and insurance inputs are percentages of estimated home price, which is a simplification.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is debt-to-income ratio?

DTI is total monthly debt payments divided by gross monthly income. Lenders use it as one measure of a borrower’s ability to manage monthly obligations.

Is 36% DTI always the maximum?

No. DTI limits vary by lender, product, underwriting method, and borrower profile. Fannie Mae, for example, publishes different limits for manually underwritten loans and DU casefiles.

Does this calculator guarantee I can borrow the result?

No. It is a planning estimate, not a prequalification, loan estimate, underwriting decision, or approval.

Why can a larger down payment increase the estimated home price?

A larger down payment reduces the financed share of the purchase price, which lowers the mortgage payment for a given home price and can allow a higher purchase price within the same monthly budget.
