What is an “Anorexic BMI Calculator”?

The search term usually refers to a BMI calculator used in the context of anorexia nervosa. BMI is weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. It can be one part of a clinical assessment, but eating disorders are diagnosed from a wider pattern of physical, behavioral, and psychological evidence.

BMI alone should not be used to decide whether someone deserves assessment or treatment. NICE specifically advises clinicians not to use a single measure such as BMI or duration of illness to determine whether to offer eating-disorder treatment.

BMI formula

Metric: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²). US customary: BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height² (in²).

How to understand the result

Standard adult BMI categories describe weight relative to height. They are screening categories, not diagnoses. A low BMI can occur for many reasons, and an eating disorder can also be present at a BMI that is not low. Recent weight loss, restrictive eating, fear of weight gain, body-image disturbance, fainting, weakness, heart symptoms, purging, or excessive exercise can all matter clinically.

Why this calculator does not label a result “anorexic”

Anorexia nervosa is not defined by a BMI number alone. NICE recommends considering multiple signs and symptoms and referring for age-appropriate specialist assessment when an eating disorder is suspected. A numeric result should never be used as a target weight or as proof that a person is “not sick enough.”

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a normal BMI rule out anorexia?

No. BMI cannot rule out an eating disorder. Diagnosis and treatment decisions require broader assessment.

Why does the calculator ask for age?

This tool is limited to adults. Children and adolescents require age- and sex-specific growth assessment rather than adult BMI category cutoffs.

What if weight loss is rapid?

Rapid weight loss can be clinically important even before BMI becomes very low. A health professional should assess the overall picture.

Is BMI a body-fat measurement?

No. BMI is a weight-for-height index and does not directly measure body fat, muscle mass, hydration, or nutritional status.

Sources and Methodology

The legacy search intent was cross-checked against Calculator.net’s Anorexic BMI Calculator, but the result wording here follows more cautious current clinical guidance. NICE eating-disorder guidance says not to use single measures such as BMI to determine treatment and lists multiple assessment factors. NIMH describes anorexia nervosa using restrictive eating, low body weight in context, fear of weight gain, and body-image disturbance. Adult BMI category context is consistent with CDC/NHS screening guidance.
