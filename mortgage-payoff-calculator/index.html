The Mortgage Payoff Calculator estimates how extra principal payments can change the time and interest remaining on a fixed-rate mortgage. Enter your loan details, current balance and payment, then add monthly, annual, or one-time extra payments to estimate interest saved and months saved.

What Is the Mortgage Payoff Calculator?

A Mortgage Payoff Calculator compares your current mortgage repayment path with an accelerated payoff scenario.

It is useful when you want to see how additional principal payments may reduce the outstanding balance faster, lower future interest, and shorten the remaining repayment period. The calculator focuses on mortgage principal and interest rather than the full housing payment.

How to Use the Mortgage Payoff Calculator

Input What to Enter
Original loan amount The original principal borrowed for the mortgage.
Original term (years) The original repayment period, such as 15, 20, or 30 years.
Interest rate (%) The annual mortgage interest rate.
Remaining years The full years still remaining on the mortgage.
Extra remaining months Additional months remaining beyond the full years.
Current principal balance The unpaid mortgage principal currently owed.
Current monthly P&I payment Your current monthly principal-and-interest payment. Do not include property taxes, homeowners insurance, or other escrow charges.
Extra each month Additional principal you plan to pay every month.
Extra once per year Additional principal you plan to pay annually.
One-time payment now A single extra principal payment you want included in the payoff scenario.

Use figures from your mortgage statement when possible, especially for the current principal balance and current principal-and-interest payment.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The base principal-and-interest payment uses the standard fixed-rate amortization formula:

M=P×r(1+r)n(1+r)n−1M=P\times\frac{r(1+r)^n}{(1+r)^n-1}

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
M Base monthly principal-and-interest payment Currency per month
P Original mortgage principal Currency
r Monthly interest rate Decimal per month
n Total number of monthly payments Months
B Remaining principal balance Currency
Eₜ Extra principal applied during month t Currency

The monthly rate is calculated from the annual percentage rate:

r=Annual Interest Rate/10012r=\frac{\text{Annual Interest Rate}/100}{12}

For a loan term stated in years:

n=Loan Term in Years×12n=\text{Loan Term in Years}\times12

Calculation Sequence

  1. Convert the annual interest rate from a percentage to a decimal and divide it by 12 to obtain the monthly rate.
  2. Convert the original loan term to months and calculate the base monthly principal-and-interest payment.
  3. Start the payoff projection using the current principal balance and current payment information.
  4. For each month, calculate interest on the outstanding principal, determine the principal portion of the payment, and reduce the balance.
  5. Apply any extra principal included for that payment period and continue until the balance reaches zero.
  6. Run the calculation without accelerated payments and with the selected extra-payment scenario, then compare the two results.

Monthly Interest

For each payment period:

It=Bt−1×rI_t=B\_{t-1}\times r

where ItI_t is the interest charged for the month and Bt−1B\_{t-1} is the balance before that month's payment.

Scheduled Principal

The part of the regular P&I payment that reduces the loan balance is:

Principalt=Payment−ItPrincipal_t=Payment-I_t

As the mortgage balance declines, monthly interest generally becomes smaller and more of the regular payment goes toward principal.

Balance After Extra Principal

The balance is reduced by both scheduled principal and any applicable extra principal:

Bt=max⁡(0, Bt−1−Principalt−Et)B_t=\max(0,\ B\_{t-1}-Principal_t-E_t)

The extra amount EtE_t can represent the monthly, annual, or one-time extra principal that applies to that point in the calculator's payoff schedule.

Interest Saved

The calculator compares remaining interest under the normal repayment path with remaining interest after extra principal payments:

Interest Saved=Baseline Remaining Interest−Accelerated Remaining InterestInterest\ Saved= Baseline\ Remaining\ Interest- Accelerated\ Remaining\ Interest

Months Saved

Months Saved=Baseline Payoff Months−Accelerated Payoff MonthsMonths\ Saved= Baseline\ Payoff\ Months- Accelerated\ Payoff\ Months

A positive result means the extra-payment scenario reaches a zero principal balance sooner.

The monthly rate is:

r=0.0612=0.005r=\frac{0.06}{12}=0.005

The original number of payments is:

Substituting these values:

M=300,000×0.005(1.005)360(1.005)360−1M=300,000\times \frac{0.005(1.005)^{360}} {(1.005)^{360}-1} M≈$1,798.65M\approx\\$1,798.65

Step 2: Calculate the First Month

Monthly interest on the current balance is:

251,056.95×0.005=1,255.28\\$251,056.95\times0.005=\\$1,255.28

Scheduled principal is approximately:

1,798.65-1,255.28=$543.37\\$1,798.65-\\$1,255.28=\\$543.37

Add the $200 monthly extra payment:

543.37+200=$743.37\\$543.37+\\$200=\\$743.37

The new balance after the first payment is approximately:

251,056.95-743.37=$250,313.58\\$251,056.95-\\$743.37=\\$250,313.58

The same monthly process continues, with interest recalculated from the new outstanding balance each month.

Understanding Your Results

Interest Saved

Interest saved estimates the difference between the remaining interest under the normal payment schedule and the remaining interest after the selected extra principal payments.

It is an estimate, not guaranteed savings. Actual results depend on how and when your mortgage servicer credits additional payments.

Months Saved

Months saved shows how many monthly payment periods may be removed from the remaining mortgage term by paying principal faster.

For example, 24 months saved represents approximately two years of earlier payoff.

Current Balance

Current balance is the unpaid mortgage principal used as the starting point for the payoff projection. It does not represent your home's value or the total of all future payments.

Base Monthly P&I

Base monthly P&I is the principal-and-interest payment associated with the fixed-rate amortization calculation.

Your actual payment to the mortgage servicer may be higher if it also includes property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, or other escrow amounts. The CFPB notes that these additional costs are commonly included in a borrower's total monthly mortgage payment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does paying extra on a mortgage reduce interest?

Extra principal can reduce future interest because interest on a typical amortizing mortgage is calculated from the remaining principal balance. Reducing that balance sooner means later interest is calculated on a smaller amount. CFPB explains that as principal is paid down, the amount of interest owed generally decreases over time.

Does the calculator include property tax and homeowners insurance?

No. The calculation is based on principal and interest. Taxes, insurance, and other escrow expenses may be part of the payment sent to your mortgage company but are separate from the P&I amortization calculation.

What balance should I enter?

Use your current unpaid principal balance rather than the original loan amount. Your latest mortgage statement is generally the best place to obtain the current balance and current P&I payment.

What does “months saved” mean?

Months saved is the difference between the estimated remaining payoff period without extra payments and the payoff period after the selected extra payments are applied.

Why might my lender show a different payoff result?

Differences can result from payment dates, interest calculation practices, rounding, fees, changes in the loan terms, or how extra payments are credited. A lender's official payoff statement should be used when an exact payoff amount is required.

Can I use this calculator for an adjustable-rate mortgage?

The calculator's main method uses fixed-rate amortization. If your interest rate may change during the remaining loan term, future payments and interest can differ from this estimate.

Is the interest saved guaranteed?

No. Interest saved is an estimate produced by comparing two amortization paths. Actual savings depend on the mortgage terms and the way your servicer processes additional principal payments.
