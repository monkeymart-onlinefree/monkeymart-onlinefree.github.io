What Is a Mortgage Payoff Calculator?

A mortgage payoff calculator estimates how additional principal payments can change the remaining life of a fixed-rate mortgage. It compares the existing amortization path with a faster-payment plan and reports estimated payoff time and interest savings.

How the Mortgage Calculation Works

The base principal-and-interest payment uses the standard fixed-rate amortization formula. Each month, interest is charged on the remaining principal; the rest of the payment reduces principal. Extra principal lowers the balance sooner, which can reduce future interest.

Important Assumptions

This page models principal and interest only. It does not include escrowed property tax, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, HOA fees, servicing fees, late charges, adjustable rates, or lender-specific prepayment rules.

Before Making Extra Payments

Confirm with your servicer that extra money will be applied to principal and review whether your loan has any prepayment restrictions. Also consider emergency savings and higher-priority debt before accelerating a mortgage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do extra mortgage payments reduce interest?

For a typical fixed-rate amortizing mortgage, extra principal reduces the balance earlier, so less interest can accrue later, assuming the servicer applies the extra amount to principal.

Does this include property taxes and insurance?

No. The payoff math here is for principal and interest. Escrow items do not normally reduce the mortgage principal.

What is a one-time principal payment?

It is an extra amount applied directly to principal now. The calculator reduces the starting balance by that amount before simulating future payments.
