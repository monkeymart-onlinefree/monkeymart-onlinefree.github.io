What Is a Present Value Calculator?

A present value calculator converts a future lump sum into an equivalent value today using a discount rate. It applies the time value of money: a future dollar is generally worth less than a dollar available now when a positive required return is assumed.

Present Value Formula

PV = FV ÷ (1 + r / m)m × t

PV is present value, FV is future value, r is the nominal annual discount rate, m is compounding periods per year, and t is time in years. When compounding is annual, the familiar form is PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)n.

Calculation Example

If you expect $25,000 in 10 years and use a 6% nominal annual discount rate compounded monthly, the calculator discounts all 120 monthly periods to estimate what that future amount is worth today under the selected assumption.

Choosing a Discount Rate

The math is precise for the inputs entered, but the discount rate is an assumption. Depending on the use case, it may represent an expected return, financing rate, hurdle rate, opportunity cost, or risk-adjusted required return.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is present value?

Present value is the value today of a future cash amount after discounting it at a required return or interest rate over time.

Why does a higher discount rate reduce present value?

A higher discount rate means you require more return for waiting or bearing risk, so a given future amount is worth less today.

How does compounding frequency affect present value?

When a nominal annual discount rate is compounded more frequently, the number of discounting periods increases and the periodic rate changes. For a positive nominal rate, more frequent compounding generally lowers present value slightly.
