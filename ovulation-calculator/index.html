The Ovulation Calculator estimates your ovulation date, fertile window, and next expected period from the first day of your last menstrual period and your average cycle length. It uses calendar-based cycle timing, so the results are estimates rather than confirmation that ovulation will occur on a specific date.

What Is the Ovulation Calculator?

An ovulation calculator is a calendar-based tool that estimates when ovulation may occur during a menstrual cycle. It can be useful for people tracking their cycle or planning pregnancy, especially when cycle lengths are reasonably regular.

CalcPro uses two inputs: the first day of your last period and your average cycle length. From these values, it calculates an estimated ovulation date, a fertile-window date range, and the expected start of the next period.

How to Use the Ovulation Calculator

Enter the following information:

First day of last period: Select the calendar date on which your most recent menstrual period began. This date is treated as cycle day 1.

Average cycle length: Enter your usual cycle length in days. The calculator accepts cycle lengths from 21 to 45 days.

Select Calculate to see your estimated ovulation date, fertile window, and next period.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

CalcPro first estimates the next period:

Next period date = LMP + C days

It then estimates the ovulation cycle day:

Ovulation cycle day = C − 14

Because the first day of the last menstrual period is already cycle day 1, the calendar-date calculation is:

Estimated ovulation date = LMP + (C − 15) days

The fertile window is then calculated around that estimated ovulation date:

Fertile window start = Ovulation date − 5 days
Fertile window end = Ovulation date + 1 day

This timing is consistent with ACOG guidance that ovulation commonly occurs about 14 days before the next menstrual period and that pregnancy can occur from about five days before ovulation through one day afterward.

Variables

  • LMP = first day of the last menstrual period, measured as a calendar date
  • C = average menstrual cycle length, in days
  • C − 14 = estimated ovulation cycle day
  • C − 15 = number of calendar days added to the LMP to obtain the estimated ovulation date

Calculation Sequence

  1. Use the entered LMP as cycle day 1.
  2. Add the average cycle length to the LMP to estimate the next period.
  3. Subtract 14 from the cycle length to determine the estimated ovulation cycle day.
  4. Convert that cycle day to a calendar date by adding cycle length − 15 days to the LMP.
  5. Subtract 5 days from the ovulation date to calculate the beginning of the fertile window.
  6. Add 1 day to the ovulation date to calculate the end of the displayed fertile window.

Next Period Calculation

The expected next period is:

Next period = LMP + average cycle length

For example, if the last period starts on September 1 and the average cycle length is 28 days, the next period is estimated to start 28 calendar days later.

Fertile Window Calculation

The calculator displays a fertile window beginning five days before the estimated ovulation date and ending one day afterward.

ACOG explains that sperm may survive for up to about five days, while an egg generally remains available for fertilization for roughly 12–24 hours after ovulation.

Worked Example

Suppose the following values are entered.

Inputs

  • First day of last period: September 1, 2026
  • Average cycle length: 28 days

Step 1: Estimate the Next Period

September 1, 2026 + 28 days = September 29, 2026

Estimated next period:

September 29, 2026

Step 2: Estimate the Ovulation Cycle Day

28 − 14 = 14

Estimated ovulation occurs around cycle day 14.

Because September 1 is already day 1, the calendar-date offset is:

28 − 15 = 13 days
September 1 + 13 days = September 14, 2026

Estimated ovulation date:

September 14, 2026

Step 3: Calculate the Fertile Window

Fertile-window start:

September 14 − 5 days = September 9, 2026

Fertile-window end:

September 14 + 1 day = September 15, 2026

Final Result

  • Estimated ovulation: September 14, 2026
  • Fertile window: September 9–15, 2026
  • Next period: September 29, 2026

Understanding Your Results

Estimated Ovulation Date

This is the calendar date on which ovulation is estimated to occur based on your average cycle length. It is a prediction, not a measurement of actual ovulation.

ACOG notes that ovulation typically occurs about 14 days before the next period, but individual timing can vary.

Fertile Window

The fertile window is the date range surrounding estimated ovulation when pregnancy may be possible after intercourse. CalcPro displays the five days before estimated ovulation through one day afterward.

The estimated ovulation date is still the central prediction, so a shift in actual ovulation can also shift the real fertile window.

Next Period

This is the expected start date of the next menstrual cycle based on the average cycle length entered.

It assumes that the upcoming cycle will be similar in length to the average value you provided.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator assumes that your average cycle length provides a useful basis for estimating your next cycle.

Ovulation does not always occur exactly 14 days before the next period. NHS guidance notes that for many people it may occur roughly 10 to 16 days before the next period, which shows why calendar calculations should be treated as estimates.

Stress, illness, postpartum hormonal changes, perimenopause, and naturally irregular cycles can change the timing of ovulation. Predictions may therefore be less reliable when cycle length changes significantly from month to month.

The calculator does not measure hormone levels, cervical mucus, basal body temperature, or ovarian activity. Ovulation predictor tests and clinical testing provide different information from a calendar estimate.

Do not use this calculator alone as a contraceptive method. ACOG notes that there is no foolproof calendar calculation for identifying fertile days, and fertility-awareness methods require more than a single estimated date.

The results are intended for planning and educational purposes and are not a diagnosis or individualized medical advice.

Current Clinical Guidance

Guidance reviewed: September 2026

ACOG currently describes ovulation as occurring approximately 14 days before the next menstrual period in an average cycle. It also describes a potential fertile period extending from approximately five days before ovulation through one day after ovulation.

Clinical guidance also emphasizes that cycle timing varies between people and between individual cycles. Calendar estimates should therefore be interpreted as approximate dates rather than confirmation of ovulation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does this ovulation calculator estimate ovulation?

It uses your average cycle length to estimate the ovulation cycle day:

Average cycle length − 14

The corresponding calendar date is calculated from the first day of your last period.

Why does the calculator subtract 15 days from the LMP date?

The formula estimates the cycle day as cycle length minus 14. Because the LMP is already counted as cycle day 1, the calendar-date offset is one day smaller.

For a 28-day cycle, the estimated ovulation day is cycle day 14, which is 13 calendar days after cycle day 1.

What is the fertile window?

The calculator displays the period from five days before estimated ovulation through one day afterward. ACOG describes this range because sperm can survive for several days and an egg remains viable for a much shorter period after ovulation.

Is ovulation always 14 days before the next period?

No. Fourteen days is a calendar estimate used by the calculator. Actual timing can vary, and NHS guidance describes ovulation as commonly occurring about 10–16 days before the next period.

Is the ovulation date exact?

No. The result is an estimate based only on cycle dates. The calculator cannot confirm that ovulation occurred on the predicted day.

Can I use this calculator if my periods are irregular?

You can calculate an estimate, but it may be less reliable because the method assumes the average cycle length is reasonably representative of the upcoming cycle.

Can I use the Ovulation Calculator for birth control?

No. Do not rely on a calendar estimate alone to prevent pregnancy. Actual ovulation can occur earlier or later than predicted.
