Use the Density Calculator to calculate density, mass, or volume when any two of these quantities are known. Enter the available density, mass, and volume values with their units, and the calculator converts the values internally before solving for the missing quantity.

What Is the Density Calculator?

The Density Calculator solves the relationship between mass, volume, and mass density. It can be used when you know any two quantities and need to find the third.

It is useful for science, engineering, materials, construction, laboratory work, and other situations where mass and volume need to be related through density.

How to Use the Density Calculator

Provide any two known quantities and use the calculator to determine the third.

Density (ρ): Enter the known mass density of the material when calculating mass or volume. Density represents mass divided by volume.

Mass (m): Enter the mass of the sample when calculating density or volume.

Volume (V): Enter the volume occupied by the sample when calculating density or mass.

The calculator handles the available metric and imperial unit conversions internally. Values are normalized to kilograms (kg), cubic meters (m³), and kilograms per cubic meter (kg/m³) before the calculation is performed.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

Mass density is calculated as:

ρ = m / V

The same relationship can be rearranged to calculate mass or volume:

m = ρ × V
V = m / ρ

NIST defines mass density as mass divided by volume and gives its SI unit as kilograms per cubic meter.

Variables

Symbol Meaning SI Unit
ρ Mass density kg/m³
m Mass kg
V Volume m³

Calculation Sequence

  1. The entered mass value is converted to kilograms when necessary.
  2. The entered volume is converted to cubic meters.
  3. The entered density is converted to kg/m³.
  4. The calculator identifies which quantity needs to be solved.
  5. It applies the appropriate density equation.
  6. The calculated value is presented in practical units.

This normalization allows equivalent measurements in different supported units to be calculated consistently.

Calculating Density

When mass and volume are known:

Density = Mass ÷ Volume
ρ = m / V

Volume must be greater than zero because division by zero is undefined.

Calculating Mass

When density and volume are known:

Mass = Density × Volume
m = ρV

Calculating Volume

When mass and density are known:

Volume = Mass ÷ Density
V = m / ρ

Density must be greater than zero when it is used as the divisor.

Worked Example

Suppose a sample has a mass of 2.5 kg and occupies a volume of 0.002 m³.

Inputs

  • Mass = 2.5 kg
  • Volume = 0.002 m³
  • Density = unknown

Step 1

The values are already in the calculator's internal SI units, so no conversion is required.

Step 2

Apply the density formula:

ρ = m / V
ρ = 2.5 / 0.002

Step 3

Calculate the result:

ρ = 1,250 kg/m³

Final Result

The density of the sample is 1,250 kg/m³.

Understanding Your Results

Density

Density shows how much mass is contained within a given volume. A result in kg/m³ means the number of kilograms of mass contained in one cubic meter at the conditions represented by your inputs.

Mass

When mass is calculated, the result represents the mass corresponding to the entered density and volume.

Volume

When volume is calculated, the result represents the amount of space occupied by the entered mass at the specified density.

Unit changes do not change the physical relationship between mass, volume, and density. The calculator converts compatible units before applying the formula.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator assumes a uniform bulk density for the material being calculated.

It does not model measurement uncertainty, internal porosity, thermal expansion, pressure effects, mixtures, or phase changes. These factors can change the real density of a material.

Temperature and pressure can be especially important for gases and can also affect liquids and solids when high precision is required.

The mass and volume values should describe the same sample under the same relevant conditions.

Use positive physical values. A zero volume cannot be used to calculate density, and a zero density cannot be used as the divisor when calculating volume.

For engineering or scientific work, use density data appropriate to the exact material, temperature, pressure, composition, and measurement method involved.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the formula for density?

Density is calculated by dividing mass by volume:

ρ = m / V

The SI unit of mass density is kg/m³.

Can this calculator find mass instead of density?

Yes. If density and volume are known, mass is calculated using:

m = ρV
Can it calculate volume?

Yes. If mass and density are known, volume is:

V = m / ρ
Why does density change when temperature or pressure changes?

Density depends on both mass and volume. If temperature or pressure changes the volume of a material, its density can also change even when its mass remains the same.

Does using different units change the density?

Changing units changes the numerical representation, not the physical density. The calculator converts compatible units to consistent internal units before solving the equation. NIST publishes conversion factors for mass, volume, and mass-density units.

Why might my result differ from a published material density?

Published density values may apply to a particular temperature, pressure, composition, moisture level, porosity, or measurement method. Your result uses the mass and volume values you enter.

Can volume be zero?

No. Density requires division by volume, so a volume of zero would cause division by zero and does not represent a usable physical volume for this calculation.
