What Is a Density Calculator?

A density calculator solves the relationship among mass, volume, and mass density. NIST defines mass density as the mass of a substance divided by its volume, with the SI unit kilogram per cubic meter (kg/m³).

Density Formula

ρ = m / V
m = ρV
V = m / ρ

The calculator converts all inputs to kilograms, cubic meters, and kg/m³ internally, solves the missing quantity, then displays practical units.

Calculation Example

A mass of 1,000 g occupying 1 L has a density of 1 g/cm³, equivalent to 1,000 kg/m³. Unit consistency is essential: mixing grams and cubic meters without conversion would produce the wrong numeric value.

Understanding Density

Objects with the same volume can have very different masses because their materials have different densities. Density can also depend on temperature, pressure, composition, and phase.

Limitations

This calculator assumes uniform bulk density and does not model uncertainty, porosity, temperature expansion, pressure effects, mixtures, or phase changes. Use condition-specific material data for engineering work.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is density?

Mass density is mass divided by volume. In SI, the coherent unit is kilograms per cubic meter (kg/m³).

Can I solve for mass or volume instead?

Yes. Enter exactly two values among density, mass, and volume. The calculator rearranges ρ = m/V to solve for the missing quantity.

Is 1 g/cm³ equal to 1000 kg/m³?

Yes. A density of 1 g/cm³ equals 1 g/mL, 1 kg/L, and 1000 kg/m³.

Does density change with temperature?

It can. Many materials expand or contract as temperature changes, which changes volume and therefore density. Use values measured or specified for the relevant conditions when precision matters.
