What Is a Voltage Drop Calculator?

This calculator estimates how much voltage is lost along a conductor because the conductor has electrical resistance. It is intended for quick planning and educational checks for DC, single-phase, and three-phase circuits.

Voltage Drop Formula

DC / 1φ: Vdrop = 2 × I × R × L
3φ: Vdrop = √3 × I × R × L
Percent drop = Vdrop ÷ Vsystem × 100

Here, I is current in amperes, R is conductor resistance per unit length, and L is one-way conductor length. The factor 2 represents the outgoing and return path for DC or single-phase resistance-only calculations.

Wire Resistance Assumptions

The copper AWG values are typical resistance values around 20°C. Aluminum is estimated using a resistivity ratio rather than a manufacturer-specific cable table. Actual installed impedance changes with conductor temperature, stranding, AC reactance, conduit arrangement, and other conditions.

Example

For 15 A over 100 ft of 12 AWG copper on a 120 V single-phase circuit, using 5.211 Ω/km gives an estimated drop of about 4.76 V, or about 3.97%.

Why Voltage Drop Matters

Excessive voltage drop can reduce equipment performance and waste energy. Design limits depend on the application and governing electrical rules, so the calculated percentage should be reviewed against the code, standard, equipment instructions, and project specifications that apply to the installation.

Limitations

This version is a resistance-only estimate and does not model power factor or conductor reactance. It also does not verify ampacity, overcurrent protection, short-circuit duty, grounding, or compliance with local electrical codes. Use a qualified electrical professional for safety-critical design.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is voltage drop?

Voltage drop is the decrease in electrical potential along a conductor as current flows through its resistance or impedance.

What formula does this calculator use?

For the resistance-only estimate, DC and single-phase use Vdrop = 2 × I × R × L. Three-phase uses Vdrop = √3 × I × R × L, where R is resistance per unit length and L is one-way distance.

Is this a substitute for electrical code design?

No. It is an engineering estimate. Conductor ampacity, temperature, insulation, termination ratings, local electrical codes, reactance, power factor, and installation conditions may require separate checks.
