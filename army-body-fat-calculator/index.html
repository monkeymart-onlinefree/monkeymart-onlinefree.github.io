What Changed in the Army Body Composition Program?

On July 7, 2026, the U.S. Army announced that Army Directive 2026-13 replaces the previous height-and-weight screening tables, circumference-based tape test, and supplemental body-fat assessments with waist-to-height ratio as the Army’s sole body composition assessment.

Current Army WHtR Formula

Waist-to-Height Ratio = Waist Circumference ÷ Height

Both measurements must use the same unit. A result below 0.55 is the stated benchmark for compliance. The Army says the ratio is recorded to three decimal places.

How to Use This Calculator

Choose inches or centimeters, enter standing height, then enter waist circumference at the navel. The calculator divides waist by height and compares the result with 0.55.

Legacy 2023–2026 Body-Fat Formula

The retired one-site method used body weight and abdomen circumference. Men used −26.97 − 0.12 × weight(lb) + 1.99 × abdomen(in). Women used −9.15 − 0.015 × weight(lb) + 1.27 × abdomen(in). It is no longer the current record assessment.

Limitations

This page is an informational implementation of published Army guidance and is not an official personnel record system. Policies can change. Soldiers should use current Army publications and their chain of command for official determinations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the U.S. Army still use a body-fat tape formula?

As of July 7, 2026, the Army replaced its prior height/weight tables, circumference tape test, and supplemental body-fat assessments with waist-to-height ratio as the sole body composition assessment.

What is the current Army waist-to-height standard?

Army Directive 2026-13 uses a waist-to-height ratio below 0.55 as the compliance benchmark, with the ratio recorded to three decimal places.

Where is waist measured for the Army WHtR assessment?

Army public guidance states that the waistline is measured at the navel and divided by height using the same unit for both measurements.

Sources and Methodology

Primary current source: U.S. Army Public Affairs, July 7, 2026 and the Army policy summary for Army Directive 2026-13. The legacy equation is documented in Army’s 2023 guidance and later military research.
