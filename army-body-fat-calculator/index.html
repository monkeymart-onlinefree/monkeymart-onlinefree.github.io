The Army Body Fat Calculator checks the current U.S. Army waist-to-height ratio (WHtR) body composition standard using your height and waist measurement at the navel. It can also show the retired one-site Army body-fat percentage estimate using sex, body weight, and abdominal circumference for historical reference.

What Is the Army Body Fat Calculator?

The Army Body Fat Calculator helps Soldiers and other users check the body composition calculation used by the current U.S. Army Body Composition Program.

As of July 7, 2026, the Army uses waist-to-height ratio as its sole authorized body composition assessment. The previous height-and-weight screening tables, circumference-based body-fat tape test, and supplemental body-fat assessment system were replaced under Army Directive 2026-13.

The calculator therefore treats WHtR as the primary result. The older one-site body-fat calculation is provided only as a historical reference and should not be used to determine current Army compliance.

How to Use the Army Body Fat Calculator

Waist at navel Waist circumference measured at the belly button Used as the numerator in the current WHtR calculation.

For the current ratio, height and waist must use the same unit. The official Army method measures the waist at the navel and divides the measurement in inches by height in inches.

Formula / Calculation Method

Current Army Waist-to-Height Ratio Formula

The current calculation is:

WHtR = W ÷ H

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
WHtR Waist-to-height ratio Unitless
W Waist circumference measured at the navel Same unit as height
H Height Same unit as waist

Because the same measurement unit appears in both parts of the formula, the units cancel.

Calculation Sequence

Step 1: Measure waist circumference at the navel.

Step 2: Enter height using the same measurement unit.

Step 3: Divide waist circumference by height.

Step 4: For the Army record value, keep three decimal places by disregarding digits after the third decimal rather than rounding them.

Step 5: Compare the recorded value with the Army standard.

The current standard is:

WHtR < 0.550 = meets the standard
WHtR ≥ 0.550 = does not meet the standard

Army guidance specifically states that the ratio is recorded to three decimal places without rounding. For example, 0.549 passes, while 0.550 fails.

Retired One-Site Body-Fat Formula

The calculator can also display the former Army one-site body-fat estimate for historical reference. This method used body weight and abdominal circumference with different equations for men and women.

Retired male equation:

Body Fat % = -26.97 - (0.12 × Weight) + (1.99 × Abdomen)

Where weight is in pounds and abdomen circumference is in inches.

Retired female equation:

Body Fat % = -9.15 - (0.015 × Weight) + (1.27 × Abdomen)

Under the retired guidance, the resulting percentage was rounded to the nearest whole percent.

These equations are not part of the current Army compliance method. Current Army guidance states that previous body-fat assessment methods have been rescinded and WHtR is the sole authorized body composition assessment.

Understanding Your Results

Waist-to-Height Ratio

WHtR compares your waist circumference with your height. It is a unitless ratio rather than a body-fat percentage.

Under current Army policy, a recorded WHtR below 0.550 meets the body composition benchmark. A value of 0.550 or greater does not meet the standard.

Compliance Indicator

The calculator can use the ratio to indicate whether the entered measurements fall below the current 0.550 benchmark.

An online result is informational. An official Army assessment must follow Army measurement, confirmation, documentation, and personnel procedures.

Historical Body-Fat Percentage

The optional historical percentage represents an estimate from the former one-site circumference method. It should not be compared with the current WHtR threshold because the two calculations measure and report different things.

Assumptions and Limitations

The current calculation assumes the waist and height measurements were entered correctly and use the same unit. Small differences in where or how the waist is measured can change the ratio, especially when the result is close to 0.550.

This calculator does not perform an official Army assessment, create a DA Form 5500 record, or replace measurements made by authorized Army personnel.

If an official initial WHtR assessment does not meet the standard, current Army guidance requires a same-duty-day confirmation measurement by a different team before further action.

The historical body-fat equations are included only to reproduce the retired one-site calculation. Previous body-fat tape methods are no longer authorized for current Army body composition compliance.

WHtR and historical body-fat calculations are body composition screening tools. They are not medical diagnoses or individualized medical advice.

Current Army Body Composition Rules

Policy checked: September 24, 2026.

Army Directive 2026-13 established WHtR as the Army's sole body composition assessment beginning in July 2026. The standard is less than, but not equal to, 0.55, and the recorded ratio uses three decimal places without rounding.

All Soldiers are screened at least twice per calendar year. If the first measurement does not meet the standard, a different team conducts a confirmation measurement on the same duty day.

The previous fitness-test-based body composition exemption has also been rescinded. Current guidance states that Soldiers must meet the WHtR standard regardless of Army Fitness Test performance.

Army body composition policy can change, so official Army guidance should be checked when the result is being used for personnel or administrative decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the current Army body fat formula?

The Army no longer uses a body-fat percentage equation as its primary body composition standard. Current policy uses waist circumference ÷ height to calculate WHtR.

What WHtR passes the Army standard?

The recorded ratio must be below 0.550. A result of 0.549 meets the standard, while 0.550 does not.

Does the Army round the waist-to-height ratio?

No. Current Army guidance says the result is recorded to three decimal places and additional digits are disregarded rather than rounded.

Where should the waist be measured?

For the current Army WHtR assessment, waist circumference is measured at the navel, or belly button.

Does sex affect the current Army WHtR result?

No. The current calculation is simply waist divided by height, with the same 0.55 benchmark. The sex input in this calculator is used only for the retired historical body-fat equation.

Is the old Army tape-test body-fat percentage still valid?

It can still be calculated for historical comparison, but it is no longer the authorized Army body composition assessment. Current Army guidance states that previous body-fat assessment methods have been rescinded.

Can this calculator be used as an official Army assessment?

No. It provides an informational calculation based on published Army methodology. Official determinations must follow current Army procedures, measurements, forms, confirmation requirements, and chain-of-command guidance.
