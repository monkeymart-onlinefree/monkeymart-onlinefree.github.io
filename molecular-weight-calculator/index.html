What Is a Molecular Weight Calculator?

This calculator parses a chemical formula, counts its atoms, and sums the standard atomic-weight contributions. In classroom and laboratory contexts, the numerical result is commonly reported as molar mass in grams per mole (g/mol).

Molecular Weight Formula

molar mass ≈ Σ (number of atoms of element i × standard atomic weight of element i)

Example: Water

H₂O contains two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom. Using H = 1.008 and O = 15.999, the total is (2 × 1.008) + 15.999 = 18.015 g/mol.

Parentheses and Hydrates

Grouped formulas multiply the contents of parentheses by the following subscript. Hydrates such as CuSO₄·5H₂O add the specified number of water molecules to the anhydrous compound.

Precision note: natural isotopic composition can make standard atomic weights vary for some elements. This tool uses practical abridged values suitable for general calculations, not isotope-specific exact masses.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is molecular weight calculated?

Count each element in the chemical formula, multiply the count by that element's standard atomic weight, then add the contributions.

What is the molar mass of water?

Using common abridged standard atomic weights H = 1.008 and O = 15.999, H₂O has a molar mass of about 18.015 g/mol.

Does the formula parser support parentheses?

Yes. This calculator supports common nested grouping symbols and hydrate separators such as the middle dot in CuSO4·5H2O.
