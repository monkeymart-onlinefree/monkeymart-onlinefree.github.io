What Is a Molarity Calculator?

A molarity calculator finds the amount concentration of a solution. IUPAC defines amount concentration as the amount of a constituent divided by the volume of the mixture. In common laboratory use, molarity is usually expressed in moles per liter (mol/L), often written M.

Molarity Formula

M = n ÷ V
n = mass ÷ molar mass
therefore M = mass ÷ (molar mass × volume)

Formula Variables

M is molarity in mol/L, n is amount in moles, and V is total solution volume in liters. When mass is used, molar mass must be in g/mol and solute mass in grams.

Calculation Example

For 10 g of sodium chloride with a molar mass of 58.44 g/mol in 500 mL (0.5 L), moles = 10 ÷ 58.44 ≈ 0.1711 mol. Molarity = 0.1711 ÷ 0.5 ≈ 0.3422 M.

Important Limitation

Use the final volume of the solution, not simply the starting solvent volume, when precision matters. Temperature can affect liquid volume, so high-accuracy laboratory work should use appropriate volumetric procedures.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is molarity?

Molarity, more precisely amount concentration, is the amount of solute in moles divided by the volume of the solution in liters. A concentration of 1 M means 1 mole per liter.

How do I calculate molarity from grams?

Convert grams to moles by dividing solute mass by molar mass, then divide the moles by solution volume in liters.

Is molarity the same as molality?

No. Molarity uses solution volume, while molality uses the mass of solvent in kilograms.
