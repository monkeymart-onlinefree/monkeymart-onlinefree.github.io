What Is a Molarity Calculator?

A molarity calculator finds the amount concentration of a solution or rearranges the same relationship to solve for mass, molar mass, or solution volume.

IUPAC defines amount concentration as the amount of a constituent divided by the volume of the mixture. A common unit is moles per liter (mol/L), which is often written as M.

The calculator is useful for chemistry problems, laboratory solution preparation, concentration calculations, and checking relationships between solute mass and solution volume.

How to Use the Molarity Calculator

Solve for

Choose the quantity you want the calculator to find:

  • Molarity or concentration
  • Solute mass
  • Molar mass
  • Solution volume

Enter the remaining known values required for that calculation.

Solute Mass

Enter the mass of the dissolved substance. In the base formula, mass is expressed in grams (g) after any required unit conversion.

Molar Mass

Enter the molar mass of the solute. The base unit used in the calculation is grams per mole (g/mol).

IUPAC defines molar mass as mass divided by amount of substance.

Solution Volume

Enter the final volume of the solution, not only the amount of solvent used before the solute was added.

The base formula uses liters. For example:

1 L = 1,000 mL

NIST defines one liter as one cubic decimeter and 10−310^{-3} cubic meters.

Concentration

Enter the molarity when concentration is already known and you want to calculate another quantity such as solute mass, molar mass, or solution volume.

Concentration is expressed in mol/L, commonly written as M.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator uses the molarity relationship:

M=nVM=\frac{n}{V}

The amount of substance can be calculated from mass and molar mass:

n=mMmn=\frac{m}{M_m}

Combining the two relationships gives:

M=mMm×VM=\frac{m}{M_m\times V}

This matches the definition of amount concentration as amount of substance divided by solution volume.

Variables

The mole is the SI unit for amount of substance.

Calculation Sequence

When calculating molarity from mass, molar mass, and volume, the calculator follows this sequence:

  1. Convert the entered values into compatible units where required.
  2. Calculate moles of solute:
    n=mMmn=\frac{m}{M_m}
  3. Calculate molarity:
    M=nVM=\frac{n}{V}
  4. Return the selected result and supporting calculation values.

Calculate Solute Mass

Rearranging the implemented molarity formula:

m=M×Mm×Vm=M\times M_m\times V

Use molarity in mol/L, molar mass in g/mol, and volume in liters to obtain mass in grams.

Calculate Molar Mass

The same relationship can be rearranged as:

Mm=mM×VM_m=\frac{m}{M\times V}

The result is expressed in g/mol when mass is in grams and volume is in liters.

Calculate Solution Volume

To solve for volume:

V=mMm×MV=\frac{m}{M_m\times M}

The result is in liters when the other values use the base units above.

Understanding Your Results

Molarity

Molarity shows how many moles of solute are present per liter of final solution. A result of 0.3422 M means approximately 0.3422 moles of the specified solute are present in each liter of solution.

Solute Mass

When solving for mass, the result is the amount of solute needed to satisfy the entered molarity, molar mass, and final solution volume.

Molar Mass

When solving for molar mass, the result represents the mass per mole of the substance based on the entered mass, concentration, and solution volume.

Solution Volume

When solving for volume, the result is the final solution volume required for the entered mass, molar mass, and concentration.

Supporting values can help confirm the quantities used in the calculation and any required unit conversions.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator uses the standard relationship between amount of substance and final solution volume. Results depend directly on the accuracy of the values entered.

Use the final solution volume, not simply the starting solvent volume. Adding a solute can change the total volume of a solution.

Molar mass and any volume or concentration used as a divisor must not be zero.

The formula assumes the entered values describe the same solute and solution. Using an incorrect molar mass will produce an incorrect concentration or mass result.

For high-accuracy laboratory work, temperature and volumetric measurement procedures can matter because measured liquid volume can vary with measurement conditions. NIST volumetric calibration procedures include temperature corrections for precision volume measurements.

Calculator rounding may also make the displayed result slightly different from a calculation performed with more decimal places.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is molarity?

Molarity is commonly used to describe the amount of substance per unit volume of solution. It is normally expressed as mol/L or M. IUPAC uses the more precise term amount concentration.

What is the formula for molarity?

The basic relationship is:

M=nVM=\frac{n}{V}

When the amount in moles is calculated from solute mass and molar mass:

M=mMm×VM=\frac{m}{M_m\times V}
Should I use solvent volume or final solution volume?

Use the final volume of the complete solution. Using only the initial solvent volume can produce a different result if adding the solute changes the total volume.

How do I convert mL to liters for molarity?

Divide milliliters by 1,000:

V(L)=V(mL)1000V(L)=\frac{V(mL)}{1000}

For example, 250 mL equals 0.250 L. NIST states that 1 liter equals 1,000 milliliters.

Can the calculator find solute mass?

Yes. If molarity, molar mass, and solution volume are known:

m=M×Mm×Vm=M\times M_m\times V
Can the calculator calculate molar mass?

Yes. When mass, molarity, and solution volume are known:

Mm=mM×VM_m=\frac{m}{M\times V}
Why might my laboratory result differ from the calculator?

Differences can come from measurement uncertainty, inaccurate molar mass or mass values, using solvent volume instead of final solution volume, temperature effects on volume, or rounding.
