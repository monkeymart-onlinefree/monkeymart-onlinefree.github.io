The Mortgage Calculator for Your Site calculates a fixed-rate monthly mortgage payment from the loan amount, loan term, and annual interest rate. It also accepts annual property tax, PMI, and other cost inputs and provides an embed option for adding the calculator to a website.

What Is the Mortgage Calculator for Your Site?

This calculator is designed for website owners and users who need a simple fixed-rate mortgage calculation directly on a web page.

The core calculation estimates the monthly principal-and-interest payment for a fixed-rate loan. Property tax, private mortgage insurance (PMI), and other annual costs can also be entered as additional cost information.

The tool is useful for mortgage estimates, financial websites, real-estate pages, educational resources, and other sites that want to provide an embedded mortgage calculator.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator uses the standard fixed-rate mortgage payment formula:

M = P × [r(1 + r)ⁿ] ÷ [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1]

The formula calculates the regular monthly principal-and-interest payment required to repay the loan over the selected term when the interest rate remains constant.

Variables

M is the monthly mortgage principal-and-interest payment, measured in dollars per month.

P is the mortgage principal or loan amount, measured in dollars.

r is the monthly interest rate expressed as a decimal.

n is the total number of monthly payments.

The monthly interest rate is calculated as:

r = Annual Interest Rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12

The number of payments is calculated as:

n = Loan Term in Years × 12

Calculation Sequence

The annual percentage rate entered by the user is first converted to a decimal monthly rate. The loan term in years is then converted to the total number of monthly payment periods.

The calculator applies the fixed-rate mortgage formula using the loan amount, monthly rate, and number of payments to determine the monthly principal-and-interest payment.

Zero-Interest Calculation

When the interest rate is 0%, the normal mortgage formula would involve division by zero. In that case, the payment is calculated directly as:

M = P ÷ n

This divides the loan principal evenly across all monthly payment periods.

Property Tax, PMI, and Other Costs

Property tax, PMI, and other costs are entered as annual dollar amounts. These fields are separate from the variables used in the principal-and-interest mortgage formula.

They allow the calculator to account for additional mortgage or homeownership costs without changing the underlying fixed-rate loan-payment formula.

Worked Example

Assume a mortgage has a $300,000 loan amount, a 30-year term, and a 6% annual interest rate. For this example, property tax, PMI, and other annual cost fields are set to $0 so the principal-and-interest calculation can be shown directly.

Inputs

Loan amount = $300,000
Loan term = 30 years
Annual interest rate = 6%
Property tax = $0/year
PMI = $0/year
Other cost = $0/year

Step 1: Calculate the Monthly Interest Rate

r = 6 ÷ 100 ÷ 12
r = 0.005

The monthly interest rate is 0.005, or 0.5%.

Step 2: Calculate the Number of Payments

n = 30 × 12
n = 360

The loan has 360 monthly payment periods.

Step 3: Apply the Mortgage Formula

M = 300,000 × [0.005(1.005)³⁶⁰] ÷ [(1.005)³⁶⁰ − 1]

Using sufficient precision during the calculation:

M ≈ 1,798.6516

Final Result

The estimated monthly principal-and-interest mortgage payment is approximately $1,798.65 per month.

Additional property tax, PMI, or other annual costs may affect the broader housing-cost estimate when those inputs are used.

Understanding Your Results

The main mortgage calculation represents the monthly principal-and-interest payment required by the fixed-rate formula.

This amount is based on the entered principal, annual interest rate, and loan term. It should not automatically be treated as the complete amount a borrower will pay each month because property taxes, mortgage insurance, insurance costs, fees, or other expenses may also apply.

The property tax, PMI, and other cost fields provide additional cost information separate from the core mortgage formula.

The embed output is intended for site owners who want to place the calculator within a web page rather than recreating the calculation themselves.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculation assumes a fixed interest rate for the loan term and regular monthly payments.

The annual interest rate is converted to a monthly rate by dividing it by 12, and the loan term is converted from years to months by multiplying it by 12.

The formula models a standard fixed-rate amortizing loan. It does not by itself model an adjustable interest rate, changing payment schedule, balloon structure, or other specialized mortgage terms.

Property tax, PMI, and other costs can vary over time. Entering an annual value should therefore be treated as an estimate rather than a guarantee of future costs.

The calculator does not require a live mortgage-rate dataset. The interest rate is supplied by the user.

Actual lender calculations can differ because of lender-specific rounding, escrow arrangements, insurance, fees, payment dates, loan terms, and other contractual details.

The result is an estimate and is not a loan offer, approval decision, or financial advice.

Frequently Asked Questions

What formula does the Mortgage Calculator for Your Site use?

It uses the fixed-rate mortgage payment formula:

M = P × r(1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1)

where P is the loan principal, r is the monthly interest rate, and n is the number of monthly payments.

How is the monthly interest rate calculated?

The annual interest rate is divided by 100 to convert it to decimal form and then divided by 12:

Monthly rate = Annual rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12
What happens if the mortgage interest rate is 0%?

The payment is calculated by dividing the loan amount by the total number of monthly payments:

Monthly payment = Principal ÷ Number of months
Are property taxes and PMI part of the main mortgage formula?

No. The fixed-rate formula calculates the principal-and-interest payment using the loan amount, interest rate, and term. Property tax, PMI, and other cost fields are additional annual-cost inputs.

Can this calculator estimate an adjustable-rate mortgage?

The documented calculation is based on a fixed interest rate. A mortgage whose rate changes over time requires additional rules that are not part of this fixed-rate formula.

Why might my lender's payment be different?

A lender may include property taxes, mortgage insurance, escrow amounts, insurance, fees, or loan-specific requirements. Rounding and payment timing may also cause small differences.

Can I add this mortgage calculator to my website?

Yes. This calculator is designed to provide embed functionality so it can be placed on another website using the generated embed output.
