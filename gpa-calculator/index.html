What Is the GPA Calculator?

The GPA Calculator estimates your grade point average by weighting each included course according to its credit value. A course with more credits therefore has a greater effect on the semester GPA than a course with fewer credits.

It is useful for students who want to estimate their semester academic performance, review how credit weighting affects GPA, or work with an existing GPA and completed-credit history.

How to Use the GPA Calculator

Each course included in the calculation contributes its grade-point value and course credits to the GPA formula.

The calculator also includes these optional prior-history inputs:

Prior GPA (optional): Enter your existing GPA before the current semester. Use the GPA recorded under the grading scale that applies to your academic record.

Prior completed credits: Enter the number of completed credits associated with the prior GPA. This value should correspond to the same academic record represented by the prior GPA.

For the most meaningful result, use grade-point values and credit amounts that match your school's GPA policy.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator uses the credit-weighted GPA formula:

GPA = Σ(Grade Points × Course Credits) ÷ Σ(Course Credits)

This means the grade points earned in each course are multiplied by that course's credit value. The resulting quality points are added together and divided by the total credits included.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Determine the grade-point value used for each included course.
  2. Multiply each course's grade points by its credits.
  3. Add the quality points from all included courses.
  4. Add the credits from all included courses.
  5. Divide total quality points by total credits.
  6. Report the semester GPA and supporting semester totals.

Semester Quality Points

For each course:

Course Quality Points = Grade Points × Course Credits

For all included courses:

Semester Quality Points = Σ(Grade Points × Course Credits)

Semester Credits

Semester Credits = Σ(Course Credits)

These credits form the denominator of the semester GPA calculation.

Courses Included

This result shows how many courses contribute to the semester calculation. The exact academic treatment of repeated courses, pass/fail courses, withdrawals, transfer courses, and similar records depends on institutional policy.

Understanding Your Results

Semester GPA is the credit-weighted average of the grade points for the courses included in the semester calculation. Higher-credit courses have a larger effect on this value.

Semester credits are the total course credits used in the calculation.

Semester quality points are the total of each course's grade points multiplied by its credits.

Courses included indicates the number of courses contributing to the semester result.

Your calculated GPA should be treated as an estimate when comparing it with an official transcript because schools can use different grading scales, course-inclusion rules, repeat-course policies, and rounding methods.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator uses a standard 4.0-scale GPA approach and a credit-weighted calculation. Your institution may assign different grade-point values or use a different grading system.

A semester GPA requires a positive total number of included credits because GPA is calculated by dividing total quality points by total credits.

The calculator does not establish which courses your school includes in an official GPA. Policies for pass/fail courses, withdrawals, repeated courses, transfer credits, remedial courses, honors weighting, and other academic records can differ.

Some institutions also use grading scales that permit values above 4.0 or apply special weighting to certain courses. Use your institution's official scale when its rules differ from the calculator's default mapping.

Displayed rounding can also differ from an official transcript. Always use your school's registrar or academic record as the authoritative source for an official GPA.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is GPA calculated?

GPA is calculated by multiplying each course's grade-point value by its credits, adding the resulting quality points, and dividing by the total course credits:

GPA = Σ(grade points × credits) ÷ Σcredits

Why are course credits important in a GPA calculation?

Credits determine how much weight a course receives. A four-credit course contributes more to a credit-weighted GPA than a one-credit course with the same grade-point value.

What are quality points?

Quality points are calculated by multiplying a course's grade-point value by its credits. The GPA calculation divides total quality points by total credits.

What should I enter for prior GPA?

Enter the GPA you had before the current semester. It should represent the same academic record associated with the prior completed-credit value you enter.

Is this GPA Calculator accurate for every school?

The mathematical weighted-average formula is standard, but institutional rules are not universal. Grade scales, repeated-course policies, excluded courses, weighting, and rounding can change an official GPA.

Can a school's GPA differ from the calculator result?

Yes. A school may use different grade-point values, exclude certain courses, treat repeats differently, or apply its own rounding or truncation rules.

What happens if there are no credits to calculate?

A GPA cannot be calculated when total included credits are zero because the formula would require division by zero.
