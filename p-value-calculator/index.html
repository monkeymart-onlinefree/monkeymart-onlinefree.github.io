A p-value is evidence under a statistical model, not the probability that the null hypothesis itself is true.

The P-Value Calculator finds probabilities from a standard normal z-score. Enter a z-score and choose the probability region—left tail, right tail, center, between, or two tails—to calculate the corresponding standard normal probability.

What Is the P-Value Calculator?

A P-Value Calculator converts a z-score into an area or probability under the standard normal distribution.

In hypothesis testing, a p-value represents the probability of obtaining a test statistic at least as extreme as the observed value when the null hypothesis is true.

This calculator is useful when your test statistic follows, or is appropriately approximated by, the standard normal distribution and you need a specific tail or central probability.

How to Use the P-Value Calculator

1. Select the Probability Region

Choose the region that matches the probability you want:

  • Left: probability to the left of the entered z-score.
  • Right: probability to the right of the entered z-score.
  • Center: probability between the mean, z = 0, and the entered z-score.
  • Between: probability between −|z| and +|z|.
  • Two tails: combined probability below −|z| and above +|z|.

2. Enter the Z-Score

Enter the standard normal z-score. A z-score is unitless and represents a position relative to the center of the standard normal distribution.

The calculator evaluates the standard normal cumulative distribution and applies the formula for your selected probability region.

Formula / Calculation Method

Let:

Φ(z) = P(Z ≤ z)

where Z follows a standard normal distribution with mean 0 and standard deviation 1.

NIST defines the standard normal distribution as the normal distribution with μ = 0 and σ = 1, and its cumulative distribution function gives the probability at or below a selected value.

Main Formulas

Left-Tail Probability

p = Φ(z)

This calculates the entire probability to the left of the entered z-score.

Right-Tail Probability

p = 1 − Φ(z)

This calculates the probability to the right of the entered z-score.

Center Probability

p = |Φ(z) − 0.5|

This calculates the area between z = 0 and the entered z-score.

Between Probability

p = 2Φ(|z|) − 1

This calculates the central area between −|z| and +|z|.

Two-Tail Probability

p = 2[1 − Φ(|z|)]

This calculates the combined probability in both tails outside −|z| and +|z|.

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
p Calculated probability for the selected region Probability from 0 to 1
z Standard normal z-score Unitless
Φ(z) Standard normal cumulative probability at z Probability
|z| Absolute value of the z-score Unitless
Z Standard normal random variable Unitless

Calculation Sequence

  1. Read the selected probability region.
  2. Read the entered z-score.
  3. Evaluate Φ(z) or Φ(|z|) as required.
  4. Apply the formula for the selected region.
  5. Return the calculated probability.

The standard normal CDF does not have a simple elementary closed-form expression and is normally evaluated numerically.

Worked Example

Suppose you want the two-tail probability for:

Inputs

  • Probability region: Two tails
  • Z-score: 1.96

Step 1: Find the Cumulative Probability

For:

z = 1.96

the standard normal cumulative probability is approximately:

Φ(1.96) = 0.9750021

NIST's standard normal table also gives approximately 0.975 for z = 1.96.

Step 2: Calculate One Tail

1 − Φ(1.96)
= 1 − 0.9750021
= 0.0249979

Step 3: Calculate Both Tails

p = 2 × 0.0249979
= 0.0499958

Final Result

p ≈ 0.0500

The combined area beyond ±1.96 is therefore approximately 5%.

Understanding Your Results

The main result is a probability between 0 and 1.

For example:

  • 0.05 represents a probability of 5%.
  • 0.01 represents a probability of 1%.
  • A value closer to 0 represents a smaller selected-region probability.
  • A value closer to 1 represents a larger selected-region probability.

For hypothesis testing, the calculated probability acts as a p-value only when the chosen probability region correctly represents the relevant hypothesis test.

A small p-value indicates that the observed test statistic would be relatively unusual under the null hypothesis. NIST notes that the significance level should normally be selected before evaluating the test result.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator uses the standard normal distribution. Its results are appropriate only when the statistic being evaluated can validly be modeled as a standard normal z statistic.

It does not replace calculations based on other statistical distributions or test methods, including:

  • Student's t distribution
  • Chi-square distribution
  • F distribution
  • Exact tests
  • Permutation tests
  • Other test-specific probability models

Selecting the correct probability region is important. A left-tailed test, right-tailed test, and two-tailed test can produce very different probabilities from the same z-score.

The Center and Between options calculate areas of the standard normal curve. These region probabilities are not automatically conventional hypothesis-test p-values in every statistical situation.

The standard normal CDF is evaluated numerically. Very large positive or negative z-scores can therefore produce extremely small tail probabilities where floating-point precision becomes relevant.

Mathematically, a z-score can be positive, negative, or zero. At z = 0, the left and right probabilities are each 0.5, while the center and between probabilities are 0.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a p-value?

A p-value measures how extreme an observed test statistic is under the assumption that the null hypothesis is true. Smaller p-values indicate that the observed result is less consistent with the null model.

What is the difference between left-tail and right-tail p-values?

A left-tail calculation measures the probability below the entered z-score:

p = Φ(z)

A right-tail calculation measures the probability above it:

p = 1 − Φ(z)

The correct option depends on the direction of the statistical test.

How is a two-tailed p-value calculated?

For a standard normal z-score, this calculator uses:

p = 2[1 − Φ(|z|)]

Using the absolute value makes the calculation symmetric for positive and negative z-scores.

What does the Center option calculate?

Center calculates the area between the standard normal mean at z = 0 and the entered z-score:

p = |Φ(z) − 0.5|

For example, z = 1.96 produces a center area of approximately 0.475.

What does the Between option calculate?

Between calculates the probability inside the symmetric interval from −|z| to +|z|:

p = 2Φ(|z|) − 1

For z = 1.96, this central probability is approximately 0.9500.

Is p < 0.05 always statistically significant?

Not automatically. Statistical significance depends on the significance level, α, chosen for the analysis. A value of 0.05 is commonly used, but the appropriate threshold depends on the study design and should normally be selected before evaluating the result.

Why might another statistical calculator give a different p-value?

Another calculator may use a different probability distribution, test statistic, tail definition, or test method. For example, a t-test requires a t distribution and degrees of freedom rather than this calculator's standard normal z-score method.
