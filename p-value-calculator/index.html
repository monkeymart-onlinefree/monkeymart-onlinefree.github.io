What Is a P-Value?

A p-value is a tail probability used in hypothesis testing. Under the null hypothesis and the assumptions of the statistical model, it quantifies how probable it would be to observe a test statistic at least as extreme as the one obtained, according to the test's defined direction or tails.

How to Use the P-Value Calculator

Choose Z → p to enter a z-score, or p → Z to reverse the calculation. Then select the probability region. This calculator uses the standard normal distribution only; the selected tail definition determines which area under the normal curve is reported.

P-Value Formulas for a Standard Normal Z-Score

Left: p = Φ(z)\nRight: p = 1 − Φ(z)\nCenter: p = |Φ(z) − 0.5|\nBetween: p = 2Φ(|z|) − 1\nTwo tails: p = 2[1 − Φ(|z|)]

Here, Φ(z) is the cumulative distribution function of a standard normal random variable.

Calculation Example

For z = 1.96, Φ(1.96) is about 0.975. The right-tail area is about 0.025, and the symmetric two-tailed p-value is about 0.05.

How Should a P-Value Be Interpreted?

Important: A p-value does not show the probability that the null hypothesis is true, and a small p-value does not measure the size or practical importance of an effect.

A significance level such as α = 0.05 is often chosen before analysis. If the p-value is below that preselected threshold, the result falls in the test's rejection region. The threshold is a decision rule, not a universal measure of scientific importance.

Limitations

This page applies only to z statistics that are appropriately modeled by a standard normal distribution. It does not replace t, chi-square, F, permutation, exact, or other test-specific p-value calculations. The normal CDF and inverse are evaluated numerically, so extremely tiny tail probabilities are subject to floating-point limits.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a p-value mean?

A p-value is the probability, assuming the null hypothesis and the test model are correct, of observing a test statistic at least as extreme as the one obtained according to the test's tail definition.

Is p ≤ 0.05 always statistically significant?

A 0.05 cutoff is common, but the significance level should be chosen for the study before interpreting the data. A p-value does not measure effect size or practical importance.

What distribution does this calculator use?

This calculator uses the standard normal distribution and converts between a z-score and tail probabilities. It is not a t-test, chi-square, or F-test calculator.

What is a two-tailed p-value for a z-score?

For a symmetric standard normal distribution, the two-tailed p-value is 2 × [1 − Φ(|z|)], where Φ is the standard normal cumulative distribution function.
