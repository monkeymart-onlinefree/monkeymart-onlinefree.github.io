What Is a Confidence Interval Calculator?

A confidence interval calculator estimates a range for a population mean using sample information and a selected confidence level. This page supports a Student's t interval when the standard deviation is estimated from the sample and a z interval when the population standard deviation is treated as known.

How to Use the Confidence Interval Calculator

Choose whether the standard deviation is a sample estimate or a known population value. Enter the sample mean, standard deviation, sample size, and confidence level. The result shows the interval endpoints, margin of error, standard error, and critical value.

Confidence Interval Formulas

For an unknown population standard deviation, the mean interval is x̄ ± t*·s/√n with n−1 degrees of freedom. If the population standard deviation σ is known, the corresponding normal interval is x̄ ± z*·σ/√n.

Calculation Example

With x̄ = 22.8, s = 2.7, n = 100, and 95% confidence, the standard error is 0.27. The t critical value with 99 degrees of freedom is slightly above 1.96, so the margin of error is about 0.536 and the interval is approximately 22.264 to 23.336.

How to Interpret a Confidence Level

A 95% confidence level describes the long-run performance of the interval-building procedure. If the same method were applied repeatedly to independent samples, about 95% of the resulting intervals would contain the true parameter under the model assumptions.

Important Assumptions and Limitations

The standard one-sample mean t interval assumes independent observations and is derived for data from a normally distributed population, though it becomes more robust in many larger samples. Severe skewness, dependence, biased sampling, or influential outliers can make the interval misleading.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I use t or z for a confidence interval?

Use the t method when the population standard deviation is unknown and you are using the sample standard deviation. Use z when the population standard deviation is genuinely known or when a specific method requires the normal critical value.

What is margin of error?

Margin of error is the critical value multiplied by the standard error. The confidence interval is the point estimate plus or minus this margin.

Does 95% confidence mean a 95% probability the true mean is inside this one interval?

In the usual frequentist interpretation, no. The 95% refers to the long-run proportion of intervals from repeated samples that would contain the true mean.

What happens when sample size increases?

Holding the standard deviation and confidence level fixed, the standard error decreases with 1/√n, so the interval generally becomes narrower.
