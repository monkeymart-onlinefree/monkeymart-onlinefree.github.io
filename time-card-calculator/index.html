What Is a Time Card Calculator?

A time card calculator totals hours worked during a week from daily start and end times, subtracts unpaid breaks, separates regular and overtime hours, and can estimate gross hourly pay. It is useful for employees, supervisors, freelancers, and payroll cross-checking.

How Work Hours Are Calculated

Daily hours = End time − Start time − Unpaid break
Weekly hours = Sum of daily hours
Overtime = max(0, weekly hours − threshold)

If an end time is earlier than its start time, the calculator treats the shift as crossing midnight into the next day.

Overtime Settings

The default threshold is 40 hours with a 1.5× overtime multiplier. The U.S. Department of Labor states that covered, nonexempt employees generally must receive at least one and one-half times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. Exemptions and other jurisdictions can use different rules, so both fields are editable.

Example

Five 9:00 AM–5:00 PM days with a 30-minute unpaid break each total 37.5 hours. At $20 per hour and no overtime, the estimated gross pay is $750.

Limitations

This page does not decide whether time is legally compensable, whether a worker is exempt, how rounding must be handled, or which local labor law applies. It also does not include taxes, benefits, bonuses, shift differentials, or other payroll items.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are work hours calculated?

For each day, the calculator subtracts start time from end time and then subtracts the entered unpaid break. Overnight shifts are handled by treating an end time earlier than the start as the next day.

How does overtime work?

The overtime threshold and multiplier are editable. The defaults are 40 hours and 1.5×, reflecting the general U.S. FLSA rule for covered, nonexempt employees; local rules and exemptions can differ.

Does the time card replace payroll records?

No. It is a planning and checking tool. Employers should use their required timekeeping and payroll systems and apply the laws, contracts, and policies that govern the worker.
