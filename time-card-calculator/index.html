Use the Time Card Calculator to total work hours across a seven-day time card, subtract unpaid breaks, calculate weekly overtime, and estimate gross pay. Enter each day's start and end time, break minutes, hourly rate, overtime threshold, and overtime multiplier to calculate the results.

What Is the Time Card Calculator?

The Time Card Calculator converts daily clock-in and clock-out times into paid work hours and adds them together for a weekly total. It can also separate hours above a selected weekly overtime threshold and use the entered hourly rate and overtime multiplier to estimate gross earnings.

It is useful for employees, hourly workers, freelancers, managers, and anyone who wants a quick weekly time-card estimate.

How to Use the Time Card Calculator

For each workday, enter the applicable start time, end time, and unpaid break.

  • Start: The time the shift begins.
  • End: The time the shift ends. If the end time is earlier than the start time, the calculator treats the shift as continuing past midnight.
  • Break (min): Unpaid break time in minutes. This amount is deducted from the shift duration.
  • Hourly rate: Your regular hourly pay rate used for the estimated gross-pay calculation.
  • Weekly overtime after: The number of weekly hours after which the calculator starts classifying additional hours as overtime.
  • Overtime multiplier: The multiplier applied to overtime hours, such as 1.5 when appropriate.

Enter only break time that should actually be excluded from paid hours. Whether a particular break is legally compensable depends on the applicable employment rules.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

For each day:

Daily paid hours = End time − Start time − Unpaid break

When the shift crosses midnight:

Shift duration = (End time + 24 hours) − Start time

The weekly total is:

Weekly hours = h₁ + h₂ + h₃ + ... + h₇

Overtime hours are:

OT = max(0, H − T)

Regular hours are:

RH = H − OT

The estimated gross-pay calculation is:

Gross pay = (RH × R) + (OT × R × M)

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
h Paid hours for one day hours
H Total paid hours for the week hours
T Weekly overtime threshold hours
OT Overtime hours hours
RH Regular hours hours
R Hourly rate currency per hour
M Overtime multiplier multiplier
B Unpaid break minutes

Calculation Sequence

  1. Convert the start and end clock times into elapsed time.
  2. If the end time is earlier than the start time, treat the end as occurring on the following day.
  3. Subtract the entered unpaid break.
  4. Convert the remaining time into paid hours.
  5. Add the paid hours from all seven days.
  6. Compare weekly hours with the entered overtime threshold.
  7. Separate regular and overtime hours.
  8. Apply the hourly rate and overtime multiplier to estimate gross pay.

Daily Work Hours

When a shift does not cross midnight:

Daily hours = (End − Start) − Break

For example, 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM is 8.5 elapsed hours. With a 30-minute unpaid break:

8.5 − 0.5 = 8.0 paid hours

Overnight Shifts

If the end time is earlier than the start time, the calculator treats the shift as crossing midnight.

For example:

Start: 10:00 PM
End: 6:00 AM

The elapsed shift is 8 hours before any unpaid break is deducted.

If the start and end times are identical, the standard calculation produces zero elapsed hours rather than automatically treating the shift as 24 hours.

Weekly Hours

The calculator adds the paid daily totals:

Weekly hours = Sum of all daily paid hours

Only the hours entered on the current seven-day time card are included.

Overtime Hours

The calculator uses the overtime threshold entered by the user:

Overtime hours = max(0, Weekly hours − Overtime threshold)

If weekly hours do not exceed the threshold, overtime is zero.

Estimated Gross Pay

Regular hours use the normal hourly rate. Overtime hours use the hourly rate multiplied by the selected overtime multiplier:

Estimated gross pay = Regular-hour pay + Overtime-hour pay

This is a gross estimate before taxes and other payroll adjustments.

Worked Example

Assume a worker enters the following schedule:

Inputs

  • Monday: 9:00 AM–5:30 PM, 30-minute break
  • Tuesday: 9:00 AM–5:30 PM, 30-minute break
  • Wednesday: 9:00 AM–5:30 PM, 30-minute break
  • Thursday: 9:00 AM–5:30 PM, 30-minute break
  • Friday: 9:00 AM–5:30 PM, 30-minute break
  • Saturday: 9:00 AM–1:00 PM, no break
  • Sunday: No hours
  • Hourly rate: $20
  • Weekly overtime after: 40 hours
  • Overtime multiplier: 1.5

Step 1: Calculate Daily Hours

Monday through Friday:

8.5 elapsed hours − 0.5-hour break = 8 paid hours per day

Five days:

8 × 5 = 40 hours

Saturday:

1:00 PM − 9:00 AM = 4 hours

Step 2: Calculate Weekly Hours

40 + 4 = 44 weekly hours

Step 3: Calculate Overtime

Overtime = max(0, 44 − 40)
Overtime = 4 hours

Regular hours:

44 − 4 = 40 hours

Step 4: Estimate Gross Pay

Regular pay:

40 × $20 = $800

Overtime rate:

$20 × 1.5 = $30 per hour

Overtime pay:

4 × $30 = $120

Final Result

  • Weekly hours: 44 hours
  • Regular hours: 40 hours
  • Overtime hours: 4 hours
  • Estimated gross pay: $920

Understanding Your Results

Daily hours show the amount of paid time remaining after the entered unpaid break is deducted from each shift.

Weekly hours are the combined paid hours from the seven-day time card.

Regular hours are weekly hours that do not fall above the selected overtime threshold.

Overtime hours are hours above the threshold entered in the calculator.

Estimated gross pay is the calculated regular and overtime pay before taxes, benefits, deductions, bonuses, commissions, or other payroll adjustments.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator uses the times and settings you enter. It does not determine whether a worker is legally entitled to overtime or whether a particular period should legally count as working time.

An entered break is treated as unpaid time and deducted from the shift. In actual payroll, some breaks may legally need to be counted as paid working time.

The calculator uses a weekly overtime threshold rather than independently determining federal, state, provincial, local, contractual, or industry-specific overtime requirements.

It does not include taxes, benefits, bonuses, commissions, shift differentials, paid leave, holiday premiums, multiple pay rates, or other payroll items.

The calculator also does not establish a legal time-clock rounding method. Employer payroll systems may use compliant rounding or other timekeeping procedures that produce different results.

Because the inputs are simple clock times rather than fully dated, time-zone-aware timestamps, shifts affected by daylight-saving clock changes may require separate verification.

Current Overtime and Break Context

As of September 24, 2026, U.S. federal Fair Labor Standards Act guidance states that covered, nonexempt employees generally must receive overtime pay for hours worked beyond 40 in a workweek at not less than one and one-half times their regular rate. A federal workweek is a fixed, recurring period of 168 hours, or seven consecutive 24-hour periods.

Federal guidance also distinguishes unpaid meal periods from short rest breaks. Short breaks, usually 20 minutes or less, generally count as hours worked, while bona fide meal periods are generally not work time when the employee is completely relieved from duty.

These federal rules should not automatically be used as the correct settings for every worker. State, local, contractual, occupational, and exemption rules can differ, including rules that require premium pay after designated daily hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Time Card Calculator handle overnight shifts?

Yes. When an end time is earlier than its start time, the calculator treats the end time as occurring on the following day. For example, 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM is treated as an overnight shift.

How are unpaid breaks calculated?

The entered break is converted from minutes and deducted from the elapsed shift time. A 30-minute unpaid break reduces paid time by 0.5 hour.

How does the calculator determine overtime?

It compares total weekly hours with the overtime threshold you enter. Only hours above that threshold are classified as overtime.

Is the gross-pay result the same as my paycheck?

No. It is an estimated gross amount based on the entered hourly rate, work hours, threshold, and overtime multiplier. Taxes, deductions, benefits, bonuses, and other payroll items are not included.

Does the calculator determine whether I legally qualify for overtime?

No. Overtime eligibility depends on the applicable employment law, exemptions, work arrangement, location, and other circumstances. The overtime threshold and multiplier are calculator inputs, not a legal determination.

Should every break be entered as unpaid?

No. Enter only time that should actually be excluded from paid hours. Under U.S. federal guidance, short rest breaks generally count as working time, while qualifying meal periods may be unpaid when the employee is completely relieved from duty.

Does the calculator account for daylight-saving time changes?

Not as a full time-zone-aware payroll system would. Because the time card uses simple start and end clock times, a shift that crosses a daylight-saving transition may need separate verification.
