What Is an APR Calculator?

An APR calculator estimates the yearly cost of borrowing after considering the interest rate and eligible upfront finance charges. APR is designed as a comparison measure, so two loans with the same note rate can have different APRs when their fees differ.

How This APR Estimate Works

The calculator first computes the regular monthly payment from the loan amount, note rate, and term. It then treats the amount received as the loan amount minus the finance charges you enter and solves for the monthly discount rate that makes the present value of all scheduled payments equal to those net proceeds.

Net Amount Received = Present Value of Scheduled Payments at the APR periodic rate

The solved monthly rate is annualized as a nominal annual rate by multiplying it by 12. This mirrors the cash-flow logic behind actuarial APR, but it is not a substitute for an official Truth in Lending disclosure.

Why APR Differs From Interest Rate

The note interest rate mainly drives the interest charged on the outstanding principal. APR can incorporate certain finance charges in addition to that rate, so it can be higher than the note rate even when the monthly payment is calculated using the note rate.

Limitations

Regulation Z APR calculations can involve fee classification, exact payment dates, odd first periods, mortgage insurance treatment, and other details. Use this tool for estimates and comparisons, not compliance reporting.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is APR?

APR is a yearly measure of the cost of credit. For closed-end credit, U.S. Regulation Z relates the amount and timing of value received by the borrower to the amount and timing of payments made.

Why can APR be higher than the note rate?

Upfront finance charges can reduce the net amount of credit you actually receive while scheduled payments are still based on the full loan, which raises the effective yearly borrowing cost.

Is this the same as a lender’s disclosed APR?

No. This calculator is an estimate using regular monthly payments and user-entered upfront finance charges. Official disclosures can require detailed treatment of specific fees, dates, irregular periods, and regulatory rules.
