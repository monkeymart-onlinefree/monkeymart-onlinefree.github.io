What Is an Amortization Calculator?

Answer: An amortization calculator shows how a fixed-rate loan is paid down through regular monthly payments. Each payment is divided between interest and principal, with the remaining balance decreasing as principal is repaid.

Early in an amortizing loan, a larger share of the payment generally goes toward interest because the outstanding balance is higher. As the balance falls, less interest is charged and more of the required payment goes toward principal.

This calculator is useful for reviewing fixed-rate loans and understanding how optional extra principal payments may affect the repayment schedule.

How to Use the Amortization Calculator

Enter the following information in order:

  1. Loan amount ($): Enter the amount of principal being borrowed.
  2. Annual rate (%): Enter the fixed annual interest rate as a percentage.
  3. Loan term (years): Enter the number of years over which the loan is scheduled to be repaid.
  4. Extra monthly payment ($): Enter any additional amount you plan to pay toward principal each month. Enter zero if you do not plan to make extra payments.

The calculator first determines the required monthly payment from the loan amount, interest rate, and term. The optional extra payment is then applied to principal when the amortization schedule is calculated.

You can also use the additional term months, first payment month, monthly extra start, yearly extra payment, yearly extra start, and one-time extra payment controls already available in this calculator.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The required monthly payment for a fixed-rate amortizing loan is calculated as:

M = P × [r(1 + r)n] / [(1 + r)n − 1]

This is the standard fixed-installment method used to calculate level principal-and-interest payments on an amortizing fixed-rate loan.

Variables

SymbolMeaningUnit
MRequired monthly paymentDollars per month
POriginal loan amountDollars
rMonthly interest rateDecimal per month
nTotal number of monthly paymentsMonths

The calculator converts the entered annual percentage rate into a monthly decimal rate:

r = Annual Rate / (100 × 12)

The loan term is converted from years to months:

n = Loan Term in Years × 12

Calculation Sequence

  1. Convert the annual interest rate from a percentage to a decimal.
  2. Divide the annual decimal rate by 12 to find the monthly interest rate.
  3. Multiply the loan term in years by 12 to determine the number of monthly payments.
  4. Apply the fixed-rate loan payment formula to calculate the required monthly payment.
  5. Start the amortization schedule with the original loan balance.
  6. Calculate each month's interest from the opening balance.
  7. Subtract that interest from the required payment to determine the scheduled principal payment.
  8. Apply any extra monthly payment directly to principal.
  9. Reduce the balance and repeat the calculation for the next month.

Monthly Interest

For each payment period:

Interest = Opening Balance × r

Interest therefore becomes smaller as the outstanding balance decreases.

Scheduled Principal

Scheduled Principal = M − Interest

Extra Principal Payment

If an extra monthly payment E is entered, it is treated as additional principal:

Principal Reduction = Scheduled Principal + E

New Balance

New Balance = Opening Balance − Principal Reduction

The new balance becomes the opening balance for the next monthly calculation.

Worked Example

Suppose a loan has these inputs:

  • Loan amount: $200,000
  • Annual interest rate: 6%
  • Loan term: 30 years
  • Extra monthly payment: $100

Step 1: Calculate the Monthly Rate

r = 6% / 12 = 0.5% = 0.005

Step 2: Calculate the Number of Payments

n = 30 × 12 = 360

Step 3: Calculate the Required Monthly Payment

M = 200,000 × [0.005(1.005)360] / [(1.005)360 − 1] ≈ $1,199.10

The required monthly payment is approximately $1,199.10.

Step 4: Calculate the First Month's Interest

$200,000 × 0.005 = $1,000

Step 5: Calculate Scheduled Principal

Using the unrounded payment value during the calculation:

$1,199.10105 − $1,000 ≈ $199.10

Step 6: Add the Extra Principal Payment

$199.10105 + $100 = $299.10105

Step 7: Calculate the New Balance

$200,000 − $299.10105 = $199,700.89895

After rounding for display, the remaining balance is approximately $199,700.90 after the first payment cycle.

Final Result

The calculated required monthly payment is approximately $1,199.10. If the borrower also makes the entered $100 extra monthly payment, that additional amount is applied to principal under this calculator's model.

The same principal-and-interest process is repeated through the amortization schedule.

Understanding Your Results

Required Monthly Payment

The required monthly payment is the regular amount calculated from the loan principal, annual interest rate, and loan term.

The optional extra monthly payment does not reduce the calculated required payment. Instead, the calculator models the extra amount as additional principal paid on top of the required payment.

Interest Portion

Interest for each month is based on the opening loan balance. Because the balance usually becomes smaller over time, the interest portion also generally decreases.

Principal Portion

Principal is the part of the payment that actually reduces the outstanding balance. With a normal amortizing loan, the principal portion usually becomes larger as the loan progresses.

Amortization Schedule

The schedule tracks how the balance changes from one payment period to the next. Extra principal payments can reduce the outstanding balance faster when they are properly applied to principal.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator uses a fixed-rate monthly amortization model. The annual interest rate is converted to a monthly rate, and the loan term is converted into monthly payment periods.

The extra-payment calculation assumes:

  • there is no prepayment penalty, and
  • the lender applies the extra monthly amount directly to the loan principal.

Confirm how your lender handles additional payments before relying on an extra-payment strategy.

Actual lender figures may differ because some loans calculate interest using different accrual methods or payment timing. The calculator has no separate inputs for lender fees, taxes, insurance, or other charges, so those costs are not independently calculated.

Dates identify scheduled monthly payments, not daily interest accrual. Yearly extras repeat every 12 months from their selected start. Any payment is capped at the amount still owed; late extras after payoff are not charged. Terms are limited to 12,000 months. Calculations retain precision internally and round only for display.

Results are estimates and should not be treated as a lender payoff statement, loan offer, or guarantee of savings.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does amortization mean?

Amortization is the process of gradually paying off a loan through scheduled payments. Each payment includes interest and principal, and the balance decreases as principal is repaid.

How is the monthly payment calculated?

The calculator uses the fixed-rate amortized loan formula based on the original loan amount, monthly interest rate, and total number of monthly payments.

Why is more interest paid near the beginning of the loan?

Monthly interest is calculated from the outstanding balance. Since the balance is highest near the beginning of the loan, the interest amount is also higher. As principal is paid down, the interest portion generally decreases.

Does an extra monthly payment reduce my required payment?

No. In this calculator, the required payment is calculated from the loan amount, rate, and original term. The entered extra payment is modeled as an additional amount applied directly toward principal.

Can extra payments help pay a loan off sooner?

Applying extra money directly to principal reduces the outstanding balance faster. For loans that permit this treatment, that can shorten the repayment period and reduce future interest.

Why might my lender's amortization schedule be different?

A lender may use different interest-accrual conventions, payment dates, rounding procedures, fees, or contractual rules. Use the calculator as an estimate and compare important figures with your lender's official loan documents.

Does the calculator account for prepayment penalties?

No prepayment penalty is assumed in the extra-payment model. Check your loan agreement or lender requirements before making additional principal payments.
