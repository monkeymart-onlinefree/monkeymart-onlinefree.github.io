Use the Gas Mileage Calculator to estimate your vehicle’s fuel economy from two odometer readings and the amount of fuel added at your latest fill-up. Enter the current and previous odometer readings, fuel added in US gallons, and optionally the fuel price to calculate MPG and supporting mileage information.

What Is the Gas Mileage Calculator?

The Gas Mileage Calculator measures fuel economy over a driving period between two fill-ups. It first determines how far the vehicle traveled, then divides that distance by the fuel used to calculate miles per gallon (MPG).

It is useful for drivers who want to track real-world fuel efficiency, compare different driving periods, or monitor changes in vehicle fuel consumption. A fuel price can also be entered for an optional cost-per-distance estimate.

How to Use the Gas Mileage Calculator

Enter the following values:

Current odometer: Enter the vehicle’s odometer reading at the latest fill-up. For an MPG calculation, the reading should be in miles.

Previous odometer: Enter the odometer reading recorded at the previous fill-up. Both odometer readings must use the same distance unit.

Fuel added (US gal): Enter the number of US gallons required to refill the tank.

Fuel price per gallon (optional): Enter the price paid for one gallon of fuel if you want the calculator to include its optional cost-related result. Fuel price does not change the MPG calculation itself.

For the most useful tank-to-tank estimate, fill the tank consistently and record the odometer and fuel amount at each fill-up.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculation starts by finding the distance traveled:

Distance traveled = Current odometer − Previous odometer

Gas mileage is then calculated as:

MPG = Miles traveled ÷ US gallons used

This is the standard odometer method described by the U.S. Department of Energy and EPA: subtract the previous odometer reading from the current reading and divide the resulting miles by the gallons required to refill the tank.

For fuel economy expressed as liters per 100 kilometers, the documented metric relationship is:

L/100 km = (Liters used ÷ Kilometers traveled) × 100

Calculation Sequence

  1. Subtract the previous odometer reading from the current odometer reading.
  2. Use the difference as the distance traveled.
  3. Divide miles traveled by US gallons added to calculate MPG.
  4. If metric fuel-use values are being evaluated, divide liters used by kilometers traveled and multiply by 100 to obtain L/100 km.
  5. If a fuel price is provided, the calculator can include its optional cost-related supporting value.

Distance Traveled

Distance = Current odometer − Previous odometer

The current odometer must be greater than the previous reading for a positive driving distance.

MPG

MPG = Distance traveled ÷ Fuel added

A higher MPG means the vehicle traveled farther for each US gallon of fuel.

L/100 km

L/100 km = (Liters used ÷ Kilometers traveled) × 100

For L/100 km, a lower number represents lower fuel consumption per 100 kilometers.

Understanding Your Results

Distance traveled is the difference between the latest and previous odometer readings. It represents the driving distance covered during the fuel-use period.

MPG shows how many miles the vehicle traveled for each US gallon of fuel. For example, 26.4 MPG means approximately 26.4 miles were traveled per gallon during that measurement period.

L/100 km expresses fuel consumption in the opposite direction: it shows how many liters are required to travel 100 kilometers. Lower L/100 km values indicate lower fuel consumption.

Fuel cost per distance, when available after entering a fuel price, provides a cost-oriented view of the same driving period. Changing the fuel price affects cost information but does not change the measured MPG.

A tank-to-tank result represents your observed fuel economy for that period. It should not be treated as an official laboratory fuel-economy rating. EPA notes that real-world mileage can vary because of driving conditions, vehicle condition, fuel differences, and other factors.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator assumes that the odometer readings and fuel-added value accurately describe the same driving period.

For the best tank-to-tank estimate, the vehicle should be filled to a reasonably consistent level at both fill-ups. An incomplete refill can make the gallons added different from the fuel actually consumed during the measured distance.

Fuel added must be greater than zero because MPG cannot be calculated by dividing by zero. The current odometer must also exceed the previous odometer for a normal positive-distance calculation.

Real-world results may be affected by odometer accuracy, fuel-pump variation, tire size, idling, traffic, weather, vehicle load, maintenance, driving style, and changing road conditions. EPA fuel-economy ratings are produced using standardized testing, so a single observed fill-up may differ from the vehicle’s official rating.

For better long-term tracking, compare several fill-ups instead of relying on one unusually short or unusual driving period.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate gas mileage from odometer readings?

Subtract the previous odometer reading from the current reading to find miles traveled. Then divide those miles by the number of US gallons added at the latest fill-up.

Does gas price affect MPG?

No. MPG depends on distance traveled and fuel used. The optional fuel-price input affects cost information, not the vehicle’s calculated fuel efficiency.

Why is my calculated MPG different from my vehicle’s EPA rating?

Your result measures fuel economy during your actual driving period. EPA ratings come from standardized testing, while real-world mileage can change with traffic, weather, driving behavior, maintenance, vehicle load, and other conditions.

Should I fill the tank completely before calculating MPG?

Using consistent complete fill-ups generally gives a better tank-to-tank estimate because the amount added at the second fill-up more closely represents the fuel consumed since the previous fill-up. FuelEconomy.gov uses complete refills in its recommended odometer method.

Can I use a trip odometer instead?

A trip odometer can be used to determine the distance traveled between fill-ups. FuelEconomy.gov describes this as an alternative method: reset the trip odometer after filling the tank, then divide the recorded trip distance by the gallons added at the next refill.

What happens if I enter zero gallons?

MPG cannot be calculated with zero gallons because the formula requires dividing distance by fuel used. Enter a fuel amount greater than zero.

Is a single fill-up enough to measure my normal fuel economy?

It provides an estimate for that specific driving period. Tracking several fill-ups can give a more representative picture because individual tanks may be affected by different routes, weather, traffic, refill levels, and driving conditions.
