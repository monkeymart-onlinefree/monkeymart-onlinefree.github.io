What Is a Gas Mileage Calculator?

A gas mileage calculator measures fuel economy from distance traveled between fill-ups and the amount of fuel added at the later fill-up. In U.S. units, the result is miles per gallon (MPG). In metric use, fuel consumption is often shown as liters per 100 kilometers (L/100 km).

Gas Mileage Formula

Distance = Current odometer − Previous odometer
MPG = Miles traveled ÷ US gallons used
L/100 km = Liters used ÷ Kilometers traveled × 100

Example

If the odometer moves from 10,000 to 10,100 miles and the next fill-up adds 4 gallons, the measured fuel economy is 100 ÷ 4 = 25 MPG. That is approximately 9.41 L/100 km.

How to Get a Better Measurement

Record the odometer when filling the tank, then refill to a similar level at the next stop and record how much fuel the pump dispensed. Averaging several tanks helps smooth out differences caused by pump shutoff, traffic, weather, route, and driving conditions.

Limitations

Tank-to-tank MPG is an observed estimate, not a laboratory certification value. Odometer error, incomplete refills, fuel-pump variation, tire size, idling, and changing driving conditions can affect the result.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate MPG from fill-ups?

Subtract the previous odometer reading from the current reading to get distance traveled, then divide that distance by the gallons added at the next fill-up.

What is L/100 km?

Liters per 100 kilometers is a fuel-consumption measure. Lower values mean less fuel is used per 100 km.

Why should I use a full tank method?

Using consistent fill-ups reduces error because the fuel added is a better estimate of fuel consumed since the prior fill-up. Pump shutoff and tank angle can still introduce variation.
