What Is a Marriage Tax Calculator?

A marriage tax calculator compares an estimated federal income tax before marriage with an estimated married-filing-jointly tax after combining incomes. This page focuses on 2026 U.S. ordinary-income tax brackets and standard or user-entered itemized deductions so the core bracket effect is transparent.

How to Use the Marriage Tax Calculator

Enter each spouse’s ordinary income, pre-tax deductions, pre-marriage filing status, and optional itemized deductions. If itemized deductions are zero, the calculator uses the 2026 standard deduction for that status. Enter a joint itemized deduction or leave it at zero to use the 2026 married-filing-jointly standard deduction.

Marriage Tax Formula

Marriage difference = Estimated joint federal tax − (Spouse 1 pre-marriage tax + Spouse 2 pre-marriage tax)

A positive result is an estimated penalty under this limited model; a negative result is estimated savings. The tax function applies each 2026 marginal bracket progressively rather than multiplying all income by one rate.

2026 Assumptions

The calculator uses the IRS 2026 ordinary-income brackets. Standard deductions are $16,100 for single filers, $24,150 for heads of household, and $32,200 for married couples filing jointly.

Understanding Your Results

The comparison is most useful for studying how combined ordinary income moves through the federal brackets. It should not be treated as a complete tax return estimate.

Limitations

This page does not calculate child tax credits, earned income credit, education credits, qualified dividends, capital gains, self-employment tax, payroll tax, state/local tax, AMT, NIIT, phaseouts, or other special provisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a marriage tax penalty or bonus?

A simplified marriage tax difference compares the estimated federal income tax for two people under their pre-marriage filing statuses with an estimated married-filing-jointly tax. A positive difference is a penalty in this model; a negative difference is a bonus.

Does this include tax credits?

No. This calculator intentionally focuses on ordinary taxable income, deductions, and 2026 federal tax brackets. Credits, payroll tax, state tax, capital-gain rates, AMT, self-employment tax, and many special rules are outside the model.

Which tax year does this page use?

The page uses 2026 U.S. federal ordinary-income tax brackets and 2026 standard deductions published by the IRS.
