The Fat Intake Calculator estimates how many grams of total fat an adult may consume per day based on estimated energy needs. Enter your age, sex used by the equation, height, weight, and activity level to calculate maintenance calories, a 20–35% total-fat range, and a 10% saturated-fat reference.

What Is the Fat Intake Calculator?

The Fat Intake Calculator converts estimated daily calorie needs into a daily fat range in grams.

It first estimates resting energy expenditure with the Mifflin–St Jeor equation, adjusts that value for activity, and then calculates fat grams from the resulting maintenance calories.

For adults, the calculator uses 20–35% of total energy for its total-fat range. The National Academies lists 20–35% of energy as the adult Acceptable Macronutrient Distribution Range (AMDR) for fat.

This tool is intended for general adult nutrition planning. It does not provide an individualized diet prescription or medical diagnosis.

Formula / Calculation Method

Unit Conversion

If US units are selected, the calculator first converts weight and height to metric units:

Weight in kg = weight in lb × 0.45359237
Height in cm = height in inches × 2.54

Metric inputs are used directly.

Step 1: Estimate Resting Energy

The calculator uses the simplified Mifflin–St Jeor equation.

For males:

BMR = 10W + 6.25H − 5A + 5

For females:

BMR = 10W + 6.25H − 5A − 161

The original Mifflin study describes these simplified sex-specific equations for estimating resting energy expenditure.

Step 2: Estimate Maintenance Calories

Maintenance calories = BMR × Activity Factor

The selected activity factor increases the resting-energy estimate to approximate total daily energy needs.

Step 3: Calculate the Total Fat Range

The calculator applies the adult 20–35% energy range:

Minimum fat = Maintenance Calories × 0.20 ÷ 9
Maximum fat = Maintenance Calories × 0.35 ÷ 9

Dietary fat provides approximately 9 kcal per gram, which is why calories from fat are divided by 9 to convert them into grams.

Step 4: Calculate the Saturated-Fat Reference

The calculator also calculates:

Saturated fat reference = Maintenance Calories × 0.10 ÷ 9

WHO guidance recommends reducing saturated fatty acid intake to no more than 10% of total energy intake.

The calculator keeps decimal precision during the calculation and rounds the displayed maintenance calories and fat values to the nearest whole number.

Understanding Your Results

Estimated Total Fat per Day

This is the calculator's main result. It represents the amount of fat corresponding to 20–35% of your estimated daily maintenance calories.

It is shown as a range rather than one fixed number because the adult AMDR allows different macronutrient distributions within the 20–35% range.

Maintenance Calories

Maintenance calories are calculated from estimated resting energy multiplied by your selected activity factor.

This value is an estimate of daily energy needs rather than a directly measured energy expenditure.

Saturated Fat Reference

The saturated-fat result converts 10% of estimated maintenance calories into grams of saturated fat.

It is separate from the total-fat result. Total fat includes different types of dietary fat, while the saturated-fat value is shown as an upper reference point.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator uses predictive equations rather than direct measurements of energy expenditure. Actual calorie needs can differ because of body composition, exercise patterns, occupational activity, health status, pregnancy, medications, metabolic adaptation, and other individual factors.

Activity levels are represented by fixed multipliers. Two people who select the same activity category can still have different actual energy requirements.

The calculator accepts ages 18–80. The original Mifflin study used healthy adults aged 19–78, so results near or beyond that study population should be interpreted as estimates.

The 20–35% calculation concerns total dietary fat. It does not calculate individual requirements for monounsaturated fat, polyunsaturated fat, omega-3 fatty acids, or trans fat.

The tool also does not account for disease-specific diets, pregnancy or breastfeeding requirements, eating-disorder treatment, or clinician-prescribed nutrition plans. People who need individualized nutrition care should use professional guidance rather than treating this estimate as a prescription.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Fat Intake Calculator calculate grams of fat?

It estimates maintenance calories from the Mifflin–St Jeor equation and an activity factor. It then multiplies calories by 20% and 35% and divides each value by 9 kcal per gram to produce the total-fat range.

Why does the calculator divide calories by 9?

Dietary fat provides approximately 9 kcal per gram. Dividing calories allocated to fat by 9 converts those calories into grams of dietary fat.

Why does the calculator give a range instead of one fat target?

The calculator uses the adult AMDR of 20–35% of total energy from fat. Different calorie percentages within this range produce different gram values.

What does the saturated-fat result mean?

It represents 10% of estimated maintenance calories converted into grams of saturated fat. It is a separate reference from the total-fat range. WHO recommends limiting saturated fatty acids to no more than 10% of total energy.

Does increasing my activity level increase the fat range?

Yes. A higher activity factor increases estimated maintenance calories. Because the fat range is calculated as a percentage of those calories, the estimated gram range also increases.

Can I use pounds and inches?

Yes. Select US units. The calculator converts pounds to kilograms and inches to centimeters internally before applying the energy equation.

Is the result a personalized diet recommendation?

No. It is an estimate based on body measurements, age, sex used by the equation, activity level, and general adult dietary reference ranges. Medical conditions and individual nutrition needs may require different targets.
