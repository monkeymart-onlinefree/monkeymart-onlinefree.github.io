What Is a Fat Intake Calculator?

A fat intake calculator estimates a daily fat range from your energy needs. This page first estimates resting energy expenditure, adjusts it for activity, then converts 20–35% of calories into grams of fat. The result is a planning estimate, not a prescribed diet.

How to Use the Fat Intake Calculator

Enter age, sex, height, weight, activity level, and your preferred unit system. The calculator estimates maintenance energy and then displays an adult total-fat range plus a saturated-fat reference limit.

Fat Intake Formula

The core conversion is fat grams = calories × fat percentage ÷ 9. Adults have an AMDR of 20–35% of energy from fat. Resting energy is estimated with the Mifflin–St Jeor equation and then multiplied by an activity factor.

Formula Variables

Calories are estimated daily energy needs; fat percentage is 0.20 to 0.35; and 9 is the approximate kilocalories supplied by one gram of dietary fat.

Calculation Example

At 2,000 kcal/day, 20–35% of energy from fat equals about 44–78 g/day: 2,000 × 0.20 ÷ 9 ≈ 44 g and 2,000 × 0.35 ÷ 9 ≈ 78 g.

Understanding Your Results

The range is intentionally broad because healthy dietary patterns can vary in macronutrient distribution. Food quality still matters: the number alone does not distinguish unsaturated, saturated, or trans fat.

Limitations

Energy equations and activity multipliers are population estimates. This calculator is designed for adults and does not replace individualized nutrition care.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many grams of fat should I eat per day?

For adults, the National Academies’ AMDR for total fat is 20–35% of daily energy. Because fat provides about 9 kcal per gram, the gram range depends on your calorie target.

How does the calculator turn calories into fat grams?

It multiplies estimated daily calories by 20% and 35%, then divides each result by 9 kcal per gram of fat.

What is the saturated fat limit shown here?

The calculator shows 10% of calories as an upper reference point for saturated fat and converts that energy amount to grams.

Is this a meal plan?

No. It is an energy-based estimate. Individual needs can differ with medical conditions, pregnancy, athletic training, medications, and clinician-directed diets.

Sources and Methodology

This page uses the formulas and assumptions described above. Health-related results are estimates and should be interpreted in context, not as a diagnosis or individualized medical advice.
