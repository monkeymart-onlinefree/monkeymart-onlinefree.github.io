What Is an Average Return Calculator?

An average return calculator summarizes investment performance. This page provides a geometric average for a series of periodic returns and a money-weighted annualized return for dated cash flows.

Return Formulas

The arithmetic mean is the sum of period returns divided by the number of periods. The geometric mean compounds each growth factor and then takes the nth root: [(1+r1)(1+r2)…(1+rn)]^(1/n) − 1. The dated cash-flow mode solves for the annual rate that makes discounted cash flows sum to zero.

Example

Returns of 10%, −5%, 12%, and 8% do not compound to the same answer as their arithmetic average. The geometric average captures the compounding path and is therefore useful for multi-period growth comparisons.

Arithmetic vs Geometric Average

Arithmetic mean describes the simple average of observations. Geometric mean reflects compound growth across periods. For volatile returns, geometric average is usually lower than arithmetic average because losses and gains compound asymmetrically.

Limitations

This calculator does not adjust for inflation, taxes, fees, benchmark risk, volatility, or irregular multiple cash flows beyond the single optional intermediate flow provided. Past returns do not guarantee future performance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is geometric average usually lower than arithmetic average?

Volatility creates a compounding drag. A gain and an equal percentage loss do not cancel because they apply to different bases. Geometric averaging captures this compounding effect, while arithmetic averaging does not.

What is a money-weighted return?

A money-weighted return accounts for the timing and size of deposits and withdrawals. It solves for the annualized rate that makes the present value of all dated cash flows equal to zero.

Can average return predict future performance?

No. Historical average return is a description of past observations under a chosen method. Future returns can differ significantly and can be negative.
