The GCF Calculator finds the greatest common factor of two or more integers entered as comma-separated values. It uses the Euclidean algorithm to determine the largest positive integer that divides every entered number without a remainder.

What Is the GCF Calculator?

A GCF Calculator finds the greatest common factor (GCF) shared by a set of integers. GCF is also commonly called the greatest common divisor (GCD) or highest common factor (HCF).

The GCF is the largest positive integer that divides every number in the set exactly. For example, the GCF of 12 and 18 is 6 because 6 divides both numbers and no larger positive integer does.

This calculator is useful when simplifying numerical relationships, working with factors, or finding a common divisor for several integers.

How to Use the GCF Calculator

  1. Enter integers: Type two or more whole integers separated by commas, such as 48, 18, 30.
  2. Calculate: Run the calculator after entering the numbers.
  3. Read the result: The calculated GCF is the largest positive integer that divides all entered values without a remainder.

The calculator is designed for integers rather than decimal values.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator uses the Euclidean algorithm:

gcd⁡(a,b)=gcd⁡(b,  a mod b)\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(b,\\;a\bmod b)

The operation is repeated until the remainder becomes zero. The last nonzero divisor is the greatest common factor. This repeated-division method is a standard way to calculate the GCD of two integers.

The stopping relationship can be written as:

gcd⁡(a,0)=∣a∣\gcd(a,0)=|a|

Calculation Sequence

For two integers, the calculation follows these steps:

  1. Divide the larger/current first integer by the second integer.
  2. Find the remainder using the modulo operation.
  3. Replace the current pair with the second integer and the remainder.
  4. Repeat the process until the remainder is zero.
  5. The last nonzero value is the GCF.

For more than two integers, the GCF operation can be applied successively:

gcd⁡(a,b,c)=gcd⁡(gcd⁡(a,b),c)\gcd(a,b,c)=\gcd(\gcd(a,b),c)

The same process can continue for additional integers. This property allows a common divisor to be calculated across an entire set of numbers.

Worked Example

Find the GCF of:

84,  30,  1884,\\;30,\\;18

Inputs

  • First integer: 84
  • Second integer: 30
  • Third integer: 18

Step 1: Find the GCF of 84 and 30

84 mod 30=2484\bmod30=24

Therefore:

gcd⁡(84,30)=gcd⁡(30,24)\gcd(84,30)=\gcd(30,24)

Continue:

30 mod 24=630\bmod24=6
gcd⁡(30,24)=gcd⁡(24,6)\gcd(30,24)=\gcd(24,6)

Then:

24 mod 6=024\bmod6=0

So:

gcd⁡(84,30)=6\gcd(84,30)=6

Step 2: Include the Third Integer

Now calculate:

gcd⁡(6,18)=6\gcd(6,18)=6

because 18 is exactly divisible by 6.

Final Result

GCF=6\boxed{\text{GCF}=6}

The result can be checked directly:

84÷6=1484\div6=14
30÷6=530\div6=5
18÷6=318\div6=3

Therefore, 6 divides every input without a remainder.

Understanding Your Results

The GCF result represents the largest positive integer that is a factor of every number you entered.

For example, if the result is 6, every input is divisible by 6. Smaller common factors may also exist, but no common factor larger than 6 divides every input exactly.

A GCF of 1 means the numbers have no common positive factor greater than 1. Such integers are described as relatively prime or coprime with respect to their common divisor.

Assumptions and Limitations

The GCF Calculator is intended for integer calculations. Decimal values are outside the intended input type.

The GCF is treated as a positive common divisor. Negative signs therefore do not change the magnitude of the greatest common factor.

For the standard mathematical definition, the set of integers should not consist entirely of zeros. A greatest common divisor for a set of integers not all equal to zero is well defined.

The Euclidean algorithm uses exact integer remainder operations, so normal decimal rounding is not part of the mathematical GCF calculation.

This calculator provides a numerical calculation and should not be treated as a symbolic mathematical proof.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does GCF mean?

GCF means greatest common factor. It is the largest positive integer that divides every number in a set without leaving a remainder.

Are GCF and GCD the same?

Yes. Greatest common factor (GCF) and greatest common divisor (GCD) refer to the same mathematical concept. Highest common factor (HCF) is another commonly used name.

How does the calculator find the GCF?

It uses the Euclidean algorithm, repeatedly replacing a pair of integers with the second integer and the remainder from dividing the first by the second until the remainder becomes zero.

Can I find the GCF of more than two numbers?

Yes. The calculator accepts two or more comma-separated integers. The GCF operation can be applied successively to determine the common factor shared by the full set.

What does a GCF of 1 mean?

A GCF of 1 means there is no positive integer greater than 1 that divides all the entered numbers.

Can the GCF be negative?

The greatest common factor is conventionally expressed as a positive integer. Negative input signs do not change the magnitude of the common factors.

Does the GCF calculation require rounding?

No. The calculation works with integers and remainders, so the mathematical GCF itself does not require decimal rounding.
