What Is a Payback Period Calculator?

A payback period calculator estimates how long it takes an investment or project to recover its initial cash outlay from net cash inflows. It is often used as a simple capital-budgeting screening measure.

Simple Payback Formula

Payback Period = Initial Investment ÷ Annual Net Cash Flow

OpenStax uses this formula when net annual cash flows are even from period to period. For example, a $100,000 investment producing $25,000 per year has a simple payback of 4 years.

Discounted Payback

Discounted payback recognizes the time value of money. This calculator discounts each future year’s cash flow at the entered rate, accumulates those present values, and interpolates within the year in which the investment is recovered.

Key Limitation

Simple payback ignores cash flows after the payback point and does not measure total profitability. Even discounted payback remains a recovery-time metric, so it should be considered alongside NPV, IRR, ROI, risk, and strategic factors.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the payback period?

The payback period is the time needed for cumulative project cash inflows to recover the initial investment.

What is the simple payback formula?

When net cash inflow is constant each year, simple payback equals the initial investment divided by annual net cash flow.

Why calculate discounted payback?

Simple payback ignores the time value of money. Discounted payback first reduces future cash flows to present value using a discount rate before measuring when the initial investment is recovered.
