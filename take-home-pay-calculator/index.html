Use the Take Home Pay Calculator to estimate your 2026 U.S. net pay after federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, pretax deductions, an estimated state/local tax rate, tax credits, and post-tax payroll deductions. Enter your annual gross salary, filing status, pay frequency, and deductions to see both annual and per-paycheck results.

What Is the Take Home Pay Calculator?

The Take Home Pay Calculator estimates how much of your gross salary remains after common employee taxes and payroll deductions.

It is designed for salary and paycheck planning. The calculator uses 2026 federal income-tax rules, separates deductions that affect federal income tax from deductions that also reduce FICA wages, and lets you enter your own state/local tax estimate because those rules vary by jurisdiction.

The result is an estimate rather than an exact payroll withholding calculation.

How to Use the Take Home Pay Calculator

Enter the information shown in the calculator:

Annual gross salary: Enter your total salary before taxes and payroll deductions.

Pay frequency: Select how often you are paid. The calculator uses this to convert annual results into a per-paycheck estimate and to account for post-tax deductions entered per paycheck.

Filing status: Select the federal filing status that applies to your estimate. Filing status determines the federal standard deduction and tax brackets used.

Income-tax deduction: Choose the available standard or custom deduction option.

Custom deduction amount: If you choose a custom deduction, enter the annual amount that should reduce estimated federal taxable income.

Pretax retirement deductions / year: Enter annual pretax retirement salary deferrals. Traditional 401(k)-type salary deferrals generally reduce federal income-taxable wages but generally remain subject to Social Security and Medicare taxes.

Payroll deductions that reduce federal and FICA wages / year: Enter qualifying payroll deductions that reduce both the federal income-tax wage base and FICA wage base.

State/local income tax estimate (%): Enter an estimated combined state/local income-tax percentage. The calculator does not reproduce every state, city, county, or local tax rule.

Federal tax credits / year: Enter the annual federal tax credits you want included in the estimate. The calculator uses this as a simplified adjustment to estimated federal income tax.

Post-tax deductions per paycheck: Enter deductions taken after taxes from each paycheck.

Formula / Calculation Method

The calculator works primarily with annual amounts and then converts the final estimate to the selected pay frequency.

Main Formula

Let:

  • G = annual gross salary
  • N = number of paychecks represented by the selected pay frequency
  • R = pretax retirement deductions per year
  • F = payroll deductions that reduce federal and FICA wages per year
  • D = federal income-tax deduction used
  • C = entered federal tax credits per year
  • P = post-tax deductions per paycheck
  • FIT = estimated federal income tax
  • SS = Social Security tax
  • MED = Medicare taxes
  • SL = estimated state/local income tax

The estimated federal taxable income is:

Federal taxable income = max(0, G − R − F − D)

The calculator then applies the applicable 2026 progressive federal tax schedule for the selected filing status. Federal tax credits entered in the calculator reduce the estimated federal tax result.

The wages used for Social Security and Medicare are calculated separately because traditional pretax retirement salary deferrals generally remain subject to FICA:

FICA wages = max(0, G − F)

Estimated take-home pay is based on:

Annual take-home pay = G − FIT − SS − MED − SL − R − F − (P × N)

The per-paycheck estimate is then:

Take-home pay per paycheck = Annual take-home pay ÷ N

Calculation Sequence

  1. Read the annual gross salary.
  2. Determine the applicable standard or custom income-tax deduction.
  3. Subtract applicable pretax retirement and payroll deductions to estimate federal taxable income.
  4. Apply the 2026 progressive federal tax brackets for the selected filing status.
  5. Apply the entered federal tax-credit adjustment.
  6. Calculate wages subject to Social Security and Medicare separately.
  7. Calculate Social Security tax.
  8. Calculate Medicare taxes.
  9. Include the state/local tax estimate entered by the user.
  10. Subtract pretax and post-tax payroll deductions.
  11. Convert the annual result to the selected pay frequency.

Federal Income Tax

Federal income tax is progressive. Only the income falling within each bracket is taxed at that bracket's rate; the highest marginal rate does not apply to all taxable income.

The calculator uses 2026 federal tax parameters.

Social Security Tax

For 2026, the employee Social Security tax rate is 6.2%, and the Social Security taxable wage base is $184,500.

For wages within the limit:

SS = 6.2% × Social Security wages

Wages above the $184,500 annual Social Security wage base are not subject to additional employee Social Security tax.

Medicare Tax

The regular employee Medicare tax rate is 1.45%, with no annual Medicare wage-base limit. Additional Medicare Tax may apply above the relevant income threshold.

Pretax Deductions

The calculator distinguishes between:

  • retirement deductions that reduce estimated federal taxable income but generally remain subject to Social Security and Medicare; and
  • qualifying payroll deductions entered as reducing both federal and FICA wages.

This separation is important because not every pretax payroll deduction receives the same tax treatment.

Effective Total Deduction Rate

The effective total deduction rate shows the percentage of gross salary that does not remain as estimated take-home pay:

Effective deduction rate = (Gross salary − Take-home pay) ÷ Gross salary × 100

It combines the overall effect of the taxes and deductions included in the calculator rather than showing your marginal federal tax rate.

Worked Example

Assume the following inputs.

Inputs

  • Annual gross salary: $80,000
  • Pay frequency: Monthly — 12 paychecks
  • Filing status: Single
  • Income-tax deduction: Standard deduction
  • Pretax retirement deductions: $6,000/year
  • Payroll deductions reducing federal and FICA wages: $2,400/year
  • State/local tax estimate: 0%
  • Federal tax credits: $0
  • Post-tax deductions: $50/paycheck

Step 1: Calculate Federal Taxable Income

The 2026 standard deduction for a single filer is $16,100.

Federal taxable income:

$80,000 − $6,000 − $2,400 − $16,100 = $55,500

Step 2: Calculate Federal Income Tax

For a single filer in 2026:

  • First 12,400at10%=1,240
  • Next 38,000at12%=4,560
  • Remaining 5,100at22%=1,122

Estimated federal income tax:

$1,240 + $4,560 + $1,122 = $6,922

The 2026 single-filer bracket thresholds used here are published by the IRS.

Step 3: Calculate FICA Wages

The $6,000 retirement contribution reduces federal taxable income but remains in FICA wages in this example.

The separate $2,400 payroll deduction reduces FICA wages:

$80,000 − $2,400 = $77,600

Step 4: Calculate Social Security

$77,600 × 6.2% = $4,811.20

The amount is below the 2026 Social Security wage base of $184,500.

Step 5: Calculate Medicare

$77,600 × 1.45% = $1,125.20

No Additional Medicare Tax applies at this income level.

Step 6: Calculate Payroll Deductions

Total pretax deductions removed from gross pay:

$6,000 + $2,400 = $8,400

Annual post-tax deductions:

$50 × 12 = $600

Step 7: Calculate Take-Home Pay

Annual take-home pay:

$80,000 − $6,922 − $4,811.20 − $1,125.20 − $8,400 − $600 = $58,141.60

Final Result

Estimated annual take-home pay: $58,141.60
Estimated monthly take-home pay: $4,845.13
Gross monthly pay: $6,666.67
Effective total deduction rate: approximately 27.32%

The result is a planning estimate. An employer's actual payroll withholding can differ because payroll systems use Form W-4 information and IRS withholding procedures rather than only an annual tax-liability calculation.

Understanding Your Results

Take-home pay: The estimated amount left after the taxes and deductions included in the calculator.

Federal income tax: Estimated annual federal ordinary income tax based on the selected filing status, deduction, 2026 brackets, and entered federal tax credits.

Social Security tax: Employee Social Security tax based on eligible FICA wages, subject to the annual Social Security wage base.

Medicare taxes: Regular Medicare tax plus any Additional Medicare Tax included by the calculation where applicable.

State/local estimate: A planning estimate based on the percentage you enter. Actual state and local tax calculations may use different deductions, exemptions, brackets, wage definitions, or credits.

Pretax deductions: Amounts deducted from gross pay before the final take-home amount. Different pretax deductions can have different federal and FICA tax treatment.

Effective total deduction rate: The percentage difference between gross salary and estimated take-home pay after the taxes and deductions included in the calculator.

2026 FICA Parameters

For employees in 2026:

  • Social Security rate: 6.2%
  • Social Security wage base: $184,500
  • Medicare rate: 1.45%
  • Medicare wage base: No annual maximum
  • Additional Medicare Tax rate: 0.9% when applicable

The IRS annual Additional Medicare Tax thresholds are $250,000 for married filing jointly, $125,000 for married filing separately, and $200,000 for other filing statuses. Employers, however, are generally required to begin withholding the additional 0.9% after an individual employee's Medicare wages exceed $200,000, regardless of filing status. This is one reason an annual estimate can differ from an actual paycheck.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator is intended for planning, not tax filing.

It uses 2026 federal tax parameters but does not calculate every federal deduction, credit, payroll exception, benefit rule, or special tax situation.

State and local income tax is entered as an estimate because state and local systems can use different brackets, deductions, exemptions, credits, and definitions of taxable wages.

Traditional pretax retirement salary deferrals and other payroll benefits do not always receive the same FICA treatment. Enter deductions in the appropriate calculator field.

Actual paycheck withholding may differ because employers generally calculate federal withholding using Form W-4 information, payroll-period withholding methods, payroll software, year-to-date wages, benefit treatment, and other payroll information. IRS Publication 15-T provides the federal withholding methods used for payroll.

The calculator does not calculate self-employment tax and should not be used as a substitute for a tax return, payroll system, employer pay statement, or individualized tax advice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this calculator use 2026 federal tax brackets?

Yes. The federal tax calculation is based on IRS tax year 2026 rate schedules and standard-deduction amounts.

Why does the calculated take-home pay differ from my actual paycheck?

Actual withholding depends on your Form W-4, year-to-date payroll, employer payroll software, benefit treatment, additional income, credits, deductions, and state or local rules. The IRS uses specific payroll withholding methods that can produce a different paycheck amount from a simplified annual estimate.

Are traditional 401(k) contributions subject to Social Security and Medicare taxes?

Generally, traditional employee elective 401(k) deferrals reduce federal income-taxable wages but remain included in wages subject to Social Security and Medicare taxes.

What is the difference between the standard and custom deduction?

The standard option uses the applicable 2026 standard deduction for the selected filing status. A custom deduction lets you enter the annual deduction amount used for the calculator's simplified federal taxable-income estimate.

Does pay frequency change my annual federal tax estimate?

The underlying annual salary and annual tax calculation remain the main basis of the estimate. Pay frequency is primarily used to express results per paycheck and to account for deductions entered on a per-paycheck basis.

Does the calculator calculate my exact state income tax?

No. You enter a state/local percentage as a planning estimate. Actual state and local income-tax systems may have progressive brackets, exemptions, deductions, credits, special wage rules, or no individual income tax at all.

Should I use this calculator instead of the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator?

Use this calculator for a quick take-home-pay estimate. For a more detailed federal withholding review based on your Form W-4 situation and current pay information, use the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator.
