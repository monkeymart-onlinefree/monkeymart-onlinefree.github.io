What Is Take Home Pay?

Take home pay is the amount of earnings left after taxes and payroll deductions. This calculator estimates 2026 U.S. net pay from annual W-2 wages using federal income-tax brackets, a standard or custom deduction, Social Security, Medicare, an optional state/local rate, and deductions you enter.

2026 Federal Income Tax Method

The calculator first reduces wages by the entered pretax items and your selected deduction. It then applies the 2026 progressive federal tax brackets for your filing status. A tax credit input is subtracted from the resulting federal tax estimate, but not below zero.

Social Security and Medicare

For 2026, the employee Social Security rate is 6.2% up to the $184,500 wage base. The employee Medicare rate is 1.45% with no wage cap. An additional 0.9% Medicare tax can apply above filing-status thresholds.

Why Your Actual Paycheck May Differ

Payroll withholding under Form W-4 is not simply annual tax liability divided by the number of paychecks. Withholding can change with multiple jobs, dependents, other income, deductions, credits, supplemental wages, benefit tax treatment, and employer payroll timing. State and local systems also vary widely, so this page lets you enter a flat estimate rather than pretending one rule applies everywhere.

Limitations

This tool is intended for planning, not tax filing. It does not calculate every credit, deduction, payroll exception, self-employment tax, benefit rule, or state/local tax provision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this calculator use 2026 federal tax brackets?

Yes. The federal bracket and standard-deduction amounts are based on IRS tax year 2026 guidance. The page is designed as an annual estimate and not a substitute for the IRS withholding estimator.

Why does my paycheck not exactly match this result?

Actual payroll withholding depends on Form W-4 entries, payroll software, benefit treatment, pay-period timing, credits, other income, and state or local rules. This calculator simplifies those items into planning inputs.

Are 401(k) contributions subject to Social Security and Medicare?

Traditional 401(k) salary deferrals generally reduce federal income-taxable wages but are generally still included in Social Security and Medicare wages. That is why this calculator separates retirement deductions from deductions entered as FICA-exempt.
