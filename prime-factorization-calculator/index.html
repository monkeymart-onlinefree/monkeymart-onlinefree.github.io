What is prime factorization?

Prime factorization expresses an integer greater than 1 as a product of prime numbers. For example, 60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5, or 2² × 3 × 5. The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic states that this prime decomposition is unique apart from the order of the factors.

How the calculator finds prime factors

The calculator repeatedly divides by the smallest possible factor. It first removes all factors of 2, then tests odd divisors while the divisor squared is no greater than the remaining number. If the remainder is greater than 1 at the end, that remainder is prime.

n = p₁a₁ × p₂a₂ × … × pₖaₖ

How to find the number of divisors

If n = p₁a₁ × p₂a₂ × …, the number of positive divisors is (a₁+1)(a₂+1)… . For 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5, the divisor count is (3+1)(2+1)(1+1) = 24.

What is a factor tree?

A factor tree repeatedly splits a composite number into factors until every branch ends at a prime. Different valid trees can branch differently, but the prime leaves multiply to the same unique prime factorization.

Limitations

Trial division is simple and reliable for modest numbers but not efficient for cryptographic-scale integers. This browser calculator caps input at one trillion so the interface remains responsive.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 1 prime?

No. Prime numbers are greater than 1 and have exactly two positive divisors.

What if the input itself is prime?

The prime factorization is simply the number itself, such as 17 = 17.

Why are exponents useful?

They compress repeated factors. For example, 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 is written 2³ × 3².

Can a number have two different prime factorizations?

No, not when factor order is ignored. Unique prime factorization is the core statement of the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.
