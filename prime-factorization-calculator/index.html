The Prime Factorization Calculator breaks a whole number from 2 to 1 trillion into its prime factors. Enter one integer to see its prime factors, exponent form, factor pairs, number of positive divisors, and a visual factor tree.

What Is the Prime Factorization Calculator?

A prime factorization calculator expresses a whole number as a product of prime numbers. For example, 360 can be written as 2³ × 3² × 5.

Every integer greater than 1 has a unique prime factorization, apart from the order in which the prime factors are written. DLMF

This tool is useful for checking factorization work and for finding information such as the prime-power form and number of positive divisors.

How to Use the Prime Factorization Calculator

Enter a whole number from 2 to 1,000,000,000,000 (1 trillion).

The calculator processes that number and returns its prime factorization along with supporting values. Decimal numbers, negative numbers, 0, and 1 are outside the calculator's supported input range.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

If a whole number n has distinct prime factors p1,p2,,pk, its prime factorization can be written as:

n=i=1kpiai

or:

n=p1a1p2a2pkak

This prime-power representation is unique for every integer greater than 1. DLMF

Variables

  • n = the whole number being factorized
  • pᵢ = a distinct prime factor of n
  • aᵢ = the positive integer exponent showing how many times pᵢ occurs
  • k = the number of distinct prime factors

These values are dimensionless because prime factorization operates on whole numbers rather than measured quantities.

Calculation Sequence

The calculator uses trial division:

  1. Start with the entered whole number.
  2. Divide out factors of 2 repeatedly while the number remains divisible by 2.
  3. Test odd divisors in increasing order.
  4. For each divisor that divides evenly, repeatedly divide it out and record how many times it occurs.
  5. Continue testing while the square of the trial divisor is less than or equal to the remaining number.
  6. If a value greater than 1 remains, that remainder is a prime factor.

Checking potential divisors only up to the square root is sufficient because a composite integer must have a factor at or below its square root. PrimePages

Exponent Form

Repeated prime factors are grouped using exponents.

For example:

360=222335

becomes:

360=23325

Number of Positive Divisors

For:

n=p1a1p2a2pkak

the number of positive divisors is:

d(n)=i=1k(ai+1)

Each exponent is increased by 1, and the resulting values are multiplied. This works because a divisor can contain each prime factor with an exponent from 0 through its exponent in n. UofT Mathematics Department

Worked Example

Factorize 360.

Inputs

Whole number:

n=360

Step 1

Repeatedly divide by 2:

3602=180 1802=90 902=45

Therefore, 2 occurs three times:

23

Step 2

Now divide 45 by 3:

453=15 153=5

Therefore, 3 occurs twice:

32

Step 3

The remaining value is 5, which is prime.

The complete factorization is therefore:

360=23325

For the number of positive divisors:

d(360)=(3+1)(2+1)(1+1) d(360)=432=24

Final Result

Prime factors: 2, 3, 5
Prime factorization: 23325
Number of positive divisors: 24

Understanding Your Results

Prime factors are the prime numbers that multiply together to produce the original number. A repeated prime still appears only once in the list of distinct prime factors.

Exponent form groups repeated factors. An exponent tells you how many times that prime occurs in the factorization.

Factor pairs show pairs of whole numbers whose product equals the entered number.

Number of divisors is the total count of positive whole-number divisors. It is calculated from the exponents in the prime factorization.

Factor tree provides a visual way to break the original number into smaller factors until the branches end in prime numbers.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator accepts only whole numbers from 2 through 1 trillion. It does not factorize decimals, negative numbers, 0, or 1 within its supported input range.

It uses trial division, which is simple and reliable for the calculator's supported range. Trial division becomes inefficient for extremely large integers, so this calculator is not designed for cryptographic-scale factorization.

Because prime factorization deals with exact integers, there is no measurement uncertainty or decimal rounding involved in the factorization itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is prime factorization?

Prime factorization is the process of expressing an integer greater than 1 as a product of prime numbers. For example, 60=2235.

Why does the calculator stop testing divisors at the square root?

If a number is composite, it must have at least one factor no greater than its square root. Therefore, if no valid divisor is found by that point, a remaining value greater than 1 is prime. PrimePages

Can I enter 1 in the calculator?

No. This calculator's supported input range begins at 2. The number 1 does not have a prime factorization.

What happens if I enter a prime number?

A prime number cannot be split into smaller positive prime factors. Its prime factorization is therefore the prime number itself with exponent 1, such as 17=171.

How is the number of divisors calculated?

If n=p1a1p2a2pkak, its number of positive divisors is (a1+1)(a2+1)(ak+1). UofT Mathematics Department

What is the largest number I can enter?

The calculator supports whole numbers up to 1,000,000,000,000, or 1 trillion.

Are prime factors and factor pairs the same thing?

No. Prime factors are prime numbers used to build the original number. Factor pairs are pairs of positive integers whose product equals the original number.
