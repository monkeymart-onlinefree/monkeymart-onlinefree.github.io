The TDEE Calculator estimates your total daily energy expenditure, or the amount of energy your body may use in a day. It uses your age, sex used by the equation, height, weight, and activity level to estimate resting energy with the Mifflin–St Jeor equation and then adjust it for activity.

What Is the TDEE Calculator?

A TDEE calculator estimates how much energy you use across a typical day rather than only while resting.

The calculator first estimates resting energy from your age, height, weight, and sex used by the equation. It then multiplies that result by an activity factor associated with your selected activity level.

Total energy expenditure includes energy used at rest, through physical activity, and through processes such as digestion. This calculator uses the simpler resting energy × activity factor approach to estimate that daily total.

It can be useful for understanding approximate daily energy needs, but the result is an estimate rather than a direct measurement of metabolism.

How to Use the TDEE Calculator

Enter the information requested by the calculator:

Units: Select the unit system you want to use for your measurements.

Age: Enter your age in years. Age is included in the resting-energy equation.

Sex used by equation: Select the applicable equation option. The Mifflin–St Jeor calculation uses different constants for males and females.

Height: Enter your height using the units displayed by the calculator. The Mifflin–St Jeor formula itself uses centimeters.

Weight: Enter your body weight using the selected units. The underlying equation uses kilograms.

Activity level: Select the option that best represents your usual activity. The calculator uses the activity factor associated with that selection when estimating TDEE.

After entering the required information, the calculator estimates your resting energy and applies the activity adjustment to produce your daily TDEE estimate.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator uses:

TDEE = REE × AF

where REE is the estimated resting energy expenditure and AF is the activity factor applied for the selected activity level.

Resting energy is estimated with the Mifflin–St Jeor equation.

For Males

REE = 10W + 6.25H − 5A + 5

For Females

REE = 10W + 6.25H − 5A − 161

The simplified versions above were published with the original Mifflin–St Jeor research and use weight, height, and age to predict resting energy expenditure.

Variables

Calculation Sequence

  1. The calculator takes age, height, weight, and the selected sex equation.
  2. Weight and height are used in the units required by the Mifflin–St Jeor equation: kilograms and centimeters.
  3. The appropriate male or female resting-energy equation is applied.
  4. The calculator identifies the activity factor associated with the selected activity level.
  5. Estimated resting energy is multiplied by that activity factor:
TDEE = REE × AF

The resulting value is an estimate of daily energy expenditure.

Worked Example

Consider an example with these inputs:

Inputs

  • Age: 30 years
  • Sex used by equation: Male
  • Height: 175 cm
  • Weight: 75 kg
  • Activity level: Selected in the calculator

Step 1: Estimate Resting Energy

Using the male Mifflin–St Jeor equation:

REE = 10W + 6.25H − 5A + 5

Substitute the example values:

REE = 10(75) + 6.25(175) − 5(30) + 5
REE = 750 + 1,093.75 − 150 + 5
REE = 1,698.75 kcal/day

Step 2: Apply the Activity Factor

The calculator then uses the factor associated with the selected activity level:

TDEE = 1,698.75 × AF

Final Result

The final numeric TDEE depends on the activity factor configured for the activity level selected in the calculator.

This example does not assign an unsupported activity multiplier, so the resting-energy calculation remains exactly aligned with the documented Mifflin–St Jeor method.

Understanding Your Results

Estimated TDEE

Your TDEE is the calculator's estimate of how much energy you may expend during a typical day after activity is considered.

A higher activity factor produces a higher TDEE estimate because more daily activity generally increases energy expenditure.

TDEE should be treated as a starting estimate rather than an exact calorie requirement. Actual daily expenditure can change from one day to another.

Estimated Resting Energy

The resting-energy calculation represents the amount of energy predicted from your age, height, weight, and sex equation before the activity adjustment is applied.

It is calculated rather than directly measured. The original Mifflin–St Jeor equation was developed from measurements in 498 adults aged 19–78 years.

Supporting Values

Any supporting values displayed with the result help explain how the calculator arrived at the final estimate, particularly the resting-energy and activity components used in the TDEE calculation.

Assumptions and Limitations

The TDEE result is a prediction, not a direct measurement of your energy expenditure.

The Mifflin–St Jeor equation uses age, body weight, height, and sex, but people with similar values can still have different actual energy expenditure because of differences in body composition, spontaneous movement, training habits, and other individual factors.

Activity level is also an estimate. A category selected from a calculator cannot capture every difference in occupational activity, exercise intensity, exercise duration, or day-to-day movement.

The original Mifflin–St Jeor study was based on adults aged 19–78 years, so pediatric energy needs should not be assumed to follow the same method.

Pregnancy, lactation, growth, and other life stages can require different energy-estimation methods. The National Academies' 2023 Dietary Reference Intakes for Energy use separate approaches for children, adolescents, pregnancy, and lactation rather than treating them as ordinary adult energy expenditure calculations.

Medical conditions and medications can also affect energy requirements. A calculator result should not be treated as a medical diagnosis or individualized nutrition prescription.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does TDEE mean?

TDEE stands for total daily energy expenditure. It represents the total amount of energy a person uses during a day.

Which formula does this TDEE calculator use?

It uses the Mifflin–St Jeor equation to estimate resting energy and then multiplies that estimate by an activity factor:

TDEE = estimated resting energy × activity factor.
Is TDEE the same as resting energy?

No. Resting energy estimates energy expenditure before the activity adjustment. TDEE applies an activity factor to estimate total daily expenditure.

Why does activity level change my TDEE?

Physical activity contributes to total energy expenditure. Selecting a higher activity level applies a larger activity adjustment within the calculator's TDEE calculation.

Is a TDEE calculator completely accurate?

No. It produces an estimate from predictive equations and an activity classification. Individual metabolism and real-world activity can differ from the values predicted by a formula.

Can children use this TDEE equation?

The Mifflin–St Jeor equation used here was developed from adult data. Children and adolescents have different considerations, including energy required for growth.

Should this calculator be used during pregnancy?

Pregnancy involves additional energy requirements that vary by life stage. Current energy-reference guidance uses pregnancy-specific methods, so a standard adult TDEE estimate should not replace pregnancy-specific nutrition guidance.
