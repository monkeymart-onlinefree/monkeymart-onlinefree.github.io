What Is TDEE?

Total daily energy expenditure is the total energy a person uses in a day. It combines resting energy with movement and other energy costs. Since directly measuring daily energy expenditure is difficult, online tools estimate it from body measurements and an activity multiplier.

How to Use the TDEE Calculator

Choose metric or US units, enter age, sex, height, weight, and select the activity level that best describes your usual week. The main result is estimated kcal/day.

TDEE Formula

This calculator uses TDEE = estimated resting energy × activity factor. Resting energy uses the Mifflin–St Jeor equation: men 10W + 6.25H − 5A + 5; women 10W + 6.25H − 5A − 161, with W in kg, H in cm, and A in years.

Calculation Example

For a 30-year-old man at 80 kg and 180 cm, Mifflin–St Jeor gives about 1,780 kcal/day at rest. With a 1.55 activity factor, estimated TDEE is roughly 2,759 kcal/day.

Understanding Your Results

The activity-adjusted result is an estimate of average daily energy expenditure, not a direct measurement. Weekly averages are generally more useful than expecting every day to match the same number.

Limitations

Predictive equations cannot fully capture differences in body composition, spontaneous movement, training efficiency, or medical conditions. Pregnancy and pediatric energy needs require different methods.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does TDEE mean?

TDEE means total daily energy expenditure: the estimated energy your body uses across a full day, including resting metabolism and activity.

How is TDEE calculated here?

The default method estimates resting energy with the Mifflin–St Jeor equation, then multiplies the result by the selected activity factor.

Why can actual TDEE differ?

Daily movement, training volume, body composition, temperature, food intake, sleep, and individual physiology all affect real energy expenditure.

Can I use TDEE as an exact calorie target?

Treat it as a starting estimate. Body-weight trends and professional guidance are more informative than a single equation when precise energy planning matters.

Sources and Methodology
