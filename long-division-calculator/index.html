The Long Division Calculator divides a nonnegative integer by a positive integer and shows the quotient, remainder, decimal result, and step-by-step working. Enter the dividend and divisor to see how the division is completed from start to finish.

What Is the Long Division Calculator?

A long division calculator performs whole-number division using the traditional digit-by-digit long division method. It is useful when you want more than a decimal answer because it also shows the integer quotient, any remainder, and the intermediate division steps.

Long division finds the quotient one digit at a time using repeated division, multiplication, subtraction, and bringing down the next digit.

How to Use the Long Division Calculator

Dividend: Enter the nonnegative whole number you want to divide.

Divisor: Enter the positive whole number that will divide the dividend. The divisor cannot be zero because division by zero is undefined.

After entering both values, the calculator shows the quotient, remainder, decimal result, and long division steps.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

For nonnegative integer division:

Dividend = Divisor × Quotient + Remainder

or:

D = d × q + r

where:

0 ≤ r < d

For a nonnegative integer dividend and positive integer divisor, the integer quotient and remainder can also be written as:

q = ⌊D ÷ d⌋
r = D − dq

These relationships define integer division and ensure that the remainder is always smaller than the positive divisor.

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
D Dividend — the number being divided None; integer
d Divisor — the number dividing the dividend None; positive integer
q Quotient — the whole-number result None; integer
r Remainder — the amount left after division None; integer

The decimal result is:

Decimal Result = D ÷ d

It can also be expressed as:

Decimal Result = q + (r ÷ d)

Calculation Sequence

  1. Start with the leftmost digit or group of digits in the dividend that can be divided by the divisor.
  2. Determine how many whole times the divisor fits into that value.
  3. Write that number as the next quotient digit.
  4. Multiply the quotient digit by the divisor.
  5. Subtract the product from the current value.
  6. Bring down the next dividend digit and repeat the process.
  7. After the final digit, the amount left is the remainder.
  8. Divide the full dividend by the divisor to obtain the decimal result.

This divide → multiply → subtract → bring-down sequence is the standard long division process.

Quotient

The quotient is the whole-number portion of the division:

q = ⌊D ÷ d⌋

Remainder

The remainder is calculated from:

r = D − (d × q)

A valid remainder satisfies:

0 ≤ r < d

Decimal Result

The exact mathematical division is:

D ÷ d

If a remainder exists:

D ÷ d = q + r/d

Worked Example

Calculate:

1,257 ÷ 12

Inputs

  • Dividend: 1,257
  • Divisor: 12

Step 1

Start with the first two digits:

12 ÷ 12 = 1

Write 1 in the quotient.

1 × 12 = 12
12 − 12 = 0

Step 2

Bring down the next digit, 5.

Because 12 goes into 5 zero whole times, the next quotient digit is 0.

Bring down the final digit, 7, giving:

57

Step 3

57 ÷ 12 = 4 whole times.
4 × 12 = 48
57 − 48 = 9

The remainder is 9.

Final Result

Quotient = 104
Remainder = 9

Check:

12 × 104 + 9 = 1,248 + 9 = 1,257

Decimal result:

1,257 ÷ 12 = 104.75

Therefore:

1,257 ÷ 12 = 104 R9 = 104.75

Understanding Your Results

Quotient: The number of complete times the divisor fits into the dividend.

Remainder: The amount left after all complete groups have been removed. It must be smaller than the divisor.

Decimal Result: The result of expressing the full division as a decimal instead of an integer quotient and remainder.

Step-by-Step Working: Shows how each quotient digit is produced through division, multiplication, subtraction, and bringing down the next digit.

You can verify any quotient and remainder by checking:

Dividend = Divisor × Quotient + Remainder

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator is designed for nonnegative integer long division. The dividend must be a nonnegative whole number, while the divisor must be a positive whole number.

A divisor of zero is not valid because division by zero is undefined.

The calculator does not perform polynomial long division.

Very large inputs beyond JavaScript's safe-integer range are rejected so integer calculations such as the quotient and remainder can remain exact. JavaScript's maximum safe integer is 9,007,199,254,740,991, or 253−12^{53}-1.

A division may produce no remainder, a remainder, a terminating decimal, or a repeating decimal depending on the numbers being divided.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the remainder in long division?

The remainder is the amount left after the divisor has been multiplied by the whole-number quotient. It is calculated as:

Remainder = Dividend − (Divisor × Quotient)

For a positive divisor, the remainder must be at least zero and smaller than the divisor.

Can the divisor be zero?

No. Division by zero is mathematically undefined, so zero cannot be used as the divisor.

What happens if the dividend is smaller than the divisor?

The whole-number quotient is 0, and the dividend becomes the remainder. For example:

7 ÷ 10 = 0 R7

The decimal result is 0.7.

How can I check a long division answer?

Multiply the divisor by the quotient and add the remainder:

Divisor × Quotient + Remainder = Dividend

If this equation is true and the remainder is smaller than the divisor, the integer division result satisfies the quotient-and-remainder rule.

Does this calculator accept decimal numbers?

No. This calculator focuses on nonnegative integer long division rather than division where the original inputs contain decimal or fractional values.

Does the calculator support negative numbers?

No. Its long division method is intended for a nonnegative dividend and a positive divisor.

Can I use this calculator for polynomial long division?

No. It performs numeric integer long division only and does not divide algebraic polynomials.
