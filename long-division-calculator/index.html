What Is Long Division?

Long division is a digit-by-digit algorithm for dividing numbers. For integer division, it produces a whole-number quotient and a remainder. Each cycle asks how many times the divisor fits into the current partial dividend, multiplies back, subtracts, and then brings down the next digit.

How to Use the Long Division Calculator

Enter a nonnegative whole-number dividend and a positive whole-number divisor. Select Divide. The result panel shows the integer quotient, remainder, a decimal approximation to 12 places, and the steps used for the whole-number portion.

Division Formula

Dividend = Divisor × Quotient + Remainder, with 0 ≤ Remainder < Divisor

Formula Variables

The dividend is the number being divided. The divisor is the number you divide by. The quotient is the whole-number result, and the remainder is what is left.

Calculation Example

For 100 ÷ 7, the largest whole-number quotient is 14 because 7 × 14 = 98. Subtracting 98 from 100 leaves 2, so the integer result is 14 R2. As a decimal, 100 ÷ 7 is approximately 14.285714285714.

Understanding the Steps

The step list follows the standard cycle: divide, multiply, subtract, and bring down. The calculator uses integer arithmetic for quotient and remainder, then ordinary floating-point division for the decimal approximation.

Limitations

This page focuses on nonnegative integer long division. It does not perform polynomial long division. Very large inputs above JavaScript's safe-integer range are rejected so quotient and remainder remain exact.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is long division?

Long division is an algorithm that determines a quotient one digit at a time by repeating divide, multiply, subtract, and bring-down steps.

What is a remainder?

For nonnegative integer division, the remainder is what is left after forming the largest whole-number quotient. It must be at least 0 and smaller than the divisor.

Can a divisor be zero?

No. Division by zero is undefined, so the calculator requires a divisor greater than zero.

How do I check a quotient and remainder?

Use dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder. If that equality holds and the remainder is smaller than the divisor, the integer-division result is consistent.
