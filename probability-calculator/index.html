What Is a Probability Calculator?

A probability calculator converts information about possible outcomes or event probabilities into numerical likelihoods from 0 to 1. This page handles simple equally likely outcomes and two independent events, including the probability that both occur, either occurs, exactly one occurs, or neither occurs.

How to Use the Probability Calculator

Choose the calculation type. For independent events, enter P(A) and P(B) as decimals from 0 to 1. For equally likely outcomes, enter the number of favorable outcomes and the total number of possible outcomes. Select Calculate to update the result panel.

Probability Formulas

For independent events, P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B). The addition rule is P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B). For equally likely outcomes, probability equals favorable outcomes divided by total outcomes.

Calculation Example

If P(A) = 0.50 and P(B) = 0.40 and the events are independent, P(A ∩ B) = 0.20. The probability that A or B or both occur is 0.50 + 0.40 − 0.20 = 0.70.

Understanding Your Results

A probability of 0 means an event is impossible under the model, while 1 means it is certain. A decimal can be converted to a percentage by multiplying by 100. For example, 0.20 corresponds to 20%.

Important Assumptions and Limitations

The two-event mode assumes A and B are independent. If one event changes the probability of the other, use conditional probability rather than multiplying P(A) and P(B). The favorable-outcomes mode also assumes all listed outcomes are equally likely.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the probability of two independent events both happening?

Multiply the probabilities: P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B). This works when the occurrence of one event does not change the probability of the other.

How do I convert probability to a percentage?

Multiply the decimal probability by 100. A probability of 0.375 is 37.5%.

Can probability be greater than 1?

No. A valid probability is between 0 and 1 inclusive. Values outside that range do not represent probabilities.

What is the complement of an event?

The complement is the probability that the event does not occur. It is calculated as 1 − P(A).
