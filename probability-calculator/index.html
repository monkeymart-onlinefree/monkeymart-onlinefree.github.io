Use the Probability Calculator to calculate probabilities for two independent events and simple equally likely outcomes. Enter P(A) and P(B) to calculate related event probabilities, or enter the number of favorable and total outcomes to find a basic probability.

What Is the Probability Calculator?

The Probability Calculator helps you calculate the likelihood of events using standard probability rules. It can work with two independent events to find intersections, unions, complements, and exclusive outcomes, and it can calculate probability from favorable outcomes when all possible outcomes are equally likely.

Probability values range from 0 to 1, where 0 represents an impossible event and 1 represents a certain event.

How to Use the Probability Calculator

Probability of A, P(A)

Enter the probability that event A occurs.

The value should be between 0 and 1. For example, a 40% probability is entered as 0.40.

Probability of B, P(B)

Enter the probability that event B occurs.

This calculation assumes events A and B are independent, meaning the occurrence of one event does not change the probability of the other.

Favorable Outcomes

Enter the number of outcomes that satisfy the event you are measuring.

For example, when rolling a fair six-sided die, there are three favorable outcomes for rolling an even number: 2, 4, and 6.

Total Equally Likely Outcomes

Enter the total number of possible outcomes.

The favorable-outcome method is valid when the possible outcomes are equally likely.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula for Independent Events

For independent events A and B, the probability that both occur is:

P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B)

OpenStax and Penn State both describe this multiplication rule for independent events.

Variables

  • P(A) = probability that event A occurs, from 0 to 1
  • P(B) = probability that event B occurs, from 0 to 1
  • P(A ∩ B) = probability that both A and B occur
  • P(A ∪ B) = probability that A, B, or both occur
  • P(A') = probability that A does not occur
  • P(B') = probability that B does not occur

Union of Two Events

The probability that A or B or both occur is:

P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B)

For independent events:

P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − [P(A) × P(B)]

The intersection is subtracted because outcomes belonging to both events would otherwise be counted twice.

Complement of an Event

The complement is the probability that an event does not occur.

P(A') = 1 − P(A)
P(B') = 1 − P(B)

The probability of an event and its complement always adds to 1.

Exclusive Outcome

If you want the probability that A or B occurs, but not both, the overlapping probability must also be excluded:

P(A Δ B) = P(A) + P(B) − 2P(A ∩ B)

For independent events:

P(A Δ B) = P(A) + P(B) − 2[P(A) × P(B)]

This represents an exclusive OR rather than the inclusive union.

If A and B are mutually exclusive and cannot happen together, then:

P(A ∩ B) = 0

and the union simplifies to:

P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B)
.

Probability from Favorable Outcomes

For equally likely outcomes:

P(E) = Favorable Outcomes ÷ Total Outcomes

or:

P(E) = f / n

where:

  • f = number of favorable outcomes
  • n = total number of equally likely outcomes

This method assumes every possible outcome has the same chance of occurring.

Calculation Sequence

For the two-event calculation:

  1. Read P(A) and P(B).
  2. Confirm that each probability is between 0 and 1.
  3. Calculate the independent-event intersection:
    P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B)
  4. Calculate the union:
    P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B)
  5. Calculate complements using 1 − P(event).
  6. Calculate the exclusive outcome by removing the intersection from both overlapping portions.

For the favorable-outcome calculation:

  1. Read the number of favorable outcomes.
  2. Read the total number of equally likely outcomes.
  3. Divide favorable outcomes by total outcomes.

Worked Example

Suppose:

Inputs

  • P(A) = 0.40
  • P(B) = 0.30
  • Favorable outcomes = 3
  • Total equally likely outcomes = 10

Step 1: Calculate the Intersection

P(A ∩ B) = 0.40 × 0.30
P(A ∩ B) = 0.12

There is a 12% probability that both independent events occur.

Step 2: Calculate the Union

P(A ∪ B) = 0.40 + 0.30 − 0.12
P(A ∪ B) = 0.58

There is a 58% probability that A, B, or both occur.

Step 3: Calculate the Complements

For event A:

P(A') = 1 − 0.40 = 0.60

For event B:

P(B') = 1 − 0.30 = 0.70

Step 4: Calculate the Exclusive Outcome

P(A Δ B) = 0.40 + 0.30 − 2(0.12)
P(A Δ B) = 0.46

There is a 46% probability that exactly one of the two events occurs.

Step 5: Calculate Probability from Favorable Outcomes

P(E) = 3 ÷ 10
P(E) = 0.30

Final Result

The example produces:

  • Both A and B: 0.12 or 12%
  • A or B or both: 0.58 or 58%
  • A does not occur: 0.60 or 60%
  • B does not occur: 0.70 or 70%
  • Exactly one of A or B: 0.46 or 46%
  • 3 favorable outcomes from 10 equally likely outcomes: 0.30 or 30%

Understanding Your Results

A probability near 0 means an event is unlikely, while a probability near 1 means it is more likely.

Intersection shows the probability that both independent events occur.

Union shows the probability that at least one of the two events occurs, including the possibility that both occur.

Complement shows the probability that a particular event does not occur.

Exclusive outcome shows the probability that exactly one of two events occurs rather than both.

Favorable-outcome probability compares the number of desired outcomes with the total number of equally likely possible outcomes.

Assumptions and Limitations

The two-event calculation assumes that A and B are independent. If one event changes the probability of the other, multiplying P(A) by P(B) is not appropriate. Dependent events require conditional probability, such as P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B|A).

P(A) and P(B) must be valid probabilities between 0 and 1.

The favorable-outcomes formula assumes that all outcomes being counted are equally likely. If outcomes have different probabilities, favorable outcomes divided by total outcomes may not give the correct result.

Results may also be affected by rounding when probabilities contain many decimal places.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does P(A) mean?

P(A) represents the probability that event A occurs. A value of 0 means the event cannot occur, while 1 means it is certain.

How do I calculate the probability of two independent events both happening?

Multiply their probabilities:

P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B)

For example, if P(A) = 0.5 and P(B) = 0.2, the probability that both occur is 0.10.

How do I calculate the probability that A or B occurs?

Use the addition rule:

P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B)

Subtracting the intersection prevents the overlapping probability from being counted twice.

What is the complement of a probability?

The complement is the probability that the event does not happen:

P(A') = 1 − P(A)

If P(A) is 0.75, then P(A') is 0.25.

What is the difference between independent and mutually exclusive events?

Independent events can occur together, and one does not change the probability of the other.

Mutually exclusive events cannot occur at the same time, so their intersection has probability 0.

Can I multiply probabilities for dependent events?

Not by simply using P(A) × P(B). For dependent events, the probability of one event may change after the other occurs, so conditional probability is required.

When can I use favorable outcomes divided by total outcomes?

Use favorable outcomes ÷ total outcomes when all possible outcomes are equally likely, such as outcomes from a fair die or fair coin.
