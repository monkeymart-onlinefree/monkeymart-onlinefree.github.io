What Is a Concrete Volume Calculator?

A concrete calculator converts geometric dimensions into volume. This version supports rectangular prisms for slabs, footings, and walls; cylinders for round columns or holes; and hollow cylinders for circular tubes. It then multiplies by quantity and optionally adds a waste allowance.

Concrete Volume Formulas

Rectangular: V = length × width × height Cylinder: V = π × (diameter ÷ 2)² × height Tube: V = π × (R² − r²) × height

All dimensions are first converted to meters, then the result is displayed in cubic meters, cubic feet, and cubic yards.

Why Add a Waste Allowance?

The waste input is a planning allowance, not a universal engineering rule. Real projects can require extra concrete for uneven excavation, formwork variation, spillage, pump priming, over-excavation, and supplier constraints. Follow project drawings, specifications, and supplier guidance for actual orders.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many cubic feet are in a cubic yard?

One cubic yard equals 27 cubic feet exactly because one yard is three feet and 3 × 3 × 3 = 27.

What formula is used for a round column?

The calculator models a round column as a cylinder: π × radius² × height.

Does the calculator estimate bags of concrete?

No. Bag yield varies by product and bag size. Use the volume result with the manufacturer’s stated yield for the specific product you plan to buy.

Is 10% waste always correct?

No. Ten percent is only the default planning allowance on this page. Appropriate extra volume depends on the project and site conditions.
