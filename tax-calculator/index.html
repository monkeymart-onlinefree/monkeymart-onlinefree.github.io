What Is the 2026 Federal Income Tax Calculator?

This page estimates U.S. federal ordinary income tax for tax year 2026 using the IRS ordinary-income rate schedule and 2026 standard deductions. It is designed for planning, not return preparation.

2026 Standard Deductions Used

The calculator uses $16,100 for Single or Married Filing Separately, $32,200 for Married Filing Jointly, and $24,150 for Head of Household. It compares the standard deduction with the itemized-deduction amount you enter and uses the larger value.

How Progressive Tax Brackets Work

A marginal rate applies only to the portion of taxable income inside that bracket. Reaching a higher bracket does not cause all taxable income to be taxed at the higher rate.

Important Limitations

This simplified estimator excludes many return-specific provisions, including tax credits, self-employment tax, preferential capital-gain rates, AMT, NIIT, QBI, and special deductions or phaseouts. Use IRS forms, instructions, or qualified tax help for filing decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the 2026 federal tax rates?

For individual ordinary income, the 2026 marginal rates are 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37%, with income thresholds depending on filing status.

Does this calculator include tax credits?

No. It estimates ordinary income tax before credits and excludes many special taxes and deductions.

Why is my effective tax rate lower than my top bracket?

Because U.S. federal income tax is progressive. Only the income inside each bracket is taxed at that bracket's marginal rate.

Formula / Algorithm Used in Tool

Taxable income = max(0, gross income − above-the-line adjustments − max(itemized deductions, applicable standard deduction)). Ordinary federal tax = sum of each progressive bracket portion × its marginal rate. Estimated refund/due = withholding/estimated payments − calculated tax.
