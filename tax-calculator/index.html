The 2026 Federal Income Tax Calculator estimates U.S. federal ordinary income tax using your filing status, gross income, adjustments, deductions, and federal tax already paid. It calculates taxable income, applies the 2026 progressive tax brackets, and estimates whether you may have a refund or an amount due.

What Is the 2026 Federal Income Tax Calculator?

This calculator provides a simplified estimate of federal ordinary income tax for tax year 2026. It is useful for checking how deductions affect taxable income, estimating federal tax liability, and comparing calculated tax with federal withholding or estimated payments.

It is an estimator rather than a complete federal tax return. It does not calculate every credit, deduction, additional tax, or special tax treatment that may apply to an actual Form 1040.

How to Use the Tax Calculator

Filing Status

Select the filing status available in the calculator that applies to your situation. Filing status determines which 2026 federal tax brackets and standard deduction are used.

Ordinary Gross Income

Enter your ordinary gross income in dollars. This is the income amount the calculator starts with before subtracting the adjustments and deduction entered in the tool.

Above-the-Line Adjustments

Enter the total dollar amount of applicable adjustments you want the calculator to subtract from gross income before calculating the deduction and taxable income.

Itemized Deductions

Enter your total itemized deductions. The calculator compares this amount with the applicable standard deduction and uses the larger amount.

Federal Tax Withheld / Estimated Paid

Enter federal income tax already withheld or estimated federal tax payments already made. This value is compared with calculated federal tax to estimate a refund or remaining amount due.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator first determines the deduction to use:

Deduction Used = max(Itemized Deductions, Standard Deduction)

Taxable income is then calculated as:

Taxable Income = max(0, Gross Income − Above-the-Line Adjustments − Deduction Used)

Federal ordinary income tax is calculated progressively:

Federal Tax = Σ(Income within each tax bracket × that bracket's tax rate)

Finally:

Refund / Due = Federal Tax Withheld or Estimated Paid − Calculated Federal Tax

A positive final value indicates an estimated refund. A negative value indicates an estimated amount due.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Determine the applicable standard deduction from filing status.
  2. Compare the standard deduction with entered itemized deductions.
  3. Use the larger deduction.
  4. Subtract adjustments and the selected deduction from gross income.
  5. Do not allow taxable income to fall below zero.
  6. Divide taxable income across the applicable 2026 federal tax brackets.
  7. Apply each marginal rate only to income within that bracket.
  8. Add the bracket amounts to calculate ordinary federal income tax.
  9. Compare calculated tax with federal tax already paid.

Progressive Tax Calculation

Federal income tax is progressive. Moving into a higher bracket does not cause all taxable income to be taxed at the higher percentage. Only the portion of taxable income inside each bracket is taxed at that bracket's rate. The IRS keeps seven individual rates for 2026: 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37%.

Worked Example

Assume a single filer enters the following values.

Inputs

  • Ordinary gross income: $90,000
  • Above-the-line adjustments: $5,000
  • Itemized deductions: $12,000
  • Federal tax withheld / estimated paid: $9,500

Step 1: Choose the Deduction

The 2026 base standard deduction for a single filer is $16,100, which is greater than the entered $12,000 itemized deduction.

Deduction Used = max($12,000, $16,100) = $16,100

Step 2: Calculate Taxable Income

Taxable Income = $90,000 − $5,000 − $16,100
Taxable Income = $68,900

Step 3: Apply the 2026 Single Tax Brackets

For this example:

  • First 12,400×10%=1,240
  • Next 38,000×12%=4,560
  • Remaining 18,500×22%=4,070

The applicable 2026 single-filer bracket thresholds come from the IRS 2026 tax rate tables.

Calculated Federal Tax = $1,240 + $4,560 + $4,070 = $9,870

Step 4: Estimate Refund or Amount Due

Refund / Due = $9,500 − 9,870=-370

Final Result

  • Taxable income: $68,900
  • Deduction used: $16,100
  • Calculated ordinary federal tax: $9,870
  • Estimated amount due: $370

This example covers only the simplified ordinary federal income tax calculation used by this calculator.

Understanding Your Results

Taxable Income

Taxable income is the amount remaining after the calculator subtracts entered above-the-line adjustments and the larger of the itemized deduction or applicable standard deduction. Federal tax brackets are applied to this amount.

Deduction Used

This shows whether the calculator used your entered itemized deductions or the applicable base standard deduction. The larger value is used in the calculation.

Effective Rate

The effective rate is a percentage summary associated with the calculated tax result. It should not be confused with your marginal tax rate, which applies only to the portion of taxable income falling inside a particular bracket.

Estimated Refund

If federal tax withheld or estimated payments are greater than calculated tax, the difference is displayed as an estimated refund.

Estimated Amount Due

If calculated tax is greater than tax already withheld or paid, the difference is displayed as an estimated amount due.

This estimate does not guarantee the refund or balance that would appear on an actual federal tax return.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator is a simplified estimator of 2026 federal ordinary income tax. It uses entered ordinary income, adjustments, deductions, filing status, and federal tax already paid.

It does not calculate many provisions that can change an actual federal tax return, including:

  • Tax credits
  • Self-employment tax
  • Preferential long-term capital-gain or qualified-dividend tax rates
  • Alternative Minimum Tax
  • Net Investment Income Tax
  • Qualified Business Income deduction
  • Special deductions, eligibility rules, or phaseouts
  • State or local income tax

The calculator also does not verify whether a deduction or adjustment entered by the user is legally allowable. Actual tax results can differ because federal tax returns contain additional income categories, deductions, credits, elections, limitations, and taxpayer-specific rules.

Use IRS forms and instructions or qualified tax assistance when making filing decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is taxable income calculated?

The calculator subtracts entered above-the-line adjustments and the larger of itemized deductions or the applicable standard deduction from gross income. If the result is below zero, taxable income is treated as zero.

Does entering a higher tax bracket mean all my income is taxed at that rate?

No. Federal income tax uses progressive brackets. Each rate applies only to the portion of taxable income within that bracket.

Does the calculator automatically choose between itemized and standard deductions?

Yes. It compares your entered itemized deduction with the applicable standard deduction and uses the larger value.

What does a negative refund/due result mean?

The calculator subtracts calculated federal tax from withholding or estimated payments. If the result is negative, the absolute value represents the estimated amount still due.

Does this calculator include tax credits?

No. Credits and several other return-specific federal tax provisions are excluded from this simplified estimator.

Does the calculator include capital gains tax?

It estimates ordinary federal income tax. Preferential long-term capital-gain rates are not included in the calculation.

Can I use the result to file my tax return?

The result can help with planning, but it is not a completed tax return or individualized tax advice. Actual filing calculations may require IRS forms, schedules, instructions, and additional taxpayer information.
