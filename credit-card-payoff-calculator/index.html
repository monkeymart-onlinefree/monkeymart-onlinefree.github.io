What Is a Credit Card Payoff Calculator?

A credit card payoff calculator builds a repayment plan across multiple card balances. This version uses the debt avalanche method: pay each entered minimum, then direct the remaining monthly budget to the active card with the highest APR.

How to Use the Credit Card Payoff Calculator

Enter your total monthly card budget and each card’s balance, APR, and minimum payment. Add or remove cards as needed. The calculator simulates monthly interest, required minimums, and highest-APR extra payments until all modeled balances reach zero.

Debt Avalanche Method

Monthly extra payment → highest-APR active card after modeled minimum payments are covered

When a card is paid off, the plan keeps the same total monthly budget and redirects available money to the remaining cards. This accelerates principal reduction without lowering the entered budget.

Understanding Your Results

The payoff estimate shows total time, starting debt, modeled interest, total paid, and the order in which cards are cleared. The schedule includes early months and periodic checkpoints.

Important Factors

Keep making at least the required payment shown on each actual statement. CFPB guidance warns that missed minimum payments can trigger fees, account consequences, or loss of promotional terms. If required payments are unaffordable, contact issuers promptly.

Limitations

This model assumes fixed APRs, no new charges, no transfer fees, no late fees, fixed entered minimums, and a constant monthly budget. Real issuers often use minimum-payment formulas that change with balance and interest.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the debt avalanche method?

The debt avalanche method sends extra payment to the active debt with the highest interest rate while required minimums are paid on the other debts. When one card is cleared, its freed payment capacity can be redirected to the remaining cards.

What if my monthly budget is below the sum of minimum payments?

The calculator stops and shows an error because the modeled avalanche plan cannot satisfy the entered minimum payments. If you cannot make required payments, contact the card issuer promptly; CFPB notes that many issuers may work with borrowers facing financial difficulty.

Does avalanche always give the lowest interest cost?

With fixed rates, no new charges, and the same payment budget, directing extra principal to the highest APR is generally the interest-minimizing ordering. Real card terms, promotions, fees, and changing APRs can alter the result.
