The Credit Card Payoff Calculator builds a multi-card repayment plan using the debt avalanche method. It keeps your total monthly credit-card budget fixed, covers modeled minimum payments first, and directs the remaining money toward the active card with the highest APR.

What Is the Credit Card Payoff Calculator?

This calculator helps organize repayment when you are paying down multiple credit cards. Instead of reducing your total payment as individual cards are cleared, the calculator keeps the same monthly budget and redirects available money toward the remaining cards.

The method prioritizes higher-interest debt after minimum payments are covered. It can be useful for comparing how a consistent monthly budget may be allocated across several credit-card balances.

How to Use the Credit Card Payoff Calculator

Enter the total monthly budget for cards. This is the total amount you plan to make available for credit-card payments each month.

The payoff model then uses the card balances, APRs, and modeled minimum payments in the payoff setup to allocate that budget.

After calculation:

  1. Modeled minimum payments are covered first.
  2. Any remaining monthly budget is directed to the highest-APR active card.
  3. When a card is paid off, the total monthly budget does not decrease.
  4. Available money is redirected toward the remaining active cards.

Choose a monthly budget you can realistically maintain. If your financial situation changes, recalculate the plan using the updated amount.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator uses a debt avalanche allocation method rather than one single closed-form loan formula.

For the active cards:

M = ∑mᵢ

When the monthly budget is sufficient to cover the modeled minimum payments:

E = B − M

The extra amount E is applied to the active card with the highest APR.

For the highest-APR active card h:

Pₕ = mₕ + E

For the other active cards:

Pᵢ = mᵢ

The total allocated payment remains:

∑Pᵢ = B

Variables

  • B = total monthly credit-card budget, in currency per month
  • mᵢ = modeled minimum payment for active card i
  • M = total modeled minimum payments for all active cards
  • E = amount remaining after modeled minimum payments are covered
  • h = active card with the highest APR
  • Pₕ = payment allocated to the highest-APR active card
  • Pᵢ = payment allocated to another active card

Calculation Sequence

  1. Identify the credit cards that still have an active balance.
  2. Determine the modeled minimum payment for each active card.
  3. Allocate the required modeled minimum payments from the monthly budget.
  4. Calculate the amount of the budget that remains.
  5. Identify the active card with the highest APR.
  6. Direct the remaining amount to that card.
  7. Continue using the same total monthly budget in later periods.
  8. When a card is paid off, redirect the available money to the remaining cards rather than reducing the monthly budget.

This rolling reallocation is the key feature of the debt avalanche approach.

Monthly Extra Payment

If the budget exceeds the combined modeled minimum payments:

Extra Payment = Monthly Budget − Total Modeled Minimum Payments

That extra amount targets the highest-APR active card.

Payment After a Card Is Paid Off

The calculator does not lower the entered monthly budget simply because one debt has been eliminated.

Instead, the money that becomes available is kept within the same overall monthly budget and redirected to the remaining active cards. This allows progressively more money to be concentrated on the debts that remain.

Worked Example

Assume the payoff setup contains three active cards:

Inputs

  • Total monthly card budget: $700
  • Card A: $4,800 balance, 24% APR, $150 modeled minimum payment
  • Card B: $3,200 balance, 19% APR, $100 modeled minimum payment
  • Card C: $1,600 balance, 14% APR, $75 modeled minimum payment

Step 1: Add the Modeled Minimum Payments

150 + 100 + 75 = 325

Total modeled minimum payments are $325.

Step 2: Find the Extra Amount

700 − 325 = 375

After the modeled minimum payments are covered, $375 remains.

Step 3: Target the Highest APR

Card A has the highest APR at 24%, so it receives the extra $375.

150 + 375 = 525

The first allocation is therefore:

  • Card A: $525
  • Card B: $100
  • Card C: $75

Final Result

525 + 100 + 75 = 700

The complete $700 monthly budget is allocated.

Once Card A is eventually paid off, the calculator continues using the same overall $700 budget and redirects available money toward the remaining active cards according to the debt avalanche method.

This example demonstrates the payment-allocation method. Actual balance reduction can differ because real credit-card issuers may calculate and post interest differently.

Understanding Your Results

Payoff Priority

The plan prioritizes the active credit card with the highest APR after modeled minimum payments have been covered.

A different card can become the priority after the current target is paid off.

Monthly Budget Allocation

Your entered monthly budget represents the total amount available for the modeled card payments.

The calculator is designed to keep this budget constant instead of reducing your total monthly payment whenever a balance reaches zero.

Supporting Values

Supporting calculations show how the monthly budget is distributed within the debt-avalanche process. These values help explain which debt receives additional payment and why.

The result is an estimate rather than an issuer statement or guaranteed payoff outcome.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator assumes:

  • APRs remain fixed during the modeled repayment period.
  • No new purchases or other new charges are added.
  • No balance-transfer fees are added.
  • No late fees are added.
  • Modeled minimum payments remain fixed.
  • The total monthly credit-card budget stays constant.
  • Money freed after paying off a card is redirected to the remaining cards.

Real credit cards can behave differently. Credit-card agreements may contain different APRs for different transaction types, promotional rates can expire, fees can apply, and interest may be compounded daily. Minimum-payment requirements can also affect actual repayment amounts.

If you cannot make a required minimum payment, the CFPB recommends contacting the credit-card company promptly rather than relying only on a calculator estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the debt avalanche method?

The debt avalanche method covers required modeled minimum payments first and then directs available extra money toward the active debt with the highest APR. After that debt is paid off, available money moves to the next eligible card.

Does the monthly budget decrease when a credit card is paid off?

No. This calculator keeps the entered total monthly budget constant. Money that becomes available after one card is paid off is redirected toward the remaining active cards.

What happens if my budget only covers the modeled minimum payments?

If the budget equals the total modeled minimum payments, there is no additional amount available for the highest-APR card beyond its modeled minimum payment.

Does the calculator assume credit-card APRs stay the same?

Yes. The model assumes fixed APRs. Actual credit-card rates may change because of promotional periods, variable-rate terms, penalty rates, or changes permitted under the card agreement.

Are new purchases included?

No. The model assumes no new charges are added while following the payoff plan.

Does the calculator include late fees or balance-transfer fees?

No. Late fees and balance-transfer fees are excluded from the model.

Why can my actual payoff differ from the calculator?

Actual results can differ because issuers may calculate interest using daily balances, minimum-payment requirements may change as balances change, APRs can change, fees may apply, and payment posting dates can affect interest charges. The CFPB notes that credit-card interest may be compounded daily and that card terms can contain multiple APRs.
