What Is a Sample Size Calculator?

This sample size calculator estimates the number of independent observations needed to estimate one population proportion with a chosen confidence level and margin of error. An optional finite-population correction reduces the required sample when the target population is not very large.

How to Use the Sample Size Calculator

  1. Select a confidence level.
  2. Enter the desired margin of error as a percentage.
  3. Enter the expected proportion. Use 50% when there is no reliable prior estimate and a conservative sample is desired.
  4. Optionally enter the total population size.

Sample Size Formula

n₀ = z² p(1−p) / e²
Finite population: n = n₀ / [1 + (n₀−1)/N]

NIST gives the large-population proportion formula. The finite-population correction shown here is the standard simple-random-sampling adjustment. Here, z is the normal critical value, p is the expected proportion, e is the margin of error, and N is population size.

Calculation Example

At 95% confidence, 5% margin of error, and p = 50%, the large-population formula gives approximately 384.15. Rounding up gives a required sample of 385.

Limitations

This is a planning formula for a simple estimate of one proportion under normal-approximation assumptions. Complex survey designs, clustering, unequal weighting, nonresponse, rare outcomes, subgroup precision, and hypothesis-testing power can require larger or differently calculated samples. The result is not a substitute for a study-specific statistical design.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does a smaller margin of error need a larger sample?

The margin of error appears squared in the denominator. As the target margin becomes smaller, the required sample increases rapidly.

Why round the sample size up?

The formula gives a minimum requirement. Rounding down could leave the sample below that requirement, so the calculator uses the next whole number.

Sources and Methodology

  • NIST Handbook 91 — Sample Size for a Proportion
  • Rochester Institute of Technology — Proportion Sample Size and Finite Population Correction

Method: two-sided normal critical values 1.645, 1.96, and 2.576 for 90%, 95%, and 99% confidence, respectively.
