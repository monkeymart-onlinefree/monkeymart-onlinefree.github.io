The Fuel Cost Calculator estimates how much fuel a trip will use and how much that fuel will cost. Enter the trip distance, vehicle fuel efficiency, fuel price, and price unit, then choose whether the journey is a round trip.

What Is the Fuel Cost Calculator?

The Fuel Cost Calculator helps drivers estimate fuel consumption and fuel expense before a trip. It uses the distance traveled, the vehicle's fuel efficiency, and the entered fuel price.

It can work with fuel economy expressed as miles per gallon (MPG) or liters per 100 kilometers (L/100 km), provided the distance and fuel-price units are compatible.

How to Use the Fuel Cost Calculator

Enter the following information:

Trip distance: Enter the distance of the trip. Use a distance unit that matches the selected fuel-efficiency method.

Fuel efficiency: Enter the vehicle's expected fuel economy. For MPG calculations, a higher value means the vehicle travels farther on each gallon. For L/100 km, a lower value means less fuel is used over 100 kilometers.

Fuel price: Enter the current price you expect to pay for fuel.

Price unit: Select a fuel-price unit that matches the fuel volume used in the calculation, such as price per gallon with MPG-based calculations or price per liter with L/100 km calculations.

Calculate a round trip: Select this option when the journey includes the return trip. The calculation then uses both the outbound and return distance.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculation depends on how fuel efficiency is entered.

MPG Formula

When distance is measured in miles and fuel economy is measured in miles per gallon:

Fuel used = Distance ÷ Fuel economy

Calculation Sequence

  1. Read the entered trip distance.
  2. If round trip is selected, include the return journey in the total distance.
  3. Apply the formula that matches the selected fuel-efficiency unit.
  4. Calculate the estimated amount of fuel required.
  5. Multiply the fuel used by the entered fuel price.
  6. Display the estimated trip fuel cost and supporting calculated values.

FuelEconomy.gov uses the same underlying relationship when calculating MPG: miles traveled are divided by gallons of fuel used.

Understanding Your Results

Fuel used is the estimated amount of gasoline or diesel required for the entered trip. The result is expressed in a fuel-volume unit such as gallons or liters.

Fuel cost is the estimated amount you would spend on that fuel based on the fuel price you entered.

The result is a trip estimate rather than a guaranteed amount. Actual fuel economy can change with driving conditions, driving behavior, vehicle condition, fuel characteristics, and other factors. FuelEconomy.gov specifically notes that real-world MPG may differ from standardized fuel-economy estimates.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator assumes that the fuel-efficiency value you enter is representative of the entire trip. If actual fuel economy changes during the journey, the real fuel use and cost can be different.

Distance and efficiency units must also be compatible. For example, an MPG calculation requires distance and fuel-volume units that work with the selected MPG definition.

The fuel price is entered by the user, so the calculator does not automatically apply a current local gasoline or diesel price.

The estimate does not include tolls, parking charges, vehicle maintenance, depreciation, insurance, or other travel expenses.

It also does not calculate electricity costs for plug-in vehicles or automatically convert between currencies.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate fuel cost for a trip?

First calculate the amount of fuel required, then multiply that amount by the fuel price. With MPG, divide miles traveled by MPG. With L/100 km, multiply kilometers by L/100 km and divide by 100.

How is fuel used calculated with MPG?

Divide the trip distance in miles by the vehicle's miles-per-gallon value:

Fuel Used=Miles÷MPGFuel\ Used = Miles \div MPG

For example, 300 miles at 30 MPG requires approximately 10 gallons.

How is fuel use calculated with L/100 km?

Multiply the distance in kilometers by the L/100 km value, then divide by 100:

Liters Used=Kilometers×L/100km÷100Liters\ Used = Kilometers \times L/100km \div 100

A 500 km trip at 8 L/100 km would use 40 liters.

Does the calculator automatically use the current fuel price?

No. The fuel price is an input. Enter the price that applies to your location or expected refueling point.

What does the round-trip option do?

It includes the return journey in the trip distance before calculating fuel use and fuel cost. If the return route covers the same distance, the entered one-way distance is effectively counted twice.

Why might my real fuel cost be different?

Actual fuel economy can vary because of traffic, speed, weather, vehicle condition, driving style, cargo, idling, and other conditions. The final fuel price may also differ from the value you entered.

Does this calculator include other driving expenses?

No. It estimates fuel use and fuel cost only. Tolls, parking, maintenance, depreciation, insurance, and other ownership or travel costs are outside this calculation.
