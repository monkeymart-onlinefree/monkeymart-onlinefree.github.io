What Is a Depreciation Calculator?

A depreciation calculator spreads an asset’s depreciable cost across its useful life. This page supports straight-line, declining-balance, and sum-of-years’-digits methods and displays annual depreciation, accumulated depreciation, and ending book value.

Depreciation Formulas

Straight-line: (cost − salvage) ÷ useful life. Declining balance: beginning book value multiplied by a rate derived from the chosen factor and useful life, without reducing below salvage. SYD: depreciable base multiplied by a declining year fraction.

Example

A $50,000 asset with a $5,000 salvage value and five-year useful life has a $45,000 depreciable base. Straight-line depreciation is $9,000 per year.

Accounting vs Tax Depreciation

Book depreciation methods are not automatically the same as tax depreciation. U.S. tax depreciation can require MACRS classes, conventions, method switching, bonus depreciation, or special elections. This calculator is a general accounting schedule, not a tax return engine.

Limitations

The model assumes full-year depreciation and a fixed salvage value. It does not apply mid-month, mid-quarter, or half-year conventions, MACRS tables, impairment, revaluation, component depreciation, or asset-disposal rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is salvage value?

Salvage value, also called residual or scrap value, is the estimated amount an asset is expected to be worth at the end of its useful life. In a basic depreciation model, cost minus salvage value is the depreciable base.

What factor should I use for double-declining balance?

Use a factor of 2. The annual declining-balance rate is then 2 divided by the useful life in years. This page also prevents book value from falling below the entered salvage value.

Is this the same as MACRS tax depreciation?

No. MACRS uses specific recovery classes, conventions, rates, and switching rules under U.S. tax law. This page provides general accounting methods and should not be used as a substitute for tax-preparation guidance.
