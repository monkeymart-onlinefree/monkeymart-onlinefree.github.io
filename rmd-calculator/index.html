What Is an RMD Calculator?

An RMD calculator estimates the minimum amount that generally must be withdrawn from certain tax-deferred retirement accounts for a year. The standard calculation divides the prior December 31 account balance by an IRS life-expectancy distribution period.

How to Use It

Enter your age at the end of the distribution year and the account balance from the previous December 31. For the special case where your spouse is your sole beneficiary and is more than 10 years younger, enter the exact Joint Life factor from IRS Publication 590-B Table II.

RMD Formula

RMD = prior year-end account balance ÷ applicable distribution period. Most owners use the Uniform Lifetime Table. A different table can apply to certain beneficiaries and spouses.

Example

At age 75, the Uniform Lifetime distribution period is 24.6. A $300,000 prior year-end balance produces an estimated RMD of about $12,195.12.

When Do RMDs Begin?

Under current federal law, the applicable beginning age is 73 for people who reach age 73 before 2033 under the SECURE 2.0 transition. The applicable age rises to 75 for later cohorts described in the statute. Account type and employment status can affect timing.

Limitations

This page does not handle inherited-account schedules, every employer-plan exception, first-year deferral choices, aggregated-account rules, QCDs, Roth-account exceptions, or penalties. Always verify the required amount with the account custodian and current IRS guidance.

Frequently Asked Questions

What balance is used for an RMD?

For a standard annual RMD, the calculation generally starts with the account balance at the close of business on December 31 of the previous year, then divides it by the applicable IRS distribution period.

Why is there a special rule for a much younger spouse?

If your spouse is your sole beneficiary and is more than 10 years younger, the IRS Joint and Last Survivor table can apply. That table typically uses a longer life-expectancy factor than the Uniform Lifetime table.

Does a Roth IRA have lifetime RMDs for the original owner?

Generally, Roth IRAs do not require lifetime RMDs for the original owner. Inherited Roth accounts can have distribution rules, so beneficiary situations should be checked separately.
