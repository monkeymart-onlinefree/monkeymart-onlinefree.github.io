What Is the Day Counter?

A day counter measures the number of calendar days between two dates. This version also separates Monday–Friday weekdays from weekend days and lets you choose whether to count both endpoint dates.

How to Use the Day Counter

Choose a start date and end date. Select elapsed days to count the distance from the start boundary to the end boundary, or inclusive days to count both dates. The page then reports total days, weeks plus remainder days, weekdays, and weekend days.

Day Counter Formula

Day difference = [UTC(end date) − UTC(start date)] ÷ 86,400,000 ms. Inclusive count = day difference + 1.

Formula Variables

Each date is converted to UTC midnight using its year, month, and day fields. Weekday counting iterates calendar dates and treats Sunday (0) and Saturday (6) as weekend days.

Calculation Example

From September 1 to September 8 is 7 elapsed days. If both September 1 and September 8 are included as counted dates, the inclusive count is 8 days.

Understanding Your Results

The primary total changes with the endpoint option. Weekday and weekend totals use the same selected counting convention, so their sum equals the displayed day count.

Important Factors

Public holidays are not automatically excluded. Country-specific working calendars, custom weekends, and historical calendar reforms require additional rules.

Limitations

This is a Gregorian browser-date calculator. It counts calendar dates, not business holidays, school days, trading days, or religious calendar dates.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the day counter include the end date?

You can choose. The default elapsed-day mode excludes the end date boundary, while the inclusive option adds one calendar day so both selected dates are counted.

How are weekdays counted?

Weekdays are Monday through Friday. The calculator does not remove public holidays because holiday calendars vary by country and year.

Why does this use UTC for date-only math?

Converting date-only values to UTC midnight avoids daylight-saving time changes creating 23- or 25-hour local days in the day count.

Sources and Methodology
