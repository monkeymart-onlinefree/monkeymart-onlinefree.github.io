What Is a Cash Back vs Low Interest Calculator?

This calculator compares two common vehicle incentives: a rebate paired with a higher APR, or a lower promotional APR without the rebate. It estimates monthly payments and total scheduled loan payments under the same vehicle, term, down payment, trade-in, tax, and fee assumptions.

How the Comparison Works

Each offer first gets an estimated financed principal. For the cash-back option, the rebate reduces that principal. Monthly payment uses the standard amortizing-loan formula. The offer with the lower total of scheduled payments is highlighted.

Example

On a $35,000 vehicle, a $3,000 rebate at 6.9% APR can be compared directly with 1.9% APR and no rebate over 60 months. The result changes with loan term, taxes, down payment, trade-in, and fees.

Why a Lower APR Is Not Automatically Cheaper

A promotional APR reduces financing cost, but giving up a large rebate increases the amount financed. The break-even point depends on the size of the rebate, rate difference, term, and principal.

Limitations

Vehicle-tax rules vary by jurisdiction, including whether rebates and trade-ins reduce the taxable amount. Dealer fees, manufacturer restrictions, credit qualification, prepayment, and timing can also change the real cost. Use the contract disclosures for a final decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I choose the offer with the lower monthly payment?

Not necessarily. A lower monthly payment can result from a lower APR, a smaller amount financed, or a longer term. Compare total cost over the same term and confirm that taxes and fees are treated the same.

Does a cash rebate always reduce sales tax?

No. Tax treatment of manufacturer rebates and trade-ins varies by jurisdiction. This calculator uses a stated generic assumption, so local rules should be checked before relying on the result.

What if I plan to repay the loan early?

Early payoff can reduce interest on many simple-interest auto loans, which may change the comparison. This calculator compares scheduled payments through the full term and does not model an early payoff date.
