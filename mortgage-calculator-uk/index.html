What Is the UK Mortgage Calculator?

The UK Mortgage Calculator is a planning tool for estimating the cost of a mortgage in British pounds. It calculates the mortgage amount from the property price and deposit, then applies a fixed-rate repayment formula to estimate the monthly principal-and-interest payment.

It can also help you compare repayment and interest-only costs, understand total interest over the mortgage term, check LTV, and see how the outstanding balance changes through an amortization schedule.

With a repayment mortgage, monthly payments reduce both the capital and interest. With an interest-only mortgage, monthly payments cover the interest while the original capital remains to be repaid separately.

How to Use the UK Mortgage Calculator

Enter the following values in the calculator:

Input What to Enter
Home price (£) The purchase price or property value in pounds.
Deposit (£) The amount you plan to pay upfront rather than borrow.
Mortgage term (years) The number of years over which the mortgage is calculated.

The council tax, insurance, and other recurring-cost fields do not change the core principal-and-interest mortgage formula.

Formula / Calculation Method

Mortgage Amount

The amount borrowed is based on the home price minus the deposit:

P = H − D

Where:

  • P = mortgage principal or amount borrowed (£)
  • H = home price (£)
  • D = deposit (£)

Monthly Interest Rate

The calculator converts the entered annual percentage rate to a monthly decimal rate:

r = (R ÷ 100) ÷ 12

Where:

  • r = monthly interest rate
  • R = annual interest rate (%)

Using an annual rate divided by 12 for monthly payments is consistent with the fixed-period loan method used by standard financial payment calculations.

Number of Payments

For monthly repayments:

n = Y × 12

Where:

  • n = total number of monthly payments
  • Y = mortgage term in years

Main Repayment Mortgage Formula

The fixed-rate monthly repayment is:

M = P × [r(1 + r)ⁿ] ÷ [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1]

Where:

  • M = monthly repayment (£)
  • P = amount borrowed (£)
  • r = monthly interest rate
  • n = total number of monthly payments

This is the standard fixed-payment amortization method for a loan with a constant periodic interest rate and regular payments.

Interest-Only Payment

For the calculator's fixed-rate planning estimate, an interest-only payment covers the interest on the mortgage balance rather than reducing the borrowed capital:

Interest-only monthly payment = P × r

With an interest-only mortgage, the capital normally remains payable at the end of the mortgage term.

Total Mortgage Payments

For a constant monthly repayment:

Total mortgage payments = M × n

Total Interest

Total interest represents the amount paid above the original mortgage principal under the calculator's constant-rate model:

Total interest = (M × n) − P

Loan-to-Value Ratio

LTV compares the amount borrowed with the property's value:

LTV = (P ÷ H) × 100

For example, borrowing £320,000 against a £400,000 property gives an LTV of 80%.

The Bank of England defines LTV as the ratio of the amount borrowed to the value of the underlying property or collateral.

Amortization Schedule

For each monthly repayment, the outstanding balance is progressively reduced.

For a payment period:

Interest portion = Previous balance × r
Principal portion = Monthly payment − Interest portion
New balance = Previous balance − Principal portion

Early payments generally contain a larger interest component because the outstanding balance is higher. As the balance falls, more of the fixed payment goes towards principal.

Worked Example

Suppose you are considering the following mortgage:

Input Example
Home price £400,000
Deposit £80,000
Mortgage term 30 years
Annual interest rate 4.25%
Council tax / other annual tax £2,400
Home insurance £360/year
Other recurring costs £1,200/year

Step 1: Calculate the Mortgage Amount

P = £400,000 − £80,000
P = £320,000

Step 2: Convert the Interest Rate

r = (4.25 ÷ 100) ÷ 12
r = 0.0035416667

The mortgage has:

n = 30 × 12 = 360 monthly payments

Step 3: Calculate the Monthly Repayment

M = 320,000 × [0.0035416667 × (1.0035416667)³⁶⁰] ÷ [(1.0035416667)³⁶⁰ − 1]
Monthly repayment ≈ £1,574.21

Step 4: Interest-Only Estimate

£320,000 × 0.0035416667 ≈ £1,133.33 per month

Step 5: Total Interest

Total repayment over 360 payments:

£1,574.207651... × 360 ≈ £566,714.75

Total interest:

£566,714.75 − £320,000 = £246,714.75

Step 6: Calculate LTV

LTV = (£320,000 ÷ £400,000) × 100
LTV = 80%

First Amortization Payment

For the first month:

Interest ≈ £1,133.33
Principal repaid ≈ £440.87
Remaining balance ≈ £319,559.13

Final Result

For this example, the estimated repayment mortgage payment is £1,574.21 per month, while the fixed-rate interest-only estimate is £1,133.33 per month. Under the calculator's constant-rate assumptions, total interest over 30 years is approximately £246,714.75, and the starting LTV is 80%.

Understanding Your Results

Monthly Repayment

This is the estimated monthly principal-and-interest payment required to amortize the mortgage over the selected term at the entered interest rate.

It does not automatically mean this is the amount a lender will quote.

Interest-Only Payment

This shows the estimated monthly interest cost without repayment of the original capital.

A lower interest-only monthly payment should not be interpreted as a lower total obligation because the borrowed capital still needs to be repaid.

Total Interest

Total interest shows the estimated interest paid over the full term if the rate and scheduled repayments remain unchanged.

A longer mortgage term can reduce the monthly payment while increasing the amount of interest paid over time.

Loan Amount

The loan amount is the home price minus your deposit. Increasing the deposit reduces the amount that needs to be financed.

LTV

LTV expresses the mortgage amount as a percentage of the property's value.

It is a financing ratio, not an affordability calculation. The calculator does not determine whether a lender will approve a mortgage.

Amortization Schedule

The amortization schedule shows how each repayment is divided between interest and principal and how the outstanding mortgage balance changes over time.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator is a fixed-rate planning model. It assumes the entered annual interest rate remains constant and uses monthly payments throughout the selected mortgage term.

It does not model product or arrangement fees, changing fixed or variable rates, lender affordability assessments, early repayment charges, mortgage overpayments, lender-specific daily-interest methods, Stamp Duty Land Tax or equivalent property taxes, conveyancing costs, or future remortgage terms.

Council tax, home insurance, and other recurring-cost inputs are supporting property-cost estimates and are separate from the core mortgage repayment formula.

Actual mortgage products can use lender-specific terms, fees, payment dates, interest calculations, and eligibility requirements. MoneyHelper also notes that calculator results are estimates and that the lender confirms the actual repayment amount.

The result should therefore be used for planning and comparison rather than treated as a mortgage offer, affordability decision, or guaranteed future payment.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a UK mortgage payment calculated?

The calculator first subtracts your deposit from the home price to find the mortgage principal. It then converts the annual interest rate to a monthly rate and applies the fixed-payment amortization formula over the selected number of monthly payments.

What is the difference between a repayment and an interest-only mortgage?

A repayment mortgage payment includes both interest and part of the capital, so the balance gradually falls. An interest-only payment covers interest while the borrowed capital remains outstanding and must be repaid separately.

Does a larger deposit reduce monthly mortgage repayments?

Yes, when all other calculator inputs remain unchanged. A larger deposit reduces the mortgage principal used in the payment formula, which reduces the calculated repayment.

What does 80% LTV mean?

An 80% LTV means the mortgage equals 80% of the property's value. For a £400,000 property, an £320,000 mortgage represents an 80% LTV.

Does the calculator include mortgage fees?

No. The fixed-rate model does not automatically include mortgage product fees, arrangement fees, conveyancing costs, early repayment charges, or similar lender-specific costs.

Will my mortgage payment stay the same for the entire term?

The calculation assumes the entered rate remains constant. An actual mortgage payment can change when a fixed-rate period ends or when a variable or tracker rate changes.

Is this calculator the same as a lender affordability assessment?

No. It estimates mortgage payments from the financial inputs entered. Lenders use their own affordability criteria and may consider income, spending, existing debts, credit information, and other factors.
