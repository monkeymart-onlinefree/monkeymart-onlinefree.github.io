What is a UK Mortgage Calculator?

A UK Mortgage Calculator estimates the regular payment required to repay a mortgage over a chosen term at a specified interest rate. This version uses pounds sterling and also shows an interest-only comparison, loan-to-value ratio (LTV), total interest, optional recurring housing costs, and an annual amortization summary.

UK mortgage repayment formula

M = P × r(1+r)n ÷ ((1+r)n − 1)

M is the monthly repayment, P is the amount borrowed, r is the monthly interest rate, and n is the number of monthly payments. For this planning estimate, the entered annual rate is divided by 12 to obtain the monthly rate.

Repayment vs interest-only

With a repayment mortgage, each monthly payment covers interest and some capital, so the balance is scheduled to reach zero by the end of the term if the rate stays constant. With an interest-only mortgage, the monthly payment only covers interest and the capital still has to be repaid separately at the end.

Understanding loan-to-value

LTV is the mortgage amount divided by the property price, expressed as a percentage. A £375,000 mortgage on a £500,000 property has an LTV of 75%.

Limitations

This is a fixed-rate planning model. It does not model product fees, changing rates, affordability checks, early repayment charges, overpayments, lender-specific daily interest, stamp duty, conveyancing costs, or future remortgage terms. Your lender’s illustration controls the actual payment.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much deposit does the calculator require?

It does not impose a lender rule. Enter the deposit amount you want to test; the tool calculates the resulting loan and LTV.

Why is the all-in monthly figure higher?

It adds any annual council tax, insurance, or recurring costs you entered, divided by 12. Those costs are not part of the mortgage principal-and-interest payment.

Does a longer mortgage term reduce monthly payments?

Usually yes, but a longer term can increase total interest because the balance remains outstanding for longer.

Is interest-only cheaper overall?

Its monthly interest payment can be lower, but the original capital remains due at the end unless you have a separate repayment plan.

Sources and Methodology

The user-facing inputs and outputs were cross-checked against Calculator.net’s UK Mortgage Calculator. Repayment and interest-only concepts were cross-checked with MoneyHelper and its mortgage repayment guidance. The calculation uses the standard fixed-payment amortization formula and assumes the entered rate stays constant for the full modeled term.
