What Is a Target Heart Rate?

Target heart rate is a training range used to approximate exercise intensity. The American Heart Association describes moderate activity at about 50%–70% of estimated maximum heart rate and vigorous activity at about 70%–85%.

Maximum Heart Rate Formulas

Haskell/Fox guide: HRmax = 220 − age
Tanaka: HRmax = 208 − 0.7 × age
Nes: HRmax = 211 − 0.64 × age

Karvonen Formula

Target HR = Resting HR + Intensity × (HRmax − Resting HR)

This method uses heart-rate reserve, so it requires an accurate resting heart rate. If you do not know your resting rate, use percentage of estimated maximum.

Understanding Your Results

Age-based maximum-heart-rate equations are estimates. The AHA says its target ranges are averages and general guides. Some medications and heart conditions can change appropriate exercise heart rate.

Safety

If you have cardiovascular disease, symptoms, take medications that affect heart rate, or are starting exercise after a long period of inactivity, get individualized guidance from a qualified healthcare professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What target heart rate does the American Heart Association suggest?

AHA describes moderate-intensity activity as about 50%–70% of estimated maximum heart rate and vigorous activity as about 70%–85%, using the numbers as general guides.

What is the Karvonen formula?

Karvonen uses heart-rate reserve: target HR = resting HR + intensity × (maximum HR − resting HR). It requires a resting heart rate.

Are age-based maximum heart-rate formulas exact?

No. They are population averages and individual maximum heart rate can differ substantially. A measured maximum from an appropriate supervised test is more individualized.
