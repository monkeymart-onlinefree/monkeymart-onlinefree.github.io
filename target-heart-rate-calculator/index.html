The Target Heart Rate Calculator estimates your maximum heart rate and exercise target heart rate range in beats per minute (bpm). Enter your age, choose a maximum heart rate formula, and optionally add your resting heart rate to calculate zones with the Karvonen heart-rate-reserve method.

What Is the Target Heart Rate Calculator?

A target heart rate is an estimated heart rate range associated with a selected exercise intensity. This calculator first estimates maximum heart rate from age using one of three supported equations, then calculates a target range using either a percentage of maximum heart rate or the Karvonen heart-rate-reserve method.

The result can be useful for planning and monitoring exercise intensity. It is an estimate rather than a measurement of your actual maximum heart rate.

How to Use the Target Heart Rate Calculator

Enter the following information:

Age: Enter your age in years. Age is used by the selected equation to estimate maximum heart rate.

Maximum HR formula: Choose the age-based equation you want the calculator to use:

  • 220 − age
  • Tanaka formula
  • Nes formula

Resting heart rate: Enter your resting heart rate in beats per minute when using the Karvonen method. Resting heart rate is normally measured while you are relaxed and at rest.

Zone method: Select whether the target heart rate should be based directly on a percentage of estimated maximum heart rate or calculated from heart rate reserve using the Karvonen method.

Heart rate values are expressed in beats per minute (bpm).

Formula / Calculation Method

Maximum Heart Rate Formulas

The calculator supports three age-based maximum heart rate estimates.

220 − age method

HRmax=220−AgeHR\_{max} = 220 - Age

The American Heart Association also uses approximately 220 minus age as a general guide for estimating maximum heart rate.

Tanaka formula

HRmax=208−(0.7×Age)HR\_{max} = 208 - (0.7 \times Age)

Tanaka, Monahan, and Seals developed this equation from a meta-analysis and laboratory validation study involving healthy adults.

Nes formula

HRmax=211−(0.64×Age)HR\_{max} = 211 - (0.64 \times Age)

The Nes equation was derived from maximal exercise testing in 3,320 healthy men and women in the HUNT Fitness Study.

Variables

  • HRmax = estimated maximum heart rate, in bpm
  • Age = age in years
  • RHR = resting heart rate, in bpm
  • HRR = heart rate reserve, in bpm
  • I = selected exercise intensity expressed as a decimal

For example, 70% intensity is entered into the calculation as 0.70.

Percent of Maximum Heart Rate Method

When the simple percentage-of-maximum method is selected:

Target HR=I×HRmaxTarget\ HR = I \times HR\_{max}

For a target intensity range:

Lower Target=Ilow×HRmaxLower\ Target = I\_{low} \times HR\_{max} Upper Target=Ihigh×HRmaxUpper\ Target = I\_{high} \times HR\_{max}

This method does not require a resting heart rate.

Karvonen Heart Rate Reserve Method

The Karvonen method first calculates heart rate reserve:

HRR=HRmax−RHRHRR = HR\_{max} - RHR

The target heart rate is then:

Target HR=RHR+I(HRmax−RHR)Target\ HR = RHR + I(HR\_{max} - RHR)

For a range:

Lower Target=RHR+Ilow(HRmax−RHR)Lower\ Target = RHR + I\_{low}(HR\_{max} - RHR) Upper Target=RHR+Ihigh(HRmax−RHR)Upper\ Target = RHR + I\_{high}(HR\_{max} - RHR)

This method accounts for resting heart rate instead of applying the exercise-intensity percentage directly to maximum heart rate. The same heart-rate-reserve calculation is used in published exercise guidance and research.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Read the entered age.
  2. Apply the selected maximum heart rate equation.
  3. Read the selected exercise intensity or intensity range.
  4. If percent-of-maximum is selected, multiply the estimated maximum heart rate by the selected percentage.
  5. If Karvonen is selected, subtract resting heart rate from maximum heart rate to calculate heart rate reserve.
  6. Multiply heart rate reserve by the selected intensity and add resting heart rate.
  7. Calculate both the lower and upper values when an intensity range is used.

Worked Example

Suppose a 40-year-old user has a resting heart rate of 70 bpm and selects:

  • Maximum HR formula: Tanaka
  • Zone method: Karvonen
  • Intensity range: 50% to 70%

Inputs

Age = 40 years
Resting heart rate = 70 bpm
Lower intensity = 50% = 0.50
Upper intensity = 70% = 0.70

Step 1: Estimate Maximum Heart Rate

Using the Tanaka equation:

HRmax=208−(0.7×40)HR\_{max} = 208 - (0.7 \times 40) HRmax=208−28=180 bpmHR\_{max} = 208 - 28 = 180\ bpm

Step 2: Calculate Heart Rate Reserve

HRR=180−70HRR = 180 - 70 HRR=110 bpmHRR = 110\ bpm

Lower=70+(0.50×110)Lower = 70 + (0.50 \times 110) Lower=70+55=125 bpmLower = 70 + 55 = 125\ bpm

Step 4: Calculate the Upper Target

Upper=70+(0.70×110)Upper = 70 + (0.70 \times 110) Upper=70+77=147 bpmUpper = 70 + 77 = 147\ bpm

Final Result

The estimated target heart rate range is:

125–147 bpm

This result applies specifically to the selected Tanaka maximum-HR formula, Karvonen method, resting heart rate, and 50%–70% intensity range.

Understanding Your Results

Estimated Maximum Heart Rate

Maximum heart rate is the estimated upper heart-rate value produced by the selected age-based equation. It is measured in bpm.

It should not be treated as a measured personal maximum. Age-prediction formulas have individual error and can produce different estimates for the same person.

Target Heart Rate Range

The target range shows the lower and upper heart rates corresponding to the selected exercise intensity.

The result depends on both the selected maximum-HR formula and the zone method. Changing either option can change the calculated range.

Heart Rate Reserve

When the Karvonen method is used, heart rate reserve represents the difference between estimated maximum heart rate and resting heart rate:

HRR=HRmax−RHRHRR = HR\_{max} - RHR

A different resting heart rate can therefore produce a different Karvonen target zone even when two people are the same age.

Current Exercise Guidance

Reviewed for current guidance: September 2026.

The American Heart Association currently describes approximately 50%–70% of maximum heart rate as a general range for moderate-intensity activity and approximately 70%–85% for vigorous activity. These figures are population-level averages and are intended as general guidance rather than individualized exercise prescriptions.

The calculator can perform calculations for the intensity range selected in the tool; these AHA ranges should not be interpreted as mandatory limits for every person.

Assumptions and Limitations

Age-based maximum heart rate equations provide estimates, not direct measurements. Tanaka's formula was developed for healthy adults, while the Nes equation was derived from healthy participants in the HUNT Fitness Study. The Nes study reported substantial individual prediction variation, with a standard error of estimate of about 10.8 bpm.

Prediction equations may also perform differently in specific populations. Research has found limitations when adult age-based equations are applied to children and adolescents, for example.

Resting heart rate can vary with fitness, stress, sleep, illness, temperature, caffeine, hydration, and other factors. An inaccurate resting value directly affects Karvonen calculations.

Medications and medical conditions can also change heart-rate responses. The American Heart Association specifically notes that some medications can lower maximum and target heart rates and recommends discussing an appropriate target with a health professional when relevant.

This calculator estimates exercise heart-rate values. It does not diagnose a health condition, assess whether strenuous exercise is safe, or replace exercise testing or individualized medical advice.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the formula for target heart rate?

The calculator supports two approaches. The simple method is:

Target HR=Intensity×HRmaxTarget\ HR = Intensity \times HR\_{max}

The Karvonen method is:

Target HR=RHR+Intensity(HRmax−RHR)Target\ HR = RHR + Intensity(HR\_{max} - RHR)
Which maximum heart rate formulas does the calculator use?

The calculator supports:

220−Age220 - Age 208−0.7(Age)208 - 0.7(Age)

and

211−0.64(Age)211 - 0.64(Age)

These equations can produce slightly different maximum heart rate and target-zone estimates.

What is the difference between percent of maximum HR and Karvonen?

Percent-of-maximum applies the intensity percentage directly to estimated maximum heart rate. Karvonen first calculates heart rate reserve and then adds resting heart rate back into the result.

Because Karvonen includes resting heart rate, two people of the same age can receive different target values.

Do I need to enter my resting heart rate?

Resting heart rate is needed for the Karvonen calculation. It is not required when the calculator uses a simple percentage of maximum heart rate.

Is 220 minus age an exact maximum heart rate?

No. The American Heart Association describes maximum heart rate from 220 minus age as an approximate value, and research shows substantial individual variation.

Why does the Tanaka or Nes formula give a different result?

Each equation was derived from different research data and uses a different relationship between age and maximum heart rate. A small difference in estimated HRmax also changes the resulting target heart rate range.

Can I use this calculator if I take heart medication?

Use the result cautiously. Certain medications, including drugs that affect heart rate, can make standard age-based exercise targets inappropriate. A health professional can provide a more suitable exercise target when medication or a cardiovascular condition affects your heart-rate response.
