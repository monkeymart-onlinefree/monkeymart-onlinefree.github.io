The Overweight Calculator uses your height and weight to calculate your adult Body Mass Index (BMI), identify the corresponding adult BMI category, and estimate the weight range that corresponds to a BMI of 18.5–24.9 for your height. Age is entered to help determine whether adult BMI interpretation is appropriate; it is not part of the BMI equation itself.

What Is the Overweight Calculator?

The Overweight Calculator is a screening tool for comparing body weight with height using BMI. It can show whether an adult BMI falls in the underweight, healthy weight, overweight, or obesity range and can calculate the reference weight range associated with BMI values from 18.5 through 24.9.

BMI is a screening measure rather than a direct measurement of body fat or a medical diagnosis. CDC recommends considering BMI together with other health information when assessing an individual.

How to Use the Overweight Calculator

Enter the information requested by the calculator:

  • Age: Enter your age. Age does not appear in the adult BMI formula, but adult BMI categories are intended for people age 20 and older.
  • Height (cm): Enter height in centimeters when using metric measurements.
  • Weight (kg): Enter weight in kilograms for the metric calculation.
  • Height (ft): Enter the feet portion of your height when using U.S. customary units.
  • Height (in): Enter the remaining inches of your height.
  • Weight (lb): Enter weight in pounds for the U.S. customary calculation.

Use either the metric height-and-weight fields or the U.S. customary fields for the calculation.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

For metric measurements:

BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ Height (m)²

If height is entered in centimeters:

Height (m) = Height (cm) ÷ 100

For U.S. customary measurements:

BMI = 703 × Weight (lb) ÷ Height (in)²

These are the standard BMI formulas used by CDC.

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
BMI Body Mass Index Conventionally expressed as kg/m²
W Body weight kg or lb
H Height used in the BMI equation meters or total inches
Hcm Height entered in metric units cm
Hft Feet portion of U.S. height ft
Hin Inches portion of U.S. height in

For U.S. customary measurements, feet and inches are combined before calculating BMI:

Total height in inches = (Feet × 12) + Inches

Calculation Sequence

  1. Read the entered height and weight.
  2. Convert centimeters to meters for a metric calculation, or convert feet and inches to total inches for a U.S. customary calculation.
  3. Calculate BMI using the appropriate formula.
  4. Compare the BMI with the adult BMI category thresholds.
  5. Rearrange the BMI formula to calculate the weights corresponding to BMI 18.5 and 24.9 for the entered height.
  6. Present the BMI, category, and reference weight range.

Reference Weight Range

The calculator uses the same BMI equation in reverse to determine the weight associated with BMI 18.5 and 24.9.

For metric measurements:

Lower reference weight (kg) = 18.5 × Height (m)²
Upper reference weight (kg) = 24.9 × Height (m)²

For U.S. customary measurements:

Lower reference weight (lb) = 18.5 × Height (in)² ÷ 703
Upper reference weight (lb) = 24.9 × Height (in)² ÷ 703

This range is based on the adult BMI healthy-weight category and the person's entered height. It is a BMI-based reference range, not an individualized target weight.

Worked Example

Suppose an adult enters:

Inputs

  • Age: 35
  • Height: 175 cm
  • Weight: 85 kg

Step 1: Convert Height to Meters

175 ÷ 100 = 1.75 m

Step 2: Calculate BMI

BMI = 85 ÷ 1.75²
BMI = 85 ÷ 3.0625
BMI ≈ 27.76

A BMI of about 27.8 falls within the CDC adult overweight range of 25 to less than 30.

Step 3: Calculate the Reference Weight Range

Lower end:

18.5 × 1.75² = 56.65625 kg

Upper end:

24.9 × 1.75² = 76.25625 kg

Final Result

For a height of 175 cm and a weight of 85 kg:

  • BMI: approximately 27.8
  • Adult BMI category: Overweight
  • BMI 18.5–24.9 reference weight range: approximately 56.7–76.3 kg

Intermediate calculations are best kept at full precision before rounding the displayed result.

Understanding Your Results

BMI

BMI represents weight relative to height. A higher or lower BMI changes the adult weight-status category, but BMI does not directly measure body fat.

BMI Category

The category shows where the calculated BMI falls within the CDC adult BMI ranges. For adults, BMI categories are interpreted independently of sex or race.

Reference Weight Range

The reference weight range shows the weights at your height that mathematically correspond to BMI values from 18.5 to 24.9.

It should not be interpreted as a personalized prescription for how much you should weigh. Individual health assessment can involve medical history, physical examination, laboratory results, body composition, and other factors.

Current Rules / Data

As of September 2026, CDC's current adult BMI categories for people age 20 and older are:

Adult BMI Category BMI Range
Underweight Below 18.5
Healthy Weight 18.5 to less than 25
Overweight 25 to less than 30
Obesity 30 or greater

The calculator uses 18.5 through 24.9 as the endpoints for its BMI-based reference weight range.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator uses the standard adult BMI relationship between height and weight. Several limitations are important when interpreting the result.

BMI does not distinguish body fat from muscle, bone, or other lean mass, and it does not indicate where body fat is distributed. Two people with the same BMI can therefore have different body compositions.

The adult categories are intended for people 20 years and older. Children and teenagers ages 2 through 19 should be assessed using sex-specific BMI-for-age percentiles instead of adult BMI categories.

The result also depends on accurate height and weight measurements. Differences in unit conversion or when values are rounded can cause small differences between calculators.

The calculated reference weight range is derived only from height and the BMI values 18.5 and 24.9. It does not account for individual body composition, medical conditions, or other personal health factors.

BMI results are screening information and should not be treated as a diagnosis or as individualized medical advice.

Frequently Asked Questions

What BMI is considered overweight for an adult?

CDC classifies an adult BMI of 25 to less than 30 as overweight. A BMI of 30 or higher falls within the obesity category.

Does age change the BMI formula?

No. The adult BMI equation uses height and weight. Age does not appear in the mathematical formula. However, age matters when choosing how BMI should be interpreted. CDC uses adult BMI categories for people age 20 and older.

How does the calculator find my reference weight range?

It rearranges the BMI equation and calculates the weight that would produce BMI 18.5 and BMI 24.9 at your entered height.

For example:

Weight = BMI × Height²

The appropriate unit conversion is applied when U.S. customary measurements are used.

Is BMI the same as body fat percentage?

No. BMI compares weight with height. It does not directly measure the percentage of the body made up of fat and cannot separate fat mass from muscle or bone mass.

Can someone with a high amount of muscle have a high BMI?

Yes. Because BMI does not distinguish muscle from body fat, greater muscle mass can contribute to a higher BMI even when body composition differs substantially from another person with the same BMI.

Can people under age 20 use adult BMI categories?

CDC recommends BMI-for-age percentiles for children and teenagers ages 2 through 19. Adult BMI categories are intended for people age 20 and older.

Is an overweight BMI a medical diagnosis?

No. BMI is a screening measure. It can provide useful information about weight relative to height, but it does not diagnose a disease or determine an individual's health by itself.
