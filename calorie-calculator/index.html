What Is a Calorie Calculator?

Answer: A calorie calculator estimates daily energy needs. This page first predicts resting energy expenditure with the Mifflin–St Jeor equation, then multiplies it by a selected activity factor to approximate maintenance calories.

How to Use the Calorie Calculator

  1. Enter sex, age, weight, and height.
  2. Select an activity factor that best represents your typical activity.
  3. Enter an optional calorie adjustment for a planning target.
  4. Review BMR, maintenance calories, and the adjusted target.

Mifflin–St Jeor Calorie Formula

Men: REE = 10W + 6.25H − 5A + 5 Women: REE = 10W + 6.25H − 5A − 161 Estimated maintenance ≈ REE × activity factor

Calculation Example

For a 30-year-old, 70 kg, 175 cm male, the Mifflin–St Jeor equation estimates resting energy expenditure first. Multiplying by 1.55 gives a moderate-activity maintenance estimate.

Assumptions and Limitations

Activity multipliers are approximate, resting-energy equations have prediction error, and calorie needs can change with activity, body composition, health status, adaptation, pregnancy, medications, and other factors.

Calorie Calculator FAQs

What is maintenance calories?

Maintenance calories are an estimate of energy intake that would match total energy expenditure and therefore tend to maintain body weight over time.

Is BMR the same as total daily calories?

No. BMR estimates resting energy needs; total daily needs also include physical activity and other components of energy expenditure.

Is a 500-calorie deficit required?

No. This calculator lets you choose the adjustment. Individual weight-management plans should consider health, sustainability, and professional guidance when appropriate.
