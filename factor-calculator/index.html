The Factor Calculator finds the positive factors of a positive integer and shows its prime factorization. Enter one whole positive number to see which integers divide it exactly and how the number can be expressed as a product of prime powers.

What Is the Factor Calculator?

A factor is an integer that divides another integer exactly, with no remainder. This calculator helps you find all positive factors of a number and its prime factorization.

It is useful for checking divisibility, studying prime factors, simplifying number problems, and working with related concepts such as greatest common factors and least common multiples.

How to Use the Factor Calculator

  1. Enter a Positive Integer: Enter the whole positive number you want to factor.
  2. Calculate: Run the calculation to find its exact positive factors.
  3. Review the Results: Check the positive factor list and the number's prime factorization.

The input must be a positive integer. Decimals, fractions, negative numbers, and zero are outside the intended input type.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

For an integer greater than 1, prime factorization can be written as:

n = p₁^a₁ × p₂^a₂ × … × pₖ^aₖ

where the number is expressed as powers of prime numbers. Every integer greater than 1 has a unique prime factorization apart from the order of the prime factors.

A positive integer d is a factor of n when it divides n with no remainder:

n mod d = 0

or equivalently:

d | n

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
n Positive integer being factored Integer
d Positive factor or divisor of n Integer
pᵢ A distinct prime factor Prime integer
aᵢ Exponent showing how many times pᵢ occurs Whole number

Calculation Sequence

  1. Read the entered positive integer n.
  2. Identify positive integers d that divide n with zero remainder.
  3. Collect those exact divisors as the positive factors.
  4. For n > 1, break the number into prime factors.
  5. Group repeated prime factors and write them using exponents.

The calculation is based on exact integer divisibility and prime-power factorization. No additional factor-count or unrelated mathematical result is required.

Positive Factors

A positive factor satisfies:

n ÷ d = an integer

For example, 6 is a factor of 30 because:

30 ÷ 6 = 5

There is no remainder.

Prime Factorization

Prime factorization expresses the number using only prime-number factors:

n = p₁^a₁ × p₂^a₂ × …

For example:

72 = 2³ × 3²

because:

2³ × 3² = 8 × 9 = 72

Understanding Your Results

Positive Factors

The factor list contains the positive integers that divide the entered number exactly. Both 1 and the number itself are factors of every positive integer.

Prime Factorization

The prime factorization shows how the number can be built entirely from prime numbers. For integers greater than 1, this representation is unique except for rearranging the order of the prime factors.

For example:

84 = 2² × 3 × 7

If a number greater than 1 has only 1 and itself as positive factors, it is a prime number.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator is designed for positive integers. It does not factor decimals, fractions, negative numbers, zero, algebraic expressions, or polynomials.

Only positive factors are reported. Although a positive integer also has corresponding negative divisors, they are outside this calculator's stated purpose.

The number 1 is a special case. Its only positive factor is 1, and it is not a prime number. The usual prime-factorization theorem applies to positive integers greater than 1.

The calculator performs numerical factorization; it is not a symbolic proof tool.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a factor of a number?

A factor is an integer that divides another integer exactly without leaving a remainder. For example, 4 is a factor of 20 because 20 ÷ 4 = 5.

What is the difference between factors and prime factors?

Factors include every positive integer that divides the number exactly. Prime factors are only the prime numbers that multiply together to produce the original number.

For example, the factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 12, while its prime factorization is 2² × 3.

Is 1 a prime number?

No. The number 1 has only one positive divisor, while a prime number has exactly two positive divisors: 1 and itself.

Does the calculator include negative factors?

No. This calculator is designed to return positive factors.

How can I tell whether a number is prime?

For an integer greater than 1, if its only positive factors are 1 and the number itself, the number is prime.

Can I enter a decimal or fraction?

No. The required input is a positive integer. Decimal and fractional values are not part of this calculator's calculation method.

Does changing the order of prime factors change the answer?

No. Multiplication is commutative, so changing the order does not change the number. The prime factorization of an integer greater than 1 is unique apart from the order in which the prime factors are written.
