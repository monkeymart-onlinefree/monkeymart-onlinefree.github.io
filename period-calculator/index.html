Use the Period Calculator to predict your upcoming period start dates and estimate an approximate ovulation date from your usual menstrual cycle pattern. Enter the first day of your last period, your typical bleeding duration, and your average cycle length to calculate the estimate.

What Is the Period Calculator?

The Period Calculator is a cycle-planning tool that estimates when your next periods may start based on the first day of your last period and your average menstrual cycle length. It also provides an approximate ovulation date using the calculator's cycle-based ovulation method.

A menstrual cycle is counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next period. Actual cycle length and ovulation timing can vary between people and from one cycle to another.

This calculator is useful for tracking expected period dates, planning around your cycle, or understanding your usual menstrual pattern. Its results are estimates rather than a medical diagnosis or a guarantee of when menstruation or ovulation will occur.

How to Use the Period Calculator

Enter the following information:

First day of last period: Select the calendar date on which your most recent period started. This date is treated as cycle day 1.

Typical bleeding (days): Enter the number of days your menstrual bleeding usually lasts. Use your usual duration rather than an unusually short or long period.

Average cycle length (days): Enter the usual number of days from the first day of one period to the first day of the next period.

After entering these values, calculate the result to see your predicted upcoming period dates and approximate ovulation timing.

Formula / Calculation Method

Next Period Formula

The calculator predicts the next period start using:

Next period start = Last period start + Average cycle length

Later projected periods repeat the same cycle interval:

Projected period n = Last period start + (n × Average cycle length)

where n = 1 is the next predicted period, n = 2 is the following period, and so on.

Estimated Ovulation Formula

The calculator estimates the ovulation cycle day as:

Estimated ovulation cycle day = Average cycle length − 14

Because the first day of the last period is cycle day 1, the calendar-date offset is one day less:

Estimated ovulation date = Last period start + (Average cycle length − 15) days

For example, with a 28-day cycle:

28 − 14 = cycle day 14

Cycle day 14 is 13 calendar days after cycle day 1.

The 14-day calculation is an approximation used by this calculator. Ovulation does not always happen exactly 14 days before the next period. Official health guidance notes that the interval between ovulation and the next period can vary.

Variables

Variable Meaning Unit
L First day of the last period Calendar date
C Average menstrual cycle length Days
n Number of cycles being projected Cycle number
Pₙ Predicted start date of a future period Calendar date
O Approximate ovulation date Calendar date

The period-start equation can be written as:

Pₙ = L + nC

The ovulation-date calculation is:

O = L + (C − 15 days)

Calculation Sequence

  1. The first day of the last period is treated as cycle day 1.
  2. The average cycle length is added to that date to predict the next period start.
  3. The same cycle interval is repeated for later period projections.
  4. The calculator subtracts 14 from the cycle length to estimate the ovulation cycle day.
  5. One day is subtracted when converting that cycle-day number into a calendar-date offset because the starting date is already day 1.

The core next-period start calculation depends on the last period start date and average cycle length. Typical bleeding duration describes the user's usual menstrual pattern but does not change the start-date formula shown above.

Worked Example

Inputs

Suppose you enter:

  • First day of last period: September 1, 2026
  • Typical bleeding: 5 days
  • Average cycle length: 28 days

Step 1: Calculate the Next Period

Next period = September 1, 2026 + 28 days
Next period = September 29, 2026

Step 2: Calculate Later Period Projections

The same 28-day interval is repeated:

Second projected period = September 29 + 28 days = October 27, 2026
Third projected period = October 27 + 28 days = November 24, 2026

Step 3: Estimate Ovulation

First calculate the estimated ovulation cycle day:

28 − 14 = cycle day 14

Because September 1 is cycle day 1, cycle day 14 is 13 calendar days later:

September 1 + 13 days = September 14, 2026

Final Result

For this example:

  • Next predicted period: September 29, 2026
  • Later projected starts: October 27 and November 24, 2026
  • Approximate ovulation date: September 14, 2026

These dates are estimates based on a consistent 28-day cycle.

Understanding Your Results

Predicted Period Date

This is the estimated start date of your next menstrual period. Future dates are calculated by repeatedly adding your average cycle length.

A prediction may be close when your cycles are consistent, but normal month-to-month variation can cause your actual period to begin earlier or later.

Future Period Projections

Later dates show what your cycle calendar may look like if the same average cycle length continues. The farther into the future a prediction goes, the more opportunity there is for small cycle variations to affect the actual date.

Estimated Ovulation Date

The calculator estimates ovulation using a simplified assumption that ovulation occurs approximately 14 days before the following period.

This should be treated as an approximate planning date. NHS guidance notes that ovulation commonly occurs roughly 10 to 16 days before the next period, while the U.S. Office on Women's Health also notes that the interval can vary between people and cycles.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator assumes that your average cycle length is a useful predictor of future cycles. If your cycle length changes frequently, the predicted dates may be less reliable.

The ovulation estimate is also simplified. Actual ovulation can occur earlier or later than the calculated date, so the result should not be treated as confirmation that ovulation occurred on that day.

Changes related to stress, illness, postpartum recovery, breastfeeding, perimenopause, medications, hormonal contraception, or other health factors can affect menstrual timing.

Do not use a predicted ovulation date as a reliable method of contraception. Government fertility tools similarly warn that calendar-based fertile-window estimates are not guarantees of pregnancy timing or birth control.

This calculator is intended for planning and educational purposes. It does not diagnose menstrual, hormonal, fertility, or pregnancy-related conditions.

Talk with a health care professional if you develop new or persistent cycle changes, unusually heavy bleeding, severe period pain, or other symptoms that concern you. ACOG identifies persistent irregularity, prolonged or heavy bleeding, and significant changes from a person's usual pattern as reasons for medical evaluation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Period Calculator predict my next period?

It adds your average menstrual cycle length to the first day of your last period. For example, a 28-day cycle beginning September 1 gives a predicted next start date of September 29.

Is ovulation always exactly 14 days before a period?

No. The calculator uses approximately 14 days as its estimation method, but real ovulation timing varies. Health guidance describes a range rather than one exact day.

Why might my period arrive before or after the predicted date?

The calculation assumes that future cycles will be similar to your average cycle length. Natural variation, stress, illness, hormonal changes, medications, postpartum changes, and other factors can change the actual date.

What should I enter for average cycle length?

Use the typical number of days from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. If you track several cycles, use a value that represents your usual pattern rather than relying on one unusually short or long cycle.

Does my bleeding duration change the next-period calculation?

The core predicted start-date formula uses the first day of your last period and your average cycle length. Bleeding duration records how long your period typically lasts but does not change that start-date formula.

Can I use this calculator to prevent pregnancy?

No. Calendar predictions cannot confirm the exact day of ovulation and should not be treated as a guaranteed contraceptive method. Fertility and ovulation timing can vary from one cycle to another.

When should I talk to a health care professional about my period?

Consider medical advice if your previously regular periods become persistently irregular, bleeding becomes unusually heavy or prolonged, pain is severe or worsening, or you experience another significant change from your usual pattern.
