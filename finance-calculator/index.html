Use the Finance Calculator to solve a time value of money variable such as future value (FV), present value (PV), periodic payment (PMT), number of periods (N), or interest rate per period. Enter the known values, select the variable you want to find, and choose whether payments occur at the beginning or end of each period.

What Is the Finance Calculator?

The Finance Calculator is a time value of money tool for evaluating a present amount, future amount, equal periodic payments, a periodic interest rate, or the number of periods.

It is useful when the cash flows occur at regular intervals and the rate remains constant. The underlying calculation combines compound growth of the present value with the accumulated value of equal periodic payments. Time value of money calculations commonly use PV, FV, PMT, rate, and number of periods as their main variables.

How to Use the Finance Calculator

  1. Solve for: Select the unknown variable you want to calculate, such as FV, PV, PMT, N, or the periodic interest rate.
  2. N (# periods): Enter the total number of periods in the calculation.
  3. Rate per period (%): Enter the interest or discount rate that applies to each individual period.
  4. PV: Enter the present value at the start of the calculation.
  5. PMT: Enter the equal payment made during each period.
  6. FV: Enter the future value at the end of the calculation when FV is a known value rather than the variable being solved.
  7. Payment timing: Choose whether each periodic payment occurs at the end or beginning of the period.

The interest rate must correspond to the same period used for N and PMT. For example, if each period represents one month, the rate input must represent the rate for one month rather than an annual rate.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

For payments made at the end of each period, the core future value relationship is:

FV = PV(1 + i)ᴺ + PMT × [((1 + i)ᴺ − 1) / i]

This combines two components:

Future value of the present value:

PV(1 + i)ᴺ

Future value of the periodic payments:

PMT × [((1 + i)ᴺ − 1) / i]

The annuity factor shown above is the standard future value factor for equal payments made at the end of each period.

Beginning-of-Period Payments

When payments are made at the beginning of each period, each payment receives one additional period of growth. The payment portion is therefore multiplied by:

(1 + i)

The relationship becomes:

FV = PV(1 + i)ᴺ + PMT × [((1 + i)ᴺ − 1) / i] × (1 + i)

This is the annuity-due adjustment.

For example, a rate input of 1% per period is used in the formula as:

i = 1 ÷ 100 = 0.01

Calculation Sequence

  1. Select the variable to solve.
  2. Convert the entered percentage rate to decimal form.
  3. Use the selected number of periods as N.
  4. Apply the end-of-period or beginning-of-period payment treatment.
  5. Solve the TVM relationship for the selected unknown.
  6. Return the calculated value along with supporting result information.

When the periodic rate itself is the unknown, the required rate is the value of i that makes the same time value of money relationship balance.

Total Scheduled Payments

The calculator also reports information about the scheduled periodic payment stream. This helps distinguish the recurring payment amount from the separate effects of present value and interest growth.

Rate Convention

The calculator uses a rate per period. It does not automatically treat the entered percentage as an annual nominal rate or convert between different payment and compounding frequencies.

If the model uses monthly periods, use the appropriate rate for each monthly period. If it uses yearly periods, use the appropriate yearly rate.

Worked Example

Suppose you want to calculate the future value of an initial $5,000, with an additional $200 payment at the end of each period, for 12 periods, at a rate of 1% per period.

Inputs

  • Solve for: FV
  • N: 12 periods
  • Rate per period: 1%
  • PV: $5,000
  • PMT: $200
  • Payment timing: End of period

Step 1: Convert the Rate

i = 1% ÷ 100 = 0.01

Step 2: Calculate the Growth Factor

(1 + i)ᴺ = (1.01)¹²
(1.01)¹² ≈ 1.12682503

The accumulated value of the initial amount is:

$5,000 × 1.12682503 ≈ $5,634.13

Step 3: Calculate the Payment Component

PMT × [((1 + i)ᴺ − 1) / i]
= $200 × [(1.12682503 − 1) / 0.01]
= $200 × 12.68250301
≈ $2,536.50

Final Result

FV ≈ $5,634.13 + $2,536.50
FV ≈ $8,170.63

Using these inputs and end-of-period payments, the future value is approximately $8,170.63.

Understanding Your Results

Calculated Value

The main result is the variable selected under Solve for. Depending on the selection, the result may be a present value, future value, periodic payment, periodic rate, or number of periods.

Total Scheduled Payments

This result summarizes the recurring payment portion of the financial scenario. It helps you understand the payment stream separately from the initial or final value.

Rate Convention

The rate convention confirms that the calculator works with the interest rate applicable to each period. Keeping the rate and period frequency consistent is essential when interpreting the result.

The output represents a mathematical time value of money calculation based on the values entered. It should not be interpreted as a guaranteed investment return, borrowing cost, or financial outcome.

Assumptions and Limitations

The Finance Calculator assumes:

  • A constant interest rate per period throughout the calculation.
  • Equal periodic payments when PMT is used.
  • Payments occur consistently at either the beginning or end of each period, based on the selected timing.
  • The entered rate already represents the appropriate effective rate for each modeled period.

The calculator does not automatically convert a nominal annual rate when payment and compounding frequencies differ. The period used for the rate should match the periods used for N and PMT.

Real financial products may use different timing rules, rate conventions, compounding methods, fees, or cash-flow schedules, so their actual results can differ from a simplified TVM calculation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What can the Finance Calculator solve?

It can solve time value of money variables including FV, PV, PMT, number of periods, and periodic interest rate, using the remaining entered values.

What does “rate per period” mean?

It is the interest or discount rate that applies to one calculation period. If one period equals one month, the rate should be a monthly periodic rate. If one period equals one year, it should correspond to one year.

What is the difference between PV and FV?

PV, or present value, is the value at the starting point of the calculation. FV, or future value, is the value at the ending point after the effects of time, interest, and any periodic payments are applied.

What is PMT?

PMT is the equal payment that occurs during each period. It can represent a regular contribution or another repeating cash flow in the TVM calculation.

Does payment timing affect the result?

Yes. An end-of-period payment is an ordinary annuity payment. A beginning-of-period payment receives one additional period of growth, which changes the calculated value when the rate is not zero.

Does the calculator automatically convert an annual interest rate to a monthly rate?

No. The calculator uses the entered rate per period. You should enter a rate that is consistent with the period frequency used in the calculation.

Why might my result differ from a bank or investment account?

A real financial product may use different compounding conventions, payment dates, rates, or other contractual rules. The calculator models a constant periodic rate with equal periodic payments and the selected payment timing.
