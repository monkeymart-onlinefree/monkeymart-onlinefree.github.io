What Is a Finance Calculator?

This finance calculator solves the standard time-value-of-money relationship between present value (PV), future value (FV), periodic payment (PMT), interest rate, and number of periods (N). It can model lump sums and equal periodic cash flows.

Core Time Value of Money Formula

For an ordinary annuity, the future-value relationship is based on PV(1+i)^N plus the accumulated value of periodic payments. Beginning-of-period payments gain one additional period of growth.

Cash-Flow Sign Convention

Financial calculators use signs to represent direction. A deposit or payment you make is normally negative; money you receive is positive. At least one side of the transaction generally needs the opposite sign for a valid solution.

Limitations

The calculator assumes a constant periodic rate and equal periodic payments. It does not automatically convert nominal annual rates when payment and compounding frequencies differ; enter the effective rate for each period you are modeling.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are some finance-calculator inputs negative?

The signs show cash-flow direction. Money you pay or invest is typically negative, while money you receive is positive.

What rate should I enter?

Enter the interest rate for one calculation period. If periods are monthly, use an appropriate monthly periodic rate.

Can the calculator solve interest rate?

Yes. It uses a numerical root search because the rate is not isolated by a simple general algebraic formula.
