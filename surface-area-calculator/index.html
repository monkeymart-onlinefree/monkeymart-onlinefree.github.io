Use the Surface Area Calculator to find the total outside area of common 3D shapes. Select a solid, enter its required dimensions, and choose a length-unit label to calculate the surface area in square units.

What Is the Surface Area Calculator?

The Surface Area Calculator finds the total area covering the outside of a three-dimensional solid. It supports spheres, cones, cubes, closed cylinders, rectangular prisms, capsules, conical frustums, and square pyramids.

It is useful for geometry problems and practical estimates involving materials such as paint, wrapping, coating, sheet material, or surface coverage.

How to Use the Surface Area Calculator

  1. Select a solid shape. Choose the geometric solid you want to calculate.
  2. Enter the required dimensions. The dimensions depend on the selected shape, such as radius, height, edge length, length, or width.
  3. Choose the length-unit label. Use the unit you want associated with your measurements.
  4. Calculate the result. The calculator applies the formula for the selected shape and returns its surface area in square units.

All dimensions used in one calculation should be expressed in the same unit.

Important: The unit option labels the result but does not convert the numerical dimensions between units. For example, if your values are entered as centimeters, select centimeters so the result is correctly labeled in cm².

Formula / Calculation Method

Surface area is measured in square units because it represents the combined area of the outside surfaces of a 3D object.

Sphere Surface Area

Formula:

SA = 4πr²

Where:

  • SA = surface area
  • r = radius
  • π ≈ 3.14159265

The formula calculates the entire curved surface of the sphere.

Cone Surface Area

For a closed cone:

SA = πr² + πr√(r² + h²)

Where:

  • r = base radius
  • h = perpendicular height
  • √(r² + h²) = slant height

The first term represents the circular base, while the second represents the curved lateral surface.

Cube Surface Area

SA = 6a²

Where:

  • a = edge length

A cube has six equal square faces, so the area of one face, a², is multiplied by six.

Closed Cylinder Surface Area

SA = 2πr² + 2πrh

or:

SA = 2πr(r + h)

Where:

  • r = base radius
  • h = cylinder height

The calculation includes two circular ends plus the curved side of the cylinder.

Rectangular Prism Surface Area

SA = 2lw + 2lh + 2wh

Where:

  • l = length
  • w = width
  • h = height

Each pair of opposite rectangular faces has the same area.

Capsule Surface Area

SA = 4πr² + 2πrh

Where:

  • r = capsule radius
  • h = length of the cylindrical section

The calculation combines the curved area of the cylindrical section with the area of the two hemispherical ends, which together form a sphere.

Conical Frustum Surface Area

SA = π(R² + r²) + π(R + r)√((R − r)² + h²)

Where:

  • R = larger base radius
  • r = smaller top radius
  • h = perpendicular height
  • √((R − r)² + h²) = slant height

The total includes both circular ends and the lateral surface.

Square Pyramid Surface Area

SA = a² + 2a√((a/2)² + h²)

Where:

  • a = base edge length
  • h = perpendicular height
  • √((a/2)² + h²) = slant height of a triangular face

The first term is the square base area. The second term is the combined area of the four triangular faces.

Calculation Sequence

The calculator follows this general process:

  1. Identify the selected solid shape.
  2. Read the dimensions required for that shape.
  3. Apply the corresponding surface-area formula.
  4. Calculate any required slant length from the entered dimensions.
  5. Add the component surface areas where necessary.
  6. Display the total in square units using the selected unit label.

Worked Example

Suppose you want to calculate the total surface area of a closed cylinder.

Inputs

  • Radius: 3 cm
  • Height: 8 cm
  • Shape: Cylinder
  • Unit label: Centimeters

Step 1: Use the Formula

SA = 2πr² + 2πrh

Step 2: Substitute the Values

SA = 2π(3²) + 2π(3)(8)
SA = 18π + 48π

Step 3: Combine the Terms

SA = 66π

Final Result

SA ≈ 207.3451 cm²

The cylinder therefore has a total surface area of approximately 207.35 cm² when rounded to two decimal places.

Understanding Your Results

The main result is the total surface area of the selected solid.

A result such as 207.35 cm² means that the combined outside surfaces cover approximately 207.35 square centimeters.

Surface area always uses squared units:

  • millimeters → mm²
  • centimeters → cm²
  • meters → m²
  • inches → in²
  • feet → ft²

For formulas containing π or square roots, the decimal result is normally an approximation because the exact value may be irrational.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator assumes that all shapes are ideal geometric solids with the dimensions required by their mathematical definitions.

All linear dimensions should be positive and use the same measurement unit. Mixing values such as centimeters and meters in the same calculation will produce an incorrect result unless you convert them first.

The unit selector acts as a label and does not automatically convert entered dimensions from one measurement system to another.

The formulas describe regular mathematical solids. Real objects may contain openings, rounded edges, wall thickness, surface texture, overlaps, missing faces, or irregular sections that are not included.

For closed cylinders and cones, the formulas include their bases. If you need only lateral or exposed surface area, the total closed-solid result may not represent the area you need.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does surface area measure?

Surface area measures the total area covering the outside of a three-dimensional object. It is expressed in square units such as cm², m², or ft².

Is surface area the same as volume?

No. Surface area measures the outside covering of a solid and uses square units. Volume measures the space inside a solid and uses cubic units.

Why is surface area measured in square units?

Each surface is a two-dimensional area. Even though the object is three-dimensional, its surfaces are measured in units squared.

Do all dimensions need to use the same unit?

Yes. Convert all dimensions to one consistent length unit before calculating. For example, do not combine a radius in centimeters with a height in meters.

Does changing the unit option convert my measurements?

No. The unit option labels the calculation result. It does not automatically convert the numerical dimension values.

Does the cylinder formula include the top and bottom?

Yes. The closed-cylinder formula 2πr² + 2πrh includes both circular ends and the curved lateral surface.

Why can my result contain many decimal places?

Formulas involving π or square roots often produce irrational values. Decimal results are therefore approximations and may be rounded for easier use.
