The Tire Size Calculator uses tire width, aspect ratio, and rim diameter to calculate sidewall height, overall tire diameter, circumference, and estimated revolutions per mile. Enter the three numbers found in a tire size such as 225/45R17 to calculate its theoretical dimensions.

What Is the Tire Size Calculator?

A Tire Size Calculator converts the numbers in a tire size into useful physical dimensions.

For a tire marked 225/45R17, 225 represents the nominal tire width in millimeters, 45 is the aspect ratio, and 17 is the wheel or rim diameter in inches. NHTSA describes the aspect ratio as the tire sidewall height relative to its width and the final number as the rim diameter.

The calculated dimensions can help when comparing tire sizes, checking dimensional differences, or understanding how a tire's width, profile, and wheel size affect its theoretical overall size.

How to Use the Tire Size Calculator

Enter the values from the tire size in the same order they appear on the sidewall:

  1. Width (mm): Enter the nominal tire width in millimeters. For 225/45R17, enter 225.
  2. Aspect Ratio (%): Enter the sidewall height as a percentage of the tire width. For 225/45R17, enter 45.
  3. Rim Diameter (in): Enter the wheel diameter in inches. For 225/45R17, enter 17.

NHTSA identifies these values as the nominal tire width, aspect ratio, and rim diameter components of a passenger tire size.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator first finds the tire's sidewall height and then adds two sidewalls to the rim diameter.

Sidewall height in millimeters:

Hmm=W×A100H\_{mm} = W \times \frac{A}{100}

Sidewall height in inches:

Hin=Hmm25.4H\_{in} = \frac{H\_{mm}}{25.4}

Overall tire diameter:

D=R+2HinD = R + 2H\_{in}

NIST defines one inch as exactly 25.4 millimeters, which is the conversion used between the metric tire width and inch-based rim diameter.

Calculation Sequence

The calculator follows this sequence:

  1. Multiply tire width by the aspect ratio divided by 100.
  2. Convert the resulting sidewall height from millimeters to inches by dividing by 25.4.
  3. Multiply the sidewall height in inches by two because the tire has a sidewall above and below the wheel.
  4. Add the rim diameter to obtain the overall theoretical tire diameter.
  5. Use the calculated diameter to determine circumference and estimated revolutions per mile.

Tire Circumference

Once overall diameter is known, theoretical circumference is calculated using the circumference of a circle:

C=πDC = \pi D

Where:

  • CC = tire circumference in inches
  • DD = overall tire diameter in inches
  • π\pi ≈ 3.14159265

Revolutions per Mile

Estimated revolutions per mile are calculated by dividing the number of inches in one mile by the tire circumference:

RPMile=63,360CRPMile = \frac{63,360}{C}

There are 5,280 feet in an international mile, and 12 inches in a foot, giving 63,360 inches per mile. NIST documents the 5,280-foot mile relationship.

This is a geometric estimate. Actual rolling revolutions can differ because a real tire changes shape under load.

Worked Example

Consider a 225/45R17 tire.

Inputs

  • Aspect ratio: 45%
  • Rim diameter: 17 in

The sidewall is 101.25 mm high.

Convert it to inches:

101.25÷25.4=3.9862 in101.25 \div 25.4 = 3.9862\text{ in}

Step 2: Calculate Overall Diameter

There are two sidewalls across the full tire diameter:

D=17+(2×3.9862)D = 17 + (2 \times 3.9862) D=24.9724 inD = 24.9724\text{ in}

The theoretical overall diameter is approximately 24.97 inches.

Step 3: Calculate Circumference

C=π×24.9724C = \pi \times 24.9724 C≈78.4532 inC \approx 78.4532\text{ in}

The theoretical circumference is approximately 78.45 inches.

Step 4: Calculate Revolutions per Mile

For a 225/45R17 tire:

  • Sidewall height: 101.25 mm / 3.99 in

Understanding Your Results

Sidewall Height shows the calculated distance between the rim and the outer edge of the tire based on its nominal width and aspect ratio.

Overall Diameter represents the theoretical distance across the complete tire from one outer edge to the opposite edge through its center.

Circumference estimates the theoretical distance around the tire based on its calculated diameter.

Revolutions per Mile estimates how many complete rotations a theoretical tire of that circumference would make over one mile.

When comparing tire sizes, these dimensions can show how the calculated geometry changes. They do not by themselves confirm that a replacement tire is suitable for a particular vehicle.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator uses the nominal tire dimensions printed in the tire size designation. Real measured dimensions may differ between manufacturers and tire models.

Actual dimensions and rolling circumference can also vary with rim width, tire construction, tread depth, inflation pressure, vehicle load, and operating conditions. This means calculated revolutions per mile may differ from manufacturer specifications or real-world measurements.

The calculator does not determine vehicle fitment, suspension or fender clearance, wheel offset, load capacity, speed rating, recommended tire pressure, or whether a different tire size is approved for your vehicle.

NHTSA recommends using the vehicle's tire information placard or owner's manual for the manufacturer's recommended tire size and consulting a tire professional when there is uncertainty about the correct replacement size.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do the numbers in 225/45R17 mean?

The 225 is the nominal tire width in millimeters, 45 is the aspect ratio showing sidewall height as a percentage of width, and 17 is the wheel diameter in inches. The R identifies radial construction.

How is tire sidewall height calculated?

Multiply the tire width by the aspect ratio divided by 100. For a 225/45 tire:

225×0.45=101.25 mm225 \times 0.45 = 101.25\text{ mm}
How is overall tire diameter calculated?

Convert the sidewall height to inches, multiply it by two, and add the rim diameter:

Diameter=Rim+2×SidewallDiameter = Rim + 2 \times Sidewall
Why is 25.4 used in the formula?

Tire width and sidewall height are expressed in millimeters, while wheel diameter is normally given in inches. NIST defines 1 inch as exactly 25.4 mm, so dividing millimeters by 25.4 converts the sidewall height to inches.

Why can the calculated diameter differ from the tire manufacturer's specification?

The calculation uses nominal dimensions. Actual tire dimensions can vary because of tire design, manufacturer specifications, rim width, inflation pressure, tread depth, and load.

Are calculated revolutions per mile exact?

No. The result is a geometric estimate based on theoretical circumference. A tire deforms where it contacts the road, so its real rolling circumference and manufacturer-rated revolutions per mile can differ.

Can this calculator tell me whether a different tire size will fit my car?

No. Tire diameter is only one factor in fitment. Vehicle specifications, rim width, wheel offset, clearances, load rating, speed rating, and manufacturer recommendations also need to be considered.
