What Is a Calories Burned Calculator?

This calculator estimates the energy cost of an activity using a metabolic equivalent (MET), body weight, and time. MET values standardize the relative intensity of many physical activities.

Calories Burned Formula

Calories = MET × 3.5 × body weight (kg) ÷ 200 × minutes

The 3.5 mL/kg/min convention is the standard reference used to define one MET. The 2024 Adult Compendium provides updated MET values for many activities.

Understanding MET Values

A higher MET represents a higher standardized energy cost. For example, brisk walking has a lower MET than fast running. The same MET activity generally produces a larger calorie estimate for a heavier person because body mass is part of the equation.

Limitations

MET-based calorie estimates are approximations. The 2024 Compendium itself notes that standard MET values were created to standardize activity energy costs and do not precisely measure an individual’s expenditure. Wearables and lab tests also have limitations.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a MET?

A metabolic equivalent (MET) expresses the energy cost of an activity relative to rest. The standard convention treats 1 MET as roughly 3.5 mL of oxygen per kilogram per minute.

Why are calories burned estimates approximate?

Standard MET values represent average energy costs. Individual expenditure varies with fitness, age, body composition, technique, environment, and resting metabolic rate.

Can I enter my own MET value?

Yes. Choose Custom MET to calculate with a value from a trusted activity source or laboratory measurement.

Sources and Methodology
