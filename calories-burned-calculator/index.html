The Calories Burned Calculator estimates the energy used during physical activity from the activity’s MET value, your body weight, and exercise duration. Enter the activity or MET value, weight, and time to calculate estimated calories burned.

What Is the Calories Burned Calculator?

The Calories Burned Calculator estimates how many kilocalories (commonly called calories) you use during an activity. It applies a standard metabolic equivalent, or MET, calculation based on activity intensity, body weight, and duration.

This tool can be useful for comparing activities, estimating workout energy expenditure, or reviewing how exercise duration and body weight affect the calculated result. MET-based calculations are estimates rather than direct measurements of an individual’s metabolism.

How to Use the Calories Burned Calculator

Activity: Choose the physical activity represented in the calculator. Activities can have different MET values depending on their type and intensity.

Custom MET value: Enter a specific MET value when you want the calculation to use that value.

Body weight: Enter your body weight. The calculation requires body weight in kilograms, so a weight entered using another supported unit is converted before the calorie formula is applied.

Weight unit: Choose the unit that matches the body-weight value you entered.

Hours: Enter the whole hours spent performing the activity.

Minutes: Enter the additional minutes of activity. The calculation uses the total exercise duration in minutes.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator uses:

Calories burned = MET × 3.5 × body weight (kg) ÷ 200 × minutes

The Compendium of Physical Activities gives the same MET-to-energy conversion as:

kcal/min = MET × 3.5 × body weight (kg) ÷ 200

The result per minute is then multiplied by the activity duration.

Variables

  • MET = metabolic equivalent value for the activity
  • 3.5 = standard oxygen-consumption reference for 1 MET, expressed as 3.5 mL/kg/min
  • Body weight = weight in kilograms
  • 200 = conversion factor used in the standard MET-to-kcal/min calculation
  • Minutes = total duration of the activity in minutes
  • Calories burned = estimated energy expenditure in kilocalories

A standard MET expresses an activity’s energy cost relative to the conventional resting reference of 3.5 mL of oxygen per kilogram of body weight per minute.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Determine the MET value for the selected activity or use the custom MET value.
  2. Use body weight in kilograms.
  3. Convert the exercise duration to total minutes.
  4. Calculate calories per minute using MET × 3.5 × weight ÷ 200.
  5. Multiply the per-minute value by the total number of minutes.

Worked Example

Suppose a person weighing 70 kg performs a vigorous aerobic activity with a MET value of 8.0 for 30 minutes. The 2024 Adult Compendium lists high-impact vigorous aerobic dance at 8.0 METs.

Inputs

  • MET value: 8.0
  • Body weight: 70 kg
  • Duration: 30 minutes

Step 1

Substitute the values into the formula:

Calories = 8.0 × 3.5 × 70 ÷ 200 × 30

Step 2

Calculate calories per minute:

8.0 × 3.5 × 70 ÷ 200 = 9.8 kcal/min

Step 3

Multiply by the exercise duration:

9.8 × 30 = 294 kcal

Final Result

Estimated calories burned = 294 kcal

Understanding Your Results

The main result is the estimated number of kilocalories used during the entered activity period.

A higher MET value increases the result because it represents a greater standardized energy cost. A higher body weight also increases the calculated energy expenditure for the same MET and duration, while exercising for more time increases the result proportionally.

The result should be treated as an estimate rather than an exact measurement of personal calorie expenditure. Standard MET values were created to provide consistent activity energy-cost values and do not fully account for individual differences.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator uses the standard MET convention of 1 MET = 3.5 mL/kg/min. This reference value does not adjust the calculation for personal characteristics such as age, sex, height, body composition, fitness level, or measured resting metabolic rate.

The 2024 Adult Compendium was specifically updated for adults 19–59 years old. Separate energy-expenditure references have been developed for older adults, so standard Adult Compendium values may not represent every population equally well.

Actual energy expenditure can also vary with exercise efficiency, pace, terrain, environmental conditions, technique, fitness, and individual physiology. The calculator therefore provides a standardized estimate, not a laboratory measurement or individualized medical assessment.

The standard formula estimates energy expenditure using the full MET value. It does not separately calculate calories burned above resting energy expenditure.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a MET?

A MET, or metabolic equivalent, is a standardized way to express the energy cost of physical activity relative to resting metabolism. The standard reference used by the Adult Compendium defines 1 MET as 3.5 mL of oxygen per kilogram per minute.

How does the calculator estimate calories burned?

It multiplies the activity MET value by 3.5 and body weight in kilograms, divides by 200 to estimate calories per minute, and then multiplies by the activity duration in minutes.

Why does body weight affect calories burned?

The MET calorie formula scales energy expenditure by body mass. For the same MET value and duration, a larger body-weight value therefore produces a higher estimated calorie result.

Can I enter my own MET value?

Yes. The Custom MET value input allows the calculation to use the MET value you provide instead of relying only on an activity value.

Are MET calorie calculations exact?

No. Standard MET values are useful for standardized estimates, but the Compendium notes that they were not designed to precisely determine an individual person’s energy expenditure.

Why might my fitness tracker show a different number?

Wearables may use heart rate, movement sensors, personal profile information, device-specific algorithms, and other data. This calculator uses the stated MET-based formula, so its result can differ from a watch, exercise machine, or laboratory measurement.

Does this calculator provide medical or nutrition advice?

No. It estimates activity energy expenditure from MET value, body weight, and time. The result should not be treated as a diagnosis, personalized exercise prescription, or individual nutrition recommendation.
