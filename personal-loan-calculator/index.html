Use the Personal Loan Calculator to estimate the monthly payment and overall cost of a fixed-rate personal loan. Enter the loan amount, annual interest rate, loan term, origination fee, and any monthly insurance or add-on cost to see your payment, interest, net proceeds, and other loan-cost estimates.

What Is the Personal Loan Calculator?

The Personal Loan Calculator estimates the cost of repaying a fixed-rate loan with equal monthly payments.

It is useful when comparing personal loan offers, planning monthly payments, or checking how interest, loan length, origination fees, and monthly add-on costs affect the amount you borrow and repay.

The calculator is designed for fixed-rate installment loans. CFPB guidance notes that personal installment loans are generally repaid through periodic fixed payments and may include fees such as origination fees or optional insurance.

How to Use the Personal Loan Calculator

  1. Loan amount: Enter the principal amount of the loan.
  2. Annual interest rate (%): Enter the stated yearly interest rate.
  3. Term (years): Enter the number of complete years in the repayment term.
  4. Extra months: Add any months that are not included in the full-year term.
  5. Origination fee (%): Enter the percentage of the loan charged as an origination fee.
  6. Monthly insurance / add-on: Enter any recurring monthly insurance or other fixed add-on cost included in your loan estimate.

The calculator then estimates the monthly loan payment and related borrowing costs.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

For a fixed-rate loan with equal monthly payments:

Payment = P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)^−n]

Where:

  • P = loan principal
  • r = monthly interest rate
  • n = total number of monthly payments

The annual percentage rate entered by the user is converted to a monthly rate:

r = (Annual Interest Rate ÷ 100) ÷ 12

The total number of payments is:

n = (Term in Years × 12) + Extra Months

For a zero-interest loan, the calculator avoids the standard interest formula and uses:

Payment = P ÷ n

Variables

Variable Meaning Unit
P Loan principal Currency
r Monthly interest rate Decimal per month
n Number of monthly payments Months
Payment Scheduled principal-and-interest payment Currency per month
A Monthly insurance / add-on Currency per month
f Origination fee rate Percentage

Calculation Sequence

  1. Convert the annual interest percentage to a decimal.
  2. Divide the annual rate by 12 to obtain the monthly rate.
  3. Convert years and extra months into the total number of monthly payments.
  4. Apply the fixed-payment amortization formula.
  5. Calculate total interest over the scheduled term.
  6. Calculate the origination fee.
  7. Subtract the origination fee from the loan amount to estimate net proceeds.
  8. Include any monthly add-on when calculating scheduled cash outflow.
  9. Report the calculator’s estimated effective APR as an additional loan-cost comparison figure.

Total Interest

For a normally amortizing loan:

Total Interest = (Monthly Payment × n) − P

This shows how much scheduled interest is paid above the original principal when payments are made according to the selected term.

Origination Fee

When the fee is entered as a percentage:

Origination Fee = P × (Origination Fee % ÷ 100)

Net Proceeds After Fee

In this calculator, the origination fee reduces the amount received by the borrower rather than increasing the stated principal:

Net Proceeds = P − Origination Fee

For example, a $10,000 loan with a 3% deducted origination fee provides $9,700 in net proceeds while repayment is still based on the $10,000 principal.

This treatment is also commonly seen in personal-loan examples where fees are deducted from loan proceeds.

Total Scheduled Outflow

A recurring monthly insurance or add-on increases the amount paid over the loan term.

The scheduled outflow can be represented as:

Total Scheduled Outflow = (Monthly Payment + Monthly Add-on) × n

If the monthly add-on is zero, total scheduled outflow equals the total of the scheduled loan payments.

Estimated Effective APR

The calculator also reports an estimated effective APR.

APR is broader than the stated interest rate because fees can increase the effective cost of borrowing. The CFPB describes APR as a measure that includes the interest rate plus applicable additional loan fees.

The calculator’s figure should be treated as an estimate for comparison purposes rather than a replacement for the APR disclosed by a lender. Official APR calculations may depend on which charges qualify as finance charges and the timing of loan proceeds and payments. Regulation Z describes APR as a yearly measure relating the amount and timing of value received to the amount and timing of payments made.

Worked Example

Assume the following personal loan:

Inputs

  • Loan amount: $15,000
  • Annual interest rate: 9%
  • Term: 3 years
  • Extra months: 6 months
  • Origination fee: 3%
  • Monthly insurance / add-on: $0

Step 1: Calculate the Monthly Rate and Term

Monthly interest rate:

r = 9% ÷ 100 ÷ 12
r = 0.0075

Total number of payments:

n = (3 × 12) + 6
n = 42 months

Step 2: Calculate the Monthly Payment

Payment = 15,000 × 0.0075 ÷ [1 − (1.0075)^−42]
Payment ≈ $417.67 per month

The calculation keeps additional decimal precision internally before the displayed result is rounded.

Step 3: Calculate Total Interest

Using the unrounded monthly payment:

Total scheduled loan payments ≈ $17,542.05

Therefore:

Total Interest = $17,542.05 − $15,000
Total Interest ≈ $2,542.05

Step 4: Calculate the Origination Fee

Origination Fee = $15,000 × 3%
Origination Fee = $450

Step 5: Calculate Net Proceeds

Net Proceeds = $15,000 − $450
Net Proceeds = $14,550

Final Result

For these example inputs:

  • Monthly payment: about $417.67
  • Total interest: about $2,542.05
  • Origination fee: $450
  • Net proceeds: $14,550
  • Total scheduled outflow with a $0 monthly add-on: about $17,542.05

The calculator may also display an estimated effective APR. The exact lender-disclosure APR should be confirmed using the lender’s official loan documents.

Understanding Your Results

Monthly Payment

This is the estimated scheduled principal-and-interest payment required each month under the selected fixed interest rate and repayment term.

A monthly insurance or add-on amount is separate from the base amortized loan payment.

Net Proceeds After Fee

Net proceeds show how much money remains after the entered origination fee is deducted from the loan amount.

A loan can therefore have a principal larger than the amount of cash you actually receive.

Total Interest

Total interest is the scheduled amount paid above principal because of the stated interest rate.

A longer repayment term can reduce the required monthly payment while increasing the time over which interest is charged.

Origination Fee

This is the fee calculated from the loan amount and the percentage entered.

Origination fees are one type of charge that may increase the overall cost of a personal installment loan.

Total Scheduled Outflow

This estimates the total scheduled cash payments over the selected loan term, including the entered recurring monthly add-on where applicable.

It does not represent late fees, penalties, unscheduled payments, or other costs that are not included in the calculator inputs.

Estimated Effective APR

This result helps show why a loan's borrowing cost can be higher than its stated interest rate when fees are involved.

Use the figure as an estimate for comparison. Your lender’s disclosed APR remains the appropriate figure for official loan documents.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator assumes a fixed interest rate and equal monthly scheduled payments for the full loan term.

The origination fee is treated as reducing net loan proceeds rather than increasing the principal used to calculate the normal monthly loan payment.

The calculation does not model variable interest rates, skipped payments, late-payment charges, early payoff or prepayment changes, lender-specific APR disclosure methods, or taxes.

Any monthly insurance or add-on entered is treated as a recurring scheduled cost. Actual insurance charges or optional loan products may have different terms.

The calculator does not determine loan eligibility, credit approval, available interest rates, or lender-specific loan limits.

Results are estimates. Actual lender calculations may differ because of payment dates, fee treatment, rounding policies, finance-charge rules, or other contractual terms.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the personal loan monthly payment calculated?

The calculator uses the fixed-payment amortization formula based on the loan principal, monthly interest rate, and total number of monthly payments. A zero-interest loan is calculated by dividing the principal by the number of months.

Does the origination fee increase my monthly payment?

Not in this calculator's model. The fee reduces the estimated net proceeds received by the borrower while the scheduled principal-and-interest payment continues to use the entered loan amount.

If a lender instead finances a fee by adding it to the loan balance, use the financed principal shown in the lender's loan documents.

What is the difference between interest rate and APR?

The interest rate represents the cost of borrowing the principal through interest. APR is a broader annualized measure that can also reflect applicable fees and finance charges.

What do extra months do?

Extra months are added to the full-year term.

For example, a term of 4 years and 6 extra months produces:

(4 × 12) + 6 = 54 monthly payments
Does monthly insurance change the loan principal?

The monthly insurance/add-on input represents a recurring cost. It does not change the principal used in the standard monthly loan-payment formula.

Why might my lender show different results?

A lender may use specific payment dates, finance-charge classifications, rounding procedures, fee rules, insurance costs, or APR disclosure requirements that are not modeled by this calculator.

Can I use this calculator for an unsecured personal loan?

Yes. It is suitable for estimating a fixed-rate loan with equal monthly payments when you know the principal, stated interest rate, repayment term, and applicable fee assumptions.
