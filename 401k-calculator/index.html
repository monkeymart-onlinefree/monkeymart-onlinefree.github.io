Use this 401(k) Calculator to estimate how your retirement balance may grow from your current account balance, salary, employee contributions, employer match, salary growth, and assumed annual return. It also shows estimated employee contributions, employer contributions, investment growth, and your final-year employee contribution.

What Is the 401(k) Calculator?

The 401(k) Calculator is a retirement projection tool that estimates your future 401(k) balance over a selected number of years.

It models annual employee contributions, applies an employee deferral limit, estimates employer matching contributions, increases salary using your selected growth rate, and compounds the account using an assumed annual investment return.

It can be useful when comparing contribution rates, employer matching benefits, salary-growth assumptions, and different retirement-saving timelines.

How to Use the 401(k) Calculator

Enter the following information in the calculator:

Current Age
Enter your age at the beginning of the projection. This provides the starting age for your retirement-planning scenario. The contribution cap used in the calculation comes from the Employee Deferral Limit field.

Years to Contribute
Enter how many years you want contributions and investment growth to be projected.

Current Balance ($)
Enter the amount currently held in your 401(k).

Annual Salary ($)
Enter your current annual salary. This is the starting salary used to calculate employee contributions and employer matching contributions.

Employee Contribution (%)
Enter the percentage of annual salary you plan to contribute. The calculated employee contribution is limited by the Employee Deferral Limit you enter.

Annual Salary Increase (%)
Enter the percentage by which you expect salary to increase each year.

Employer Match Rate (%)
Enter the percentage of eligible employee contributions your employer matches. For example, a 50% match rate means the employer contributes $0.50 for each eligible $1 contributed by the employee.

Match Up to Salary (%)
Enter the percentage of salary up to which employee contributions qualify for the employer match.

Annual Return (%)
Enter the annual rate of return you want the projection to assume. This is an estimate rather than a guaranteed investment return.

Employee Deferral Limit
Enter the annual dollar limit applied to employee contributions. For a standard 401(k), the IRS employee elective-deferral limit is $24,500 for 2026. Different limits may apply when catch-up contributions are permitted.

The calculator projects the account one year at a time.

Calculation Sequence

For every projected year, the calculator:

  1. Determines the salary for that year using the annual salary increase.
  2. Multiplies salary by the employee contribution percentage.
  3. Caps the employee contribution at the selected Employee Deferral Limit.
  4. Calculates the amount of salary eligible for employer matching.
  5. Applies the employer match rate to the eligible employee contribution.
  6. Adds employee and employer contributions to the existing account balance.
  7. Applies the assumed annual return.
  8. Repeats the process for the remaining projection years.

Employee Contribution

The employee contribution for a year is:

Et=min⁡(St×c, L)E_t=\min(S_t \times c,\ L)

If the percentage-based contribution is below the selected deferral limit, the full calculated amount is used.

If it exceeds the limit, the contribution is capped at the selected limit.

Employer Contribution

First calculate the employee contribution eligible for matching:

EligibleMatcht=min⁡(Et, St×u)EligibleMatch_t=\min(E_t,\ S_t \times u)

Then:

Mt=EligibleMatcht×mM_t=EligibleMatch_t \times m

For example, with a 50% employer match up to 6% of salary, the calculator applies the 50% match only to employee contributions within the 6%-of-salary matching cap.

Actual employer plans may use different matching formulas.

The annual return is then applied:

Bt=BalanceBeforeReturn×(1+r)B_t=BalanceBeforeReturn \times (1+r)

The ending balance becomes the starting balance for the next year.

Understanding Your Results

Projected Ending Balance

This is the estimated 401(k) balance at the end of the selected projection period after employee contributions, estimated employer contributions, and assumed investment growth.

It is a projection, not a guaranteed retirement balance.

Employee Contributions

This is the total amount contributed from your salary during the projection.

Each year's employee contribution is limited by the Employee Deferral Limit entered in the calculator.

Employer Contributions

This is the total estimated employer matching contribution generated by the match rate and the percentage of salary eligible for matching.

Actual employer matching rules depend on your plan document.

Investment Growth

Investment growth represents the estimated increase produced by the assumed investment return after separating your starting balance and direct employee and employer contributions.

Investment markets do not provide a fixed annual return, so actual results can be higher or lower.

Final-Year Employee Contribution

This is the employee contribution calculated for the last year of the projection.

It can be affected by salary growth, your contribution percentage, and the selected deferral limit.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator uses a simplified year-by-year projection. Contributions are added to the balance and the assumed annual return is then applied.

The entered salary-growth rate and annual return are treated as constant throughout the projection. Actual salaries and investment returns may change from year to year.

The selected Employee Deferral Limit is used as the contribution cap in the projection. The calculator does not forecast future IRS inflation adjustments automatically.

Actual 401(k) plans can have plan-specific employer match formulas, eligibility requirements, vesting schedules, investment choices, fees, and contribution restrictions.

The projection does not fully model traditional-versus-Roth tax treatment, future income taxes, investment-management fees, withdrawals, loans, required minimum distributions, or every IRS limit that may apply.

It also does not automatically determine whether you qualify for age-based catch-up contributions. Use an appropriate employee deferral limit only when it applies to your situation and plan.

The assumed annual return is not a prediction or guarantee. Investor.gov notes that compounding can increase investment values over time, but actual investment results depend on the returns earned.

Current 2026 401(k) Rules and Limits

For tax year 2026, the standard employee elective-deferral limit for most traditional and safe-harbor 401(k) plans is:

$24,500 per year.

Participants who are age 50 or older at the end of 2026 may be permitted by their plan to make an additional $8,000 catch-up contribution. Employees who are ages 60, 61, 62, or 63 during the calendar year can have a higher 2026 catch-up limit of $11,250.

The separate 2026 limit on total annual additions to a participant's account is generally the lesser of 100% of compensation or $72,000, excluding qualifying catch-up contributions from that annual-additions limit. The compensation amount generally taken into account for these contribution rules is limited to $360,000 for 2026.

Beginning in 2026, certain participants making catch-up contributions in plans with Roth features may be required to make those catch-up contributions on a Roth basis when prior-year wages from the plan sponsor exceed the applicable $150,000 threshold described by the IRS.

These IRS rules do not mean every 401(k) plan allows the maximum contribution. A plan's own terms can impose lower contribution limits or different matching rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 401(k) contribution limit for 2026?

The standard employee elective-deferral limit is $24,500 for 2026. Eligible participants age 50 or older may also have access to catch-up contributions if their plan permits them.

What happens if my contribution percentage exceeds the deferral limit?

The calculator compares your salary-based employee contribution with the Employee Deferral Limit you entered. If the percentage-based contribution is higher, the calculator uses the deferral limit instead.

For example, if the calculated contribution is $27,000 but the entered limit is $24,500, the projected employee contribution for that year is $24,500.

How does the calculator estimate my employer match?

It first determines how much of your contribution falls within the entered Match Up to Salary percentage. It then applies the Employer Match Rate to that eligible contribution.

Actual employer plans can use more complex match formulas, so compare the estimate with your plan documents.

Does salary growth affect my 401(k) projection?

Yes. A higher projected salary can increase percentage-based employee contributions and the amount eligible for an employer match.

However, employee contributions can stop increasing once they reach the selected annual deferral limit.

Does the calculator automatically apply age-50 catch-up contributions?

The projection uses the Employee Deferral Limit entered in the calculator. It does not replace that value automatically simply because of the Current Age field.

If you are eligible for catch-up contributions, use the limit that accurately reflects your plan and applicable rules.

Why might my actual 401(k) balance differ from the estimate?

Actual investment returns fluctuate, salaries can change differently from the assumed rate, employer matching rules vary, and fees or plan restrictions can affect your balance.

The calculator therefore provides a planning estimate rather than a guaranteed future account value.

Does the projected balance show how much I will have after taxes?

No. The projected ending balance is an account-value estimate. It does not calculate your future income-tax liability or fully model differences between traditional and Roth 401(k) contributions.
