The Heat Index Calculator estimates how hot the air feels when air temperature and relative humidity are combined. Enter the air temperature and relative humidity to calculate the apparent temperature using the Rothfusz regression equation used by the National Weather Service.

What Is the Heat Index Calculator?

A heat index calculator estimates apparent, or “feels-like,” temperature from air temperature and relative humidity. Higher humidity can make hot conditions feel warmer because moisture in the air can reduce the effectiveness of evaporative cooling from sweat.

This calculator is useful for comparing hot and humid conditions, but the result is an environmental estimate rather than a measurement of body temperature or an individualized medical limit.

How to Use the Heat Index Calculator

Enter these two values:

  1. Air temperature: Enter the measured air temperature. In the Rothfusz equation, temperature is evaluated in degrees Fahrenheit (°F).
  2. Relative humidity (%): Enter the relative humidity as a percentage.

The calculator applies the Rothfusz regression formula and returns the calculated heat index, representing apparent temperature in degrees Fahrenheit.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator uses the Rothfusz regression equation:

HI = −42.379 + 2.04901523T + 10.14333127RH − 0.22475541TRH − 0.00683783T² − 0.05481717RH² + 0.00122874T²RH + 0.00085282TRH² − 0.00000199T²RH²

The National Weather Service documents this regression as an approximation of heat-index values based on the work of Robert Steadman.

Variables

Variable Meaning Unit
HI Calculated heat index or apparent temperature °F
T Air temperature °F
RH Relative humidity %

Calculation Sequence

The calculator:

  1. Reads the entered air temperature and relative humidity.
  2. Calculates the squared temperature and humidity terms required by the regression.
  3. Calculates the interaction terms between temperature and relative humidity.
  4. Substitutes those values into the Rothfusz regression equation.
  5. Returns the resulting heat index.

No additional output formula should be inferred beyond the calculations supported by this method.

Worked Example

Suppose the air temperature is 90°F and relative humidity is 60%.

Inputs

T = 90°F
RH = 60%

Step 1

Calculate the squared terms:

T² = 90² = 8,100
RH² = 60² = 3,600

Step 2

Substitute the values into the formula:

HI = −42.379 + 2.04901523(90) + 10.14333127(60) − 0.22475541(90)(60) − 0.00683783(8,100) − 0.05481717(3,600) + 0.00122874(8,100)(60) + 0.00085282(90)(3,600) − 0.00000199(8,100)(3,600)

Step 3

Evaluating the full equation gives:

HI = 99.6777179°F

Final Result

The calculated heat index is approximately 99.68°F.

This means that an air temperature of 90°F with 60% relative humidity corresponds to an apparent temperature of about 99.68°F under the conditions represented by the heat-index method.

Understanding Your Results

The main result is the heat index, expressed as an apparent temperature. It combines temperature and humidity into one value that helps describe how hot conditions may feel compared with air temperature alone.

The result should not be interpreted as body temperature, a diagnosis, or a personal medical threshold. Individual heat stress can also depend on activity, clothing, health, hydration, sun exposure, wind, and other environmental factors.

Assumptions and Limitations

The Rothfusz regression is designed for appropriate combinations of hot temperatures and relative humidity. The National Weather Service notes that the regression is not valid for extreme temperature and humidity combinations outside the data range on which the method was based.

Heat-index values are designed around shady, light-wind conditions. Current NWS guidance states that exposure to full sunshine can increase the apparent heat burden represented by heat index values by as much as 15°F.

The broader NWS heat-index procedure can also use a preliminary calculation and specific low- or high-humidity adjustments under certain conditions. The calculation documented for this CalcPro tool is the Rothfusz regression formula shown above, so results near those edge conditions may differ from an NWS operational calculation that applies additional steps.

Measurement accuracy also matters. An inaccurate air-temperature or humidity reading will directly affect the calculated heat index.

Current Rules / Data

As of September 2026, the National Weather Service continues to describe heat index as a measure of apparent temperature based on air temperature and relative humidity and notes that it represents shaded, light-wind conditions.

No yearly rate, tax value, or other regularly changing numerical input is required for this calculator. The main items that should be reviewed over time are official NWS methodology, validity guidance, and heat-safety recommendations.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does heat index mean?

Heat index is an apparent temperature that combines air temperature and relative humidity to describe how hot conditions feel to the human body.

What temperature unit does the Rothfusz formula use?

The Rothfusz regression shown on this page uses air temperature in degrees Fahrenheit and relative humidity as a percentage. Its output is heat index in degrees Fahrenheit.

Why can the heat index be higher than the air temperature?

High humidity can reduce evaporation of sweat, making hot conditions feel warmer. Because the formula combines temperature with relative humidity, the apparent temperature can be higher than the measured air temperature.

Why might my result differ from an official NWS heat index?

The NWS calculation procedure can use a preliminary equation and humidity adjustments in certain conditions. Input precision, rounding, and whether those additional steps are applied can therefore produce differences near the limits of the Rothfusz regression.

Does heat index include direct sunlight?

No. Standard NWS heat-index values are intended for shady, light-wind conditions. Direct sunshine can increase the effective heat burden beyond the standard heat-index value.

Is the heat index a measure of body temperature?

No. Heat index is an environmental apparent-temperature estimate. It does not predict a person's body temperature and should not be treated as individualized medical advice.
