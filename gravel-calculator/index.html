What Is the Gravel Calculator?

A gravel calculator converts a project footprint and layer depth into the bulk volume of aggregate needed. This version also supports an optional supplier-provided bulk density so you can estimate weight without assuming every gravel product weighs the same.

How to Use the Gravel Calculator

Select imperial or metric units, enter the project length, width, and compacted or target depth, then add an optional overage. If your quarry or supplier gives a bulk density, enter it to estimate mass.

Gravel Calculator Formula

Volume = length × width × depth. Imperial cubic yards = [L(ft) × W(ft) × D(in ÷ 12)] ÷ 27. Mass = volume × bulk density.

Formula Variables

Dimensions must use compatible units. Density is intentionally user-entered because aggregate weight varies with material and moisture. Overage increases the calculated volume after geometry is complete.

Calculation Example

A 30 ft × 12 ft area at 4 in deep equals 120 ft³, or about 4.44 yd³ before overage.

Understanding Your Results

Volume is the most reliable geometric result. Weight appears only when a density is supplied. Whole truckloads, bags, or tons should be matched to the supplier’s stated product data.

Important Factors

Irregular boundaries, excavation tolerances, slope, base preparation, compaction targets, and aggregate grading can change the amount delivered versus the simple geometric estimate.

Limitations

This calculator is not a geotechnical design tool and does not determine structural base thickness. Use engineering specifications for driveways, roads, foundations, drainage, or load-bearing work.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate gravel volume?

Multiply length by width by depth after converting all dimensions to compatible units. In imperial measurements, divide cubic feet by 27 to get cubic yards.

Why is gravel weight optional?

Weight depends on the bulk density of the specific aggregate, grading, moisture, and compaction. Enter a density from your supplier for a better mass estimate.

Does the calculator include compaction?

Not automatically. Use the optional overage field only if you intentionally want a planning allowance; supplier recommendations should take priority.
