What Is a BAC Calculator?

A blood alcohol concentration (BAC) calculator estimates alcohol concentration from reported drink volume and strength, body weight, sex-based distribution assumptions, and elapsed time. It is only a rough educational estimate.

How to Use the BAC Calculator

Enter body weight, elapsed time since drinking began, and the number of typical beers, glasses of wine, and spirit shots consumed. Presets use common US serving sizes and ABVs; real pours may be stronger or larger.

Widmark-Style Formula

The calculator first converts beverage volume and ABV into grams of ethanol using ethanol density (~0.789 g/mL). A simplified form is BAC% ≈ [alcohol grams ÷ (r × body weight kg)] ÷ 10 − 0.015 × hours, where r is 0.68 for males and 0.55 for females in the classic model.

Why BAC Estimates Vary

NHTSA notes that BAC is affected by number of drinks, drinking speed, sex, weight, and food. Forensic literature also shows meaningful variation in the Widmark distribution factor between individuals.

Safety Limitation

This page cannot measure your BAC and cannot tell whether you are safe to drive. If alcohol has been consumed, use a sober driver, taxi/ride service, public transport, or stay where you are.

Frequently Asked Questions

What formula does this BAC calculator use?

It uses a simplified Widmark-style estimate based on grams of ethanol, body weight, a sex-based distribution factor, and an average elimination rate.

What counts as a standard drink in the US?

CDC states that a US standard drink contains about 14 grams of pure alcohol, such as 12 oz of 5% beer, 5 oz of 12% wine, or 1.5 oz of 40% spirits.

Can BAC be predicted exactly from drinks?

No. Actual BAC varies with absorption, food, drinking rate, body composition, medications, health, and individual metabolism.

Can I use this result to decide whether to drive?

No. Never use an online BAC estimate to decide whether you are safe or legally allowed to drive or do safety-critical work.
