Use the Refinance Calculator to compare an existing fixed-rate loan with a proposed refinance. Enter your current balance, rates, remaining term, new term, closing costs, and any cash-out amount to estimate payment changes, interest costs, and the refinance break-even point.

What Is the Refinance Calculator?

The Refinance Calculator estimates how replacing an existing fixed-rate loan with a new fixed-rate loan could change your monthly principal-and-interest payment and total remaining interest.

It also accounts for refinance closing costs, cash-out borrowing, and whether closing costs are paid upfront or financed into the new loan. This makes it useful for comparing the existing loan with the proposed refinance before considering a lender offer.

How to Use the Refinance Calculator

Enter the following information in the calculator:

  • Current loan balance: The principal amount you still owe.
  • Current interest rate (%): The annual interest rate on the existing loan.
  • Time remaining (years): The number of years left before the current loan is fully repaid.
  • New interest rate (%): The annual rate offered for the refinance.
  • New loan term (years): The repayment period for the new loan.
  • Refinance closing costs: Costs associated with obtaining the new loan.
  • Cash-out amount: Any additional amount you want to borrow above the current balance.
  • Closing-cost treatment: Choose whether closing costs are paid upfront or added to the new loan principal.

The calculator returns the current payment, new payment, remaining interest on the current loan, interest on the new loan, new principal, and an upfront-cost break-even estimate when applicable.

Formula / Calculation Method

The calculator compares two fixed-rate, fully amortizing loan scenarios.

Monthly Payment Formula

For each loan, the monthly principal-and-interest payment is calculated using:

M=P×r1−(1+r)−nM = \frac{P \times r}{1-(1+r)^{-n}}

Where:

  • M = monthly principal-and-interest payment
  • P = loan principal
  • r = monthly interest rate
  • n = number of monthly payments

The monthly rate is:

r=Annual Interest Rate100×12r = \frac{\text{Annual Interest Rate}}{100 \times 12}

The number of payments is:

n=Years×12n = \text{Years} \times 12

The CFPB explains that fixed-rate mortgage principal-and-interest payments are calculated from the loan amount, interest rate, and loan term using a standard mathematical method.

Current Monthly Payment

For the existing loan:

Mc=B×rc1−(1+rc)−ncM_c = \frac{B \times r_c}{1-(1+r_c)^{-n_c}}

Where:

  • B = current loan balance
  • r₍c₎ = current monthly interest rate
  • n₍c₎ = remaining monthly payments

New Principal

The new principal depends on how closing costs are handled.

If closing costs are paid upfront:

Pn=B+CaP_n = B + C_a

If closing costs are financed:

Pn=B+Ca+CfP_n = B + C_a + C_f

Where:

  • P₍n₎ = new loan principal
  • B = current loan balance
  • C₍a₎ = cash-out amount
  • C₍f₎ = refinance closing costs

Financing closing costs increases the new loan amount rather than requiring those costs to be paid fully upfront. The CFPB notes that lenders may structure refinancing by adding closing costs to the loan amount.

New Monthly Payment

The refinanced payment is:

Mn=Pn×rn1−(1+rn)−nnM_n = \frac{P_n \times r_n}{1-(1+r_n)^{-n_n}}

Where:

  • M₍n₎ = new monthly payment
  • P₍n₎ = new principal
  • r₍n₎ = new monthly interest rate
  • n₍n₎ = new number of monthly payments

Current Remaining Interest

The estimated interest remaining on the current loan is:

Ic=(Mc×nc)−BI_c = (M_c \times n_c)-B

New Loan Interest

Estimated interest over the new loan term is:

In=(Mn×nn)−PnI_n = (M_n \times n_n)-P_n

This amount represents interest on the new principal. If cash-out or financed closing costs increase the principal, they can also increase the amount of interest paid over time.

Monthly Payment Savings

When the refinance payment is lower:

S=Mc−MnS = M_c-M_n

Where S is monthly payment savings.

A lower interest rate does not automatically guarantee a lower payment because the new principal and loan term may also change.

Break-Even Formula

When closing costs are paid upfront and the new payment is lower:

Break-even months=Upfront refinance costsMonthly payment savings\text{Break-even months} = \frac{\text{Upfront refinance costs}} {\text{Monthly payment savings}}

For example, if upfront refinance costs are $5,000 and the monthly payment falls by $200:

5,000÷200=255,000 \div 200 = 25

The simple payment-savings break-even point is therefore 25 months.

This break-even measure does not apply when the new monthly payment is equal to or higher than the current payment. When closing costs are financed instead of paid upfront, this calculator does not use the simple upfront-cost break-even calculation.

In this example:

  • Current monthly payment: $1,900.91
  • New monthly payment: $1,755.21
  • Monthly payment reduction: $145.70
  • Current remaining interest: $206,218.41
  • New loan interest: $171,250.10
  • New principal: $250,000

The break-even figure means it takes approximately 34 months of monthly payment savings to equal the $5,000 paid upfront. It does not mean that every financial cost or benefit of refinancing has been recovered.

Understanding Your Results

Current Monthly Payment

This is the estimated monthly principal-and-interest payment required to repay the current balance over the remaining term at the existing fixed rate.

It does not represent a complete mortgage payment if taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, or other escrow items are also collected. CFPB guidance distinguishes principal-and-interest payments from the larger total monthly mortgage payment that may include these additional costs.

New Monthly Payment

This is the estimated principal-and-interest payment for the refinanced loan using the new principal, interest rate, and term.

A longer new term can lower the monthly payment while potentially increasing the time over which interest is paid.

Current Remaining Interest

This estimates the interest you would pay if you kept the existing loan for its remaining scheduled term under the entered assumptions.

New Loan Interest

This estimates interest over the full new loan term.

Compare this figure with the current remaining interest rather than looking only at the interest rate or monthly payment.

New Principal

This is the balance used to calculate the refinanced loan.

Cash-out increases the new principal. Closing costs also increase the principal when you choose to finance them.

Break-Even on Upfront Costs

This estimates how many months of payment savings are required to recover closing costs paid upfront.

If you expect to replace or repay the loan before reaching that point, the simple monthly-payment break-even calculation will not have been reached.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator is an estimate based on the values you enter.

It assumes fixed interest rates and scheduled monthly principal-and-interest payments. It does not model changes in escrow, property taxes, homeowners insurance, or mortgage insurance.

The calculator does not include prepayment penalties, lender underwriting, qualification requirements, tax consequences, or points as a separate interest-rate-versus-upfront-cost tradeoff.

Cash-out increases the amount borrowed. If refinance closing costs are financed, they are added to the new principal.

The simple break-even calculation considers upfront refinance costs and monthly payment savings. It does not account for opportunity cost, taxes, investment returns, or every difference caused by changing the loan term.

Actual refinance charges can differ from early estimates. For mortgage refinancing, use the lender's Loan Estimate and final Closing Disclosure when evaluating actual loan terms and costs. CFPB explains that the Loan Estimate includes the estimated interest rate, monthly payment, and closing costs, while some costs may later change under permitted circumstances.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does a refinance break even?

For closing costs paid upfront, the calculator divides upfront refinance costs by monthly payment savings. If the refinance saves $150 per month and costs $4,500 upfront, the simple break-even point is 30 months.

What happens if I finance the closing costs?

Financed closing costs are added to the new principal. This can increase both the monthly payment and total interest compared with paying the same costs upfront. Because the costs are not being recovered from an upfront cash payment, the calculator does not apply its simple upfront-cost break-even formula.

Does a lower refinance rate always mean a lower monthly payment?

No. The payment also depends on the new principal and loan term. Cash-out, financed closing costs, or a shorter term can produce a higher payment even when the interest rate is lower.

Does this calculator include property taxes and insurance?

No. The calculated monthly amounts represent loan principal and interest under the calculator's fixed-rate model. Taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance, and escrow can make an actual mortgage payment higher.

How does cash-out affect the refinance?

The cash-out amount is added to the balance being refinanced. This increases the new principal and therefore affects the new monthly payment and interest cost.

Why might my lender's refinance figures be different?

A lender may use exact transaction dates, specific fees, points, credits, escrow adjustments, insurance charges, or other loan terms that this calculator does not model. Actual lender closing costs should be checked against the Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure.

Should I compare more than the monthly payment?

Yes. The calculator provides the new payment together with interest estimates, new principal, closing-cost treatment, and break-even information because changing the loan term or adding fees can affect the overall comparison even when the monthly payment falls.
