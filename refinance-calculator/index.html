What Is Refinancing?

Refinancing replaces an existing loan with a new loan. Borrowers may refinance to change the interest rate, payment, loan term, or amount borrowed. The transaction can also involve closing costs and, in some cases, cash-out.

How This Refinance Calculator Compares Loans

The current-loan payment is calculated from the remaining balance, current rate, and time left. The new payment is calculated from the proposed rate and term. If closing costs are financed, they are added to the new principal; otherwise they remain an upfront cash cost.

Break-Even Formula

Break-even months = Upfront refinance costs ÷ Monthly payment savings

Break-even only makes sense when closing costs are paid upfront and the new payment is lower. It does not capture tax effects, opportunity cost, or differences in total loan duration.

Compare More Than the Monthly Payment

A longer new term can reduce the monthly payment while increasing how long interest is paid. Review remaining interest on the current loan, estimated new-loan interest, the new principal, and how long you expect to keep the loan.

Limitations

This model does not include escrow changes, mortgage insurance, prepayment penalties, points as a separate interest-rate tradeoff, tax consequences, or lender underwriting. Actual closing costs should be taken from a Loan Estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does a refinance break even?

A simple break-even point is the upfront refinance cost divided by monthly payment savings. If the new payment is not lower, this simple payment-savings break-even measure does not apply.

Should I refinance only because the rate is lower?

A lower rate can reduce interest cost, but the new term, fees, cash-out, and how long you keep the loan also matter. Compare the complete new loan rather than the rate alone.

What if closing costs are financed?

This calculator adds financed closing costs to the new principal. Because the costs are not paid upfront, the simple upfront-cost break-even calculation is shown as not applicable.
