The Lean Body Mass Calculator estimates adult lean body mass from your sex, height, and weight. It calculates separate results using the Boer, James, and Hume equations so you can compare how the three methods estimate your lean mass.

What Is the Lean Body Mass Calculator?

Lean body mass (LBM) represents the portion of body weight estimated to be outside body-fat mass. This calculator does not directly measure body composition. Instead, it uses three published height-and-weight equations to estimate LBM.

The calculator is useful for comparing the Boer, James, and Hume estimates from the same measurements. Because the equations use different coefficients and mathematical methods, their results may differ.

How to Use the Lean Body Mass Calculator

  1. Select Sex: Choose the sex used by the equations because each formula has separate male and female coefficients.
  2. Enter Height: Enter your height in centimeters (cm).
  3. Enter Weight: Enter your total body weight in kilograms (kg).
  4. Calculate: The calculator applies the Boer, James, and Hume formulas and displays the estimated lean body mass results for comparison.

Formula / Calculation Method

This calculator uses three adult lean body mass equations. In every formula:

Symbol Meaning Unit
LBM Estimated lean body mass kg
W Total body weight kg
H Height cm

Boer Formula

For males:

LBM = 0.407W + 0.267H − 19.2

For females:

LBM = 0.252W + 0.473H − 48.3

The Boer equation was published in 1984 as an anthropometric method for estimating lean body mass from height and weight.

James Formula

For males:

LBM = 1.1W − 128(W/H)²

For females:

LBM = 1.07W − 148(W/H)²

Unlike the Boer and Hume equations, the James formula includes a squared weight-to-height term. The same sex-specific James equations are documented in peer-reviewed clinical literature.

Hume Formula

For males:

LBM = 0.32810W + 0.33929H − 29.5336

For females:

LBM = 0.29569W + 0.41813H − 43.2933

Hume's original study developed separate equations for males and females using height and weight to predict lean body mass.

Calculation Sequence

The calculator first reads the selected sex, height in centimeters, and weight in kilograms. It then selects the appropriate male or female coefficients and calculates the Boer, James, and Hume estimates separately.

The three results are kept separate rather than treated as identical values because each equation is a different predictive model.

Implied Body-Fat Mass

When total body weight and estimated lean mass are known, the remaining mass can be expressed as:

Implied body-fat mass = Total body weight − Estimated LBM

This is an implied value based on the equation result, not a direct measurement of body fat. Body-composition models commonly separate total body mass into fat and non-fat components, although modern terminology distinguishes carefully between lean mass, fat-free mass, and related measurements.

Worked Example

Consider a male with:

Height: 180 cm
Weight: 80 kg

Step 1: Boer Formula

LBM = 0.407(80) + 0.267(180) − 19.2
LBM = 32.56 + 48.06 − 19.2
Boer LBM = 61.42 kg

Implied body-fat mass:

80 − 61.42 = 18.58 kg

Step 2: James Formula

LBM = 1.1(80) − 128(80/180)²
LBM = 88 − 128(0.444444...)²
LBM = 88 − 25.28395...
James LBM ≈ 62.72 kg

Implied body-fat mass:

80 − 62.72 ≈ 17.28 kg

Step 3: Hume Formula

LBM = 0.32810(80) + 0.33929(180) − 29.5336
LBM = 26.248 + 61.0722 − 29.5336
Hume LBM ≈ 57.79 kg

Implied body-fat mass:

80 − 57.79 ≈ 22.21 kg

Final Result

For the same 80 kg, 180 cm male, the formulas produce different estimates:

Formula Estimated LBM Implied Body-Fat Mass
Boer 61.42 kg 18.58 kg
James 62.72 kg 17.28 kg
Hume 57.79 kg 22.21 kg

The difference between the results reflects differences in the equations, not a change in the person's actual body composition.

Understanding Your Results

Boer result is the lean body mass estimated using the Boer coefficients for the selected sex.

James result uses a different model that includes the squared weight-to-height ratio, so it can respond differently to unusual combinations of height and weight.

Hume result is another independent height-and-weight estimate derived from sex-specific equations.

The results should be interpreted as estimates. None of these equations directly measures muscle, body fat, bone, or other individual tissues.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator assumes that sex, height, and weight provide enough information to generate a useful statistical estimate. Two people with the same sex, height, and weight will receive the same equation results even if one has substantially more muscle or body fat than the other.

The equations may be less representative at unusual heights, weights, or body compositions. They are adult prediction equations and should not be treated as individualized measurements for children.

Lean body mass estimates are also not equivalent to a clinical body-composition assessment. Modern body-composition literature notes that terms such as lean body mass, lean mass, lean soft tissue, and fat-free mass can have different technical meanings depending on the measurement model being used.

When lean mass is being used for a medical decision, medication calculation, or another clinical purpose, the required equation or measurement method should be determined by an appropriate healthcare professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does lean body mass mean?

Lean body mass is an estimate of the portion of body weight that is not attributed to storage body fat. It includes components such as muscle, water, bone, and organs, although exact terminology can vary between body-composition methods.

Which formulas does this calculator use?

The calculator uses the Boer, James, and Hume formulas. Each uses height, weight, and sex but applies different coefficients and calculations.

Why do the three formulas give different results?

They are separate predictive equations developed from different data and mathematical relationships. A difference between their results is therefore expected and does not mean that one calculation was performed incorrectly.

Is lean body mass the same as muscle mass?

No. Lean body mass includes more than skeletal muscle. It can also include water, organs, bone, and other non-fat components.

Does the calculator directly measure body fat?

No. It estimates lean body mass mathematically from height, weight, and sex. Any body-fat mass derived from the result is therefore also an estimate rather than a direct body-composition measurement.

Can athletes use this calculator?

The calculator can provide an estimate, but the equations cannot detect unusually high muscularity. A muscular athlete and another person with the same sex, height, and weight receive the same equation result even when their actual body compositions differ.

Can this calculator be used for medical decisions?

It can provide general informational estimates, but clinical applications may require a particular validated equation or directly measured body composition. Do not substitute an online estimate for a method specified by a healthcare professional.
