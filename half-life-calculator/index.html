What Is a Half-Life Calculator?

A half-life calculator estimates the amount remaining after a specified time when a quantity follows exponential decay. It applies to mathematical models of radioactive decay and other processes that can reasonably be represented by a constant half-life.

How to Use the Half-Life Calculator

  1. Enter the initial amount.
  2. Enter the half-life and elapsed time using the same unit.
  3. Calculate to see the remaining amount, fraction, number of half-lives, and decay constant.

Half-Life Formula

N(t) = N₀ × (1/2)^(t / t½)    and    λ = ln(2) / t½

After each full half-life, the modeled quantity is multiplied by one-half. OpenStax gives the equivalent exponential-decay form and the relationship between the decay constant and half-life.

Calculation Example

Starting with 100 units and a half-life of 10 years, after 20 years exactly two half-lives have elapsed. The model gives 100 × (1/2)² = 25 units.

Limitations

This page is a mathematical model. Real systems may involve mixtures of isotopes, biological clearance, changing rates, measurement uncertainty, or other mechanisms not represented by a single constant half-life.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much remains after three half-lives?

The remaining fraction is (1/2)³ = 1/8, or 12.5% of the initial amount.

Can I use days instead of years?

Yes. The half-life and elapsed time only need to use the same unit. The selected label is displayed with the decay constant.
