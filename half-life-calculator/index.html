The Half-Life Calculator estimates how much of an exponentially decaying quantity remains after a given time. Enter the initial amount, half-life, elapsed time, and time unit to calculate the remaining amount and supporting decay values.

What Is the Half-Life Calculator?

The Half-Life Calculator models a quantity that decreases by half during each half-life.

It can be used for mathematical decay problems involving radioactive materials or other processes that follow a constant exponential half-life model. The calculation can show the remaining amount, amount that has decayed, number of elapsed half-lives, and decay constant.

In an exponential decay model, one half remains after one half-life, one quarter after two half-lives, one eighth after three half-lives, and so on.

How to Use the Half-Life Calculator

Enter the following values:

  1. Initial Amount: Enter the quantity present at the beginning of the calculation. The result uses the same amount unit.
  2. Half-Life: Enter the time required for the modeled quantity to decrease to half its current amount.
  3. Elapsed Time: Enter how much time has passed since the initial amount was measured.
  4. Time Unit: Select the applicable time unit. The half-life and elapsed time must be expressed consistently for the calculation.

The calculator then applies the exponential half-life formula to determine the remaining quantity and supporting values.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator uses:

N(t)=N0(12)t/t1/2N(t)=N_0\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t/t\_{1/2}}

This models exponential decay directly in terms of half-life. OpenStax gives the equivalent exponential form as N=N0e−λtN=N_0e^{-\lambda t}.

Decay Constant

The decay constant is related to half-life by:

λ=ln⁡(2)t1/2\lambda=\frac{\ln(2)}{t\_{1/2}}

where ln⁡(2)\ln(2) is approximately 0.693147. This relationship is standard for exponential decay.

Elapsed Half-Lives

The number of half-lives represented by the elapsed time is:

n=tt1/2n=\frac{t}{t\_{1/2}}

The main formula can therefore also be written as:

N(t)=N0(12)nN(t)=N_0\left(\frac12\right)^n

For a whole number of half-lives, this is equivalent to repeatedly dividing the starting amount by two.

Decayed Amount

After the remaining amount has been calculated, the amount no longer remaining in the model is:

Decayed Amount=N0−N(t)\text{Decayed Amount}=N_0-N(t)

Calculation Sequence

  1. Read the initial amount, half-life, elapsed time, and time unit.
  2. Calculate elapsed half-lives using t/t1/2t/t\_{1/2}.
  3. Raise 1/21/2 to the number of elapsed half-lives.
  4. Multiply that value by the initial amount to find the remaining amount.
  5. Compare the remaining amount with the initial amount to obtain the decayed amount.
  6. Calculate the decay constant from ln⁡(2)/t1/2\ln(2)/t\_{1/2}.

Understanding Your Results

Remaining Amount shows the quantity predicted to remain after the entered elapsed time. It uses the same unit as the initial amount.

Decayed Amount represents the difference between the original amount and the calculated remaining amount.

Elapsed Half-Lives indicates how many half-life periods fit into the elapsed time. It does not need to be a whole number; the exponential formula also handles fractional half-lives.

Decay Constant expresses the exponential decay rate in reciprocal time units. A shorter half-life corresponds to a larger decay constant, while a longer half-life corresponds to a smaller decay constant.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator uses a single constant half-life throughout the calculation. It assumes the quantity follows ideal exponential decay and that the half-life does not change with time.

The half-life must be greater than zero because it is used as a divisor in the formula. The elapsed time and half-life also need compatible time units.

The model does not account for measurement uncertainty, changing decay rates, mixtures of substances with different half-lives, or multiple simultaneous processes.

For real biological systems, a simple mathematical half-life may not fully represent absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination, or other biological processes. Results from this calculator should therefore not be treated as individualized medical or clinical calculations.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does half-life mean?

Half-life is the amount of time required for an exponentially decaying quantity to decrease to half its current amount.

How much remains after one half-life?

After one complete half-life, 50% of the initial modeled quantity remains. After two half-lives, 25% remains, and after three, 12.5% remains.

Does the initial amount affect the half-life?

No. In a constant exponential decay model, the half-life is determined by the decay rate rather than the starting quantity. Changing the initial amount changes the remaining amount but not the half-life.

Can the elapsed time be less than one half-life?

Yes. The exponential formula supports fractional numbers of half-lives, so the elapsed time does not need to be an exact multiple of the half-life.

What is the relationship between half-life and decay constant?

They are related by:

λ=ln⁡(2)t1/2\lambda=\frac{\ln(2)}{t\_{1/2}}

A longer half-life produces a smaller decay constant, while a shorter half-life produces a larger one.

Do the time units matter?

Yes. The elapsed time and half-life must use consistent units. If half-life is expressed in hours, the elapsed time should also be represented in hours before applying the formula.

Can this calculator model any type of decay?

It is appropriate when a process can reasonably be represented by a single constant exponential half-life. Processes with changing rates, multiple components, or more complex mechanisms may require a different model.
