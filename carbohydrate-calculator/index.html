What Is a Carbohydrate Calculator?

This calculator estimates a daily carbohydrate range from total energy intake. It uses the adult Acceptable Macronutrient Distribution Range (AMDR) of 45% to 65% of calories from carbohydrate and converts carbohydrate calories to grams at 4 kcal per gram.

Carbohydrate Formula

Carb grams = Daily calories × Carb percentage ÷ 4

If you do not enter known daily calories, the calculator estimates basal metabolic rate with the Mifflin–St Jeor equation and multiplies it by the selected activity factor. That energy estimate is a planning assumption, not a clinical prescription.

Understanding Your Results

The lower and upper outputs correspond to 45% and 65% of estimated daily energy. The midpoint uses 55%. The adult RDA of 130 g/day is shown as a reference from the Dietary Reference Intakes, but it does not replace individualized nutrition advice.

Limitations

Energy expenditure and carbohydrate needs vary with training volume, pregnancy, lactation, age, medical conditions, medications, and goals. Athletes doing prolonged exercise may require sport-specific carbohydrate planning.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories are in one gram of carbohydrate?

Dietary carbohydrate is commonly calculated at about 4 kilocalories per gram, so carbohydrate grams can be estimated by dividing carbohydrate calories by 4.

What percentage of calories should come from carbohydrates?

The Dietary Reference Intake Acceptable Macronutrient Distribution Range for adults is 45% to 65% of total energy from carbohydrate.

Is 130 grams of carbohydrate a universal target?

The adult RDA is 130 g/day, but individual energy needs and medical needs vary. The AMDR produces a broader range based on total energy intake.

Sources and Methodology

Macronutrient ranges are based on U.S. Dietary Reference Intake material summarized by the USDA DRI Calculator and federal Dietary Guidelines tables. Functionality was also cross-checked against Calculator.net.
