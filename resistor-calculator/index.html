The Resistor Calculator decodes 4-, 5-, and 6-band resistor color codes and calculates equivalent resistance for resistors connected in series or parallel. It can also determine tolerance, minimum and maximum resistance, and temperature coefficient information when supported by the selected band configuration.

What Is the Resistor Calculator?

A resistor calculator helps identify a resistor from its color bands or combine several resistor values into one equivalent resistance.

Use the color-code section when you need to interpret a banded resistor. Use the resistor-network section when you already know the resistor values and want the total resistance of a series or parallel connection.

The color-coding method follows the band structure used for resistor marking under IEC 60062.

How to Use the Resistor Calculator

Band Count

Select whether the resistor has 4, 5, or 6 bands.

A 4-band resistor uses two significant digits, followed by a multiplier and tolerance band. A 5-band resistor uses three significant digits, a multiplier, and tolerance. A 6-band resistor adds another band for information such as the temperature coefficient used by this calculator.

Standard

Choose the resistor standard available in the calculator. This setting determines how the resistor's coded information is interpreted.

Resistor Values

For a resistor network, enter the individual resistance values you want to combine.

Connection

Select the way the resistors are connected:

  • Series: resistances are added together.
  • Parallel: the reciprocal of the total resistance equals the sum of the reciprocals of the individual resistances.

Input Unit

Choose the unit that applies to the entered resistor values. The unit tells the calculator how the numerical resistance values should be interpreted before the total is calculated.

Formula / Calculation Method

Resistor Color Code Formula

A color-coded resistor is calculated from its significant digits and multiplier.

For a 4-band resistor:

Resistance = Two-digit significant value × Multiplier

The bands are interpreted as:

  1. First significant digit
  2. Second significant digit
  3. Multiplier
  4. Tolerance

For a 5- or 6-band resistor:

Resistance = Three-digit significant value × Multiplier

The first three bands form the significant value. The following band supplies the multiplier. The tolerance band defines the permitted variation around nominal resistance. A 6-band resistor can also provide a temperature coefficient value.

Variables

  • R = nominal resistance, in ohms (Ω)
  • S = number formed by the significant-digit bands
  • M = multiplier represented by the multiplier band
  • t = tolerance percentage
  • TCR = temperature coefficient of resistance, normally expressed in ppm/K

The basic color-code calculation can be written as:

R = S × M

Tolerance Range

Tolerance describes how far the actual resistance may vary from its nominal value.

Minimum Resistance = R × (1 − t/100)
Maximum Resistance = R × (1 + t/100)

For example, a 1,000 Ω resistor with ±1% tolerance has an expected resistance range of 990 Ω to 1,010 Ω.

Temperature Coefficient of Resistance

For band configurations that encode it, TCR indicates how resistance changes with temperature and is commonly expressed in parts per million per kelvin (ppm/K).

The calculator reports the encoded TCR value. It does not require a temperature input, so it does not calculate a temperature-adjusted operating resistance.

Series Resistance

For resistors connected in series:

Rtotal = R1 + R2 + R3 + ... + Rn

or:

Rtotal = ΣR

The same current passes through each resistor in a series path, and their resistance values add directly.

Parallel Resistance

For resistors connected in parallel:

1 / Rtotal = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3 + ... + 1/Rn

Therefore:

Rtotal = 1 / Σ(1/R)

The equivalent resistance of a parallel network is lower than its smallest individual resistance.

Calculation Sequence

For color-code calculations, the calculator:

  1. Identifies the significant digits from the selected bands.
  2. Forms the two- or three-digit significant value.
  3. Applies the multiplier.
  4. Determines the nominal resistance.
  5. Applies the tolerance percentage.
  6. Calculates the minimum and maximum resistance.
  7. Reports the TCR when the selected band configuration provides it.

For resistor networks, the calculator interprets the entered resistor values and units, then applies either the series or parallel resistance formula according to the selected connection.

Worked Example

Consider a 6-band resistor with these colors:

  • 1st band: Brown
  • 2nd band: Black
  • 3rd band: Black
  • Multiplier: Brown
  • Tolerance: Brown
  • TCR: Red

Understanding Your Results

Nominal Resistance

The nominal resistance is the resistance represented by the resistor's significant bands and multiplier. It is normally expressed in ohms and may be displayed in a larger unit when appropriate.

Tolerance

Tolerance is the permitted percentage variation around the nominal resistance. A tighter tolerance means the resistor's actual value is expected to remain closer to its stated nominal value.

Minimum Resistance

The minimum result is the lower end of the resistance range after the tolerance is applied.

Maximum Resistance

The maximum result is the upper end of the tolerance range.

TCR

The temperature coefficient of resistance describes the resistor's specified change in resistance with temperature, normally in ppm/K. It is an encoded characteristic and should not be confused with an actual temperature-adjusted resistance calculation.

Equivalent Resistance

For a network calculation, equivalent resistance is the single resistance value that represents the combined resistors under the selected series or parallel arrangement.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator uses ideal resistor color-code and equivalent-resistance formulas.

Actual resistor behavior can differ because of manufacturing tolerance, operating temperature, self-heating, aging, lead or contact resistance, and frequency-dependent effects.

The series and parallel calculations model resistor resistance only. They do not perform complete circuit analysis or determine current, voltage, resistor wattage, thermal limits, or safe component ratings.

Real AC circuits may also contain capacitance, inductance, and other effects that require impedance rather than simple resistance calculations.

For safety-critical electrical or electronic design, verify component ratings and circuit conditions instead of relying only on an equivalent-resistance result.

Current Rules / Data

As of September 24, 2026, IEC lists IEC 60062:2016+AMD1:2019 as the consolidated version of its marking-code standard for resistors and capacitors. The standard includes methods for resistance-value marking, tolerance, resistor color coding, and TCR marking.

The calculator's formulas themselves do not require frequently changing market rates or external numerical data, but the color-code methodology should be reviewed if the applicable resistor-marking standard changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do resistor color bands mean?

The bands encode significant digits, a multiplier, tolerance, and, for supported configurations, temperature coefficient information. Their meaning depends on both their color and their position on the resistor.

What is the difference between 4-band and 5-band resistors?

A standard 4-band calculation uses two significant digits before the multiplier. A 5-band calculation uses three significant digits, allowing the resistance value to be represented with greater precision.

What does the sixth resistor band mean?

In the configuration used by this calculator, the sixth band can represent the temperature coefficient of resistance, reported in ppm/K.

How is resistor tolerance calculated?

The calculator applies the tolerance percentage above and below the nominal resistance:

Minimum = R × (1 − tolerance)
Maximum = R × (1 + tolerance)

where the tolerance percentage is first converted to decimal form.

How do I calculate resistors in series?

Add all resistor values together. For example, 220 Ω, 330 Ω, and 470 Ω in series have a total resistance of 1,020 Ω.

How do I calculate resistors in parallel?

Add the reciprocals of the resistor values and take the reciprocal of that total:

Rtotal = 1 / (1/R1 + 1/R2 + ... + 1/Rn)

For positive resistor values, the resulting parallel resistance is lower than the smallest individual resistor.

Does the resistor calculator account for self-heating or resistor wattage?

No. The implemented resistance calculations do not model power dissipation, self-heating, circuit current, operating voltage, or component power ratings. These factors may be important in real circuit design.
