The Mileage Calculator finds trip distance from starting and ending odometer readings. It also estimates driving time, fuel used, and fuel cost from your average speed, fuel economy, and fuel price.

What Is the Mileage Calculator?

A mileage calculator helps estimate key values for a vehicle trip. Instead of entering the trip distance directly, this calculator determines distance from the difference between the ending and starting odometer readings.

It then uses that distance to estimate travel time, fuel consumption, and fuel cost. You can work with miles and MPG or kilometers and L/100 km.

How to Use the Mileage Calculator

Enter the following values in order:

  1. Starting odometer: Enter the vehicle's odometer reading at the beginning of the trip.
  2. Ending odometer: Enter the odometer reading at the end of the trip.
  3. Distance unit: Select miles or kilometers to match your odometer readings.
  4. Average speed: Enter the average speed for the trip in the corresponding unit per hour, such as mph or km/h.
  5. Fuel economy: Enter the vehicle's fuel-efficiency value.
  6. Fuel economy format: Select MPG or L/100 km.
  7. Fuel price: Enter the price per gallon or liter, matching the fuel-economy method being used.

For meaningful calculations, average speed and fuel economy must be greater than zero. The ending odometer should normally be equal to or greater than the starting reading.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formulas

The calculator first finds the distance traveled:

Distance = Ending Odometer − Starting Odometer

Estimated driving time is:

Time = Distance ÷ Average Speed

For fuel economy expressed in MPG:

Fuel Used = Miles Traveled ÷ MPG

For fuel economy expressed in liters per 100 kilometers:

Fuel Used = Kilometers Traveled × (L/100 km) ÷ 100

Fuel cost is then:

Fuel Cost = Fuel Used × Fuel Price

The distance-and-time relationship follows the standard average-speed equation, where average speed equals distance divided by time. The U.S. Department of Energy also uses distance divided by MPG when calculating gasoline consumption.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Subtract the starting odometer from the ending odometer.
  2. Divide the resulting distance by average speed to estimate driving time.
  3. Calculate fuel consumption using the selected fuel-economy format.
  4. Multiply fuel used by the entered fuel price.
  5. Display the trip distance, estimated time, fuel use, and fuel cost.

Trip Distance

D = Oₑ − Oₛ

This calculation uses the change in the vehicle's odometer reading rather than a map or route distance.

Estimated Driving Time

t = D ÷ v

This is an estimate based on the average speed entered. Average speed is mathematically defined as distance traveled divided by elapsed time.

Fuel Used with MPG

F = D ÷ Eₘₚg

For example, traveling 300 miles in a vehicle averaging 30 MPG requires an estimated 10 gallons.

Fuel economy is commonly expressed as the distance a vehicle travels per unit of fuel, including miles per gallon.

Fuel Used with L/100 km

F = D × Eₗ ÷ 100

For example, a 500 km trip at 8 L/100 km requires an estimated 40 liters of fuel.

Fuel Cost

C = F × P

The calculator multiplies estimated fuel consumption by the fuel price entered by the user.

Worked Example

Suppose a vehicle starts a trip with an odometer reading of 12,450 miles and finishes at 12,750 miles.

Inputs

  • Starting odometer: 12,450 miles
  • Ending odometer: 12,750 miles
  • Average speed: 60 mph
  • Fuel economy: 30 MPG
  • Fuel price: $3.50 per gallon

Step 1: Calculate Distance

Distance = 12,750 − 12,450
Distance = 300 miles

Step 2: Calculate Driving Time

Time = 300 ÷ 60
Time = 5 hours

Step 3: Calculate Fuel Used

Fuel Used = 300 ÷ 30
Fuel Used = 10 gallons

Step 4: Calculate Fuel Cost

Fuel Cost = 10 × $3.50
Fuel Cost = $35.00

Final Result

For this example, the estimated trip values are:

  • Distance: 300 miles
  • Driving time: 5 hours
  • Fuel used: 10 gallons
  • Fuel cost: $35.00

Understanding Your Results

Trip distance is the difference between the ending and starting odometer readings.

Estimated driving time shows how long the calculated distance would take at the average speed entered. It is not a traffic-aware arrival-time estimate.

Fuel used estimates the amount of fuel required for the trip based on the entered fuel economy.

Fuel cost estimates how much that fuel would cost using the entered price per gallon or liter.

Keep full precision when checking the calculations manually. Rounding intermediate values can cause small differences from the displayed result.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator provides estimates based on the values entered. Actual travel time can change because of traffic, stops, weather, road conditions, speed changes, and route conditions.

Real-world fuel economy can also vary with driving behavior, vehicle condition, speed, terrain, load, tires, temperature, and other operating conditions. U.S. Department of Energy guidance notes that fuel economy can vary significantly with driving, vehicle condition, and environmental factors.

Odometer measurement error or changes in tire size can affect calculated trip mileage. Fuel cost will also differ if the actual fuel price or real fuel consumption changes during the trip.

An average speed or MPG value of zero cannot be used in a division calculation. An ending odometer below the starting odometer would normally produce a negative distance and should be checked for an incorrect entry, odometer reset, or rollover.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is mileage calculated from odometer readings?

Subtract the starting odometer reading from the ending odometer reading:

Mileage = Ending Odometer − Starting Odometer
How does the calculator estimate driving time?

It divides calculated trip distance by the average speed entered:

Time = Distance ÷ Average Speed

This assumes the entered speed represents the average for the entire trip.

How is fuel use calculated with MPG?

For MPG, divide miles traveled by miles per gallon:

Gallons Used = Miles ÷ MPG

The U.S. Department of Energy uses the same distance-per-MPG relationship in its vehicle fuel-use methodology.

How does L/100 km work?

L/100 km represents the number of liters used to travel 100 kilometers. Estimated liters used are calculated as:

Liters Used = Kilometers × L/100 km ÷ 100
Why can actual driving time be different?

The calculator uses average speed only. Traffic, rest stops, signals, roadworks, weather, and changes in driving speed can make actual travel time shorter or longer.

Why can actual fuel use differ from the estimate?

Fuel economy is not constant in every driving condition. Speed, acceleration, vehicle load, terrain, temperature, tires, mechanical condition, and driving habits can change actual fuel consumption.

Does the calculator automatically know the current fuel price?

The fuel-cost calculation uses the fuel price entered into the calculator. This allows you to use the price that applies to your location and fuel type.
