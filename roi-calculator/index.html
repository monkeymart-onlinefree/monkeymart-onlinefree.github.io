What Is an ROI Calculator?

An ROI calculator measures the percentage gain or loss produced by an investment relative to its original cost. It is useful for stocks, projects, marketing spend, equipment, property improvements, and other decisions where you can identify a starting cost and ending value or proceeds.

ROI Formula

ROI = (Ending Value − Investment Cost) ÷ Investment Cost × 100

Fidelity describes the same basic method: subtract total investment cost from proceeds or current value, divide by total cost, then convert the result to a percentage.

Annualized ROI

Annualized ROI = (Ending Value ÷ Investment Cost)1 / Years − 1

Annualizing adds the time dimension, but it assumes a smooth compound rate between the starting and ending values.

Calculation Example

If you invest $10,000 and the investment is worth $12,500 after two years, the net gain is $2,500 and the basic ROI is 25%. The equivalent compound annualized return is about 11.80% per year.

Understanding Your Results

Basic ROI is easy to compare, but it does not automatically account for taxes, inflation, fees that were not included in cost, risk, interim cash flows, or the timing of those cash flows. Include all relevant costs in the investment-cost input for a more meaningful result.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does ROI measure?

ROI compares an investment’s net gain or loss with its cost. A positive ROI means the ending value or proceeds exceed the cost; a negative ROI means the investment lost value.

Does ROI include time?

Basic ROI does not. This calculator also shows annualized ROI when you provide a holding period, which makes returns over different lengths of time easier to compare.

Can ROI be used for every investment?

It is a useful screening metric, but it does not by itself capture risk, taxes, inflation, cash-flow timing, or liquidity.
