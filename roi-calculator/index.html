The ROI Calculator measures the return on an investment using the amount invested, ending value or proceeds, and holding period. It calculates your overall return on investment and provides supporting values such as net gain and time-based investment results.

What Is the ROI Calculator?

The ROI Calculator helps you measure how much an investment gained or lost compared with its original cost.

It can be useful for reviewing an investment, business project, asset purchase, or another situation where you know the initial amount invested and the final value or proceeds. The holding period also provides the time information needed for the calculator's annualized ROI result.

How to Use the ROI Calculator

Enter the following values:

  1. Amount Invested — Enter the original amount invested. Use the same currency as the ending value.
  2. Ending Value / Proceeds — Enter the final or current value of the investment, or the proceeds received.
  3. Holding Period (Years) — Enter how many years the investment was held. This provides the time basis for the calculator's annualized result.

After entering the values, the calculator determines the ROI and supporting investment results.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main ROI Formula

The calculator uses:

ROI = ((Ending Value − Investment Cost) ÷ Investment Cost) × 100

This measures the gain or loss relative to the original investment and converts it to a percentage.

Fidelity describes the same basic ROI method as subtracting total investment cost from proceeds or current value, dividing by total cost, and converting the result to a percentage.

Variables

  • ROI = Return on investment, expressed as a percentage
  • Ending Value = Final value or proceeds from the investment
  • Investment Cost = Original amount invested
  • Holding Period = Length of time the investment was held, measured in years

Calculation Sequence

  1. Subtract the amount invested from the ending value.
  2. Divide the resulting gain or loss by the amount invested.
  3. Multiply the result by 100.
  4. Express the final result as an ROI percentage.
  5. Use the entered holding period for the calculator's time-based annualized ROI output.

Net Gain

The difference used in the numerator of the ROI formula is:

Net Gain = Ending Value − Amount Invested

A positive value represents a gain, while a negative value represents a loss.

Holding Period and Annualized ROI

Basic ROI measures the total return over the entire investment period and does not itself account for how long the investment was held. This calculator therefore asks for a holding period in years so it can also provide an annualized ROI result.

Annualizing a return can make the time period easier to consider when reviewing investments held for different lengths of time. CFI and Fidelity both distinguish total return from time-adjusted or annualized measures.

Worked Example

Suppose:

Inputs

  • Amount invested: $8,000
  • Ending value: $10,000
  • Holding period: 3 years

Step 1: Calculate the Gain

$10,000 − $8,000 = $2,000

The investment gained $2,000.

Step 2: Divide the Gain by the Investment Cost

$2,000 ÷ $8,000 = 0.25

Step 3: Convert to a Percentage

0.25 × 100 = 25%

Final Result

The total ROI is:

25%

This means the ending value is 25% higher than the original amount invested. The three-year holding period provides the time information used for the calculator's annualized ROI result.

Understanding Your Results

ROI

ROI shows the investment's total gain or loss relative to the original amount invested.

A positive ROI means the ending value is greater than the amount invested. A negative ROI means the ending value is lower.

For example, a 25% ROI means the investment gained an amount equal to 25% of its original cost.

Net Gain

Net gain shows the monetary difference between the ending value and the amount invested.

Unlike ROI, which is expressed as a percentage, net gain is expressed in the same currency used for the input values.

Value Multiple

The value multiple provides another way to compare the ending value with the original amount invested. It expresses the relationship as a multiple rather than an ROI percentage.

Annualized ROI

Annualized ROI expresses investment performance on a yearly basis using the entered holding period. This can provide additional context when the total ROI covers more or less than one year.

The total ROI and annualized ROI answer different questions: total ROI describes the return over the complete holding period, while the annualized result adds a time-based perspective.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator uses the monetary values you enter, so both the amount invested and ending value should use the same currency.

The basic ROI formula compares an ending value with an investment cost. It does not automatically identify separate taxes, transaction costs, management fees, dividends, interest payments, inflation, or intermediate cash flows because these are not separate calculator inputs.

Fidelity notes that costs and investment proceeds can materially affect an ROI calculation, and that taxes, inflation, fees, and holding periods may need additional consideration when evaluating real investment performance.

A zero investment amount cannot produce a standard ROI percentage because the formula would require division by zero.

ROI is a mathematical performance measure, not a prediction of future returns. Real investment results can also depend on risk, timing, costs, taxes, and cash flows that are outside this calculator's inputs.

Rounding may cause displayed results to differ slightly from calculations performed with more decimal places.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does ROI mean?

ROI stands for return on investment. It compares the gain or loss from an investment with the original amount invested and normally expresses the result as a percentage.

How is ROI calculated?

The calculator subtracts the investment cost from the ending value, divides the difference by the investment cost, and multiplies the result by 100.

ROI = ((Ending Value − Investment Cost) ÷ Investment Cost) × 100
What does a negative ROI mean?

A negative ROI means the ending value is lower than the original investment amount. For example, investing $1,000 and ending with $900 produces a loss relative to the initial investment.

Does holding period change the basic ROI?

No. The basic ROI formula compares the ending value with the investment cost regardless of how long the investment was held. The holding period is used separately for the calculator's annualized ROI result.

Why should the same currency be used for both amounts?

ROI depends on directly comparing the ending value with the investment cost. Using different currencies without first converting them to a consistent unit would make that comparison unreliable.

Does this calculator include investment fees or taxes?

There are no separate fee or tax inputs. The calculation is based on the amount invested and ending value or proceeds that you enter. Real-world returns may differ when fees, taxes, income payments, or other cash flows apply.

Can ROI be used to compare two investments?

ROI can provide a useful comparison, but total ROI alone does not fully account for differences in holding period, risk, taxes, fees, or other investment characteristics. Annualized results can provide additional time-based context when holding periods differ.
