The Pregnancy Conception Calculator estimates a likely conception date from an estimated due date or the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP). If you use the LMP method, your average menstrual cycle length is included in the calculation.

What Is the Pregnancy Conception Calculator?

This calculator works backward from pregnancy dating information to estimate when conception may have occurred. You can calculate from an estimated due date or use your last menstrual period and average cycle length.

The result is a calendar estimate, not a way to determine the exact day fertilization occurred. ACOG notes that standard LMP dating assumes a 28-day cycle with ovulation around day 14, while real ovulation timing can vary.

How to Use the Pregnancy Conception Calculator

Use the inputs that match the information you know:

  • Estimated due date: Enter the EDD if one has already been established.
  • First day of LMP: Enter the first day of your last menstrual period when calculating from your cycle.
  • Average cycle length: Enter your usual cycle length in days. This adjusts the estimate when your cycle is shorter or longer than 28 days.

After entering the required information, the calculator estimates the conception date and displays its supporting date results.

Formula / Calculation Method

From an Estimated Due Date

The calculator estimates conception by subtracting 266 days from the estimated due date:

Estimated Conception Date ≈ EDD − 266 days

This represents approximately 38 weeks before the due date. Standard obstetric dating uses about 280 days, or 40 weeks, from the first day of the LMP.

From the Last Menstrual Period

When the LMP and average cycle length are used:

Estimated Conception Date ≈ LMP + 14 days + (Cycle Length − 28 days)

A 28-day cycle therefore produces an estimate approximately 14 days after the first day of the LMP. Longer or shorter cycles shift the estimate by the difference from 28 days.

ACOG explains that ovulation typically occurs about 14 days before the next menstrual period, which is why cycle length affects a calendar-based ovulation estimate.

Variables

  • EDD = Estimated due date
  • LMP = First day of the last menstrual period
  • Cycle Length = Average number of days from the first day of one period to the first day of the next
  • 266 days = Approximate time from conception to the estimated due date used by this calculator
  • 28 days = Reference menstrual-cycle length used for the cycle adjustment

Calculation Sequence

  1. Choose whether the estimate is based on an EDD or LMP.
  2. If using EDD, subtract 266 days.
  3. If using LMP, begin with 14 days after the LMP.
  4. Add or subtract the difference between the entered cycle length and 28 days.
  5. Display the estimated conception date and supporting date information.

Approximate 5-Day Prior Window

The calculator also displays an approximate 5-day period before the estimated conception date. This range provides context because sperm may survive in the reproductive tract for several days before ovulation. ACOG states that sperm can survive about 3 days and sometimes up to 5 days.

The window should not be interpreted as proof that conception or intercourse occurred on a particular date.

Worked Example

Suppose the first day of the last menstrual period was September 1, 2026, and the average cycle length is 30 days.

Inputs

  • LMP: September 1, 2026
  • Average cycle length: 30 days

Step 1: Calculate the Cycle Adjustment

30 − 28 = 2 days

The cycle is 2 days longer than the 28-day reference cycle.

Step 2: Apply the Formula

Estimated conception ≈ September 1 + 14 days + 2 days

Total offset:

14 + 2 = 16 days

Step 3: Add 16 Days to the LMP

September 1, 2026 + 16 days = September 17, 2026

Final Result

Estimated conception date: September 17, 2026

This is a calendar estimate. Actual ovulation and fertilization may occur earlier or later.

Understanding Your Results

Estimated Conception Date

This is the calculator's best calendar estimate of when conception may have occurred based on the information entered. It is not a confirmed biological date.

Approximate 5-Day Prior Window

This supporting range shows days before the central estimate that may also be relevant because sperm can remain viable for several days. ACOG describes pregnancy as possible from intercourse occurring up to about 5 days before ovulation.

Corresponding Estimated Due Date

The calculator also provides the EDD associated with the calculated pregnancy timeline. An EDD is an estimate used for pregnancy dating rather than a guaranteed delivery date.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculation uses calendar-based pregnancy dating and cannot identify the exact biological moment of conception.

The LMP method assumes that ovulation timing is related to average cycle length. Ovulation may occur earlier or later because menstrual cycles and individual ovulation timing vary.

The estimate can also be less reliable when menstrual cycles are irregular or the LMP date is uncertain. NHS guidance notes that pregnancy dating scans can provide a more accurate estimate of gestational age than a calendar calculation alone.

For clinical pregnancy dating, ACOG states that first-trimester ultrasound is the most accurate method for establishing or confirming gestational age. Pregnancies resulting from assisted reproductive technology should be dated using ART-specific information rather than a general calendar estimate.

This calculator provides an estimate and is not a substitute for prenatal care or professional medical assessment.

Current Rules / Data

As of 2026, ACOG's Methods for Estimating the Due Date guidance is listed as reaffirmed in 2025. It continues to use the conventional 280-day pregnancy dating framework from the first day of the LMP and recommends first-trimester ultrasound as the most accurate method for confirming gestational age.

No current government rate, yearly threshold, or mandatory numerical value needs to be entered into this calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is conception estimated from a due date?

The calculator subtracts 266 days from the estimated due date:

Conception ≈ EDD − 266 days

This represents the approximate 38-week interval between conception and the estimated due date.

How does cycle length affect the conception estimate?

The LMP calculation adjusts for the difference between your average cycle and a 28-day reference cycle:

LMP + 14 days + (Cycle Length − 28 days)

A longer cycle moves the estimate later, while a shorter cycle moves it earlier.

Is the estimated conception date exact?

No. Ovulation, fertilization, and implantation timing vary, so a calendar calculator cannot establish the exact biological date of conception.

Why does the calculator show a 5-day prior window?

Sperm may remain viable for several days before ovulation. ACOG states that sperm can sometimes survive for up to 5 days, so intercourse before the estimated ovulation date may still result in pregnancy.

What if my menstrual cycles are irregular?

The estimate may be less reliable because the calculation depends partly on average cycle length. Clinical pregnancy dating, particularly early ultrasound, may provide better information when cycle dates are uncertain or irregular.

Is conception date the same as the start of gestational age?

No. Obstetric gestational age is conventionally counted from the first day of the LMP, which is usually about two weeks before estimated conception in a 28-day cycle.

Can this calculator replace an ultrasound dating estimate?

No. ACOG identifies first-trimester ultrasound as the most accurate method for establishing or confirming gestational age. The calculator should be treated as a calendar estimate.
