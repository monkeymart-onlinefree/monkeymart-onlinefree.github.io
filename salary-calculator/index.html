The Salary Calculator estimates your 2026 U.S. take-home pay from annual salary, filing status, pre-tax deductions, federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, and a state/local tax percentage you enter. It also uses your number of pay periods to show how your annual salary relates to each paycheck.

What Is the Salary Calculator?

The Salary Calculator is a planning tool for estimating how much of an annual salary may remain after major federal payroll and income taxes and entered deductions.

It uses 2026 federal tax rules, including the applicable standard deduction and progressive federal income tax brackets. It also estimates employee Social Security and Medicare taxes and includes a user-entered state/local tax estimate.

The calculator is useful for comparing job offers, reviewing a salary change, estimating annual take-home income, or understanding how taxes and deductions affect gross pay.

How to Use the Salary Calculator

Input What to Enter
Annual salary ($) Your gross annual salary before taxes and deductions.
Filing status Select the filing status available in the calculator. This determines the 2026 standard deduction and federal tax bracket schedule used in the estimate.
Pre-tax deductions ($/year) Enter the annual amount of deductions that the calculator subtracts before estimating federal taxable income.
State/local tax estimate (%) Enter a percentage to represent your estimated state and local income taxes. The calculator does not automatically determine a jurisdiction-specific tax rate.
Pay periods per year Enter the number of pay periods used to divide your annual salary into paycheck amounts.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator first estimates federal taxable income:

Federal Taxable Income = max(0, Annual Salary − Pre-Tax Deductions − 2026 Standard Deduction)

Federal income tax is then calculated progressively using the 2026 federal income tax brackets for the selected filing status.

In general:

Federal Income Tax = Sum of the taxable amount within each bracket × that bracket's tax rate

Only the income falling within a bracket is taxed at that bracket's rate. Moving into a higher bracket does not cause all taxable income to be taxed at the higher rate.

Calculation Sequence

Step 1: Estimate federal taxable income

The calculator subtracts the entered pre-tax deductions and applicable 2026 standard deduction from annual salary.

TI = max(0, S − D − SD)

Step 2: Calculate federal income tax

The taxable income is divided across the applicable 2026 tax brackets. Each portion is multiplied by its marginal tax rate and the bracket amounts are added together.

The 2026 individual federal rates are 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37%, with income thresholds depending on filing status.

Step 3: Calculate Social Security tax

For 2026, the employee Social Security rate is 6.2% and the wage base is $184,500:

SS = 0.062 × min(S, $184,500)

The maximum employee Social Security tax under this calculation is therefore $11,439 for 2026.

Step 4: Calculate Medicare tax

The regular employee Medicare rate is 1.45%, with no annual Medicare wage-base limit. The calculator also applies an additional 0.9% withholding above $200,000 of wages:

M = (0.0145 × S) + [0.009 × max(0, S − $200,000)]

The $200,000 figure is the employer Additional Medicare Tax withholding threshold. Actual Additional Medicare Tax liability on an individual tax return can depend on filing status.

Step 5: Include the state/local estimate

The calculator uses the state/local percentage entered by the user to produce a planning estimate. State and local tax systems can use different brackets, deductions, exemptions, taxable-income definitions, and local rules, so this input should not be treated as a complete state tax calculation.

Step 6: Estimate total taxes and take-home pay

The federal income tax, Social Security tax, Medicare tax, and state/local estimate are combined into the calculator's total estimated taxes. The resulting tax estimate and entered pre-tax deductions are used to estimate the portion of annual salary remaining as take-home pay.

Gross Per Paycheck

The gross paycheck amount is based on the annual salary and number of pay periods:

Gross Per Paycheck = Annual Salary ÷ Pay Periods Per Year

This is a gross amount before the taxes and deductions included in the calculator.

Worked Example

Assume an employee enters an $80,000 annual salary, uses Single filing status for this example, enters $0 in pre-tax deductions, uses a 0% state/local estimate, and enters 26 pay periods per year.

For tax year 2026, the IRS standard deduction for a single filer is $16,100.

Inputs

Input Example Value
Annual salary $80,000
Filing status used for example Single
Pre-tax deductions $0
State/local tax estimate 0%
Pay periods 26

Step 1: Federal Taxable Income

Taxable Income = $80,000 − $0 − $16,100
Taxable Income = $63,900

Step 2: Federal Income Tax

For a single filer in 2026, the first $12,400 of taxable income is taxed at 10%, income from $12,400 to $50,400 is taxed at 12%, and the remaining portion of this example falls in the 22% bracket.

First bracket:

$12,400 × 10% = $1,240

Second bracket:

($50,400 − $12,400) × 12% = $4,560

Remaining taxable income:

($63,900 − $50,400) × 22% = $2,970

Estimated federal income tax:

$1,240 + $4,560 + $2,970 = $8,770

Step 3: Social Security and Medicare

Social Security:

$80,000 × 6.2% = $4,960

Regular Medicare:

$80,000 × 1.45% = $1,160

The salary is below the calculator's $200,000 Additional Medicare Tax withholding threshold, so no additional 0.9% amount applies.

Final Result

With a 0% state/local estimate in this example:

Total Estimated Taxes = $8,770 + $4,960 + $1,160 = $14,890

With no entered pre-tax deductions:

Estimated Take-Home Pay = $80,000 − $14,890 = $65,110 per year

Gross pay per paycheck:

$80,000 ÷ 26 = $3,076.92

These figures are planning estimates. Actual payroll withholding can differ because of Form W-4 information, benefits, credits, payroll procedures, other income, and state or local rules.

Assumptions and Limitations

This Salary Calculator is an estimation tool rather than a payroll withholding system or tax-return calculator. It uses the entered annual salary, pre-tax deductions, filing status, state/local percentage, and pay-period count with the calculator's 2026 federal calculation method.

It does not model every tax credit, itemized deduction, special deduction, employee benefit, employer payroll adjustment, Form W-4 entry, state-specific tax bracket, local tax rule, or other payroll adjustment.

The federal taxable-income calculation uses the 2026 standard deduction rather than comparing the standard deduction with a user's itemized deductions.

The state/local input is intentionally simplified. Actual state and local income taxes vary significantly by jurisdiction and may not be accurately represented by a single percentage.

The calculator's additional 0.9% Medicare calculation uses the $200,000 employer withholding threshold. The amount ultimately owed for Additional Medicare Tax can differ because tax-return thresholds depend on filing status.

Results should therefore be used for budgeting and comparison rather than as tax, legal, payroll, or financial advice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this Salary Calculator use 2026 tax brackets?

Yes. The calculator estimates federal income tax using the 2026 federal tax brackets and the standard deduction associated with the applicable filing status. The IRS states that the 2026 individual marginal rates are 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37%.

How are pre-tax deductions handled?

The calculator subtracts the entered annual pre-tax deduction amount when estimating federal taxable income. The calculation is:

Annual Salary − Pre-Tax Deductions − Standard Deduction

The Formula Map does not support treating every possible benefit or payroll deduction as identical, so actual payroll treatment may differ.

Why can my actual paycheck be different from the calculator result?

Real payroll withholding can depend on Form W-4 information, tax credits, benefits, payroll timing, other income, employer payroll settings, and state or local tax rules. The calculator uses a simplified annual planning method rather than duplicating a payroll system.

Is Social Security tax charged on my entire salary?

Only up to the annual Social Security wage base. For 2026, employees pay 6.2% on covered wages up to $184,500. Earnings above that limit are not subject to the employee Social Security portion for the year.

Does Medicare have the same wage limit as Social Security?

No. The regular 1.45% employee Medicare tax has no wage-base limit. The calculator also includes an additional 0.9% withholding calculation for wages above $200,000.

Does the state/local percentage calculate my exact state income tax?

No. It is a user-entered planning estimate. Actual state and local taxes may use progressive brackets, deductions, credits, exemptions, different taxable-income definitions, and local rules.

Is estimated take-home pay the same as a tax return result?

No. This calculator estimates salary-related taxes and take-home pay using its defined inputs. It does not calculate a complete federal or state tax return, refund, tax credits, itemized deductions, or every payroll adjustment.
