What Is an IRA Calculator?

An IRA calculator estimates how retirement savings may grow and compares the after-tax effect of Traditional IRA, Roth-style after-tax saving, and a simplified taxable account. It is a planning model, not an eligibility determination.

How to Use the IRA Calculator

Enter your current balance, planned annual contribution, expected return, ages, and tax rates. The result shows a projected Traditional balance before tax, an after-tax Traditional value, a Roth-style comparator, and a taxable-account estimate.

IRA Growth Formula

For end-of-year contributions, future value is the compounded current balance plus the future value of a contribution annuity. Traditional after-tax value is then estimated as future balance × (1 − retirement tax rate).

Tax treatment used in this comparison

To compare the same gross contribution budget, the Roth-style and taxable contributions are reduced by the current marginal tax rate. The taxable account assumes annual returns are reduced by that same tax rate, which is deliberately simplified.

Example

A 35-year-old with $30,000 saved, contributing $7,500 per year until age 65 at 7%, can compare how a current 22% marginal rate and a 15% retirement rate change the projected after-tax outcomes.

2026 IRA Contribution Limit

For 2026, the IRS states that total annual contributions to Traditional and Roth IRAs are generally limited to $7,500, or $8,600 for people age 50 or older, subject to compensation and other eligibility rules.

Limitations

This model does not determine deductibility, Roth MAGI eligibility, early-withdrawal taxes, state taxes, capital-gains rates, investment fees, required minimum distributions, or contribution timing during each year. Actual returns are not guaranteed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this calculator decide whether my IRA contribution is deductible?

No. Traditional IRA deductibility can depend on filing status, modified adjusted gross income, and workplace retirement-plan coverage. This page only models growth and a simplified tax comparison.

Is a Roth IRA always better when my future tax rate is higher?

A higher future tax rate can improve the relative appeal of paying tax earlier, but account rules, contribution eligibility, investment returns, timing, and personal tax circumstances also matter.

Are investment returns guaranteed?

No. The expected return is only an assumption used to create a projection. Actual investment results can be higher, lower, or negative.
