What Is an IRA Calculator?

An IRA Calculator is a retirement-planning tool that projects the future value of savings held in an Individual Retirement Account. It combines your current balance with recurring annual contributions and compound growth over the years remaining until retirement.

This calculator is useful for estimating long-term IRA growth and seeing how tax assumptions may affect simplified after-tax comparisons. Traditional and Roth IRAs have different tax treatment, and actual eligibility or deductions depend on IRS rules and your individual tax situation.

How to Use the IRA Calculator

Enter the following information:

Current balance
Enter the amount currently saved in the account.

Annual before-tax contribution
Enter the amount you plan to contribute each year. The growth formula assumes these contributions are added at the end of each year.

Expected annual return (%)
Enter the annual rate of return you want to use for the projection. This is an assumption, not a guaranteed investment return.

Current age
Enter your age at the beginning of the projection.

Retirement age
Enter the age at which you want the projection to end. The difference between retirement age and current age determines the number of projection years.

Current marginal tax rate (%)
Enter your estimated current marginal tax rate. This tax assumption is used in the calculator's comparison of pre-tax and after-tax retirement saving approaches.

Expected tax rate in retirement (%)
Enter the tax rate you expect to apply in retirement. The calculator uses this rate when estimating the after-tax value of the Traditional IRA balance.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main IRA Growth Formula

For end-of-year annual contributions, the projected future value is:

FV=B(1+r)n+C((1+r)n−1r)FV = B(1+r)^n + C\left(\frac{(1+r)^n-1}{r}\right)

Variables

  • FV = projected IRA value at retirement, in dollars
  • B = current account balance, in dollars
  • C = annual before-tax contribution, in dollars
  • r = expected annual return as a decimal
  • n = number of years until retirement

The number of years is:

n=Retirement Age−Current Agen = Retirement\ Age - Current\ Age

The percentage return is converted to decimal form:

r=Annual Return100r = \frac{Annual\ Return}{100}

The first part of the formula compounds the current balance:

B(1+r)nB(1+r)^n

The second part calculates the future value of equal contributions made at the end of each year:

C((1+r)n−1r)C\left(\frac{(1+r)^n-1}{r}\right)

This is consistent with standard compound-growth modeling in which an initial balance and recurring contributions grow over time.

Traditional IRA After-Tax Value

The calculator estimates the Traditional IRA's value after retirement tax using:

AfterTaxValue=FV(1−t)AfterTaxValue = FV(1-t)

where:

  • FV = projected Traditional IRA balance
  • t = expected retirement tax rate as a decimal

For example, a 22% retirement tax rate becomes:

t=0.22t = 0.22

The calculation then retains 78% of the projected balance:

AfterTaxValue=FV×0.78AfterTaxValue = FV \times 0.78

This is a simplified modeling assumption. Traditional IRA taxation in real life depends on the tax treatment of withdrawals and other circumstances. Traditional IRA earnings are generally tax-deferred until withdrawal.

When the Expected Return Is 0%

If the expected annual return is exactly 0%, the annuity formula would otherwise divide by zero. The equivalent calculation becomes:

FV=B+CnFV = B + Cn

because no investment growth is applied.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Calculate the number of years until retirement.
  2. Convert the annual return percentage to decimal form.
  3. Compound the current IRA balance for the projection period.
  4. Calculate the future value of end-of-year annual contributions.
  5. Add the two amounts to obtain the projected IRA balance.
  6. Convert the retirement tax rate to decimal form.
  7. Apply the retirement tax rate to estimate the Traditional IRA's simplified after-tax value.
  8. Use the entered tax assumptions for the calculator's supporting Traditional, Roth-style, and taxable-savings comparisons.

Understanding Your Results

Projected IRA Balance

This is the estimated account value at your selected retirement age before applying the calculator's retirement-tax adjustment.

Increasing the contribution, return assumption, starting balance, or projection period generally increases the projected balance.

Traditional IRA After-Tax Value

This is the projected balance multiplied by one minus your expected retirement tax rate.

It is intended to make the projected Traditional IRA balance easier to compare with savings modeled on an after-tax basis.

Roth-Style Comparison

Roth IRAs generally use after-tax contributions, while qualified Roth withdrawals are generally tax-free. The calculator uses the entered tax assumptions to provide a simplified Roth-style comparison rather than determining whether you are personally eligible to contribute to a Roth IRA.

Taxable Savings Comparison

The taxable-savings result is intended as a comparison with retirement-account saving. Actual taxation of investments can depend on dividends, interest, capital gains, holding periods, tax rates, and other factors that are outside this simplified IRA model.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator assumes the expected annual return remains constant throughout the projection.

Annual contributions are modeled at the end of each year. Contributions made monthly, at the beginning of a year, or at irregular times can produce different results.

The calculator does not determine whether your Traditional IRA contribution is deductible.

It does not calculate Roth IRA eligibility from modified adjusted gross income or filing status.

It does not model early-withdrawal taxes or penalties, state taxes, detailed capital-gains taxation, investment management fees, changing investment returns, required minimum distributions, or year-by-year changes in tax rates.

The expected return is an assumption only. Investment returns can be higher, lower, or negative.

The retirement tax calculation applies one assumed tax rate to the projected Traditional IRA value. Actual retirement withdrawals may be taxed differently depending on future income, tax law, withdrawal timing, account basis, and other factors.

Current IRA Rules and Limits for 2026

For tax year 2026, total annual contributions across a person's Traditional and Roth IRAs are generally limited to the smaller of:

  • $7,500, or
  • the person's taxable compensation for the year.

For individuals age 50 or older, the 2026 catch-up amount is 1,100,makingthecombinedannuallimit8,600, subject to the compensation limit.

These limits apply across Traditional and Roth IRAs combined; they are not separate $7,500 limits for each account.

2026 Traditional IRA Deduction Phase-Outs

A Traditional IRA contribution can still be subject to deduction restrictions depending on workplace retirement-plan coverage, filing status, and income.

For 2026, when the IRA contributor is covered by a workplace retirement plan, the deduction phase-out range is:

  • Single or head of household: 81,000–91,000
  • Married filing jointly, contributor covered by a workplace plan: 129,000–149,000
  • Married filing separately, contributor covered: 0–10,000

For a married IRA contributor who is not covered by a workplace plan but whose spouse is covered, the 2026 phase-out range is 242,000–252,000.

2026 Roth IRA Income Phase-Outs

For 2026, Roth IRA contribution phase-outs are:

  • Single or head of household: 153,000–168,000
  • Married filing jointly: 242,000–252,000
  • Married filing separately when the applicable spouse rules apply: 0–10,000

The calculator does not use filing status or modified AGI to determine Roth IRA eligibility, so these rules should be checked separately when choosing a realistic contribution amount.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this IRA Calculator determine whether my contribution is tax-deductible?

No. Traditional IRA deductibility may depend on your filing status, modified adjusted gross income, whether you or your spouse participates in a workplace retirement plan, and other IRS rules. The calculator focuses on growth and simplified tax comparisons.

Does the calculator automatically enforce the annual IRA contribution limit?

This calculator is a projection model rather than an IRA eligibility calculator. When entering contributions, compare your amount with the applicable IRS limit and your taxable compensation. For 2026, the general combined Traditional and Roth IRA limit is $7,500, or $8,600 for someone age 50 or older, subject to compensation limits.

Are contributions assumed to be made at the start or end of each year?

They are modeled at the end of each year. Contributing earlier would normally give the contribution more time to grow and could produce a different projected result.

What happens if I enter a 0% return?

The account receives no investment growth. The projected value becomes the current balance plus the total annual contributions made during the projection period.

Is a Roth IRA always better when my future tax rate is higher?

Not necessarily. Tax rates are only one factor. Contribution eligibility, deductibility, withdrawal rules, investment performance, time horizon, and individual tax circumstances can also affect the outcome.

Are the projected investment returns guaranteed?

No. The expected annual return is only an assumption used for the calculation. Actual investments can gain or lose value.

Why could my real IRA balance differ from the calculator?

Real results can differ because investment returns change over time, contributions may occur at different dates, fees can reduce returns, tax laws can change, withdrawals may occur, and actual tax treatment can be more complex than a single assumed rate.
