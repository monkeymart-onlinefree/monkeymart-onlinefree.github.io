Use the Circle Calculator to find a circle’s radius, diameter, circumference, and area from any one known measurement. Enter the known value, select the length unit label, and the calculator uses standard circle formulas to determine the remaining measurements.

What Is the Circle Calculator?

The Circle Calculator is a geometry tool for calculating the main measurements of a circle. You can start with the radius, diameter, circumference, or area and calculate the other values.

It is useful for geometry problems, construction measurements, design work, school assignments, and other situations where you know one circle measurement but need the others.

How to Use the Circle Calculator

Enter one known circle measurement and select the unit you are using.

  • Length Unit Label: Select or enter the unit associated with your measurements. The unit setting labels the results; it does not convert the entered value between different measurement systems.
  • Radius (r): Enter the distance from the center of the circle to its edge.
  • Diameter (d): Enter the distance across the circle through its center.
  • Circumference (C): Enter the distance around the circle.
  • Area (A): Enter the amount of two-dimensional space inside the circle.

Radius, diameter, and circumference use regular length units such as cm, m, or in. Area uses the corresponding square unit, such as cm², m², or in².

Formula / Calculation Method

The calculator first determines the circle's radius from the known measurement. It then uses that radius to calculate the diameter, circumference, and area.

Main Formulas

Diameter

d = 2r

Circumference

C = 2πr

or:

C = πd

Area

A = πr²

These are the standard geometric relationships between a circle's radius, diameter, circumference, and area.

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
r Radius Length unit
d Diameter Length unit
C Circumference Length unit
A Area Square length unit
π Pi, the circle constant No unit

π represents the constant relationship between a circle's circumference and diameter.

Calculation Sequence

The calculation depends on which measurement is known.

If radius is known:

r = entered radius

If diameter is known:

r = d ÷ 2

If circumference is known:

r = C ÷ (2π)

If area is known:

r = √(A ÷ π)

After finding the radius, the calculator determines the remaining measurements:

d = 2r
C = 2πr
A = πr²

Diameter Calculation

The diameter is twice the radius:

d = 2r

For example, a radius of 5 cm gives:

d = 2 × 5 = 10 cm

Circumference Calculation

Circumference is the distance around the circle:

C = 2πr

It can also be calculated directly from diameter:

C = πd

Area Calculation

The area inside a circle is:

A = πr²

Because area is two-dimensional, the result is expressed in square units.

Worked Example

Suppose a circle has a radius of 5 cm.

Inputs

Radius:

r = 5 cm

Length unit:

cm

Step 1: Calculate Diameter

d = 2r
d = 2 × 5
d = 10 cm

Step 2: Calculate Circumference

C = 2πr
C = 2 × π × 5
C = 10π
C ≈ 31.4159 cm

Step 3: Calculate Area

A = πr²
A = π × 5²
A = 25π
A ≈ 78.5398 cm²

Final Result

For a circle with a radius of 5 cm:

  • Radius: 5 cm
  • Diameter: 10 cm
  • Circumference: approximately 31.4159 cm
  • Area: approximately 78.5398 cm²

Understanding Your Results

Radius shows the distance from the center of the circle to its outer edge.

Diameter is the full distance across the circle through its center and is always twice the radius.

Circumference represents the total distance around the outside edge of the circle.

Area represents the amount of flat space enclosed by the circle. Area results use square units rather than regular length units.

Small differences may appear when results are rounded because calculations involving π generally produce non-terminating decimal values.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator uses formulas for an ideal circle in Euclidean geometry.

Measurements should use a consistent unit. If the radius is entered in centimeters, for example, diameter and circumference are also interpreted in centimeters, while area is expressed in square centimeters.

The unit control labels the results but does not automatically convert a value from one measurement system to another.

Negative radius, diameter, circumference, or area values do not represent ordinary circle measurements.

Displayed decimal results may be rounded, so a rounded value can differ slightly from a calculation that keeps more decimal places.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I calculate a circle from only one measurement?

Yes. A radius, diameter, circumference, or area is enough to determine the other main circle measurements because all four values are mathematically related.

How do I find the radius from the diameter?

Divide the diameter by 2:

r = d ÷ 2

A diameter of 20 cm therefore gives a radius of 10 cm.

How do I find the radius from circumference?

Divide the circumference by 2π:

r = C ÷ (2π)
How do I find the radius from area?

Use:

r = √(A ÷ π)

This reverses the area formula A = πr².

Why is area shown in square units?

Area measures two-dimensional space. If the radius is measured in meters, the resulting area is expressed in square meters (m²).

Does changing the unit automatically convert my measurement?

No. The unit option is used as a label for the calculation. Enter the known measurement in the same unit you want used for the resulting length values.

Why can my result differ slightly from a manually calculated answer?

Calculations involving π can contain many decimal places. Results can differ slightly depending on how many decimal places are retained or how the final value is rounded.
