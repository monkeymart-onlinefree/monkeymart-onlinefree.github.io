Use the Conversion Calculator to convert common units of length, mass, temperature, area, volume, speed, pressure, and energy. Select the measurement category, choose the From unit and To unit, and the calculator returns the equivalent converted result.

What Is the Conversion Calculator?

The Conversion Calculator changes a measurement from one compatible unit to another. It is useful when comparing metric, U.S. customary, imperial, scientific, or other supported measurement units without calculating the conversion manually.

The calculator uses category-aware conversion relationships. Temperature needs special formulas because Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Kelvin do not all share the same zero point.

How to Use the Conversion Calculator

  1. Measurement category: Select the type of measurement you want to convert, such as length, mass, temperature, area, volume, speed, pressure, or energy.
  2. From: Choose the unit the original measurement uses.
  3. To: Choose the unit you want the measurement converted into.

The calculator then applies the conversion relationship for the selected unit pair and displays the result.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

For compatible units that have a fixed ratio, the general conversion relationship can be written as:

y = x × f

Where the conversion factor f represents the relationship between the selected source and target units. NIST describes unit conversion as applying the appropriate numerical conversion factor, together with suitable significant digits and rounding.

Temperature is different because an offset must also be considered.

Temperature Conversion Formulas

Fahrenheit to Celsius

°C = (°F − 32) × 5/9

Celsius to Fahrenheit

°F = °C × 9/5 + 32

Celsius to Kelvin

K = °C + 273.15

Equivalent temperature relationships include:

K to Celsius

°C = K − 273.15

Fahrenheit to Kelvin

K = (°F − 32) × 5/9 + 273.15

Kelvin to Fahrenheit

°F = (K − 273.15) × 9/5 + 32

NIST confirms that 0 °C corresponds to 273.15 K, that a Celsius-degree interval equals a kelvin interval, and that Celsius/Fahrenheit conversions require both scaling and an offset.

Variables

  • x = measurement being converted
  • y = converted measurement
  • f = conversion factor from the source unit to the target unit
  • °C = temperature in degrees Celsius
  • °F = temperature in degrees Fahrenheit
  • K = temperature in kelvins

Calculation Sequence

  1. Identify the selected measurement category.
  2. Identify the From and To units.
  3. Apply the appropriate conversion relationship for that compatible unit pair.
  4. For temperature, apply both the scale factor and required offset instead of using only a simple ratio.
  5. Return the equivalent value in the selected target unit.

Worked Example

Suppose you want to convert 68 °F to degrees Celsius.

Inputs

  • Measurement category: Temperature
  • From: Fahrenheit
  • To: Celsius
  • Measurement used: 68 °F

Step 1

Use the Fahrenheit-to-Celsius formula:

°C = (°F − 32) × 5/9

Substitute 68:

°C = (68 − 32) × 5/9

Step 2

Subtract 32:

68 − 32 = 36

Step 3

Multiply by 5/9:

36 × 5/9 = 20

Final Result

68 °F = 20 °C

This follows the exact Fahrenheit-to-Celsius relationship published by NIST.

Understanding Your Results

The main result is the equivalent measurement expressed in the selected To unit. The physical quantity itself does not change; only the unit used to describe it changes.

For example, converting a distance from meters to feet changes the numerical representation, not the actual distance.

Conversion results should also be interpreted with appropriate precision. NIST recommends avoiding more significant digits than are justified by the original measurement.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator is intended for conversions between compatible units within the selected measurement category. A length unit, for example, should be converted to another length unit rather than to an unrelated quantity such as mass.

Temperature conversions require offset formulas and cannot be handled correctly using only multiplication or division.

A conversion also does not improve the accuracy of the original measurement. If the starting measurement is rounded or uncertain, the converted value carries that underlying limitation.

Displayed results can vary slightly between calculators when different rounding or significant-digit rules are used. The underlying standard conversion relationship should remain consistent when the same unit definitions are used.

Frequently Asked Questions

What units can the Conversion Calculator convert?

The calculator is designed for common units of length, mass, temperature, area, volume, speed, pressure, and energy.

Why is temperature conversion different from other conversions?

Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Kelvin have different scale relationships and zero points. Because of this, temperature conversions can require both multiplication and addition or subtraction rather than only a conversion factor.

Is 0 °C exactly 273.15 K?

Yes. The Celsius scale is related to the kelvin scale so that 0 °C = 273.15 K.

Why can two conversion calculators show slightly different answers?

The difference is often caused by rounding or the number of decimal places displayed. Some conversions may also be presented using approximate factors rather than all available precision.

Does converting a measurement make it more accurate?

No. Unit conversion changes how a measurement is expressed but does not improve the accuracy or precision of the original measurement.

Can I convert between unrelated measurement categories?

A meaningful unit conversion requires compatible physical quantities. Length should be converted to length, mass to mass, pressure to pressure, and so on.

How many decimal places should I use?

Use enough decimal places for your purpose without implying more precision than the original measurement supports. NIST recommends considering the significant digits of the source value when rounding a conversion.
