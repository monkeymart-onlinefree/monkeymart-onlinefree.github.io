What Is a Rental Property Calculator?

A rental property calculator estimates operating income, mortgage cash flow, cap rate, cash-on-cash return, and an investment IRR from a buy-hold-sell scenario. It separates property operations from financing so you can see what the asset earns before and after debt service.

Net Operating Income and Cap Rate

NOI = Collected rental income − Management − Operating expenses
Cap rate = First-year NOI ÷ Purchase price × 100

Mortgage principal and interest are not included in NOI. They are deducted afterward to estimate cash flow to the investor.

Cash-on-Cash Return

Cash-on-cash return = First-year cash flow ÷ Initial cash invested × 100

Initial cash invested is modeled as down payment plus entered closing and repair cash.

Rental Property IRR

The calculator creates one annual cash flow for each holding year. The final year adds estimated net sale proceeds after selling costs and remaining mortgage payoff. IRR is the discount rate that makes the net present value of those annual cash flows equal zero.

Limitations

Long-range real estate returns are highly sensitive to rent, vacancy, repairs, taxes, insurance, financing, appreciation, and sale assumptions. The model does not include income taxes, depreciation, capital gains tax, depreciation recapture, major capital expenditures, refinancing, or irregular cash-flow timing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is NOI in rental property analysis?

Net operating income is property income after vacancy and operating expenses, but before mortgage payments and income taxes. This calculator also subtracts the entered management fee from collected income.

What is cap rate?

Cap rate is first-year net operating income divided by purchase price in this calculator. It is a property-level measure and does not include the effect of mortgage financing.

Why can rental property IRR change a lot?

IRR depends on every projected cash flow, including rent growth, operating expenses, financing, holding period, appreciation, selling costs, and remaining loan balance. Small assumption changes can compound over many years.

Cap rate excludes debt service; cash-on-cash and IRR incorporate financing, so each metric answers a different question.
