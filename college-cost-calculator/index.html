The College Cost Calculator estimates how much college may cost over multiple years and compares that projected cost with grants, scholarships, current savings, investment growth, and ongoing family contributions. The main result is an estimated college funding gap based on the assumptions you enter.

What Is the College Cost Calculator?

The College Cost Calculator is a planning tool for estimating future college expenses instead of looking only at today's annual price.

It projects annual college costs using an assumed yearly cost increase, accounts for grants and scholarships, estimates the savings available, and shows the remaining amount that may need to be funded.

Federal Student Aid explains that a school's cost of attendance can include expenses such as tuition, fees, books, supplies, food, and housing, while the amount a student actually needs to cover can be reduced by grants and scholarships.

The calculator can be useful for students, parents, and families comparing expected college costs with the money they are preparing for education.

How to Use the College Cost Calculator

Input What to Enter
Current annual cost of attendance ($) Enter the current yearly college cost you want to use as the starting amount.
Years of attendance Enter the number of years of college costs you want to project.
Annual cost increase (%) Enter the percentage by which you expect college costs to increase each year.
Annual grants & scholarships ($) Enter the annual amount of grants and scholarships you expect to reduce college costs.
Current college savings ($) Enter the amount already saved for college.
Savings annual return (%) Enter the assumed annual return on the college savings balance.
Monthly family contribution to savings ($) Enter the amount the family plans to add to college savings each month.

Use estimates that reflect your own situation. Actual college prices, financial aid, scholarships, and investment returns may differ from the assumptions entered.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator projects the cost for each college year using:

Projected Year Cost = Current Annual Cost × (1 + Annual Cost Increase)^Year

In symbolic form:

Cᵧ = C₀ × (1 + g)ʸ

The projected costs for the years of attendance are then combined to determine the total projected sticker cost.

Total Projected Sticker Cost = Σ Cᵧ

The projected net cost is based on:

Projected Net Cost ≈ Total Projected Sticker Cost − Total Grants & Scholarships

The estimated funding gap is:

Estimated Funding Gap ≈ Projected Net Cost − Projected Savings

Calculation Sequence

The annual cost increase percentage is first converted to decimal form. The calculator then projects college cost for each attendance year using the annual cost-growth formula and combines those projected yearly costs into the total projected sticker cost.

Grants and scholarships are applied to determine the projected net cost. College savings are projected using the entered current savings, annual return, and monthly family contribution. Finally, projected savings are subtracted from projected net cost to estimate the funding gap.

Projected College Costs

For each projected year:

Cᵧ = C₀ × (1 + g)ʸ

A higher annual cost increase produces progressively higher projected annual costs.

Total Projected Sticker Cost

The projected yearly costs are combined:

Ctotal = Σ Cᵧ

This is the estimated total college cost before grants, scholarships, and savings are applied.

Projected Net Cost

Federal Student Aid describes net price as cost of attendance minus grants and scholarships.

For this calculator:

Projected Net Cost ≈ Total Projected Sticker Cost − Total Grants & Scholarships

Loans are not treated as grants or scholarships in this calculation because loans are borrowed money that generally must be repaid.

Projected Savings

Projected savings depend on three calculator inputs: current college savings, the assumed annual savings return, and monthly family contributions.

The available calculator specification does not define the exact monthly contribution timing or the precise compounding sequence used for this secondary calculation. Because those details can change a future-value result, an unsupported closed-form savings formula is not stated here.

Estimated Funding Gap

After the projected net cost and projected savings have been determined:

Funding Gap ≈ Projected Net Cost − Projected Savings

The result estimates how much of the modeled college cost is not covered by the savings and aid included in the calculation.

Worked Example

The following example uses zero cost growth, zero savings growth, no new monthly contributions, and no grants so that every calculation can be verified without making assumptions about unspecified timing or compounding rules.

Inputs

Current annual cost of attendance: $30,000

Years of attendance: 4 years

Annual cost increase: 0%

Annual grants and scholarships: $0

Current college savings: $20,000

Savings annual return: 0%

Monthly family contribution: $0

Step 1: Project the Annual Cost

Because the annual cost increase is 0%:

Projected Year Cost = $30,000 × (1 + 0)ʸ = $30,000

The projected cost remains $30,000 for each of the four years.

Step 2: Calculate Total Projected Cost

$30,000 × 4 = $120,000

With no grants or scholarships entered:

Projected Net Cost = $120,000 − $0 = $120,000

Step 3: Compare With Savings

With a 0% savings return and no additional monthly contributions, the available savings remain:

$20,000

The estimated funding gap is therefore:

$120,000 − $20,000 = $100,000

Final Result

For these assumptions, the total projected sticker cost is 120,000,projectednetcostis120,000, projected savings available are 20,000,andtheestimatedfundinggapis100,000.

Understanding Your Results

Estimated funding gap shows the portion of projected net college costs that is not covered by the savings included in the calculation. It is a planning estimate, not a loan recommendation or guaranteed amount you will need to borrow.

Total projected sticker cost is the estimated combined college cost across the selected attendance period before grants, scholarships, or savings reduce the amount.

Total grants & scholarships represents the grant and scholarship assistance included in the calculator's projection. Federal Student Aid distinguishes this type of aid from student loans because grants and scholarships generally do not require repayment, while loans are borrowed funds.

Projected net cost estimates the cost remaining after applicable grants and scholarships are deducted from projected sticker cost.

Projected savings available estimates the college savings available based on current savings, the assumed annual return, and monthly family contributions entered in the calculator.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator is a planning estimate. Actual college costs can be different because schools may include different expenses in their cost of attendance, and actual expenses such as transportation, books, supplies, housing, and personal costs can vary. Federal Student Aid also notes that each school can have a different cost of attendance.

The annual cost increase is an assumption rather than a guaranteed future inflation rate. Actual tuition and other college expenses may rise faster or slower.

Grants and scholarships may change from one academic year to another. A school's financial aid offer is the more appropriate source for the exact amounts and types of aid offered to a student.

The savings annual return is also an assumption. Investment returns are not guaranteed, so actual savings may be higher or lower than the projection.

The calculation does not treat student loans as grants or scholarships. Borrowing can help cover a funding gap, but loans create repayment obligations and may include interest and fees.

No automatic current tuition inflation rate, investment return, scholarship amount, or student-loan rate is imposed by this calculation. These values depend on the assumptions or amounts entered by the user.

The precise monthly contribution timing and compounding sequence for the projected-savings calculation are not specified in the available methodology, so those details should not be inferred from the simplified formulas above.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the College Cost Calculator calculate?

It projects college costs across the selected number of years, accounts for grants and scholarships, estimates available savings, and calculates the potential difference between projected net cost and projected savings.

What should I enter as the current annual cost of attendance?

Use the annual college cost you want as the starting point. Depending on the school, cost of attendance can include tuition, fees, housing, food, books, supplies, and other education-related expenses.

What is the difference between sticker cost and net cost?

Sticker cost is the projected college cost before aid is deducted. Net cost is the amount remaining after grants and scholarships are subtracted. Federal Student Aid uses the same basic distinction when explaining college net price.

Are student loans subtracted from college cost?

Not as grants or scholarships in this calculator. Loans are borrowed funds that generally must be repaid, so they are different from grant and scholarship aid.

Does the calculator predict the exact amount of financial aid I will receive?

No. The annual grants and scholarships amount is an input used for planning. Actual aid is determined by the school and may change between years. Federal Student Aid states that a school's financial aid offer contains the actual amounts and types of aid being offered.

Is the savings return guaranteed?

No. The savings annual return is an assumption used for projection. Actual investment performance can differ from the rate entered.

Why can my actual college funding gap be different?

Actual costs, financial aid, scholarships, family contributions, investment returns, attendance length, and other expenses can all differ from the assumptions entered. The calculator is designed for planning rather than guaranteeing a future college bill.
