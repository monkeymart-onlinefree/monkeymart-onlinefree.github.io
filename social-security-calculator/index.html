What Is a Social Security Calculator?

This Social Security calculator estimates how claiming age can change a U.S. retirement benefit relative to the monthly benefit available at full retirement age. It applies SSA early-retirement reductions and delayed-retirement credits, then compares modeled present values from claim ages 62 through 70.

How to Use the Social Security Calculator

Enter your birth year, your estimated monthly benefit at full retirement age from your Social Security Statement, the age you are considering claiming, life expectancy, an assumed COLA, and a discount or investment rate.

Full Retirement Age and Claiming Adjustments

For people born in 1960 or later, SSA sets full retirement age at 67. For birth years 1955 through 1959, FRA rises by two months per birth year from age 66 and 2 months to 66 and 10 months.

Early reduction = 5/9 of 1% per month for the first 36 early months + 5/12 of 1% per additional early month

For people born in 1943 or later, delayed retirement credits are 8% per year, applied by month, until age 70.

Understanding the Present-Value Comparison

Claiming later usually produces a larger monthly payment but fewer months of payments. The present-value table combines the modeled payment stream with your life expectancy, COLA, and discount rate so you can compare that tradeoff consistently.

Limitations

This page does not model spousal or survivor benefits, disability, earnings-test withholding, taxation of benefits, Medicare premiums, future law changes, exact SSA rounding, or your actual earnings record. For an official personalized estimate, use a my Social Security account.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is full retirement age for someone born in 1960 or later?

SSA states that full retirement age is 67 for people born in 1960 or later. Earlier birth years can have a full retirement age between 66 and 67.

How are early retirement reductions calculated?

SSA applies a reduction of 5/9 of 1% for each of the first 36 months before full retirement age, plus 5/12 of 1% for each additional early month.

Do benefits keep increasing after age 70 if I wait to claim?

No. SSA states that delayed retirement credits stop increasing retirement benefits after age 70.
