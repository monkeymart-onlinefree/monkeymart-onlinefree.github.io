What Is an Engine Horsepower Calculator?

This calculator estimates a vehicle engine's horsepower from a quarter-mile run. The elapsed-time method uses race weight and total time; the trap-speed method uses race weight and speed at the 1/4-mile finish. Both are performance-based estimates, not direct dynamometer measurements.

Engine Horsepower Formulas

ET method: HP = Weight(lb) / (ET / 5.825)³
Trap-speed method: HP = Weight(lb) × (Speed(mph) / 234)³

Weight should include the vehicle, driver, passengers, fuel, and meaningful cargo. Metric inputs are converted internally before the empirical formulas are applied.

Calculation Example

A 3,500 lb vehicle running a 13.2-second quarter mile produces an estimate from the ET formula. A separate estimate can be produced if its finish-line trap speed is known. Differences are normal because traction, aerodynamics, gearing, weather, and measurement error affect a real run.

Understanding the Result

The result is shown in mechanical horsepower and kilowatts. It is best used as an approximate performance comparison under similar test conditions rather than as a certified engine rating.

Limitations and Safety

These empirical formulas do not isolate drivetrain losses, aerodynamic drag, tire slip, wind, road grade, launch quality, or atmospheric correction. Never perform high-speed testing on public roads.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is quarter-mile horsepower the same as dyno horsepower?

No. Quarter-mile formulas estimate engine power from vehicle weight and performance. A dynamometer directly measures power under controlled test conditions, so the numbers can differ.

What weight should I enter?

Use the vehicle's race weight: vehicle, driver, passengers, fuel, and meaningful cargo. The source formula assumes total moving weight.

Should trap speed be average speed?

No. Use the speed recorded at the quarter-mile finish, commonly called trap speed, rather than the average speed over the run.

Can I use this on public roads?

No. Performance testing should be done only in legal, controlled facilities such as a drag strip. Do not time high-speed runs on public streets.
