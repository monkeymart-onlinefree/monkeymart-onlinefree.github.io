Use the Payment Calculator to estimate the monthly payment required to repay a fixed-rate loan over a chosen term. You can also use a fixed monthly payment to estimate how long repayment may take based on the loan balance and annual interest re.

What Is the Payment Calculator?

The Payment Calculator helps evaluate a loan with a fixed interest rate and regular monthly payments. It is useful when comparing loan terms, planning a monthly payment, or checking how a chosen payment amount affects repayment time.

The calculation uses the standard amortization relationship between principal, interest rate, payment amount, and number of payment periods. Fixed-rate amortizing loans are structured so regular principal-and-interest payments reduce the balance to zero by the end of the loan term.

How to Use the Payment Calculator

Loan Amount ($)

Enter the amount being financed or the current principal balance of the loan. Use a positive dollar amount.

Annual Interest (%)

Enter the annual interest rate as a percentage. The calculation converts this annual rate into a monthly rate by dividing the decimal rate by 12.

For example:

6% annual interest = 0.06 ÷ 12 = 0.005 per month.

Using matching units for the interest rate and payment periods is required for standard loan calculations.

Loan Term (Years)

Enter the number of years over which the loan will be repaid when calculating the required monthly payment.

The calculator converts years into monthly periods:

Number of payments = Years × 12

Monthly Payment ($)

Enter the amount you plan to pay each month when estimating how long repayment will take.

For a positive interest rate, the monthly payment must be high enough to cover the periodic interest and reduce principal. Otherwise, the loan balance cannot be fully amortized under the fixed-payment model.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

For a fixed loan term, the monthly payment is calculated as:

A = P × [r(1 + r)ⁿ] ÷ [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1]

This is the standard fixed-payment amortization formula used for loans with a constant rate and regular payments.

Variables

Variable Meaning Unit
A Required monthly payment Dollars per month
P Loan principal or amount borrowed Dollars
r Monthly interest rate Decimal per month
n Total number of monthly payments Months

The calculator converts the entered annual interest rate using:

r = Annual Interest Rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12

And converts the loan term using:

n = Loan Term in Years × 12

Calculation Sequence

  1. Convert the annual percentage rate entered by the user into decimal form.
  2. Divide the annual decimal rate by 12 to obtain the monthly rate.
  3. Multiply the loan term in years by 12 to determine the number of monthly payment periods.
  4. Apply the amortization formula to calculate the required monthly payment.
  5. Use the unrounded intermediate values before presenting the result.

Fixed Monthly Payment Payoff Calculation

When a monthly payment is provided instead of a fixed repayment term, the amortization relationship can be solved for the number of payment periods:

n = ln[A ÷ (A − P × r)] ÷ ln(1 + r)

Where:

  • n = estimated number of monthly payment periods
  • A = fixed monthly payment
  • P = loan amount
  • r = monthly interest rate

This calculation requires:

A > P × r

That means the payment must exceed the periodic interest charge for the balance to decline. Standard financial functions such as NPER use the same relationship to determine the number of periods for constant payments and a constant interest rate.

Worked Example

Suppose you have the following fixed-rate loan.

Inputs

  • Loan amount: $20,000
  • Annual interest rate: 6%
  • Loan term: 5 years

Step 1: Calculate the Monthly Interest Rate

r = 0.06 ÷ 12
r = 0.005

The monthly interest rate is 0.5%, or 0.005 as a decimal.

Step 2: Calculate the Number of Payments

n = 5 × 12
n = 60 months

Step 3: Apply the Payment Formula

A = 20,000 × [0.005(1.005)⁶⁰] ÷ [(1.005)⁶⁰ − 1]

Using full precision during the calculation:

A ≈ 386.6560

Final Result

The estimated required monthly payment is approximately:

$386.66 per month

This result represents the regular payment needed to amortize a $20,000 balance over 60 months at a fixed 6% annual interest rate.

Understanding Your Results

Required Monthly Payment

The required monthly payment is the estimated amount that must be paid each month to reduce the entered loan balance to zero over the selected term under the calculator's fixed-rate assumptions.

Each payment effectively covers interest accrued on the outstanding balance and reduces part of the principal. As the principal declines, the interest portion generally decreases and more of the regular payment goes toward principal.

Estimated Payoff Time

When using a fixed monthly payment, the payoff calculation estimates how many payment periods are required for the loan balance to reach zero.

A larger monthly payment generally shortens the calculated repayment period, while a smaller payment extends it, provided the payment remains greater than the periodic interest charge.

The estimate should not be treated as an official lender payoff quote. Actual payoff amounts can include accrued interest, fees, penalties, or other charges that are not represented by a basic amortization model.

Assumptions and Limitations

  • The interest rate is assumed to remain fixed.
  • Payments are assumed to be made regularly each month.
  • The calculation does not model variable interest rates.
  • Late fees are not included.
  • Payment holidays or skipped-payment arrangements are not included.
  • Lender-specific rounding methods can produce small differences from the calculator result.
  • A fixed-payment payoff calculation requires a payment large enough to reduce the principal after periodic interest is charged.
  • Actual loan payments may include additional costs or fees that are outside the standard principal-and-interest calculation.

The results are estimates for planning and comparison. Your lender or loan servicer should be used for contractual payment amounts and official payoff figures.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the monthly loan payment calculated?

The calculator uses the standard amortization formula:

A = P × [r(1+r)ⁿ] ÷ [(1+r)ⁿ − 1]

The result depends on the loan principal, monthly interest rate, and total number of monthly payments.

Why is the annual interest rate divided by 12?

The calculator models monthly payments, so the annual interest rate must be converted to a monthly rate. For example, a 6% annual rate becomes 0.5% per month for this calculation.

Can I calculate how long a loan will take to repay?

Yes. When a fixed monthly payment is used, the amortization relationship can be solved for the number of payment periods required to repay the balance.

What happens if my monthly payment is too low?

For a positive interest rate, the payment must exceed the interest charged for the period if the balance is to decline. If the payment is not greater than P × r, the standard fixed-payment payoff formula cannot produce a normal repayment period.

Does the result include fees and other loan costs?

The standard payment calculation is based on principal, interest, and the repayment period. Late fees, payment holidays, variable-rate changes, and other lender-specific charges are not included in this model.

Why can my lender's payment differ from the calculator?

A lender may use specific rounding procedures or include additional charges. Mortgage payments, for example, can include insurance, property taxes, mortgage insurance, or other amounts in addition to principal and interest.

Is the calculator's payoff amount the same as an official payoff quote?

No. An official payoff amount may include interest through a specific payoff date, unpaid fees, or applicable prepayment charges. Contact the lender or servicer when you need the exact amount required to close a loan.
