Use the Future Value Calculator to estimate how a starting amount and recurring deposits can grow over a selected number of periods. Enter the starting value, periodic interest rate, deposit amount, number of periods, and whether deposits are made at the beginning or end of each period.

What Is the Future Value Calculator?

A future value calculator estimates how much money may be worth after compound growth over multiple periods.

This calculator can combine an initial amount with equal periodic deposits. It also accounts for deposit timing, which matters because a deposit made at the beginning of a period has one additional period to earn growth compared with an end-of-period deposit.

Future value calculations are useful for savings projections, investment illustrations, financial planning examples, and other time-value-of-money calculations. Compound growth means previously earned interest can itself earn interest in later periods.

How to Use the Future Value Calculator

Enter the following values:

Starting amount (PV): Enter the amount available at the beginning of the calculation. This is the present value before future compound growth and recurring deposits.

Number of periods: Enter how many growth periods the calculation should cover. The period must be consistent with the interest-rate period and deposit period.

Interest rate per period (%): Enter the percentage growth rate for each period. The calculator converts this percentage to a decimal before applying the formula. For example, 6% becomes 0.06.

This field is a rate per period, not automatically an annual interest rate. If each period represents one month, the entered rate should also represent the rate for one month.

Periodic deposit (PMT): Enter the amount added during each period.

Deposit timing: Select whether the recurring deposit is made at the beginning or end of each period. Comparable future-value calculators use these same core inputs—period count, starting amount, periodic rate, recurring deposit, and deposit timing.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

For a non-zero periodic interest rate, future value is calculated as:

FV = PV(1 + r)ⁿ + PMT × [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1] / r × (1 + r)ᵈ

The formula combines two components:

Future value of the starting amount:

PV(1 + r)ⁿ

and the future value of the recurring deposits:

PMT × [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1] / r × (1 + r)ᵈ

Variables

Variable Meaning Unit
FV Future value at the end of the calculation Money
PV Starting amount or present value Money
r Interest rate per period as a decimal Decimal per period
n Number of periods Periods
PMT Recurring deposit made each period Money per period
d Deposit-timing adjustment 0 or 1

For deposit timing:

  • d = 0 for deposits made at the end of each period.
  • d = 1 for deposits made at the beginning of each period.

The percentage entered by the user is converted to decimal form:

r = Interest rate (%) ÷ 100

Zero-Interest Formula

When the periodic interest rate is 0%, the division by r in the main formula cannot be used. The calculation simplifies to:

FV = PV + n × PMT

This simply adds the starting amount and all periodic deposits because no compound growth occurs.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Convert the entered interest rate from a percentage to a decimal.
  2. Calculate the compounded value of the starting amount.
  3. Calculate the accumulated value of all recurring deposits.
  4. Apply the deposit-timing adjustment.
  5. Add the two components to obtain the future value.
  6. Calculate supporting results such as deposited principal and interest earned.
  7. Build the period-by-period schedule using the selected deposit timing.

Total Periodic Deposits

If the same deposit is made every period:

Total Periodic Deposits = PMT × n

Total Amount Contributed

Including the starting amount:

Total Contributions = PV + (PMT × n)

Interest Earned

The growth above the money contributed can be calculated as:

Interest Earned = FV − PV − (PMT × n)

This represents mathematical compound growth under the entered assumptions. It does not represent a guaranteed investment return.

Period-by-Period Schedule

For an end-of-period deposit, each period can be represented as:

Interest = Starting Balance × r
Ending Balance = Starting Balance + Interest + PMT

The ending balance becomes the next period's starting balance.

For a beginning-of-period deposit, the deposit is added before interest is calculated:

Balance After Deposit = Starting Balance + PMT
Interest = Balance After Deposit × r
Ending Balance = Balance After Deposit + Interest

The difference in timing explains why beginning-of-period deposits generally produce a higher future value when the interest rate is positive.

Worked Example

Suppose you enter:

Inputs

  • Starting amount (PV): $1,000
  • Number of periods: 10
  • Interest rate per period: 6%
  • Periodic deposit (PMT): $100
  • Deposit timing: End of period

Step 1: Convert the Rate

r = 6 ÷ 100 = 0.06

Because deposits are made at the end of each period:

d = 0

Step 2: Calculate Growth of the Starting Amount

$1,000 × (1.06)¹⁰
= $1,790.8476965

Step 3: Calculate the Future Value of Periodic Deposits

$100 × [(1.06)¹⁰ − 1] ÷ 0.06
= $1,318.0794942

Final Result

FV = $1,790.8476965 + $1,318.0794942
FV = $3,108.9271908

Rounded to cents:

Future Value = $3,108.93

Total periodic deposits are:

$100 × 10 = $1,000

Total money contributed is:

$1,000 + $1,000 = $2,000

Estimated interest earned is therefore:

$3,108.93 − $2,000 = $1,108.93

This result is consistent with the standard future-value treatment of an initial balance plus end-of-period recurring deposits.

Understanding Your Results

Future Value

Future value is the estimated ending balance after applying the entered periodic growth rate and recurring deposits for the selected number of periods.

A larger future value can result from a higher starting balance, larger deposits, more periods, a higher positive interest rate, or making deposits earlier.

Total Periodic Deposits

This is the amount added through recurring deposits before considering growth:

PMT × number of periods

It helps separate money you contributed from compound growth.

Interest Earned

Interest earned is the portion of the ending value above the starting amount and periodic deposits.

It is based entirely on the interest rate you entered. It should not be interpreted as a guaranteed return.

Period Schedule

The schedule shows how the balance changes from one period to the next. Depending on deposit timing, each row can show the starting balance, deposit, interest for the period, and ending balance.

This makes it easier to see how compound growth builds over time. Investor.gov similarly illustrates compound-growth projections using an initial investment, recurring contributions, a time horizon, an interest assumption, and compounding.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator assumes the entered periodic interest rate remains constant throughout the calculation.

It assumes the recurring deposit remains the same for every period and is consistently made at the selected beginning or end of the period.

The number of periods, deposit frequency, and interest-rate period should represent the same time interval. For example, a monthly deposit schedule requires a rate that represents growth per month.

The calculation does not automatically model changing market returns, investment losses, taxes, account fees, inflation, irregular deposits, or changes in the interest rate.

Future value is a mathematical projection rather than a prediction or guarantee. Actual investment results can vary significantly from a fixed-rate illustration.

Small differences between calculators can also occur because of different payment-timing assumptions, rate conventions, or rounding methods.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does future value mean?

Future value is the amount a current sum of money and future deposits are calculated to reach after applying an assumed growth rate for a specified number of periods.

Does this calculator use an annual interest rate?

The input is an interest rate per period. The rate period should match the number-of-periods and deposit interval. An annual rate should only be entered directly when each calculation period represents one year.

Why does deposit timing change the result?

A beginning-of-period deposit is added before that period's growth is calculated, giving it an additional period to earn interest. An end-of-period deposit begins earning growth in the following period.

What happens if the interest rate is 0%?

No compound growth occurs. The calculation becomes:

FV = PV + n × PMT

The ending value is simply the starting amount plus all recurring deposits.

Can I calculate future value without periodic deposits?

Yes. If the periodic deposit is zero, the formula reduces to compound growth of the starting amount:

FV = PV(1 + r)ⁿ
Is the calculated future value guaranteed?

No. It is a projection based on the numbers entered. Real investment returns may change and can also be affected by losses, fees, taxes, inflation, and contribution changes.

Why might another future value calculator give a different answer?

Check whether both calculators use the same periodic rate, number of periods, deposit amount, deposit timing, and rounding method. A calculator using beginning-of-period contributions can produce a different result from one using end-of-period contributions.
