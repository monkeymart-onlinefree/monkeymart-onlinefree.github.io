The Time Zone Calculator converts a specific date and local time from one time zone to another. Enter the date, local time, source time zone, and destination time zone to find the corresponding local date and time in the selected destination zone.

What Is the Time Zone Calculator?

The Time Zone Calculator helps you find what a local date and time in one time zone becomes in another time zone.

Instead of applying a fixed hour difference, the calculator uses named time zones and the time-zone information available through your browser or operating system. This allows the conversion to account for applicable daylight-saving and historical offset rules.

It can be useful for international meetings, travel planning, remote work, online events, calls, deadlines, and other situations where people are in different time zones.

How to Use the Time Zone Calculator

Enter the following information:

Date

Select the calendar date that applies to the time you want to convert.

Time-zone offsets can change depending on the date, so the date is an important part of the calculation.

Local Time

Enter the local clock time in the source time zone.

This is the wall-clock time that someone in the selected From time zone would see.

From Time Zone

Select the time zone in which the entered date and local time occur.

For example, a location may use an IANA-style identifier such as Asia/Karachi or America/New_York.

To Time Zone

Select the destination time zone.

The calculator converts the source date and time to the corresponding local date and time in this zone.

Formula / Calculation Method

A time-zone conversion does not always use one fixed difference between two locations. UTC offsets can change according to daylight-saving rules, government decisions, and historical time-zone changes.

The calculator therefore converts the source local time to a single UTC instant and then expresses that same instant in the destination time zone.

Main Formula

For an ordinary, unambiguous local time:

UTC Instant = Source Local Time − Source UTC Offset

Then:

Destination Local Time = UTC Instant + Destination UTC Offset

The important point is that both offsets are determined for the relevant date and instant using the selected time-zone rules.

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
Lₛ Source local date and time Date and time
Oₛ UTC offset for the source time zone Hours/minutes from UTC
U Resolved UTC instant UTC date and time
Oₑ UTC offset for the destination zone at that instant Hours/minutes from UTC
Lₑ Destination local date and time Date and time

The calculation can be represented as:

U = Lₛ − Oₛ
Lₑ = U + Oₑ

Calculation Sequence

  1. Read the selected date and local time.
  2. Interpret that wall-clock date and time in the selected From time zone.
  3. Determine the applicable source UTC offset using the time-zone data available to the browser or operating system.
  4. Resolve the source value to a UTC instant.
  5. Determine the destination zone's UTC offset for that same instant.
  6. Convert the UTC instant to the local date and time in the To time zone.
  7. Display the resulting destination date and time.

This method is important because the time difference between two places can vary during the year if their daylight-saving schedules differ.

Worked Example

Suppose you want to convert a time from Karachi to New York.

Inputs

  • Date: September 24, 2026
  • Local time: 3:00 PM
  • From time zone: Asia/Karachi
  • To time zone: America/New_York

Step 1: Resolve the Source Offset

For this example date, Karachi is UTC+05:00.

So:

UTC = 15:00 − 05:00

Step 2: Convert to UTC

UTC = 10:00

The source time therefore represents 10:00 UTC on September 24, 2026.

Step 3: Apply the Destination Offset

On this date, New York is using UTC−04:00.

Destination time = 10:00 − 04:00

Final Result

6:00 AM on September 24, 2026 in New York

The same instant is therefore 3:00 PM in Karachi and 6:00 AM in New York on this date. The applicable offsets can change on other dates because daylight-saving and time-zone rules are date-dependent.

Understanding Your Results

The main result is the local date and time in the destination time zone.

The Date Can Change

A conversion can move into the previous or next calendar day.

For example, converting an evening time from Asia to a time zone far to the west may produce a morning time on the same date or, depending on the locations and time, the previous date.

The Time Difference Is Not Always Fixed

Two locations can be a certain number of hours apart during one part of the year and a different number of hours apart later.

This happens when one location changes clocks for daylight saving while another location does not, or when their clock-change dates differ.

The Conversion Represents the Same Instant

Changing time zones does not change the moment in time. It changes only the local date and clock time used to represent that instant.

Current Time-Zone Data

As of September 24, 2026, the latest published IANA Time Zone Database release is 2026d, released on September 11, 2026. IANA updates the database when governments or regions change UTC offsets, daylight-saving rules, zone definitions, or related timekeeping information.

JavaScript internationalization standards require time-zone-aware implementations to use IANA Time Zone Database information for named time-zone identifiers and time-zone calculations. However, the exact data available to this calculator depends on the version included with the user's browser or operating system.

Because software updates may not adopt a new IANA release immediately, two devices with different time-zone database versions can occasionally produce different results after a recent government time-zone change.

Assumptions and Limitations

Browser and Operating-System Data

The calculator depends on the time-zone information supplied by the user's browser or operating system. It does not guarantee that the device has adopted the newest IANA database release.

Future Time-Zone Changes

Future conversions use the rules currently known to the installed time-zone database.

Governments can change time zones or daylight-saving rules after a future event has already been scheduled. IANA specifically notes that future predictions can become incorrect when governments change their rules.

Ambiguous Local Times

When clocks move backward, some local clock times can occur twice.

For example, a time such as 1:30 AM may correspond to two different UTC instants during certain daylight-saving transitions.

The calculator's available specification does not define a separate user-controlled option for choosing between two occurrences of an ambiguous time. For critical scheduling near a clock change, verify the intended UTC instant and current local rules.

Nonexistent Local Times

When clocks move forward, some local clock times may not occur at all.

A local time entered inside that skipped period can therefore require special interpretation by the underlying time-zone implementation. Avoid DST transition gaps for safety-critical scheduling unless the result is independently confirmed.

Historical Time-Zone Accuracy

IANA maintains extensive historical time-zone information, but it states that its database is not authoritative and that historical coverage, particularly before 1970, can be incomplete or uncertain in some regions.

Time-Zone Abbreviations Can Be Ambiguous

Short abbreviations such as CST or IST can refer to different time zones in different regions. IANA recommends more precise identifiers or numeric UTC offsets when ambiguity matters.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Time Zone Calculator account for daylight saving time?

Yes. The calculator uses the time-zone rules available through the browser or operating system, so applicable DST offsets can be reflected for the selected date.

The result still depends on how current the device's time-zone database is.

Why can the time difference between two cities change?

The UTC offset of one or both locations may change during the year.

If the locations start or end daylight saving on different dates, the difference between their local clock times can temporarily change.

Why can two devices give different time-zone results?

Different devices may contain different versions of the IANA time-zone data.

This is most likely to matter shortly after a government announces or implements a time-zone or daylight-saving change.

Can I convert a future date?

Yes. The calculator can apply the future rules currently contained in the device's time-zone data.

However, future rules can later change, so important future travel, meetings, or deadlines should be checked again closer to the event.

What happens if I enter a time during a daylight-saving clock change?

Some local times can be duplicated when clocks move backward or skipped when clocks move forward.

Because these times can be ambiguous or nonexistent, verify critical conversions around DST transition periods.

Is UTC affected by daylight saving time?

No. UTC itself does not move forward or backward for daylight saving.

Local time zones may change their offset relative to UTC.

Why are named time zones better than using a fixed UTC offset?

A fixed offset such as UTC−05:00 describes only a numerical difference from UTC.

A named time zone such as America/New_York can represent date-dependent offset changes and historical or future rules stored in the time-zone database.
