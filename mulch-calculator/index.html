What Is the Mulch Calculator?

A mulch calculator estimates the volume of mulch needed to cover a measured area at a chosen depth. It converts the geometric volume into practical purchasing units such as cubic yards, cubic feet, cubic meters, and bag counts.

How to Use the Mulch Calculator

Choose imperial or metric units, enter the length, width, desired mulch depth, and optional overage. If you buy bagged mulch, enter the bag volume so the page can estimate the number of whole bags to purchase.

Mulch Calculator Formula

Volume = length × width × depth. For imperial inputs: cubic yards = [length(ft) × width(ft) × depth(in ÷ 12)] ÷ 27.

Formula Variables

Length and width define the surface area. Depth must be converted into the same length unit before multiplying. Overage is applied after the base volume is calculated.

Calculation Example

A 20 ft × 10 ft bed at 3 in deep has 50 ft³ of base volume. Dividing by 27 gives about 1.85 yd³ before any optional overage.

Understanding Your Results

The main result is the purchase volume. Bag count is rounded up because stores sell whole bags. The overage result is intentionally separate so you can see the measured requirement and the planning allowance.

Important Factors

Real beds may contain curves, plants, edging, slopes, or existing mulch. Measure irregular areas in smaller rectangles and add them together for a better estimate.

Limitations

This calculator estimates geometric volume; it cannot predict settling, compaction, moisture content, or supplier-specific bag fill. Confirm product coverage guidance before ordering large quantities.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many cubic yards of mulch do I need?

Multiply area in square feet by mulch depth in feet, then divide cubic feet by 27 to convert to cubic yards.

How many cubic feet are in a cubic yard?

One cubic yard contains 27 cubic feet.

Should I add extra mulch for waste?

An optional overage can help cover uneven ground or measurement uncertainty. Use a percentage that fits your project rather than assuming one fixed value.
