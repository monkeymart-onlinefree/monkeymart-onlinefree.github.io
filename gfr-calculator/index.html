What Is a GFR Calculator?

This calculator estimates glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), a widely used index of kidney filtration, from serum creatinine, age, and sex. It uses the 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine equation recommended by the National Kidney Foundation and supported by NIDDK.

How to Use the GFR Calculator

Enter age, sex, serum creatinine, and the creatinine unit. If you enter µmol/L, the value is converted to mg/dL by dividing by 88.4 before the equation is applied.

2021 CKD-EPI Creatinine Formula

eGFR = 142 × min(SCr/κ,1)^α × max(SCr/κ,1)^−1.200 × 0.9938^Age × 1.012 if female. For women κ=0.7 and α=−0.241; for men κ=0.9 and α=−0.302.

G1 or G2 alone does not establish CKD without evidence of kidney damage.

Limitations

eGFR is an estimate, not a direct filtration measurement. Creatinine-only equations may be less reliable in unusual muscle mass, rapidly changing kidney function, or near drug-dosing/transplant decision thresholds.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which eGFR equation does this calculator use?

It uses the 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine equation for adults, which is race-free and uses age, sex, and standardized serum creatinine.

Can eGFR diagnose chronic kidney disease by itself?

No. CKD classification depends on persistence and other evidence such as albuminuria. NKF notes that eGFR below 60 or uACR at least 30 mg/g must be present for at least three months, or other kidney-damage markers must be present.

Why can creatinine-based eGFR be inaccurate?

Serum creatinine is influenced by factors beyond filtration, including muscle mass and diet. NIDDK notes that creatinine-cystatin C equations can be more accurate near important clinical decision points.

Is this calculator for children?

No. This implementation uses the adult CKD-EPI equation and requires age 18 or older.
