The GFR Calculator estimates eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate) for adults age 18 or older using age, sex used by the equation, and serum creatinine. It uses the 2021 race-free CKD-EPI creatinine equation and reports eGFR in mL/min/1.73 m².

What Is the GFR Calculator?

The GFR Calculator estimates how effectively the kidneys filter blood using serum creatinine together with age and sex. The result is an estimated GFR, not a direct measurement of filtration.

This calculator uses the 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine equation for adults and does not include a race coefficient. eGFR can help healthcare professionals assess kidney function, but it should be interpreted with other clinical information and kidney tests.

How to Use the GFR Calculator

Enter the following information:

  1. Age (18+) – Enter age in years. This equation is designed for adults age 18 and older.
  2. Sex used by the equation – Select female or male because the CKD-EPI equation uses different constants for each.
  3. Serum creatinine – Enter the creatinine result from a blood test.
  4. Creatinine unit – Select the unit that matches the laboratory result.

The CKD-EPI equation uses serum creatinine in mg/dL. NIDDK gives the conversion:

Creatinine in mg/dL = Creatinine in µmol/L ÷ 88.4

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The calculator uses the 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine equation:

eGFR = 142 × min(SCr/κ, 1)^α × max(SCr/κ, 1)^−1.200 × 0.9938^Age × 1.012 [if female]

This is the current race-free CKD-EPI creatinine equation described by the National Kidney Foundation and NIDDK.

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit / Value
eGFR Estimated glomerular filtration rate mL/min/1.73 m²
SCr Standardized serum creatinine mg/dL
Age Age years
κ Sex-specific creatinine constant 0.7 female; 0.9 male
α Sex-specific exponent −0.241 female; −0.302 male
min Smaller value of SCr/κ or 1 —
max Larger value of SCr/κ or 1 —

For females, the final equation also includes a multiplier of 1.012.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Convert serum creatinine to mg/dL when necessary.
  2. Select the appropriate κ and α values.
  3. Calculate SCr ÷ κ.
  4. Use the smaller of that value or 1 for the min term.
  5. Use the larger of that value or 1 for the max term.
  6. Apply the corresponding exponents.
  7. Calculate the age factor, 0.9938^Age.
  8. Multiply all terms together.
  9. Apply the 1.012 female factor when applicable.

The final value is expressed as mL/min/1.73 m².

Worked Example

Consider a 50-year-old female with serum creatinine of 1.0 mg/dL.

Inputs

  • Age: 50 years
  • Sex used by equation: Female
  • Serum creatinine: 1.0 mg/dL
  • κ: 0.7
  • α: −0.241

Step 1: Calculate the Creatinine Ratio

SCr ÷ κ = 1.0 ÷ 0.7 = 1.428571

Therefore:

min(1.428571, 1) = 1
max(1.428571, 1) = 1.428571

Step 2: Apply the Formula

eGFR = 142 × 1^−0.241 × 1.428571^−1.200 × 0.9938^50 × 1.012

The main intermediate values are approximately:

  • 1.428571^−1.200 = 0.651805
  • 0.9938^50 = 0.732740

Step 3: Calculate the Result

eGFR ≈ 68.63 mL/min/1.73 m²

Final Result

The estimated GFR is approximately:

68.6 mL/min/1.73 m²

This value is an estimate and should not by itself be interpreted as a diagnosis of chronic kidney disease.

Understanding Your Results

eGFR is reported in mL/min/1.73 m². Lower values generally indicate lower estimated kidney filtration, but a single result does not provide the complete picture of kidney health.

Common GFR categories are:

GFR Category eGFR (mL/min/1.73 m²)
G1 90 or higher
G2 60–89
G3a 45–59
G3b 30–44
G4 15–29
G5 Below 15

Being in the G1 or G2 range does not automatically mean CKD. In the absence of other evidence of kidney damage, G1 and G2 alone do not meet CKD criteria.

CKD generally requires evidence lasting 3 months or longer, such as an eGFR below 60 or markers of kidney damage such as elevated urine albumin.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator provides an estimated, not directly measured, GFR.

The 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine equation is intended for adults and is designed for standardized creatinine measurements that are traceable to IDMS reference methods.

Creatinine-based eGFR may be less reliable when serum creatinine does not accurately reflect kidney filtration. This can occur with unusual muscle mass or when kidney function is changing rapidly.

NIDDK notes that equations using both creatinine and cystatin C can provide greater accuracy than creatinine alone, particularly when an eGFR is close to an important clinical decision point such as some drug-dosing or transplant decisions.

The equation was derived primarily from populations in the United States and Europe, so accuracy can vary across other populations.

Do not use a single calculator result as a diagnosis or as a substitute for professional medical assessment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does eGFR mean?

eGFR means estimated glomerular filtration rate. It estimates kidney filtration using laboratory and demographic information rather than directly measuring GFR.

Does this GFR Calculator use race?

No. It uses the 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine equation, which does not contain a race coefficient.

Can this calculator be used for children?

This calculator is intended for people 18 years or older. Pediatric kidney-function assessment uses different equations.

What creatinine unit should I use?

Use the unit reported by the laboratory. The CKD-EPI formula is written using creatinine in mg/dL. NIDDK states that µmol/L can be converted to mg/dL by dividing by 88.4.

Why might this result differ from my laboratory's eGFR?

A laboratory may use a different GFR equation, rounding method, creatinine assay, or calculation standard. Some laboratories may also use older equations. The formula used should be checked when comparing results.

Does a low eGFR automatically mean chronic kidney disease?

Not from one result alone. CKD generally involves persistent reduced kidney function or kidney-damage markers for at least 3 months. Urine albumin and other clinical findings may also be important.

Is creatinine-based eGFR always accurate?

No. eGFR is an estimate. Accuracy may be reduced when creatinine is affected by factors unrelated to kidney filtration. When greater accuracy is needed, a healthcare professional may consider cystatin C or measured GFR.
