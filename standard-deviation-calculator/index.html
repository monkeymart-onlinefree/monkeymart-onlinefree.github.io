Standard Deviation Calculator

Use the Standard Deviation Calculator to measure how spread out a set of numbers is around its mean. Enter your numbers and choose whether the dataset represents a sample or a population to calculate standard deviation, variance, mean, count, sum, standard error, and an approximate 95% margin.

What Is the Standard Deviation Calculator?

The Standard Deviation Calculator measures the variability of numerical data. A smaller standard deviation means the values are generally closer to the mean, while a larger standard deviation indicates greater spread.

The calculator supports both population standard deviation and sample standard deviation, which use different denominators in their formulas. It is useful for statistics, research, data analysis, education, quality measurement, and other tasks where the spread of numerical values matters.

How to Use the Standard Deviation Calculator

  1. Enter Numbers: Enter the numerical values you want to analyze.
  2. Choose Dataset Type: Select Population if your values represent the entire group of interest, or Sample if they represent a subset used to estimate a larger population.
  3. Calculate: Run the calculation to see the standard deviation and supporting statistics.

Choosing the correct dataset type is important because population calculations divide the squared deviations by the total number of values, while sample calculations divide by one less than the sample size.

Formula / Calculation Method

Population Standard Deviation Formula

When the entered values represent the complete population:

σ=∑(xi−μ)2N\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x_i-\mu)^2}{N}}

Sample Standard Deviation Formula

When the entered values represent a sample:

s=∑(xi−xˉ)2n−1s = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x_i-\bar{x})^2}{n-1}}

The sample formula uses n−1n-1 rather than nn, while the population formula uses the full population count NN.

Calculation Sequence

The calculator follows the selected sample or population method:

  1. Count the entered values.
  2. Calculate their sum.
  3. Calculate the arithmetic mean.
  4. Subtract the mean from each value.
  5. Square each deviation from the mean.
  6. Add the squared deviations.
  7. Divide by NN for a population or n−1n-1 for a sample.
  8. Take the square root to obtain standard deviation.

Mean

The arithmetic mean is:

Mean=∑xin\text{Mean}=\frac{\sum x_i}{n}

For a population, the mean is commonly written as μ\mu. For a sample, it is written as xˉ\bar{x}.

Variance

Variance is the squared standard deviation.

For a population:

σ2=∑(xi−μ)2N\sigma^2=\frac{\sum(x_i-\mu)^2}{N}

For a sample:

s2=∑(xi−xˉ)2n−1s^2=\frac{\sum(x_i-\bar{x})^2}{n-1}

Standard deviation is the square root of variance. Variance therefore has squared units, while standard deviation returns to the same unit as the original data.

Sum

The sum is:

Sum=∑xi\text{Sum}=\sum x_i

It is the total of all numbers entered into the calculator.

Count

Count is the total number of values in the dataset:

Count=n\text{Count}=n

Standard Error

For the standard error of the mean, the calculation is based on standard deviation divided by the square root of the number of observations:

SE=SDnSE=\frac{SD}{\sqrt{n}}

For sample data, the estimated standard error is commonly written as:

SE=snSE=\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}

This measures the estimated sampling variability of the mean.

Approximate 95% Margin

The calculator uses the following simple normal approximation:

\text{Approx. 95% Margin}=1.96\times SE

The value 1.96 is the standard normal critical value associated with a central 95% range. This is an approximation rather than a universal confidence-interval method.

Understanding Your Results

Standard Deviation

Standard deviation shows how spread out the values are around their mean. It is expressed in the same unit as the original data.

A value of zero means every entered number is identical. Larger values indicate greater spread.

Variance

Variance measures spread using squared deviations from the mean. Because the deviations are squared, variance is expressed in squared units.

Mean

The mean is the arithmetic average of all entered values.

Count

Count shows how many numbers were included in the calculation.

Sum

Sum is the total of all entered numbers.

Standard Error

Standard error describes the sampling variability of the mean. In general, it becomes smaller as the number of observations increases when other factors remain similar.

Approximate 95% Margin

The approximate 95% margin equals 1.96 × standard error. It provides a simple normal-approximation margin, but it should not automatically be treated as a formal confidence interval in every situation.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator uses different formulas for sample and population datasets, so the correct dataset type must be selected.

A population calculation divides by NN, while a sample calculation divides by n−1n-1. Because of the n−1n-1 denominator, sample standard deviation requires at least two observations.

Standard deviation cannot be negative. If every entered value is identical, the variance and standard deviation are both zero.

When reproducing a result manually, keep sufficient decimal precision during intermediate steps and round only the final result to reduce rounding differences.

The displayed approximate 95% margin uses 1.96 × standard error. This is a normal approximation. Formal statistical inference may require additional assumptions and may use a t critical value, especially when the population standard deviation is unknown or the sample is small.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between sample and population standard deviation?

Population standard deviation is used when the dataset contains the entire population being studied and divides variance by NN. Sample standard deviation is used for a sample from a larger population and divides by n−1n-1.

Why does sample standard deviation use n − 1?

Using n−1n-1 accounts for the lost degree of freedom when the sample mean is estimated from the same data and is the standard formula for sample variance and standard deviation.

Can standard deviation be zero?

Yes. Standard deviation is zero when every number in the dataset is identical because none of the values differs from the mean.

Can standard deviation be negative?

No. Variance is based on squared deviations, and standard deviation is its square root, so standard deviation cannot be negative.

What is the difference between variance and standard deviation?

Variance is the average squared deviation from the mean using the appropriate sample or population denominator. Standard deviation is the square root of variance and is expressed in the same unit as the original values.

What does standard error mean?

Standard error measures the estimated variability of a sample mean across repeated samples. For sample data, it is commonly estimated as s/ns/\sqrt{n}.

Is 1.96 × standard error always an exact 95% confidence margin?

No. The calculator reports it as an approximate normal-based 95% margin. Statistical confidence intervals can require different critical values or assumptions, particularly for small samples or when the population standard deviation is unknown.
