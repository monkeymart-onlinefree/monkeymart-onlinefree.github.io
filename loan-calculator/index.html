Use the Loan Calculator to estimate the monthly payment for a fixed-rate amortized loan. Enter the loan amount, annual interest rate, term in years, and any additional months to calculate the required monthly payment.

What Is the Loan Calculator?

The Loan Calculator estimates the fixed monthly payment needed to repay a loan over a specified term at a constant annual interest rate.

It can be used when comparing borrowing scenarios, planning a budget, or checking how changes in the loan amount, interest rate, or repayment period affect the monthly payment. For a typical fixed-rate amortizing loan, the payment is designed to repay the principal and interest by the end of the term.

How to Use the Loan Calculator

Enter the following values:

Loan Amount ($)
Enter the principal amount being borrowed.

Annual Interest (%)
Enter the fixed annual interest rate as a percentage. For example, enter 7.5 for an annual rate of 7.5%.

Years
Enter the full number of years in the repayment term.

Additional Months
Enter any months remaining beyond the full years. For example, a term of 4 years and 6 months is entered as 4 years and 6 additional months.

The calculator converts the loan term into total monthly payments and calculates the required monthly payment.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

For a fixed-rate loan with monthly payments, the calculator uses:

M=P×r(1+r)n(1+r)n−1M=P\times\frac{r(1+r)^n}{(1+r)^n-1}

This is the standard fixed-payment amortization formula.

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
MM Monthly payment Dollars per month
PP Loan principal or amount borrowed Dollars
rr Monthly interest rate Decimal per month
nn Total number of monthly payments Months

Monthly Interest Rate

The annual percentage rate entered in the calculator is converted to a decimal monthly rate:

r=Annual Interest Rate100×12r=\frac{\text{Annual Interest Rate}}{100\times12}

For example, an annual interest rate of 7.5% becomes:

r=7.5100×12=0.00625r=\frac{7.5}{100\times12}=0.00625

Total Number of Payments

The calculator combines the years and additional months:

n=(Years×12)+Additional Monthsn=(\text{Years}\times12)+\text{Additional Months}

A term of 4 years and 6 months therefore contains:

(4×12)+6=54(4\times12)+6=54

monthly payments.

Zero-Interest Loan

When the annual interest rate is 0%, the standard formula would involve division by zero. The calculator instead uses:

M=PnM=\frac{P}{n}

The principal is simply divided equally across the total number of payments.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Convert the annual interest percentage to a decimal monthly rate.
  2. Convert years and additional months into the total number of monthly payments.
  3. If the interest rate is greater than zero, apply the fixed loan payment formula.
  4. If the interest rate is zero, divide the principal by the number of payments.
  5. Return the estimated monthly payment.

Worked Example

Suppose you borrow $25,000 at a fixed annual interest rate of 7.5% for 4 years and 6 months.

Inputs

  • Loan amount: $25,000
  • Annual interest rate: 7.5%
  • Years: 4
  • Additional months: 6

Step 1: Calculate the Monthly Rate

r=7.5100×12r=\frac{7.5}{100\times12} r=0.00625r=0.00625

Step 2: Calculate the Number of Payments

n=(4×12)+6n=(4\times12)+6 n=54n=54

Step 3: Apply the Formula

M=25,000×0.00625(1+0.00625)54(1+0.00625)54−1M=25,000\times \frac{0.00625(1+0.00625)^{54}} {(1+0.00625)^{54}-1}

Using full precision:

(1.00625)54≈1.39996843(1.00625)^{54}\approx1.39996843

Therefore:

M≈546.905832M\approx546.905832

Final Result

The estimated monthly payment is approximately:

$546.91\boxed{\\$546.91}

The example keeps full precision during the calculation and rounds only the final displayed result.

Understanding Your Results

Monthly Payment

The monthly payment is the estimated amount required each month to repay the entered loan amount over the selected term at the entered fixed interest rate.

With an amortizing loan, each payment generally contains both principal and interest. Earlier payments typically contain a larger interest portion, while more of later payments goes toward principal as the outstanding balance falls.

The result is an estimate rather than a lender quote. Actual required payments can differ when a loan includes fees, changing interest rates, different payment schedules, lender-specific rounding, missed payments, or other contractual terms.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator assumes:

  • The interest rate remains fixed for the entire loan term.
  • Payments are made regularly each month.
  • The entered annual interest rate is converted to a monthly rate by dividing it by 12.
  • The repayment term consists of the entered years plus additional months.
  • No loan fees are added to the calculation.
  • No missed or late payments occur.
  • No prepayment penalties are included.
  • The interest rate does not change during the repayment period.

The result may therefore differ from an official loan agreement or lender calculation when the actual loan uses different terms, fees, payment timing, or rate rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

What formula does the Loan Calculator use?

For a fixed-rate loan with a positive interest rate, it uses:

M=P×r(1+r)n(1+r)n−1M=P\times\frac{r(1+r)^n}{(1+r)^n-1}

where PP is the loan amount, rr is the monthly interest rate, and nn is the number of monthly payments.

How are years and additional months handled?

The calculator converts both into one total repayment period:

n=(Years×12)+Additional Monthsn=(\text{Years}\times12)+\text{Additional Months}

For example, 5 years and 3 months equals 63 monthly payments.

What happens if the interest rate is 0%?

For a zero-interest loan, the monthly payment is calculated by dividing the loan amount by the total number of payments:

M=P/nM=P/n
Why can my lender's payment be different?

A lender may include fees or use terms and calculations that are not part of this calculator. Actual contracts can also involve different payment timing, rounding practices, changing rates, insurance, taxes, or other charges.

Does this calculator include loan fees?

No. The calculation assumes no additional loan fees.

Can I use it for a variable-rate loan?

The calculation assumes one fixed interest rate for the entire term. A variable or adjustable rate can change over time, so a single fixed-rate calculation may not represent future payments.

Does a longer loan term reduce the monthly payment?

With the same principal and fixed interest rate, spreading repayment over more monthly periods generally lowers the required monthly payment. However, extending an amortizing loan can increase the amount of interest paid over the life of the loan.
