What is mortgage amortization?

Mortgage amortization is the process of paying a loan down over time through scheduled payments. In a typical fixed-rate mortgage, the combined principal-and-interest payment stays the same, but the interest portion generally starts higher and the principal portion increases as the balance declines.

Mortgage payment formula

M = P × r(1+r)n ÷ ((1+r)n − 1)

P is principal, r is the monthly rate, and n is the number of monthly payments. Each month’s interest is the starting balance multiplied by the monthly rate; the rest of the scheduled payment reduces principal.

How to read an amortization schedule

The schedule separates each payment into interest and principal. Early in the loan, the balance is large, so more interest accrues. As principal is paid down, the interest charge falls and more of the same scheduled payment goes toward principal.

Calculation example

A $500,000 home with a $100,000 down payment creates a $400,000 loan. At a 6.5% annual rate over 30 years, the calculator uses 360 monthly periods to determine the scheduled payment and then iterates the balance month by month.

Limitations

This page models a fixed rate with scheduled monthly payments. It excludes taxes, insurance, PMI, HOA dues, rate changes, late charges, escrow adjustments, prepayment penalties, and lender-specific rounding. Extra payments are not included in this version.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does interest fall over time?

Interest is calculated on the remaining balance, so less interest is due as the balance decreases.

Does the monthly payment always stay fixed?

The principal-and-interest payment does for the constant-rate model, but total housing payments can change when taxes, insurance, or adjustable rates change.

What does “principal paid” mean?

It is the part of payments that reduces the amount borrowed. Principal repayment builds equity, while interest is the cost of borrowing.

Can I compare annual and monthly views?

Yes. Use the buttons above the schedule to switch between annual totals and the first 12 individual payments.
