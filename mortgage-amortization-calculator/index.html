Use the Mortgage Amortization Calculator to estimate your fixed monthly principal-and-interest payment and see how the mortgage balance changes over time. Enter the home price, down payment, loan term, and annual interest rate to calculate the financed principal and build an amortization schedule.

What Is the Mortgage Amortization Calculator?

A mortgage amortization calculator shows how a fixed-rate mortgage is gradually paid down through scheduled monthly payments. Each payment contains two parts: interest charged on the remaining balance and principal that reduces the amount owed.

With a typical fixed-rate mortgage, the principal-and-interest payment stays constant while the split changes over time. Early payments generally contain more interest because the outstanding balance is higher. As the balance declines, less interest is charged and more of the payment goes toward principal.

This calculator is useful for comparing different home prices, down payments, loan terms, and interest rates before reviewing an actual lender quote.

How to Use the Mortgage Amortization Calculator

Enter the following four values:

  1. Home Price ($): Enter the purchase price of the home.
  2. Down Payment ($): Enter the amount you plan to pay upfront. The calculator subtracts this amount from the home price to determine the mortgage principal.
  3. Loan Term (years): Enter the number of years over which the mortgage will be repaid.
  4. Annual Interest Rate (%): Enter the annual mortgage interest rate as a percentage.

The calculator then estimates the fixed monthly principal-and-interest payment and generates the supporting amortization values.

Formula / Calculation Method

Loan Principal

The amount financed is calculated first:

P = H − D

Where:

  • P = mortgage principal, in dollars
  • H = home price, in dollars
  • D = down payment, in dollars

Monthly Interest Rate

The annual percentage rate entered by the user is converted to a decimal and then divided by 12:

r = (Annual Interest Rate ÷ 100) ÷ 12

Where:

  • r = monthly interest rate as a decimal

Number of Payments

The loan term is converted from years to monthly payment periods:

n = Loan Term × 12

Where:

  • n = total number of monthly payments

Main Mortgage Payment Formula

The fixed monthly principal-and-interest payment is:

M = P × [r(1 + r)ⁿ] ÷ [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1]

Where:

  • M = monthly principal-and-interest payment
  • P = mortgage principal
  • r = monthly interest rate
  • n = total number of monthly payments

This is the standard fully amortizing fixed-payment approach. The payment is determined from the loan amount, interest rate, and loan term so that scheduled payments reduce the balance over the full term.

Calculation Sequence

The calculator follows this sequence:

  1. Subtract the down payment from the home price to find the mortgage principal.
  2. Convert the annual interest rate to a monthly decimal rate.
  3. Convert the loan term from years to months.
  4. Apply the fixed-rate mortgage payment formula.
  5. Calculate each month's interest from the starting balance.
  6. Subtract that interest from the scheduled payment to determine the principal paid.
  7. Subtract the principal paid from the balance.
  8. Repeat the process for each scheduled monthly payment.

Monthly Interest

For each payment period:

Interestₜ = Starting Balanceₜ × r

Monthly Principal

The principal portion of the payment is:

Principalₜ = M − Interestₜ

Ending Mortgage Balance

The remaining balance after each payment is:

Ending Balanceₜ = Starting Balanceₜ − Principalₜ

The ending balance becomes the next month's starting balance.

Total Interest

Total mortgage interest can be obtained by adding the interest charged across all scheduled payment periods:

Total Interest = Interest₁ + Interest₂ + ... + Interestₙ

For a fully amortizing fixed-rate calculation without additional costs, this is also equivalent to the total scheduled principal-and-interest payments minus the original mortgage principal, subject to any rounding applied during calculation.

Worked Example

Suppose a home has the following financing details.

Inputs

  • Home price: $300,000
  • Down payment: $60,000
  • Loan term: 30 years
  • Annual interest rate: 6%

Step 1: Calculate the Mortgage Principal

P = $300,000 − $60,000
P = $240,000

Step 2: Calculate the Monthly Rate

r = (6 ÷ 100) ÷ 12
r = 0.005

The monthly rate is 0.5%, or 0.005 as a decimal.

Step 3: Calculate the Number of Payments

n = 30 × 12
n = 360 payments

Step 4: Calculate the Monthly Payment

M = 240,000 × [0.005(1.005)³⁶⁰] ÷ [(1.005)³⁶⁰ − 1]

This gives a monthly principal-and-interest payment of approximately:

$1,438.92 per month

Step 5: Calculate the First Month's Interest

Interest = $240,000 × 0.005
Interest = $1,200.00

Step 6: Calculate the First Month's Principal

Principal = $1,438.92 − $1,200.00
Principal ≈ $238.92

Step 7: Calculate the New Balance

New Balance = $240,000 − $238.92
New Balance ≈ $239,761.08

The same process continues each month. As the remaining balance declines, the interest portion decreases and more of the fixed payment is applied to principal.

Using full calculation precision, the estimated total interest over 360 scheduled payments is approximately $278,011.65.

Understanding Your Results

Mortgage Principal

This is the amount being financed after subtracting the down payment from the home price.

For example, a $300,000 home with a $60,000 down payment produces a $240,000 mortgage principal.

Monthly Principal-and-Interest Payment

This is the scheduled fixed payment calculated from the mortgage principal, annual interest rate, and loan term.

It covers only principal and interest in this calculator. A borrower's actual total monthly mortgage payment may also include property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, or other charges.

Interest Portion

The interest amount is calculated from the balance outstanding at the beginning of each monthly period.

Because the balance is usually largest near the start of the mortgage, the interest portion is generally larger during the early part of the amortization schedule.

Principal Portion

Principal is the part of the payment that directly reduces the mortgage balance.

As monthly interest falls over time, a larger share of the fixed payment is normally applied to principal.

Remaining Balance

The remaining balance shows how much mortgage principal is still unpaid after each scheduled payment.

Total Interest

Total interest represents the cumulative interest paid across the modeled mortgage term. It does not include taxes, insurance, PMI, HOA charges, lender fees, penalties, or other housing expenses.

Amortization Schedule

The amortization schedule shows how the loan changes payment by payment, including the amount assigned to interest, the amount assigned to principal, and the remaining balance.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator is designed as a fixed-rate mortgage planning tool. Its results are estimates based on the values entered.

It assumes a fixed annual interest rate and scheduled monthly payments throughout the full loan term.

The calculation does not include:

  • Property taxes
  • Homeowners insurance
  • Private mortgage insurance (PMI)
  • HOA dues
  • Adjustable or changing interest rates
  • Late charges
  • Escrow adjustments
  • Prepayment penalties
  • Extra principal payments
  • Lender-specific fees
  • Lender-specific rounding methods

The calculator also does not model extra payments in its current version.

Actual lender figures can differ because lenders may apply specific payment dates, rounding procedures, servicing rules, escrow calculations, fees, or other contractual terms. CFPB also notes that a borrower's total mortgage payment can be higher than the principal-and-interest amount because taxes, insurance, and mortgage insurance may be included.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a mortgage amortization calculator show?

It estimates the fixed monthly principal-and-interest payment and shows how each payment is divided between interest and principal as the mortgage balance declines.

How is the mortgage loan amount calculated?

The financed principal is calculated by subtracting the down payment from the home price:

Mortgage Principal = Home Price − Down Payment
Why does more of my payment go toward interest at the beginning?

Interest is calculated from the remaining mortgage balance. Because that balance is largest near the beginning of the loan, the interest charge is also higher. As principal is repaid, the balance and monthly interest charge decrease.

Does the calculator include property taxes and insurance?

No. This CalcPro version calculates the mortgage using home price, down payment, term, and interest rate. Property taxes, homeowners insurance, PMI, HOA costs, and similar expenses are excluded.

Can I calculate extra mortgage payments with this calculator?

No. Extra payments are not included in this version. Use a mortgage payoff or amortization calculator designed for additional principal payments if you want to compare accelerated repayment scenarios.

Why might my lender's monthly amount be different?

A lender's figures may include taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance, fees, escrow adjustments, or lender-specific rounding and servicing methods. The calculator's monthly result represents the modeled principal-and-interest payment only.

Does a larger down payment reduce mortgage interest?

A larger down payment reduces the mortgage principal when the home price, interest rate, and loan term remain unchanged. Because interest is then calculated on a smaller financed balance, the modeled monthly payment and total interest will also be lower.
