Use the Body Surface Area Calculator to estimate your body surface area (BSA) from height and weight. The main result uses the Mosteller formula, while additional estimates let you compare the Du Bois, Haycock, Gehan & George, and Schlich methods.

What Is the Body Surface Area Calculator?

Body surface area is an estimate of the total external surface area of the human body, usually expressed in square meters (m²). BSA is derived from body measurements rather than measured directly in routine practice.

BSA is used in several clinical calculations, including some medication-dosing methods and body-size-indexed physiological measurements. Different BSA equations can produce slightly different estimates because they were developed from different populations and mathematical models.

How to Use the Body Surface Area Calculator

Enter the information requested by the calculator:

  1. Units: Select the measurement format you want to use for height and weight.
  2. Sex for Schlich formula: Choose the applicable sex used by the Schlich comparison equation. Sex does not change the primary Mosteller calculation.
  3. Height: Enter the person's height.
  4. Weight: Enter the person's body weight.

The published equations below use height in centimeters and weight in kilograms.

Height and weight should be positive, realistic values. BSA is an estimate and should be interpreted in the context in which it will be used.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

The primary calculation uses the Mosteller formula:

BSA (m²) = √[(H × W) / 3600]

An algebraically equivalent form is:

BSA = 0.016667 × W⁰·⁵ × H⁰·⁵

Mosteller published this simplified method in 1987.

Variables

Symbol Meaning Unit
BSA Estimated body surface area m²
H Height centimeters (cm)
W Weight kilograms (kg)

Calculation Sequence

  1. Use height in centimeters.
  2. Use weight in kilograms.
  3. Multiply height by weight.
  4. Divide the result by 3600.
  5. Take the square root.
  6. Report the estimated BSA in square meters.

Comparison Formulas

The calculator also compares the Mosteller result with other published BSA equations.

Du Bois formula

BSA = 0.007184 × W⁰·⁴²⁵ × H⁰·⁷²⁵

Haycock formula

BSA = 0.024265 × W⁰·⁵³⁷⁸ × H⁰·³⁹⁶⁴

Gehan & George formula

BSA = 0.0235 × W⁰·⁵¹⁴⁵⁶ × H⁰·⁴²²⁴⁶

Schlich formula for men

BSA = 0.000579479 × W⁰·³⁸ × H¹·²⁴

Schlich formula for women

BSA = 0.000975482 × W⁰·⁴⁶ × H¹·⁰⁸

These equations use different coefficients and exponents, so their BSA estimates do not have to be identical. Published reviews document these differences among commonly used BSA formulas.

Worked Example

Suppose a person has:

  • Weight: 70 kg

Step 1

Apply the Mosteller formula:

BSA = √[(170 × 70) / 3600]

Step 2

Multiply height by weight:

170 × 70 = 11,900

Then divide by 3600:

11,900 / 3600 = 3.305555...

Step 3

Take the square root:

√3.305555... = 1.818118...

BSA ≈ 1.82 m²

Understanding Your Results

Mosteller BSA

This is the calculator's primary estimated body surface area. It is expressed in square meters (m²).

Formula Comparison Results

Du Bois, Haycock, Gehan & George, and Schlich may return slightly different values for the same height and weight. This happens because each equation was derived using a different mathematical model or study population.

The Schlich comparison is also sex-specific, which is why the calculator requests sex for that formula.

A difference between formulas does not by itself indicate that a measurement or calculation is wrong. The appropriate equation can depend on the clinical or research context.

Assumptions and Limitations

BSA is an estimated value, not a direct measurement of the body's physical surface.

The formulas rely mainly on height and weight and cannot account for every difference in body composition, proportions, age, or clinical condition. Research has documented meaningful variation among BSA equations, particularly when comparing different body sizes and populations.

The calculator should not be used by itself to select chemotherapy doses, prescription doses, or other medical treatment. Drug dosing may depend on the specific medicine, indication, kidney or liver function, treatment protocol, laboratory results, and professional clinical judgment.

NIDDK notes that BSA is also relevant when converting certain BSA-indexed kidney-function estimates, but BSA adjustment is not automatically required for every drug-dosing situation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does BSA mean?

BSA stands for body surface area. It is an estimated measure of the body's external surface and is normally reported in square meters (m²).

Which formula does this calculator use for the main result?

The main BSA result uses the Mosteller formula:

BSA = √[(height in cm × weight in kg) / 3600].
Why does the calculator show several BSA values?

Different published equations use different coefficients and exponents. Comparing them shows how the estimated BSA can vary depending on the selected mathematical method.

Why is sex requested?

Sex is used for the Schlich comparison formula, which has separate equations for men and women. It is not a variable in the primary Mosteller formula.

Is BSA the same as BMI?

No. BSA estimates total body surface area and is expressed in m². BMI compares body weight with height squared and is expressed in kg/m². The two measurements serve different purposes.

Can I use the BSA result to calculate a medication dose?

Do not use an online BSA estimate alone to determine a prescription or chemotherapy dose. Medication dosing can require drug-specific protocols and other clinical information.

What does 1.73 m² mean in kidney-function calculations?

A BSA of 1.73 m² is commonly used as a reference value for indexing estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). It is a reference convention, not a target or universally normal BSA for every individual.
