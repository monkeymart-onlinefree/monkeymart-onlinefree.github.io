What Is an Ohm’s Law Calculator?

An Ohm’s Law calculator solves relationships among voltage (V), current (I), resistance (R), and electrical power (P) for an ideal resistive circuit. Enter two independent positive values and the calculator algebraically finds the other two.

Ohm’s Law Formulas

V = I × R
I = V ÷ R
R = V ÷ I
P = V × I = I²R = V²/R

The calculator converts millivolts, volts, kilovolts, milliamps, amps, ohms, kilo-ohms, mega-ohms, milliwatts, watts, and kilowatts to base units before solving.

Calculation Example

If a 12 V source is connected across a 6 Ω ideal resistor, current is 2 A and power is 24 W. Those results follow directly from I = V/R and P = V×I.

Understanding the Result

Voltage is electric potential difference, current is the rate of electric charge flow, resistance opposes current, and power is the rate of energy transfer. The equations here are internally consistent for a simple resistive model.

Limitations

Real components can be nonlinear, temperature-dependent, reactive, frequency-dependent, or subject to tolerances. AC circuits with inductance or capacitance can require impedance and phase calculations rather than resistance alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

What values can I enter?

Enter exactly two known values among voltage, current, resistance, and power. The calculator derives the remaining two for an ideal resistive relationship.

Does Ohm's law apply to every component?

No. Ohm's law in this simple form describes ohmic or resistive behavior where resistance is treated as constant over the operating range. Nonlinear components can require a different model.

What is electrical power in this calculator?

For the resistive relationships used here, power is P = V × I. Equivalent forms include P = I²R and P = V²/R.

Can I mix units such as mA and kΩ?

Yes. Each field has a unit selector. The calculator converts values to base SI units before solving, then reports common units.
