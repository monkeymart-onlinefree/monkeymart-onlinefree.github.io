Use the Right Triangle Calculator to find the missing measurements of a right-angled triangle from its two perpendicular sides. Enter leg a and leg b to calculate the hypotenuse, acute angles, area, and perimeter using standard geometry formulas.

What Is the Right Triangle Calculator?

Answer: A Right Triangle Calculator determines the dimensions of a triangle containing one 90-degree angle.

It uses the lengths of two perpendicular legs to calculate the third side, known as the hypotenuse, along with the remaining angles and geometric measurements.

This calculator is useful for geometry problems, construction measurements, technical drawings, and situations where two perpendicular distances are known.

How to Use the Right Triangle Calculator

Enter the two known side lengths:

  1. Leg a: Enter the length of the first perpendicular side.
  2. Leg b: Enter the length of the second perpendicular side.

Both values should be positive and expressed in the same measurement unit.

The calculator uses these inputs to determine the hypotenuse and supporting triangle measurements.

Formula / Calculation Method

The calculator uses the Pythagorean theorem as its primary calculation method.

Main Formula – Hypotenuse

For a right triangle:

c = √(a² + b²)

Where:

  • a: Length of the first perpendicular leg.
  • b: Length of the second perpendicular leg.
  • c: Hypotenuse, the side opposite the 90-degree angle.

The hypotenuse is always the longest side of a valid right triangle.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Square the length of leg a.
  2. Square the length of leg b.
  3. Add the two squared values.
  4. Calculate the square root of their sum to obtain the hypotenuse.
  5. Determine the remaining angles and geometric measurements from the calculated dimensions.

Acute Angle Calculations

A right triangle contains one 90-degree angle and two acute angles that add up to 90 degrees.

Using standard right-triangle trigonometry, the acute angles can be determined from the two legs.

For angle A opposite leg a:

A = tan⁻¹(a / b)

For the remaining acute angle:

B = 90° − A

Where:

  • A: Acute angle opposite leg a, in degrees.
  • B: Acute angle opposite leg b, in degrees.
  • tan⁻¹: Inverse tangent function.

The angle relationships follow standard right-triangle trigonometry.

Area of a Right Triangle

The two perpendicular legs act as the base and height.

Area = (a × b) / 2

Area is expressed in square units, such as cm², m², or ft².

Perimeter of a Right Triangle

The perimeter is the total distance around the triangle.

P = a + b + c

Where P represents the perimeter.

The result uses the same length unit as the two entered legs.

Worked Example

Suppose a right triangle has perpendicular side lengths of 6 cm and 8 cm.

Inputs

Input Value
Leg a 6 cm
Leg b 8 cm

Step 1: Calculate the Hypotenuse

Apply the Pythagorean theorem:

c = √(6² + 8²)
c = √(36 + 64)
c = √100 = 10

Hypotenuse = 10 cm

Step 2: Calculate the Acute Angles

For angle A:

A = tan⁻¹(6 / 8)
A ≈ 36.87°

For angle B:

B = 90° − 36.87°
B ≈ 53.13°

Step 3: Calculate the Area

Area = (6 × 8) / 2
Area = 24 cm²

Step 4: Calculate the Perimeter

P = 6 + 8 + 10
P = 24 cm

Final Result

Measurement Result
Leg a 6 cm
Leg b 8 cm
Hypotenuse 10 cm
Angle A 36.87°
Angle B 53.13°
Right angle 90°
Area 24 cm²
Perimeter 24 cm

The three interior angles total 180 degrees, confirming the expected angle relationship for a triangle.

Understanding Your Results

The calculated measurements describe different properties of the same right triangle.

Result What It Represents
Hypotenuse (c) The longest side, opposite the right angle.
Angle A The acute angle opposite leg a.
Angle B The acute angle opposite leg b.
Area The amount of two-dimensional space enclosed by the triangle.
Perimeter The combined length of all three sides.

Side lengths and perimeter use linear measurement units, while area uses squared units.

Angles are expressed in degrees.

For example, when both legs are measured in meters, the hypotenuse and perimeter are also expressed in meters, while the area is measured in square meters.

Assumptions and Limitations

The Right Triangle Calculator follows standard mathematical relationships under the following conditions:

  • Right-angle requirement: The two entered legs must be perpendicular, forming a 90-degree angle.
  • Positive side lengths: Both legs must have lengths greater than zero to form a valid, non-degenerate triangle.
  • Consistent units: Both measurements must use the same unit before calculation.
  • Geometric model: The calculations assume a standard right triangle in flat, two-dimensional Euclidean geometry.
  • Numerical precision: Floating-point arithmetic can introduce tiny representation differences, and displayed values may be rounded.
  • Input accuracy: Calculated dimensions depend on the accuracy of the entered measurements.

The calculator performs numerical geometry calculations rather than providing a formal mathematical proof.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Can I calculate a right triangle using only two sides?

Yes. When the two known sides are the perpendicular legs, their lengths are sufficient to determine the hypotenuse, both acute angles, area, and perimeter.

2. Does the Right Triangle Calculator use the Pythagorean theorem?

Yes. Its primary calculation uses the Pythagorean theorem, which states that the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the two perpendicular legs.

3. What happens if both perpendicular legs are equal?

The triangle is an isosceles right triangle. Its two acute angles are each 45 degrees, and its hypotenuse is the leg length multiplied by the square root of 2.

4. Can I enter different measurement units for leg a and leg b?

Both inputs should use the same unit. For example, if one side is measured in meters and the other in centimeters, convert them to a common unit before entering the values.

5. Why might the calculated hypotenuse contain decimal places?

The square root of the sum of the squared legs is not always a whole number.

For example, legs of 2 and 3 units produce a hypotenuse of approximately 3.60555 units.

6. Can this calculator solve a triangle that does not contain a 90-degree angle?

No. The calculation method assumes that the two supplied legs meet at a right angle. Other triangle types require different geometric relationships.

7. What is the difference between area and perimeter?

Area measures the space enclosed by the triangle and is expressed in square units. Perimeter measures the total length of its three sides and is expressed in linear units.
