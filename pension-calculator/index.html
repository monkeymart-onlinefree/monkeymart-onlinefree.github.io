What Is a Pension Calculator?

A pension calculator compares retirement-income choices by converting future pension payments into present values under explicit assumptions. This page includes three common planning questions: lump sum versus monthly pension, single-life versus joint-and-survivor income, and retiring now versus working longer for a larger pension.

How the Pension Comparisons Work

The calculator projects monthly pension payments, applies the entered annual cost-of-living adjustment, and discounts future cash flows using the entered return/discount rate. For delayed retirement, future pension payments are also discounted through the waiting period.

Present Value Formula

Present value = Σ [Future pension payment ÷ (1 + monthly discount rate)^month]

A month-by-month cash-flow model is used because COLA changes and survivor periods are easier to represent directly than with a single closed-form formula.

Understanding the Lump Sum Comparison

If the modeled present value of pension payments is above the lump-sum offer, the cash-flow model assigns greater financial value to the pension stream under the entered assumptions. That does not account for investment risk, plan guarantees, liquidity needs, or mortality uncertainty.

Single-Life vs Joint-and-Survivor

The joint option models the lower joint payment while the retiree is alive and then the entered survivor percentage for any additional modeled years the spouse lives after the retiree.

Limitations

Real pension decisions can involve plan-specific elections, survivor rules, taxes, Social Security interactions, inflation, PBGC protection, health, longevity, and market risk. Review plan documents and professional advice before making an irrevocable pension election.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the lump-sum comparison work?

The calculator discounts projected pension payments using your entered investment-return/discount rate and compares that present value with the lump-sum offer. It is a mathematical comparison, not a recommendation.

What is a joint-and-survivor pension?

A joint-and-survivor option generally pays a lower monthly amount while the retiree is alive, then continues a stated percentage to the surviving spouse after the retiree dies, subject to the plan’s terms.

Does this model pension guarantees or insurer risk?

No. The calculator models cash flows only. Plan funding, PBGC protection, insurer strength, taxes, mortality uncertainty, inflation, survivor needs, and investment risk are separate considerations.
