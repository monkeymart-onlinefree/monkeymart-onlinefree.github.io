What Is an Annuity Calculator?

An annuity accumulation calculator estimates how a starting principal and recurring contributions can grow over time. It is useful for modeling the accumulation phase of an annuity-like cash flow before withdrawals begin.

How to Use the Annuity Calculator

Enter starting principal, annual and monthly additions, an annual growth rate, the number of years, and whether contributions occur at the beginning or end of each period. The calculator produces an ending balance and annual accumulation schedule.

Annuity Future Value Method

Monthly rate = (1 + annual effective rate)^(1/12) − 1

The page then simulates each month, adding contributions either before or after that month’s growth depending on the selected timing. Beginning-of-period contributions receive one additional period of growth.

Understanding Your Results

Total additions exclude the starting principal. Growth earned is the ending balance minus starting principal and all later additions. The schedule shows how compounding changes the balance over time.

Limitations

This is a mathematical accumulation model, not an insurance-contract quote. It does not include surrender charges, mortality and expense fees, caps, participation rates, guarantees, taxes, riders, or insurer crediting rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an ordinary annuity and an annuity due?

With an ordinary annuity, recurring deposits are made at the end of each period. With an annuity due, deposits are made at the beginning, so each recurring deposit generally earns one additional period of growth.

Does this calculator price an insurance annuity contract?

No. It models annuity-style accumulation mathematics. Insurance annuities can include fees, surrender charges, guarantees, riders, taxes, and insurer-specific crediting methods.

How is the monthly growth rate derived?

The calculator treats the entered annual growth rate as an effective annual rate and converts it to an equivalent monthly rate using (1 + annual rate)^(1/12) − 1.
