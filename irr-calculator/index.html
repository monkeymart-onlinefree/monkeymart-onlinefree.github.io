What Is Internal Rate of Return?

Internal rate of return (IRR) is the discount rate that makes the net present value of a sequence of cash flows equal to zero. For periodic IRR, the cash flows are assumed to occur at equal intervals such as monthly, quarterly, or annually.

IRR Formula

0 = Σ [ CFt ÷ (1 + IRR)t ]

There is usually no simple algebraic formula for IRR, so calculators solve the rate iteratively or numerically. This page scans a broad rate range for sign changes in NPV and then uses bisection to refine each detected root.

How to Use the IRR Calculator

Enter the initial investment as a negative number and later receipts as positive numbers. Additional investments should also be negative. Add or remove periods as needed, keeping the intervals consistent.

Why Can There Be More Than One IRR?

When a cash-flow series changes sign more than once, the NPV equation can have multiple valid roots. In that situation IRR by itself can be ambiguous. The calculator reports how many roots it detects in its scan range and shows the first detected root as the primary result.

IRR vs. XIRR

Use periodic IRR only when cash flows are evenly spaced. If actual dates are irregular, a date-aware calculation such as XIRR is more appropriate because timing differences affect the annualized result.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does an IRR of 10% mean?

For the entered equally spaced cash flows, a 10% IRR means 10% per period is the discount rate that makes their net present value equal to zero. The period must be interpreted consistently with the cash-flow interval.

Why does IRR require positive and negative cash flows?

A return rate is solved from an exchange of cash outflows and inflows. If every cash flow has the same sign, there is generally no discount rate that makes NPV equal zero.

Can IRR have multiple answers?

Yes. Non-conventional cash flows that change sign more than once can produce multiple IRRs. In that case, review NPV at relevant discount rates and use other investment measures as well.

CalcPro Method

IRR is solved as the root of the periodic NPV equation. The browser scans −99.9% through 1000% per period and refines sign-change intervals by bisection.

The scan can identify multiple roots, but no numerical method guarantees that every possible root of an arbitrary cash-flow polynomial will be found.
