What Is a Common Factor Calculator?

A common factor calculator finds positive integers that divide every number in a set with no remainder. A factor is also called a divisor. When several numbers share a divisor, that divisor is a common factor. The largest shared value is the greatest common factor (GCF), also called the greatest common divisor.

How to Use the Common Factor Calculator

Enter at least two positive integers separated by commas, then select Find common factors. The calculator first finds the GCF with the Euclidean algorithm, then lists the positive divisors of that GCF. Those divisors are exactly the positive factors shared by all entered numbers.

Common Factor Method

Common factors of a₁, a₂, …, aₙ = positive divisors of GCD(a₁, a₂, …, aₙ)

If a number d divides each input exactly, then it is a common factor. Finding the GCD first is efficient because every common factor must divide the GCD.

Understanding Your Results

The factor chips show every positive divisor shared by all inputs. “GCF” is the largest common factor. The per-number lines are a useful check: each shared factor appears in every individual factor list.

Limitations

This tool is intended for positive whole numbers, not decimals, fractions, algebraic expressions, or zero. Inputs are limited to one billion so the browser can enumerate factor lists quickly without freezing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a common factor?

A common factor is a positive integer that divides every number in a group with no remainder. For example, 1, 2, and 4 are common factors of 12 and 16.

Is the greatest common factor included?

Yes. The greatest common factor, or GCF, is the largest value in the common-factor list.

Can I enter more than two numbers?

Yes. Enter two or more positive integers separated by commas. A factor is returned only when it divides every entered integer.

Why is 1 always a common factor?

For positive integers, 1 divides every number exactly, so any group of positive integers has at least the common factor 1.
