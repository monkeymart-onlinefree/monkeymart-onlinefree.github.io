The Common Factor Calculator finds the positive factors shared by two or more positive integers. Enter your numbers to see their common factors, supporting factor lists, and greatest common factor (GCF).

What Is the Common Factor Calculator?

A common factor is a positive integer that divides each given number exactly, with no remainder. This calculator finds every common factor shared by the numbers and identifies the greatest common factor, which is the largest shared factor.

It is useful for checking divisibility, simplifying fractions, studying factors, and solving basic number-theory problems.

How to Use the Common Factor Calculator

Enter two or more positive integers.

Each input must be a positive whole number. The calculator does not support zero, decimals, fractions, negative values, or algebraic expressions. Input values are limited to 1,000,000,000.

After entering valid numbers, review the factor lists, common factors, and greatest common factor.

Formula / Calculation Method

Main Formula

For positive integers:

a₁, a₂, …, aₙ

first define:

g = GCD(a₁, a₂, …, aₙ)

The common factors are then:

Common Factors = {d ∈ ℤ⁺ : d divides g}

In other words, every positive divisor of the GCD is a common factor of all the input numbers.

The greatest common factor is:

GCF = g

The GCD of two or more positive integers is the largest positive integer that divides every input exactly.

Calculation Sequence

  1. The calculator checks the entered values as positive whole numbers.
  2. It determines the greatest common divisor of all input numbers.
  3. It finds the positive divisors of that GCD.
  4. Those divisors form the complete list of common factors.
  5. The largest common factor is the GCF.

This works because any number that divides every input must also divide their greatest common divisor.

Common Factors

A number d is a common factor when it divides every input without a remainder:

a₁ mod d = 0
a₂ mod d = 0
…
aₙ mod d = 0

Rather than checking every possible value against every input, the calculator uses the fact that all common factors are positive divisors of the GCD.

Greatest Common Factor

The GCF is the largest number in the common-factor set:

GCF(a₁, a₂, …, aₙ) = largest common factor

If the GCF is 1, the inputs have no common positive factor greater than 1 and are relatively prime as a group.

Worked Example

Find the common factors of:

24, 36, and 60

Inputs

  • First number: 24
  • Second number: 36
  • Third number: 60

Step 1: Find the GCD

The greatest positive integer that divides 24, 36, and 60 exactly is:

GCD(24, 36, 60) = 12

Step 2: Find the Positive Divisors of the GCD

The positive factors of 12 are:

1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12

Step 3: Verify the Common Factors

Factors of 24:

1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24

Factors of 36:

1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36

Factors of 60:

1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20, 30, 60

The factors present in all three lists are:

1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12

Final Result

Common Factors: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12

Greatest Common Factor: 12

Understanding Your Results

Common Factors

The common-factor list contains every positive integer that divides all the entered numbers exactly.

For example, if 2 appears in the common-factor list, every input is divisible by 2 without a remainder.

Greatest Common Factor

The GCF is the largest value in the common-factor list. It represents the largest positive integer by which all the inputs can be divided exactly.

Factor Lists

Supporting factor lists show the positive divisors of the relevant numbers. Comparing these lists can help you see why particular values are common factors.

A positive divisor or factor is an integer that divides another integer exactly.

Assumptions and Limitations

This calculator works with positive whole numbers only. It does not accept zero, negative numbers, decimals, fractions, or algebraic expressions.

Inputs are limited to 1,000,000,000 so factor lists can be generated efficiently in the browser.

The results are integer-based, so no decimal rounding is required. Every listed factor divides the applicable number exactly.

If one of the entered numbers is 1, the only possible common factor is 1.

If the numbers have no shared factor greater than 1, the result is a GCF of 1.

A common factor is a positive integer that divides every number in a group without leaving a remainder. For example, 2 and 4 are common factors of 8 and 12.

What is the difference between a common factor and the GCF?

Common factors include all positive factors shared by the numbers. The GCF is only the largest of those common factors.

For 12 and 18, the common factors are 1, 2, 3, and 6, while the GCF is 6.

Do two positive integers always have a common factor?

Yes. Every positive integer is divisible by 1, so two or more positive integers always share at least the common factor 1.

What does a GCF of 1 mean?

A GCF of 1 means the numbers do not share any positive factor greater than 1. Such numbers are described as relatively prime or coprime.

Can I enter decimals or fractions?

No. This calculator is designed for positive whole numbers. Decimals and fractions are outside its supported input domain.

Can I use zero?

No. The CalcPro Common Factor Calculator is designed specifically for positive integers and put numbers must divide their GCD. Once the GCD is known, finding its positive divisors gives the complete common-factor list.
