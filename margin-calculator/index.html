What Is a Margin Calculator?

A profit margin calculator shows how much of sales revenue remains after a stated cost. It also calculates markup on cost and the selling price required to reach a chosen target margin.

Margin and Markup Formulas

Profit = revenue − cost. Margin % = profit ÷ revenue × 100. Markup % = profit ÷ cost × 100. To solve for a selling price from a target margin, use price = cost ÷ (1 − target margin).

Example

If an item costs $60 and sells for $100, profit is $40, profit margin is 40%, and markup is about 66.67%. Margin and markup are different because they use different denominators.

Margin vs Markup

Margin compares profit with revenue. Markup compares profit with cost. A 50% markup does not equal a 50% margin: a $100 cost marked up 50% sells for $150, which produces a 33.33% margin.

Limitations

This is a basic gross-profit model. Businesses may need to include shipping, payment fees, payroll, overhead, returns, tax, discounts, inventory shrinkage, and other operating expenses before treating the result as net profit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Margin divides profit by revenue, while markup divides profit by cost. Because the denominators differ, the percentages are not interchangeable.

How do I price for a 40% margin?

Divide cost by 1 minus 0.40. For a $60 cost, $60 ÷ 0.60 = $100 selling price, producing $40 profit and a 40% margin.

Can margin be negative?

Yes. If cost exceeds revenue, profit is negative and the margin is negative. That indicates the stated sale price does not cover the entered cost.
