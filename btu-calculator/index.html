What Is a BTU Calculator?

This BTU calculator estimates the cooling capacity of a room air conditioner in British thermal units per hour (BTU/h). It uses ENERGY STAR room-size guidance for spaces with an 8-foot ceiling and then applies published adjustments for sunlight, occupancy, and kitchen use.

How the Estimate Works

The calculator first maps room square footage to a base cooling capacity. For example, ENERGY STAR lists 350–400 ft² at 9,000 BTU/h and 400–450 ft² at 10,000 BTU/h. The final recommendation is then adjusted for room conditions.

Heavy shade: −10%
Very sunny: +10%
More than 2 occupants: +600 BTU/h per extra person
Kitchen: +4,000 BTU/h

Calculation Example

A 400 ft² general room with typical sunlight and two occupants starts at 9,000 BTU/h. A very sunny version of the same room would be adjusted upward by 10% to about 9,900 BTU/h.

What Is a BTU?

The U.S. Energy Information Administration defines one British thermal unit as the amount of heat needed to raise one pound of water by 1°F. Cooling equipment uses BTU per hour to express a heat-removal rate.

Limitations

This is a room A/C sizing estimate, not a Manual J HVAC load calculation. Ceiling height, building envelope, windows, air leakage, climate, equipment efficiency, duct losses, and whole-home system design can materially change the correct capacity. For central HVAC sizing, use a qualified professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many BTUs do I need for a room air conditioner?

Cooling capacity depends on room area and conditions. This calculator starts with the ENERGY STAR room-size table, then applies its published adjustments for heavy shade, very sunny rooms, extra occupants, and kitchens.

What does BTU/h mean?

BTU per hour is a rate of heat transfer. Room air-conditioner cooling capacity is commonly expressed in BTU/h.

Why can an oversized air conditioner be a problem?

ENERGY STAR notes that an oversized unit can cool a room before removing enough humidity, which may leave the space cool but clammy.
