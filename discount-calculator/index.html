Use the Discount Calculator to find the price you pay after a percentage or fixed-amount discount. Enter the original price, choose the discount type, enter the discount value, and optionally add a second stackable percentage discount to calculate the final price, total savings, and effective discount percentage.

What Is the Discount Calculator?

The Discount Calculator helps you work out how much an item or service costs after a discount. It supports both percentage-off deals and fixed-amount discounts, plus an additional stackable percentage discount.

It is useful when comparing sale prices, coupons, promotional offers, and other deals where you want to know the actual price after the discount rather than calculating it manually.

How to Use the Discount Calculator

  1. Original Price: Enter the price before any discount is applied.
  2. Discount Type: Choose whether the first discount is a percentage off or a fixed amount off.
  3. Discount: Enter the value of the first discount. For a percentage deal, enter the percentage. For a fixed discount, enter the amount to subtract from the original price.
  4. Additional Stackable Discount (%): Enter a second percentage discount if another coupon or promotion applies after the first discount. Leave it at zero when there is no second discount.

The calculator then determines the discounted price and supporting savings information.

Formula / Calculation Method

Percentage Discount Formula

For a percentage discount:

Final Price = Original Price × (1 − Discount Rate)

The percentage is first converted to decimal form:

Discount Rate = Discount % ÷ 100

An equivalent calculation is:

Discount Amount = Original Price × Discount Rate
Final Price = Original Price − Discount Amount

For example, a 20% discount means you pay 80% of the original price.

Fixed-Amount Discount Formula

For a fixed discount:

Final Price = Original Price − Fixed Discount

The calculator does not allow a fixed discount to reduce the calculated price below zero.

Stackable Discount Formula

A second percentage discount is applied to the price remaining after the first discount:

Final Price = Price After First Discount × (1 − Second Discount Rate)

This means two percentage discounts are applied sequentially rather than simply added together. For example, 20% off followed by another 15% off produces an effective discount of 32%, not 35%.

Variables

  • O = Original price
  • d₁ = First percentage discount as a decimal
  • F = Fixed discount amount
  • P₁ = Price after the first discount
  • d₂ = Additional stackable percentage discount as a decimal
  • P₂ = Final price after all applicable discounts

For a percentage first discount:

P₁ = O × (1 − d₁)

For a fixed first discount:

P₁ = O − F

For an additional percentage discount:

P₂ = P₁ × (1 − d₂)

Total Savings

Total savings are the difference between the original price and the final discounted price:

Total Savings = Original Price − Final Price

Effective Discount Percentage

When the original price is greater than zero, the overall percentage saved can be calculated as:

Effective Discount % = (Total Savings ÷ Original Price) × 100

This is especially useful for stacked discounts because the effective percentage is usually different from simply adding the advertised discount percentages.

Calculation Sequence

  1. Read the original price.
  2. Identify whether the first discount is percentage-based or a fixed amount.
  3. Apply the first discount to the original price.
  4. Prevent a fixed discount from reducing the price below zero.
  5. If a stackable percentage discount is entered, apply it to the already-discounted price.
  6. Compare the final price with the original price to determine total savings.
  7. Express those savings as an effective percentage of the original price.

Worked Example

Suppose an item originally costs $250. It is on sale for 20% off, and you also have an additional 10% off stackable coupon.

Inputs

  • Original price: $250
  • First discount: 20%
  • Additional discount: 10%

Step 1: Apply the First Discount

Convert 20% to decimal form:

20 ÷ 100 = 0.20

Calculate the first discounted price:

$250 × (1 − 0.20)
$250 × 0.80 = $200

The price after the first discount is $200.

Step 2: Apply the Stackable Discount

Convert 10% to decimal form:

10 ÷ 100 = 0.10

Apply it to the $200 discounted price:

$200 × (1 − 0.10)
$200 × 0.90 = $180

Step 3: Calculate Total Savings

$250 − $180 = $70

Total savings are $70.

Step 4: Calculate the Effective Discount

($70 ÷ $250) × 100 = 28%

Final Result

  • Final Price: $180
  • Total Savings: $70
  • Effective Discount: 28%

Although the advertised discounts are 20% and 10%, their combined effective discount is 28%, not 30%, because the second discount is applied to the already-reduced price.

Understanding Your Results

Final Price is the amount remaining after the selected discounts have been applied. It does not automatically include sales tax, shipping, or other charges.

Total Savings shows how much less the calculated final price is than the original price.

Effective Discount Percentage expresses the total savings as a percentage of the original price. This makes it easier to understand the true overall discount when multiple offers are stacked.

When a second discount is used, it applies to the price after the first discount rather than to the original price.

Assumptions and Limitations

The calculator focuses on the discount calculation itself. It does not automatically include sales tax, shipping costs, coupon caps, minimum-spend requirements, product exclusions, membership pricing, cashback, loyalty rewards, or quantity-based discounts.

A retailer may also have rules controlling which promotion is applied first or whether two discounts can be combined at all.

A fixed discount is prevented from reducing the calculated price below zero.

Actual checkout totals can differ when a store applies additional fees, taxes, promotion restrictions, or its own rounding rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate a percentage discount?

Convert the discount percentage to a decimal and multiply it by the original price to find the amount saved. Subtract that amount from the original price.

For 25% off $100:

$100 × 0.25 = $25 saved
$100 − $25 = $75 final price
What is the difference between percent off and a fixed discount?

A percentage discount removes a percentage of the price, so the amount saved depends on the original price.

A fixed discount subtracts a specific amount. For example, $20 off removes $20 regardless of whether the original price is $50 or $200, subject to the calculator's zero-price floor.

How do I combine 20% off and 15% off?

Apply the discounts one after another.

After 20% off, 80% of the original price remains. The additional 15% discount means 85% of that discounted price remains:

0.80 × 0.85 = 0.68

You pay 68% of the original price, which means the effective discount is 32%.

Why don't stacked discounts simply add together?

Each discount uses a different price as its base. The first percentage is taken from the original price, while the second percentage is taken from the already-discounted price.

Because the second discount is calculated from a smaller amount, 20% followed by 15% is less than a straight 35% reduction.

Can a fixed discount make the final price negative?

No. If the fixed discount is larger than the price, the calculator floors the discounted price at zero rather than returning a negative purchase price.

Does the Discount Calculator include sales tax?

No. The calculator determines the discounted price before any separate sales-tax calculation. The taxable amount and treatment of coupons or discounts can depend on local rules and the type of promotion.

Why might the store's checkout price differ from the calculator?

The retailer may apply sales tax, shipping, minimum-purchase requirements, discount caps, exclusions, membership offers, different coupon ordering, or other promotion rules that are not included in this calculator.
